# Minimal desorption-rate diversity and an exact two-pool rebound criterion in source-free indoor air

## Finding

Consider a source-free, well-mixed air compartment reversibly exchanging one nonreactive contaminant with finitely many independent, unsaturated storage pools. If the pools have \(m\) distinct desorption rates, an airborne concentration that is initially *decreasing* can have at most \(\lfloor m/2\rfloor\) later intervals of strict increase. In particular, one rate class cannot produce a decline–rebound–decline event, no matter how many physical pools share that rate. Two distinct rate classes suffice, and for two pools there is an explicit necessary-and-sufficient inequality for the rebound. The minimum of two rate classes is attained by a rational-parameter, nonnegative-initial-state example whose rebound is proved with an exact rational error certificate.

This is a shape statement about airborne concentration after external emissions cease, rather than a claim that generic two-surface systems rebound or that secondary concentration maxima require a new pollutant source.

## Assumptions and scope

Fix an integer \(n\geq0\), an air-removal rate \(v>0\), and uptake/desorption coefficients \(a_j,b_j>0\) for \(1\le j\le n\). Write \(C(t)\) for airborne contaminant inventory per normalized air volume, and \(S_j(t)\) for surface-pool inventories in compatible units. For \(t\ge0\), with no external source or further injections,
\[
\dot C=-(v+\textstyle\sum_{j=1}^n a_j)C+\sum_{j=1}^n b_jS_j,
\qquad \dot S_j=a_j C-b_jS_j.
\]
The initial state is componentwise nonnegative and nonzero. A *rebound episode* means a connected open interval on which \(\dot C>0\) that is preceded and followed by intervals of \(\dot C<0\). Assume \(\dot C(0)<0\) when counting such episodes. Let \(m\) be the number of distinct values among the \(b_j\); for \(n=0\) put \(m=0\).

For the exact two-pool test, take \(n=2\) with \(b_1\ne b_2\), and write
\[
C(t)=A_1e^{-\lambda_1t}+A_2e^{-\lambda_2t}+A_3e^{-\lambda_3t},\qquad 0<\lambda_1<\lambda_2<\lambda_3.
\]
The strictly positive coefficient \(A_1\) is guaranteed by nonnegative, nonzero initial data. Define
\[
p=\lambda_1A_1>0,\quad q=-\lambda_2A_2,\quad r=\lambda_3A_3,\quad \kappa=\frac{\lambda_3-\lambda_1}{\lambda_2-\lambda_1}>1.
\]
These quantities are uniquely determined by the stated coefficients and initial data through spectral decomposition. Under \(\dot C(0)<0\), a rebound occurs **if and only if**
\[
q>0,\quad r>0,\quad 0<\frac q{\kappa r}<1,\quad
\frac{\kappa-1}{\kappa}\,q\left(\frac q{\kappa r}\right)^{\!1/(\kappa-1)}>p.
\]
In that case there is exactly one rebound episode and exactly two simple stationary points, a strict concentration minimum followed by a strict maximum. Equality in the final inequality instead creates a stationary shoulder but no increase; other cases have no rebound. No assertion about further processes, nonlinear sorption, or field calibration is made.

## Proof

**Reduction by distinct desorption rates.** For each desorption value \(b\), sum the pool masses and uptake coefficients in its rate class: \(S_b=\sum_{j:b_j=b}S_j\), \(a_b=\sum_{j:b_j=b}a_j\). Then \(\dot S_b=a_bC-bS_b\); the air equation depends only on these sums. Thus the observed output is generated by exactly one effective pool for each distinct rate; hidden differences within a class cannot affect \(C(t)\).

**Real, simple, strictly stable modes.** The reduced generator is similar through the positive diagonal matrix \(T=\operatorname{diag}(1,\sqrt{a_b/b})\) to the real symmetric arrowhead matrix
\[
Q=\begin{pmatrix}-(v+\sum_ba_b)&\sqrt{a_{b_1}b_1}&\cdots\\
\sqrt{a_{b_1}b_1}&-b_1&0\\\vdots&0&\ddots\end{pmatrix}.
\]
For every nonzero real vector \(z\),
\[
z^{\mathsf T}Qz=-vz_0^2-\sum_b(\sqrt{a_b}z_0-\sqrt b\,z_b)^2<0.
\]
The strict inequality follows because vanishing of every square forces \(z_0=0\) and then every \(z_b=0\). Hence the eigenvalues are strictly negative and real. They are simple: an eigenspace of dimension at least two would contain a nonzero vector with air coordinate zero. Since the pool diagonal entries \(-b\) are pairwise different and each arrowhead coupling is nonzero, no such eigenvector exists. The reduced concentration is therefore a linear combination of at most \(m+1\) distinct decaying exponentials.

**Zero count and late-time sign.** A nonzero linear combination of \(m+1\) real exponentials with distinct exponents has at most \(m\) real zeros, counted with multiplicity. Indeed divide by the slowest exponential, differentiate, use Rolle's theorem with multiplicities, and induct on the number of terms. Applied to \(\dot C\), this gives at most \(m\) zeros. The generator is irreducible and Metzler; its unique dominant eigenvalue has positive left and right eigenvectors. Since the initial state is nonnegative and nonzero, the dominant contribution to the air coordinate has a strictly positive amplitude, so \(\dot C(t)<0\) for all sufficiently large \(t\). Starting with \(\dot C(0)<0\), each strict-increase interval bounded by strict-decrease intervals uses at least two distinct derivative zeros. Thus the number of rebound episodes cannot exceed \(\lfloor m/2\rfloor\).

**Complete two-pool phase test.** For \(m=2\), factor the slowest exponential and introduce \(u=e^{-(\lambda_2-\lambda_1)t}\in(0,1)\):
\[
\dot C(t)=e^{-\lambda_1t}[-p+qu-ru^\kappa].
\]
The endpoint values at \(u=0\) and \(u=1\) are negative, by \(p>0\) and initial decline. If \(r\le0\), the bracket is convex or affine and cannot exceed its negative endpoint maximum; if \(r>0\) but \(q\le0\), it is strictly negative throughout. For \(q,r>0\), it is strictly concave with unique unrestricted positive maximizer \(u_*=(q/(\kappa r))^{1/(\kappa-1)}\). This maximizer lies in \((0,1)\) precisely when \(q/(\kappa r)<1\), and the maximal bracket equals \(-p+(\kappa-1)q u_*/\kappa\). Hence the displayed strict inequality is necessary and sufficient for two derivative zeros. Concavity makes both zeros simple, while equality gives a tangential zero only.

**Exact attained witness.** Take \(v=a_1=a_2=b_1=1/10\), \(b_2=1\), and \((C(0),S_1(0),S_2(0))=(2,5,0)\). Then
\[
M=\begin{pmatrix}-3/10&1/10&1\\1/10&-1/10&0\\1/10&0&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\dot C(0)=-1/10.
\]
Writing \(x_0=(2,5,0)^{\mathsf T}\) and \(e_1=(1,0,0)^{\mathsf T}\), the rational tenth-order Taylor approximation to \(\dot C(1)=e_1^{\mathsf T}Me^M x_0\) equals
\[
P_{10}=e_1^{\mathsf T}M\left(\sum_{k=0}^{10}\frac{M^k}{k!}\right)x_0
=\frac{138133084986931}{22680000000000000}>0.
\]
Since \(\|M\|_\infty=7/5\), \(\|x_0\|_\infty=5\), and \(e^{7/5}<5\), the matrix exponential remainder gives
\[
|\dot C(1)-P_{10}|<35\frac{(7/5)^{11}}{11!}<P_{10}.
\]
The strict positive derivative at \(t=1\), initial decline, and eventual negative derivative show that at least two stationary points exist. The established two-zero bound proves there are exactly two and therefore exactly one delayed rebound, without reliance on floating-point root finding. This also proves that two distinct desorption rates are the smallest number capable of a rebound under the stated source-free model.

## Verification

Run the standalone Python 3 standard-library script `python3 verify.py` in this directory. It reconstructs the rational generator, confirms the exact tenth-order derivative polynomial and the rigorous remainder inequality, checks an independent rational 75th-order derivative approximation at \(t=5\), and reports bounded derivative signs at the three observation times. The analytical all-parameter spectral and zero-count arguments above are independent of this finite script. The script uses exact `fractions.Fraction` arithmetic; it does not assume that sampled trajectories prove infinite-time behavior.

## Relationship to prior work

Shaw et al. (2023) motivate gas-to-surface removal, ventilation, and emissions in indoor box models, but their full indoor chemistry model is not the source-free reversible-star closure here. Singer et al. (2004) formulate measured reversible gas/surface exchange and a sequential embedded-sink alternative; their indexed text does not assert a bound controlled solely by distinct desorption rates or the necessary-and-sufficient two-parallel-pool inequality. Sherman and Hult (2013) discuss multiple storage classes and time scales; their exposed storage model and applications do not provide this exact concentration-rebound classification in the inspected sections. The exponential-sum zero bound and symmetrization are standard tools; no general Chebyshev or spectral theorem is claimed as new. The proposed contribution is their exact physical specialization, minimal heterogeneity witness, and full two-pool threshold. Uninspected older sorption analyses could overlap, so historical priority requires independent examination.

## Limitations

The pools are linear, independent, instantly mixed, and each couples directly to air; the model excludes chemical reactions, nonlinear saturation, explicit source pulses after time zero, outdoor concentration, surface-to-surface transport, and dynamically changing ventilation. An independent self-review is not a peer-review or external verification. A bounded zero-count does not assert the upper bound is attainable for every larger number of rate classes. A declining concentration can rebound without additional emissions only for suitable stored initial loads; distinct desorption rates alone do not guarantee it.
