# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The reversible-air system reduces exactly by identical desorption rates. A positive diagonal similarity produces a symmetric negative-definite arrowhead matrix, whose eigenvalues are simple because distinct pool desorption rates and nonzero couplings rule out an eigenvector with zero air coordinate. Its strictly positive leading air coefficient ensures eventual concentration decline for every nonzero nonnegative initial condition. A classical real exponential Chebyshev bound gives at most \(m\) derivative zeros and hence at most \(\lfloor m/2\rfloor\) rebound episodes after an initial decrease. For two distinct rates the derivative reduces to a strictly concave scalar curve with an exact if-and-only-if condition. The rational witness has \(\dot C(0)<0\), certified \(\dot C(1)>0\), and certified \(\dot C(5)<0\), so the upper bound is attained for two pools. The standalone standard-library script rechecks the rational certificate. Risks: the count does not assert sharpness at every larger dimension, and no finite sample replaces the all-parameter proof.

## Originality — PASS on inspected evidence

Comparisons included the full freely accessible 2023 indoor chemistry box-model article (Shaw et al.), material on model equations and observations from the 2004 furnished-room VOC paper (Singer et al.), and model construction and time-scale sections of the 2013 contaminant-storage report (Sherman and Hult). They document gas/surface exchange but do not, in inspected material, give the distinct-rate bound or exact two-parallel-pool phase boundary. Standard Chebyshev bounds and reversible spectral symmetrization are preexisting; only the motivated source-free sorption specialization and sharp two-rate onset are advanced as a finding. Related published mathematical records on delayed external Bateman injections, compartment exchange-rate optimization, and reservoir recession were compared by assumptions and implications rather than titles. Risk: the entirety of one plausible older sorption paper could not be reread on the accessible host, and unindexed older treatments may cover the result; priority is not guaranteed.

## Value — PASS

Source-free post-event air measurements need not decrease monotonically when contaminant remains stored in differently releasing surfaces. The sharp distinction between one and two desorption-rate classes separates a genuine heterogeneity mechanism from merely splitting the same surface into many numerical compartments. The exact criterion and rational witness allow models and interpretation of post-emission transient rebounds to be stress-tested without asserting empirical calibration. Risks: it is a linear, source-free, strictly coupled benchmark with no chemistry or nonlinearity; identification of actual surfaces from one airborne trace remains unproved.
