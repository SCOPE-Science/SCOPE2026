# Minimum local online-shattering witnesses in the pentagonal torus

## Finding
Let \(G=C_5\square C_5\) on \(V=\mathbb F_5^2\), with distinct \(p=(a,b)\) and \(c=(u,v)\) adjacent exactly when \((a-u)^2+(b-v)^2=1\) in \(\mathbb F_5\). For each \(E\subseteq V\), form the **self-restricted** Boolean concept class
\[
\mathcal N(E)=\{\{p\in E:p\sim c\}:c\in E\},
\]
where both the possible unknown centers **and the allowed adaptive query points** belong to the same subset \(E\).

The least size of \(E\) with \(\operatorname{Ldim}(\mathcal N(E))=3\) is eight. **Exactly 2,050** of the \(\binom{25}{8}\) eight-point sets attain this minimum. Under the coordinate-translation, reflection and coordinate-exchange group \((D_5\times D_5)\rtimes C_2\) of order \(200\), these sets form **12 orbits**, nine of size \(200\), two of size \(100\), and one of size \(50\). For 2,000 of the 2,050 sets exactly one member of \(E\) can serve as the root of a depth-three shattered decision tree; for the other 50 sets exactly two members can.

Every one of the 2,050 sets additionally has \(\operatorname{VCdim}(\mathcal N(E))=2\), hence yields a **minimum-size depth-three local strict online/static gap** \(\operatorname{VCdim}=2<3=\operatorname{Ldim}\). The explicit twelve orbit representatives and an eight-leaf decision-tree witness appear in the accompanying finite certificates. Exactly \(84\) eight-point supports containing \((0,0)\) admit a depth-three tree rooted at \((0,0)\).

## Assumptions and scope
A complete binary adaptive decision tree of depth \(h\) has a query from \(E\) at each internal node; each of its \(2^h\) label paths must be realized by some center from \(E\). The Littlestone dimension is the largest possible such depth. No centers or queries outside \(E\) are permitted. A Boolean trace of a concept on a fixed support \(S\subseteq E\) defines ordinary VC shattering; \(\operatorname{VCdim}\) is the largest shattered support cardinality.

All squares and equalities are evaluated over the prime field \(\mathbb F_5\). Since the solutions of \(x^2+y^2=1\) are exactly \((\pm1,0),(0,\pm1)\), the adjacency relation is the ordinary Cartesian pentagonal torus. Coordinate translations, independent sign changes, and coordinate exchange preserve it. Only this specified self-restriction is classified; the classification is **not** about retaining all 25 centers while restricting only queries, or conversely.

## Proof
**1. Sharp cardinality and root reduction.** Any complete binary decision tree of depth three has eight distinct binary answer paths, which must be realized by eight **distinct** Boolean concepts and hence by at least eight different center vertices. Thus \(|E|\ge8\). For \(|E|=8\), the positive and negative root branches each require at least four distinct centers. The positive centers are neighbors in \(G\) of the root \(r\in E\). Each vertex has degree exactly four; therefore the root and all four of its neighbors **must** belong to \(E\). With \(r=(0,0)\), the mandatory five-point set is \(\{(0,0),(1,0),(4,0),(0,1),(0,4)\}\). The remaining three vertices are chosen from exactly twenty, reducing the exhaustive question to \(\binom{20}{3}=1140\) cases for this fixed root. Translation symmetry gives every possible root in the full graph.

**2. Exact depth-two criterion.** Fix an eight-point candidate \(E\) and let \(M_q=\{c\in E:c\sim q\}\) for \(q\in E\). For each possible query \(q\), represent \(M_q\) as an eight-bit mask. For any nonempty version space \(B\subseteq E\), define \(T_0(B)\) to mean \(B\ne\varnothing\), and recursively
\[
T_h(B)\iff\exists q\in E:\ T_{h-1}(B\cap M_q)\ \wedge\ T_{h-1}(B\setminus M_q).
\]
This is exactly the definition of a complete depth-\(h\) tree, by induction, not a statistical approximation. A depth-two tree on a four-center version space \(B\) exists precisely when some \(q\in E\) separates \(B\) into two pairs and each resulting pair can be distinguished by some (possibly different) query in \(E\). In particular, a pair \(\{u,v\}\subseteq E\) is distinguishable exactly if \(\mathbf1_{u\sim p}\ne\mathbf1_{v\sim p}\) for some \(p\in E\).

**3. Complete finite certificate and count.** For each of the \(1140\) possibilities \(E=\{r\}\cup N_G(r)\cup X\), where \(|X|=3\) and \(X\subseteq V\setminus(\{r\}\cup N_G(r))\), evaluate \(T_2(M_r)\) and \(T_2(E\setminus M_r)\) using the exact criterion of Step 2. These two tests hold simultaneously for **exactly 84** choices of \(X\). Both standalone accompanying implementations reconstruct this count from the graph definition, one by explicit four-center pair partitions and one by the full recurrence \(T_h\), independently of the claimed 84.

Translate each rooted candidate by all 25 translations to obtain the complete set of \((E,r)\) satisfying the root condition: there are exactly \(25\cdot84=2100\) such **rooted pairs**. Discard the root and deduplicate the eight-element sets to obtain exactly \(2050\) distinct \(E\). Re-evaluating all eight potential roots on each such \(E\) shows exactly \(2000\) have one valid root and exactly \(50\) have two, confirming \(2000+2\cdot50=2100\). No candidate set outside this enumeration can support a depth-three tree, since Step 1 forces it to appear at one of these roots. The enumeration thus proves both necessity and sufficiency. `verify_census.cpp` and `verify_census.py` provide readable, independent, deterministic implementations with no external dependencies, while `witness_tree.json` gives a completely explicit eight-leaf witness.

**4. Symmetry and orbit completeness.** Generate all maps \((x,y)\mapsto(\epsilon_1 u+t_1,\epsilon_2 v+t_2)\), where \((u,v)\) equals either \((x,y)\) or \((y,x)\), \(\epsilon_1,\epsilon_2\in\{\pm1\}\), and \(t_1,t_2\in\mathbb F_5\). There are \(2\cdot2\cdot2\cdot25=200\) different maps and each preserves adjacency and local shattering. Partition the explicitly enumerated 2050 sets into orbits under these maps. Iteratively removing the orbit of the lexicographically first remaining set produces exactly twelve classes of sizes \(200\) (nine times), \(100\) (twice), and \(50\) (once), summing to \(2050\); `orbit_representatives.json` lists canonical representatives and their valid root counts. Both verification programs check the orbit-size census, and the Python implementation checks all twelve exact representatives.

**5. VC and strict gap.** The general fixed-radius conic shattering classification already gives \(\operatorname{VCdim}\le2\) on the entire \(\mathbb F_5^2\) under this open-neighborhood family, so restricting hypotheses and queries cannot increase static VC dimension. Independently, for each of the 2050 enumerated supports, the Python verifier tests every unordered query pair until it finds four distinct binary traces \(00,01,10,11\) realized by centers inside the same \(E\). Thus the VC dimension of **each** support equals exactly two. Step 3 supplies a full depth-three online tree for each set and Step 1 rules out a depth-three witness on fewer than eight points. Since each such \(E\) contains eight concepts at most, a depth-four tree (requiring 16 distinct concepts) is impossible. Hence its Littlestone dimension is exactly three, proving the claimed strict gap.

## Verification
Use a Python 3 interpreter and a C++17 compiler. With the two JSON certificates in the working directory, run `python3 verify_census.py`, then `c++ -O2 -std=c++17 verify_census.cpp -o verify_census` and `./verify_census`. Python reconstructs the incidence relation from arithmetic over \(\mathbb F_5\), enumerates rooted candidates, recomputes all viable roots, checks the full static two-query traces for all 2050 sets, reconstructs all 12 orbit representatives, and directly verifies each node-to-leaf trace of the supplied witness tree. C++ independently constructs the four-neighbor graph and tests the four-center splitting criterion; it checks the rooted count, total number, root multiplicities, and full symmetry orbit-size distribution. Successful outputs have identifiers `PYTHON_PENTAGONAL_CENSUS_PASS` and `PENTAGONAL_LOCAL_ONLINE_PASS` respectively.

The exhaustive bound is exactly \(\binom{20}{3}\), not a sample or timeout-truncated search. The mathematical reduction to this finite search is proved in Steps 1 and 2; the program implements the final finite enumeration and can be inspected or independently ported. All counts are exact integers. No inference about other grid sizes or other finite fields is made from the computation.

## Relationship to prior work
Adler, Benediktsson and Macpherson (2020) established foundational VC-dimension properties of neighborhoods on Hamming and Johnson graphs, including hereditary restrictions; these static statements do not enumerate self-restricted online witnesses in \(C_5\square C_5\). Fitzpatrick, Iosevich, McDonald and Wyman (2021) investigated fixed-radius finite-field distance predicates and the static VC dimension of restricted point configurations, but did not give this local Littlestone-minimizer census. Ascoli et al. (2022) study static distance-chain classifiers, not the same adaptive restriction.

Published exact results on fixed-radius circles determine the full-ambient static shattering polynomial (and its \(q=5\) VC dimension two), while results on four-point translation families determine adaptive dimension with **unrestricted query positions**, and results on Hamming-neighborhood online learning concern different graph classes. None of those statements supplies the count of eight-point vertex sets supporting depth three **when centers and queries are both restricted to the same eight vertices**, nor its symmetry orbit spectrum or valid-root multiplicity. These are comparisons of actual quantified domains, not just title differences. The comparison does not certify exhaustive historical priority under other graph-theoretic language.

## Limitations
The result is an exact finite classification for the 25-vertex graph, not an all-field or infinite-rank claim. The enumeration of 1140 rooted candidates is essential, and its results are supported by two source-available exact implementations, not a separate computer-formalized proof. Historical literature might contain an equivalent count under induced-subgraph decision-tree, incidence-matrix or identifying-substructure terminology that escaped the inspected sources. A complete external priority review and independent scientific audit have not been performed.
