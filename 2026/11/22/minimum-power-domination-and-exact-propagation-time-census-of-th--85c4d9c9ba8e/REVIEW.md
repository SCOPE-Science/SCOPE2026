The finite claim follows from the explicit modular graph construction, the exact synchronous forcing recurrence, and complete enumeration of every subset up to cardinality six. A second independent graph construction from fifteen labelled Fano planes and thirty-five triples verifies the \((50,7,0,1)\) parameters and reproduces the five- and six-sensor enumerations. All eight nonzero cells agree, including the absence of any successful five-set; no random sampling, unbounded extrapolation or assumed program output enters the conclusion. The proof is computational and can be reproduced from the standalone code.

The closest indexed result records concern five-cubes, generalized Petersen graphs and Kneser graphs, so their statements do not imply the new object-specific census. No identical table was found by direct object, parameter, property and alias searches. Full text of Smith–Hicks was inaccessible and an unindexed graph database may contain these values; there is no novelty guarantee beyond the searched material.

The Hoffman–Singleton graph is the unique \((50,7,0,1)\) strongly regular graph and a standard high-degree Moore-graph benchmark. Its exact minimum monitoring number, high multiplicity of optimal placements, induced-edge restriction and complete monitoring-delay distribution are motivated, reproducible invariants useful for testing propagation algorithms and distinguishing optimal sensors by latency. The result has finite scope and does not assert a general theorem about Moore graphs.
