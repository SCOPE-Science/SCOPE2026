# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The space is a union of the join of internal-edge path independence complexes with exactly two cone attachments per petal. A path independence-complex recurrence determines the three residue classes modulo three. Crucially, for a petal of length \(3k+2\), its spoke-link inclusion into the base is a homotopy equivalence: the missing endpoint adds a cone on the contractible complex \(\operatorname{Ind}(P_{3k-2})\). Classifying contractible, spherical and equivalence links then gives all four cases by actual homotopy cofibers. This includes triangular petals using the augmented empty-path convention. Since the proof is by simplicial homotopy equivalences, it proves the integral torsion-free wedge types. Separate finite chain calculations over three prime fields checked 48 small bouquets; no infinite claim rests on extrapolation.

## Originality — PASS relative to inspected evidence

Donovan–Scoville, arXiv:2207.13780, Theorem 5.8 gives the complete **homogeneous** Dutch-windmill classification. The newly claimed result is only the mixed-length formula with its four interacting regimes and exact criterion involving distinct petals. Bayer–Jelić Milutinović–Vega, arXiv:2411.04601, Theorem 2 establishes a general outerplanar wedge alternative but neither sphere dimensions nor mixed-length multiplicities. arXiv:2211.12559 studies different gluings. Semantic research-record searches for vertex bouquets, arbitrary unequal cycle lengths, matching complexes, Dutch windmills, and vertex amalgamations identified no result covering the precise four-regime statement. The search is not a uniqueness proof; a theorem on arbitrary one-vertex graph gluing could imply the result without matching wording.

## Value — PASS

Mixed-length cycle bouquets are canonical one-vertex cactus graphs. The result supplies all integral homotopy types for infinitely many heterogeneous arrangements, with a sharp contractibility transition between zero, one and two or more petals of length divisible by three. It distinguishes cases that general outerplanar wedge theorems leave unresolved and makes the dimension and number of spheres explicit rather than reporting finite data or an equal-length substitution.
