# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The result is proved for all integers \(d\ge2\) and all centered equilateral triples in the closed cube, without an assumption of vertex or boundary contacts. The six-vertex planar hexagon gives the exact coordinate bound; the convex-polynomial endpoint argument and coordinate-rounding inequality give uniform modular deficit bounds and the \(6/5\) near-equality stability threshold. Exact block concatenations establish the feasible upper deficit bounds. The extracted symbolic and rational checker passed, but the infinite-parameter claims depend on the written proof, not samples.

**Originality — PASS, subject to historical priority limitations.** The most pertinent full primary source, Ionin's *American Mathematical Monthly* 2015 solution to the unrestricted maximal equilateral triangle problem, has a different feasible set and strictly larger optimal side in dimensions divisible by three. It neither states the centered equality classification nor the count, dimension-independent modular deficit gaps or quantitative distance rigidity. Wenzel's 2002 cube-vertex full-dimensional regular-simplices theorem and Ambrus–Gárgyán's 2023 extremal-volume central-section study do not dominate the new statement. The superficially related OEIS A344854 counts cube-vertex triangles rather than these centrally constrained configurations (distinct counts already in dimension six). Related, unindexed centered-triangle results may still exist; this is not an independently established priority claim.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** A central-constraint version of a classical extremal cube geometry problem admits an unexpectedly exact divisibility law, complete maximizing orbit/class count, uniform residue obstructions, and a square-root metric stability estimate. These are structural comparisons useful for centered sectional regular-simplex questions, not merely an unmotivated small numerical improvement. The optimum remains unknown here in the two nonsaturated residue classes, and no analogous theorem for higher simplices is asserted.
