# Scientific review

## Correctness

PASS. In characteristic \(p\ge3\), the Frobenius identity makes every linear coordinate substitution an automorphism of the truncated group algebra. Quadratic rank reduces the possible homogeneous multipliers to zero, a square, a hyperbolic pair and a hyperbolic pair plus a square. Diagonal monomial chains and the modular Jordan tensor rule give the explicit block multiplicities. The progression count in the tensor rule supplies the nontrivial \(4\min(j,p-j)\) terms; dimension conservation supplies the \((p^2+1)/2\) projective blocks. A filtered Morse argument removes any higher terms following a nonsingular quadratic initial form. All field-size counts follow from elementary symmetric-form rank counts. Standalone tests check the direct multiplication matrices and arithmetic identities in specified finite cases.

## Originality

PASS, subject to historical-priority uncertainty. Standard modular Jordan tensor decomposition is prior work and is credited, as are the elementary abelian group algebra and quadratic-form normal forms. The new assessed statement combines the explicit ternary quadratic multiplication partitions, the precise finite-field rank-stratum counts, and all-higher-order Morse rigidity of the nonsingular stratum. A related two-generator group-algebra result concerns a smaller regular module and does not contain the ternary fusion multiplicities. The relevant 2011 representation paper studies linear shifted-subgroup restrictions, not this quadratic multiplication census. A broad tensor-product theorem supplies a powerful computational input and could make the final formula derivable; a previously published equivalent corollary cannot be definitively excluded.

## Value

PASS. The full regular representation of \(\mathbb F_q[(C_p)^3]\) is a canonical object in modular representation theory. Its quadratic augmentation layer contains four natural symmetric-form rank strata. Their exact Jordan partitions reveal the sharp jump from \(p\) to \((p^2+1)/2\) maximal blocks on passing from quadratic rank two to rank three. The nonsingular stratum extends uniformly to all higher-order lifts rather than an arbitrary finite sample or a renamed computation. This distinguishes quadratic-degree operators from linear shifted cyclic subgroup operators.
