# Scientific review: mortality-matched serial passage

**Correctness — PASS.** An unconditional joint density for two successful passage times leads to an even hyperbolic-sine kernel that grows strictly with the absolute difference of total stage-exit rates. Swapping unequal investment keeps the rate sum and product fixed while increasing this kernel exactly when more investment goes to the higher-loss stage. The strict derivative at parity excludes equality, and compactness supplies interior maxima. In the equal-hazard nonincreasing-reward case, the success prefactor and a conventional stochastic order for the conditional two-exponential passage time both favor balance. Uniform differentiated Taylor and dominated convergence arguments establish unique short/long deadline optima and the stated limits. Supplementary numerical checks compare distinct integral representations and sample explicit parameter regimes; finite tests are not universal proofs.

**Originality — PASS on inspected evidence.** The fully read 2024 open-access journal version of Oehri et al. (preprint posted 2023-11-24) models habitat networks, species-specific dispersal kernels and connectivity indicators rather than a fixed traversal-rate budget with competing mortality and weighted completion. The accessible Saura, Bodin and Fortin (2014) stepping-stone network study concerns across-generation dispersal potential, not competing exponential clocks or rate allocation. The full accessible Gholamialam et al. landscape-vulnerability study addresses least-cost corridors rather than these stochastic success controls. Bon and Păltănea (1999) already study exponential-convolution stochastic orders, which subsume the elementary conditional passage ordering used only for the equal-hazard subcase; they do not by themselves identify the competing-death unconditional-reward rate allocation. The inspected two-stage server-scheduling work optimizes a multi-customer controlled queue with abandonment costs and does not state the single-path universal allocation result. Similar metapopulation recolonization budget results optimize mean extinction instead of successful arrival before loss. The novelty judgment is provisional pending wider historical queueing and stochastic-network-control review.

**Value — PASS.** Allocating corridor improvements when two habitats have different mortality is a structural planning question; the theorem says the *direction* of the optimal bias is invariant under the deadline and even under arbitrary bounded completion-time rewards. It provides a precise near-zero quadratic budget bias and a distinct large-horizon optimal fraction, so an equal allocation can systematically misestimate successful arrivals. The result is a theoretical benchmark rather than empirical guidance.

**Risks and limitations.** Competing exponential clocks and a linear sum-of-rates budget are restrictive assumptions. No intermediate-deadline uniqueness or robustness to shared environmental dependence is claimed. The priority search was not exhaustive, and a potentially relevant 2024 three-patch modeling paper was not available in full; it uses a distinct metapopulation framework but is not asserted to be fully ruled out. No independent external audit or field validation has occurred.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
