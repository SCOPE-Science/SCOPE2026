# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS (self-review).** The peak statistic is an initial same-part run in each color; the conditional survival probability is a hypergeometric no-switch probability. Distinct colors have independent relative priority permutations. The exact mean follows by a binomial beta integral. For the growing-palette limit, all color/part counts concentrate uniformly when each has expectation much larger than \(\log n\). On this event geometric-tail bounds give a uniform conditional Berry–Esseen estimate. A Lipschitz extension and Efron–Stein show the random conditional centering fluctuates by \(o(\sqrt{k_n})\). Total variance then gives the limiting variance constant. Finite checker evidence supports the conditional combinatorics only.

**Originality — PASS (within inspected evidence).** The originating 2026 paper supplies the general peak interpretation and its Proposition 9 treats random bipartite graphs and fixed initial-color counts. The present finite multipartite law for three or more parts and, especially, the growing-color triangular Gaussian regime with its explicit mixed-geometric variance were not found in that source. The 1989 random-local-maxima papers provide a general normal-approximation context but not an examined statement covering this color-conditioned high-degree regime. A published random-greedy independent-set result on multipartite graphs has a different statistic and does not imply this limit. Search completeness and inaccessible historical literature are residual priority risks; a no-hit search is not a uniqueness proof.

**Value — PASS.** This theorem exposes a genuine non-Gaussian-to-Gaussian change in an exact self-stabilization runtime statistic on dense networks with communities: the number of randomly available initial colors, rather than graph sparsity, controls the asymptotic law. The finite color-by-color factorization is reusable for nonuniform part sizes and for exact numerical evaluation. No sharpness of the color-growth boundary is asserted.

Closest primary literature: de Panafieu et al., arXiv:2611.17989v1, especially Lemma 1 and Proposition 9; Baldi–Rinott–Stein (1989), *On the Distribution of the Number of Local Minima of a Random Function on a Graph*. Limits include strictly positive part proportions, fully complete cross-part adjacency, iid uniform initial colors, iid priorities, and the sufficient color-growth condition. Independent mathematical assessment remains outstanding.
