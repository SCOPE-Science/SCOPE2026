# Review of the square-zero domain-spectrum claim

**Correctness (PASS).** The square-zero property is exactly disjointness of the image and the domain, not a fixed-point condition. The increasing-domain/complement-index construction has an explicit inverse for each \(n\ge2\) and domain size \(k\). MacMahon's rectangle formula then gives the stated Narayana coefficients, Vandermonde gives the Catalan total, and the shifted strict-decrease bijection gives the large Schröder nilpotent denominator. The asymptotics use the exact algebraic Schröder generating function. Finite brute-force checks independently corroborated every domain size through \(n=9\), but cannot alone establish the infinite theorem.

**Originality (PASS, historically qualified).** The motivating complete arXiv text focuses on structural relations, idempotents, and generating rank, not square-zero enumeration. Laradji–Umar already established general lattice-path enumerations, the Catalan count of *full* transformations, and rank-refined known Narayana counts for that different class. Those source statements do not by themselves identify square-zero partial transformations with the counted arrays. The all-\(n\) structural bijection and its interpretation as an exact square-zero domain spectrum are the proposed distinction, not the standard binomial/Narayana identities. A missed historical reduction remains the main priority risk.

**Mathematical value (PASS).** The square-zero locus is a natural multiplication-defined subset of a recently studied nonunital transformation semigroup. Exact domain-size classification, its unexpected classical Narayana law, and sharp exponential rarity within all nilpotents give structural information not supplied by its known order, idempotent count, or ideal generators. The scope is infinite in the chain size and addresses all allowed domain sizes rather than an ad hoc numerical slice.

**Limits and related literature.** Zubairu–Umar–Al-Kharousi, arXiv:2512.19422, §§1–3; Laradji–Umar (2004) §§2–4 and *Lattice paths and order-preserving partial transformations* (arXiv:1304.7574) §§2–4. The standard Narayana numbers themselves are not new; no exhaustive proof of historical uniqueness is claimed. Independent scrutiny of priority and the all-size argument remains desirable.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
