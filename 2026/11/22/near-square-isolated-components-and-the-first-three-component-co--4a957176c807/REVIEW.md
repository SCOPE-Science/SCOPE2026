# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS
The fixed-point triangulation follows from orbit-constant barycentric coordinates in a finite simplicial complex; the flag condition reduces compatibility to complete bipartite adjacency between conjugation orbits. The four near-square transfer neighbors are exhaustive, occur as two conjugation pairs, and both pairs have Ferrers row-distance four rather than two. This rigorously isolates the near-square point for every integer \(m\ge3\). The finite first-threshold statement is justified by complete partition enumeration for each \(1\le n\le24\) and two independently computed adjacency relations. The checker is included and reproducible. No untested infinite component classification is claimed.

## Originality — PASS, with residual priority risk
The source of the ambient sphere-wedge theorem is arXiv:2603.14370; the one-cell-transfer local classification arXiv:2603.18696 and the graph-theoretic conjugation axis arXiv:2603.22546 do not imply a connected-component classification of the geometric fixed locus without additional orbit-simplex analysis. A published record on this fixed locus gives a compatible-orbit simplicial model and first homological thresholds through \(n=22\); the present proof does not claim that model as new. Its new content is the exact first three-component threshold \(24\) and an all-order structural family of isolated fixed components. Indexed comparisons found no theorem stating either conclusion. Unindexed or inaccessible full-text extensions of the earlier fixed-locus calculation remain a material priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
A connected, simply connected ambient sphere bouquet can have an increasingly disconnected symmetry-fixed space. The near-square construction gives a natural infinite mechanism; the exact \(n=24\) threshold locates the first three-component phase. This is an intrinsic, reproducible topological invariant of the fixed locus, not merely a new face count or arbitrary parameter substitution. It supports future classification of equivariant partition-transfer topology but does not itself establish a universal component formula.

## Limitations
No full homotopy classification of the fixed spaces, no closed component-count formula above \(24\), and no independent mathematical audit. The earlier published orbit-compatibility model is credited rather than repackaged as a novel lemma.
