# Nine integral parity obstructions in the six-pointed stable-curve boundary

## Finding

Let \(X=\overline{\mathcal M}_{0,6}\) be the fine moduli space of stable genus-zero curves with six **distinctly labelled** markings, over \(\mathbb Z\) or after base change to any field. Let \(\mathcal C\) be its set of \(105\) geometric closed one-dimensional boundary strata, and \(\mathcal P\) its set of \(105\) zero-dimensional boundary strata. Define the **formal incidence homomorphism**

\[\Phi:\mathbb Z^{\mathcal C}\longrightarrow\mathbb Z^{\mathcal P},\qquad [C]\longmapsto\sum_{p\in \mathcal P,\,p\subset C}[p].\]

The Smith normal form of its incidence matrix is

\[\operatorname{diag}(\underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{81},\underbrace{2,\ldots,2}_{9},\underbrace{0,\ldots,0}_{15}).\]

Consequently,

\[\operatorname{coker}\Phi\cong\mathbb Z^{15}\oplus(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^9.\]

In particular, the subgroup of formal boundary-point combinations generated by the **three-point sums on boundary curves** is not saturated: it has exactly nine independent order-two obstructions and a free quotient of rank fifteen. The same Smith form holds for the transposed point-to-containing-curve incidence map.

## Assumptions and scope

The points and curves here are **labelled boundary strata**, not rational-equivalence classes of cycles. In this statement \(\mathbb Z^{\mathcal C}\) and \(\mathbb Z^{\mathcal P}\) are free abelian groups on the indicated finite sets. The homomorphism is coefficientwise unsigned, with incidence one when the zero-dimensional stratum lies in the curve and zero otherwise. The result is characteristic-independent because the stable labelled-tree incidence poset is defined over \(\mathbb Z\). No Frobenius action, field-size bound, or choice of embedding is required.

A stable split is an unordered bipartition \(A\mid A^c\) of the label set \(\{0,1,2,3,4,5\}\), both sides of size at least two. Two splits \(A\mid A^c\) and \(B\mid B^c\) are compatible precisely when at least one of \(A\cap B\), \(A\cap B^c\), \(A^c\cap B\), \(A^c\cap B^c\) is empty. A collection of pairwise compatible splits uniquely specifies the corresponding stable labelled tree (the standard split-compatibility theorem for trees). A boundary stratum of codimension \(r\) is specified by its \(r\) internal-edge splits.

## Proof

The six-label stable split set contains \((2^6-2-12)/2=25\) elements. Pairwise-compatible two-split sets number exactly \(105\); they index codimension-two, one-dimensional strata. Pairwise-compatible three-split sets also number \(105\); they index codimension-three points. Every one-dimensional stratum is canonically a copy of \(\overline{\mathcal M}_{0,4}\cong\mathbb P^1\), hence contains precisely three distinguished zero-dimensional boundary strata. Conversely, each trivalent six-leaf tree has three internal edges, and contracting any one produces one of its three containing one-dimensional boundary strata.

Enumerate the twenty-five splits in increasing order by the lesser of the two six-bit masks representing the complementary subsets. Enumerate compatible pairs and triples lexicographically. Form the integer matrix \(A\), whose rows are compatible pairs, whose columns are compatible triples, with \(A_{C,p}=1\) if the pair is a subset of the triple and zero otherwise. All row sums and column sums equal three. The matrix for \(\Phi\) is \(A^{\mathsf T}\), which has the same integer Smith invariants as \(A\).

The embedded certificate `SMITH_CERT.json` supplies 1,948 explicit elementary **unimodular integer** row/column operations, comprising only row/column swaps, additions of an integer multiple of another row/column, and sign reversal of a row. Applying them to the matrix reconstructed from split compatibility yields, by exact integer arithmetic, a diagonal with eighty-one entries \(1\), nine entries \(2\), and fifteen zero entries, in divisibility order. Each operation preserves the isomorphism class of the cokernel up to an integral change of generators or relations. This establishes the stated Smith form and quotient without inference from floating-point rank or modular experiments.

The independent replay script `VERIFY.py` does not rely on a computer algebra library. It regenerates all splits and compatible pairs and triples, builds the exact matrix, checks the three incidences per row/column, replays every elementary operation in the certificate, checks **every** off-diagonal entry vanishes, and checks the final divisibility-ordered diagonal. Therefore the executable finite certificate proves the complete integer computation.

## Verification

Run `python3 VERIFY.py` in the directory containing `SMITH_CERT.json`; the expected line is

`VERIFIED_M06_BOUNDARY_INCIDENCE_SNF_1x81_2x9_0x15 1948`

The complete matrix has \(105^2\) entries, exactly \(315\) of which are one. Before packaging, a second computation using an independently implemented Smith-normal-form routine over \(\mathbb Z\) gave the same \(81,9,15\) invariant-factor multiplicities. The included certificate is self-contained, with no reliance on that second routine. The construction is exhaustive, not a random sample.

## Relationship to prior work

Maya–Mostovoy (2019) develop the stable labelled-tree combinatorics of \(\overline{\mathcal M}_{0,n}\) and explicit equations for \(\overline{\mathcal M}_{0,6}\), including the stratification by labelled trees. Bae–Schmitt (2021) develop Chow-group generators and WDVV-type relations for genus-zero moduli stacks and recall the corresponding geometry of stable-curve spaces. Those results supply the natural geometric setting, but the present theorem concerns the **unsigned integral incidence homomorphism between two specific free groups on boundary strata** and its complete integer cokernel. It is not the Chow-group presentation or the oriented cellular differential of the tropical boundary complex.

## Limitations

The free summand is not identified as a representation of the permutation group on the six markings. No assertion is made that the formal point generators are independent in \(\mathrm{CH}_0(X)\): rational equivalence on each boundary curve identifies its three special points and is a different relation from their **unsigned sum**. The result is an exact finite integral incidence classification, not a calculation of Chow groups, moduli-space homology, or cohomology. Priority is subject to older or unindexed equivalent computations of the unsigned face-incidence matrix.
