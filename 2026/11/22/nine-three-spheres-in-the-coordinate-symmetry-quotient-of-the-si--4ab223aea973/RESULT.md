# Nine three-spheres in the coordinate-symmetry quotient of the signed-partition lattice

## Finding

Let \(Q_5(C_2)\) be the rank-five Dowling lattice for the two-element group (equivalently, the type-\(B_5\) signed-partition lattice). Its proper-part order complex is \(K=\Delta(\overline{Q_5(C_2)})\). The subgroup \(S_5\) of the hyperoctahedral group permutes the five coordinate labels without individually changing their signs. For the **ordinary geometric quotient** \(Y=|K|/S_5\), there is a homotopy equivalence

\[
Y\simeq \bigvee^{9}S^3.
\]

Consequently, \(\pi_1(Y)=0\), \(\widetilde H_3(Y;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^9\), and all other reduced integral homology groups vanish. This concerns precisely the rank-five lattice and the indicated pure permutation subgroup; it does not assert a uniform formula for every rank or for the full hyperoctahedral quotient.

## Assumptions and scope

Use the signed ground set \(\{0,\pm1,\ldots,\pm5\}\). An element is a partition invariant under \(a\mapsto-a\), whose unique sign-stable block includes \(0\) (the *zero block*), while all other blocks occur in opposite pairs \(B,-B\). The lower relation \(p\le q\) is ordinary refinement of these signed blocks. Its rank is \(5-b\), where \(b\) is the number of nonzero block pairs. The minimum and maximum elements, of ranks zero and five, are omitted from \(K\). A simplex is a strict refinement chain of elements with ranks in \(\{1,2,3,4\}\); its orientation is the order of its ranks. All coordinate permutations preserve rank and orientation.

The initial group is the **unsigned permutation copy** of \(S_5\), rather than \(C_2\wr S_5\) or a group of independent sign reversals. The action is simplicial and has nonfree stabilizers; the result refers to the actual geometric orbit space.

## Proof

**1. Connectivity of the cover and quotient.** As the intersection lattice of the rank-five real hyperplane arrangement \(x_i=0\), \(x_i=x_j\) and \(x_i=-x_j\), the signed-partition lattice is geometric. Geometric-lattice shellability gives the proper-part order complex the homotopy type of a wedge of three-spheres; its reduced top rank is \(1\cdot3\cdot5\cdot7\cdot9=945\). Thus \(K\) is connected and simply connected.

Each coordinate transposition \((i\ j)\in S_5\) fixes a vertex of \(K\): take the signed partition whose zero block is \(\{0,\pm i,\pm j\}\) and whose remaining nonzero blocks are singleton pairs. Its rank is two, so it belongs to the proper part. Armstrong's orbit-space theorem for a finite simplicial group action on a simply connected complex identifies the fundamental group of the orbit space with the acting group modulo the normal subgroup generated by elements fixing points. Transpositions generate \(S_5\), hence \(\pi_1(Y)=1\).

**2. Exact orbit-chain construction.** To enumerate all signed partitions, choose the set of coordinates in the zero block, then a set partition of the remaining positive coordinates; for each nonzero block select signs relative to its least coordinate. Each such choice gives exactly one sign-stable partition. Direct enumeration yields \(648\) lattice elements, grouped by ranks zero through five as \((1,25,170,330,121,1)\). Removing the extrema leaves \(646\) vertices. Refinement-chain enumeration in increasing rank yields the dimension-by-dimension simplex counts

\[
(646,7910,20720,14400).
\]

The group of \(120\) coordinate permutations acts on these chains. Because every vertex in a simplex has a distinct rank, a simplex stabilizer fixes all its vertices; consequently the orbit cells form a regular rank-oriented quotient simplicial cell structure. Enumerating orbits gives the cell counts

\[
(c_0,c_1,c_2,c_3)=(37,242,471,274).
\]

For each oriented orbit-cell representative \([v_0<\cdots<v_d]\), its cellular boundary is the signed sum of the orbits of \([v_0<\cdots<\widehat v_i<\cdots<v_d]\), with coefficients \((-1)^i\). The embedded independent-of-external-data `verify.py` constructs these exact sparse integer boundary matrices, checks \(\partial_{d-1}\partial_d=0\), and performs only unit-pivot integral basis cancellations. The cancellation counts in dimensions one, two, three are \((36,206,265)\). The remaining complex contains one generator in degree zero and nine in degree three, no others, and zero differential. Since every pivot is \(\pm1\), the cancellations preserve integral chain-homotopy type. Thus

\[
H_i(Y;\mathbb Z)=\begin{cases}\mathbb Z,&i=0,\\\mathbb Z^9,&i=3,\\0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}
\]

**3. Homotopy classification.** The quotient is a three-dimensional CW complex, is simply connected by the first step, and has no positive homology below degree three. By Hurewicz, \(\pi_3(Y)\cong H_3(Y;\mathbb Z)\). Choose nine representative maps \(S^3\to Y\) realizing a homology basis; their wedge induces an integral homology isomorphism and is therefore a homotopy equivalence by the simply connected homological Whitehead theorem. This proves the displayed classification.

## Verification

Run `python3 verify.py` with Python 3.8 or newer and the standard library. The script reconstructs the \(648\) elements, enumerates every strict chain, computes all group orbit indexes, forms the signed integer cellular differentials, verifies their compositions, checks ranks modulo \(2\), \(3\), and \(5\), and executes the integral cancellation. It asserts all reported intermediate counts and requires terminal `PASS`. The modular checks are additional consistency tests, not substitutes for the integral unit-pivot certificate. The mathematical proof of simple connectivity uses the explicitly given fixed vertex and Armstrong's theorem, not computational inference.

## Relationship to prior work

Björner–Wachs construct integral top-homology bases for type-\(B\) partition lattices and establish the labeled sphere-wedge background, without identifying this ordinary permutation orbit-space homotopy type. Henderson recovers Hanlon's wreath-product *rational homology characters* and proves related equivariant formulas for Dowling posets; rational invariant multiplicities do not determine a nonfree quotient's fundamental group or integral torsion. Arone studies ordinary partition complexes and their quotients by Young subgroups, but those objects are not the signed-partition poset with signs retained under \(S_5\). In particular, forgetting sign labels does not identify the \(S_5\)-orbits of signed flags. The calculation is therefore a new integral quotient classification relative to the inspected statements, without a claim of exhaustive bibliographic priority.

## Limitations

The theorem is rank-specific. The verifier supplies an exhaustive finite computation, not an infinite-rank or all-subgroups classification. It does not produce an equivariant homotopy splitting for the covering complex, or describe the homotopy type of the corresponding Borel construction. The most relevant residual priority risk is an equivalent result in less accessible literature about Dowling-lattice actions or signed partition complexes; this has not been independently audited.
