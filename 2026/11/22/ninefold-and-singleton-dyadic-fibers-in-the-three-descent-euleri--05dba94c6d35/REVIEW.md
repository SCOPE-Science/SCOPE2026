# Scientific review: three-descent Eulerian dyadic fibers

## Correctness — PASS

The claim concerns exactly three descents and every dyadic modulus, with a sufficient start \(a\ge\max\{4,k\}\). The classical inclusion-exclusion formula is independently checked against Eulerian recurrence terms. For all such indices, the terms with bases two and four vanish modulo \(2^k\). On the even half, the odd divided difference of the cubic binomial component dominates the even divided difference of the \(9^t\) term, making a dyadic isometry. On the odd half, the exact shift-by-four identity has quotient congruent to \(2\pmod4\), implying four isometric charts with image residue balls \(0,10\pmod{16}\). The counts and period minimality follow algebraically. The extracted checker passed 101 recurrence equalities, 24,570 fiber comparisons, 49,140 period checks, and 3,810 exact isometry differences. Finite checks corroborate rather than establish the unrestricted proof.

## Originality — PASS within inspected evidence

The inspected full published result for two descents has **uniform** two-per-residue fibers, with a different Eulerian column and limiting polynomial. The inspected full fixed cubic-binomial result has five-to-one fibers supported by a cubic binomial mapping, but it cannot imply the present three-descent column with an additional exponential term and a different nine-to-one distribution. The original Cao–Pan congruences sum across Eulerian columns at a fixed permutation size, while Krattenthaler–Müller treat central Eulerian indices and principally ternary congruences. OEIS A000498 records the exact three-descent formula and terms, not the evaluated infinite dyadic fiber claim in its accessible excerpt. Castro–González–Medina (2015) study valuations along index classes, but full text could not be inspected, so an equivalent hidden within it remains a concrete historical-priority risk. Targeted published-finding corpus and public-literature searches produced no identified stronger theorem covering the exact ninefold/singleton distribution; searches cannot establish uniqueness.

## Mathematical value — PASS

For a natural fixed Eulerian column, the theorem exactly classifies every residue, its asymptotic density and the minimum eventual period at all dyadic levels. It gives a substantive boundary to the nearby two-descent uniformity theorem: adding one descent turns perfect uniformity into two exceptional residue balls with ninefold density, due to an identifiable four-chart critical structure. This is not merely a numerical table or a reformulation of the known Eulerian expression.

## Limitations

No claims cover other descent counts or odd moduli. The starting threshold is sufficient, not sharp. The priority assessment is qualified by the incomplete access to a plausible 2015 valuation paper and to the complete OEIS entry; independent mathematical validation and exhaustive historical priority checking have not occurred.
