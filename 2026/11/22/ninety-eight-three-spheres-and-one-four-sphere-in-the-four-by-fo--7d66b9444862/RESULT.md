# Ninety-eight three-spheres and one four-sphere in the four-by-four rook general-position complex

## Finding
Let \(R=K_4\square K_4\), with vertices the sixteen pairs \((r,c)\in\{0,1,2,3\}^2\). Let \(\operatorname{GP}(R)\) be the abstract simplicial complex of all vertex subsets containing no three distinct vertices on a common graph shortest path. Then the complete **integral homotopy type** is
\[
\boxed{|\operatorname{GP}(R)|\simeq\left(\bigvee^{98}S^3\right)\vee S^4.}
\]
In particular, its reduced integral homology is \(\widetilde H_3\cong\mathbb Z^{98}\), \(\widetilde H_4\cong\mathbb Z\), and zero in every other degree. The simplicial complex has facets of dimension five, but collapses through an explicit acyclic face-poset matching to a CW model with only one vertex, ninety-eight three-cells, and one four-cell with zero attaching map in homotopy.

## Assumptions and scope
Graphs are finite, undirected, simple, unweighted. In \(K_4\square K_4\), distinct board squares are adjacent precisely if they share a row or column; the graph distance is the sum of the two coordinate-inequality indicators. General position forbids a triple \(u,v,w\) whenever one lies on a shortest path between the other two, equivalently whenever one ordering satisfies \(d(u,v)+d(v,w)=d(u,w)\). The symbol \(\bigvee\) denotes a based wedge; no group-equivariant homotopy classification is asserted.

## Proof
**Step 1: exact finite simplicial model.** Label the vertices by \(i=4c+r\) with \(0\le r,c\le3\), so a face is represented by a nonzero 16-bit integer mask. Two distinct vertices have graph distance one if they share a row or column, and two otherwise. There are exactly seventy-two unordered nonadjacent vertex pairs, each with two distinct common neighbors; each such middle vertex determines one unique forbidden geodesic triple. Thus there are \(144\) forbidden triples. A face is exactly a nonempty bit mask not containing any such forbidden triple. Direct exhaustive enumeration gives the face vector, in degrees zero through five,
\[
(16,120,416,632,240,16),
\]
for a total of \(1440\) nonempty simplices. An independent recognition test identifies the same faces as edge subsets of \(K_{4,4}\) forming disjoint unions of stars, under the usual rook-square/edge correspondence. For this equivalence, the rook graph is the line graph of the complete bipartite graph: a general-position set induces a disjoint union of cliques, and such an induced line graph corresponds precisely to a star forest. The face-recognition equivalence was established previously in graph-general-position literature; it is not claimed as new.

**Step 2: an explicit acyclic matching.** Start with the complete nonempty face set. For each label \(i=0,1,\ldots,15\), pair every still-unpaired face \(F\) that omits \(i\) with \(F\cup\{i\}\), provided both belong to the remaining face set; remove the two paired faces. This deterministic rule matches \(670\) pairs (\(1340\) faces). The unmatched faces consist of exactly one vertex, ninety-eight three-simplices and one four-simplex. To verify acyclicity, orient every Hasse-cover relation from coface to face, reversing precisely the matched covers. The accompanying standalone checker explicitly builds all \(5312\) Hasse-cover relations and verifies by full topological sorting that the resulting directed graph on all \(1440\) faces is acyclic. Hence this is an acyclic discrete-Morse matching, yielding a CW model with exactly the stated critical cells.

**Step 3: integral attaching map.** Since there is exactly one critical vertex and no critical one- or two-cells, the resulting CW model is obtained from a wedge of ninety-eight \(3\)-spheres by attaching one \(4\)-cell. Its homotopy class of attachment belongs to \(\pi_3(\bigvee^{98}S^3)\cong\mathbb Z^{98}\). It is therefore determined by the integer degrees into the ninety-eight three-sphere summands. The verifier computes these integer coefficients by the algebraic Morse gradient-path cancellation rule: start with the signed simplicial boundary of the sole critical four-face; whenever a nonzero three-face coefficient belongs to a matched lower face of a three-to-four pair, subtract the signed boundary of its matched four-face divided by the corresponding unit incidence coefficient. It performs \(191\) exact integer unit substitutions and obtains the zero chain, without residual coefficients. All incidence signs use the **positions of omitted vertices in the ordered simplex**, not their numerical vertex labels. Because the directed matching is acyclic, the cancellation procedure terminates and computes the exact integral Morse boundary; in this case it is zero. The single four-cell thus attaches nullhomotopically, proving the stated wedge decomposition.

## Verification
The package includes `verify.py` and `modp_check.py`, both using only the Python 3 standard library. Run them from the directory containing these files. `verify.py` independently builds faces by shortest-path metrics and by the star-forest criterion; compares the entire resulting sets; enumerates every Hasse relation; checks acyclicity; constructs the integer Morse boundary; and independently computes mod-two chain ranks. Its printed result includes `VERIFY_OK`. `modp_check.py` derives the complex from metric triples again and computes all simplicial boundary ranks over fields with two, three and five elements, finding reduced Betti ranks \((98,1)\) in degrees three and four. The exact computational output is provided in `verification_output.txt`. No finite experiment is extrapolated beyond the stated sixteen-vertex complex.

## Relationship to prior work
Anand, Chandran, Changat, Klavžar and Thomas (2018/2019) characterize graph-geodesic general-position sets through induced disjoint cliques. Iršič, Klavžar, Rus and Tuite (2024) introduce the general-position polynomial and study graph products, while Rather (first public preprint 2026-03-06) develops more enumerative formulas. These are used as the combinatorial foundation. Previously published *Complete general-position enumerators and gapped maximal spectra of rook graphs* gives all cardinality distributions and the star-forest reduction for the **same rook-graph family**. Previously published *Maximal induced cluster subgraphs of rook graphs* gives another formulation through induced cluster subgraphs. Those results determine face counts, not the integer homotopy type: a face polynomial, maximal-set enumerator or generic star-forest equivalence does not determine attaching maps or the homotopy groups. The present result supplies the new exact integral homotopy classification at the first balanced four-row square, with an explicit acyclic matching and zero integral attachment. Searches did not find a previously published exact homotopy calculation for this object, but absence from search results cannot prove priority.

## Limitations
Only the four-by-four rook graph is classified. The result does not assert a uniform formula for all rectangular rook graphs, an equivariant homotopy equivalence, a shelling, or a classification of related neighborhood/independence complexes. The exact verifier is finite and provides an exhaustive proof of this finite case, not of a larger family. Publication-priority risk remains for unindexed or inaccessible prior computations; an independent mathematical audit has not been performed.
