# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The two quaternion lists give the exact same 0, \(\pi\) and \(2\pi/3\) relative-angle matrix, with precisely three opposite pairs and twelve undirected cross-pairs. Unit-quaternion projective span dimensions are four and three, excluding congruence. Schur orthogonality identifies the normalized Wigner sample Gram as \(\chi_\ell/d\). A three-by-two block diagonalization supplies all eigenvalues and multiplicities. Explicit six-residue comparisons prove positivity for every \(\ell\ge1\) and the globally sharp \(2/5\) and \(8/5\) bounds, achieved only at degree two. Conjugation-group averaging proves equal-weight minimax conditioning for every degree. Independent symmetric-power checks through degree six and finite numerical scans corroborate, but do not replace, the proof.

## Originality — PASS under inspected literature, with priority risk
The 2020 Wigner-coherence paper computes bounds for Euler-angle grids, not the exact six-point octahedral conjugacy-class Gram; the 2015 Wigner sampling theorem treats a bandlimited equiangular grid. A publicly indexed twelve-node icosahedral class calculation provides the closest exact character-kernel method, but has different pairwise rotation angles, cardinality and interpolation onset and does not imply this geometric projective-rank twin. A publicly indexed full-octahedral metric result lists the separate octahedral classes but does not derive internal classwise Gram isospectrality or the Wigner spectrum. Full scientific comparisons were made where accessible, including the 2020 arXiv HTML article and two exact published mathematical records. An unindexed six-point projective counterpart or implicit consequence of older finite-group association schemes remains possible; no claim of definitive historical priority is made.

## Value — PASS
The six quarter-turn and six edge-half-turn classes are canonical finite orientation designs, not arbitrarily selected coordinate slices. The fact that all central two-point measurements agree despite differing projective span dimensions identifies a concrete obstruction to orientation-configuration inference, while the complete all-degree Wigner spectrum and optimal-weight sharp condition bound furnish a nontrivial, reproducible interpolation benchmark on a compact nonabelian group. This benchmark is a classification of six data constraints, not a full-degree function reconstruction theorem.
