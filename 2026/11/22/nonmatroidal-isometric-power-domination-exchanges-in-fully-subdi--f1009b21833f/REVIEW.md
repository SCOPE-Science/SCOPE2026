# Scientific notes

## Correctness and scope

The proof uses a cycle obstruction and a forest-peeling argument, including the no-selected-originals case. Equality in the forest edge bound characterizes the optimum sets. Explicit paths attain the Johnson lower bound for every pair of optima, and the exhibited pair obstructs matroid basis exchange for all \(n\ge4\). The finite verification programs supplement, but do not replace, the all-order proof.

## Relationship to known results

Two distinct same-day results on token slides give the same optimum-set classification. One also gives the all-set forest criterion, which implies the polynomial by counting. These facts are used as self-contained groundwork, not claimed as distinct findings. The novelty claim concerns unrestricted replacement, in particular the coexistence of all-pairs isometry with failure of the basis-exchange axiom. The slide and replacement graphs use different moves and their distances already differ at \(n=3\). Ordinary zero-forcing and paired-domination results on the same graph concern different constraints. Priority beyond the inspected sources is not established.

## Significance

The result identifies when replacement sequences can avoid detours without requiring matroidal feasibility. The exact degree and eccentricity profiles quantify the unrestricted exchange geometry; no claim is made for arbitrary graphs or for edge-constrained moves.
