# Nonsense distance correlation persists in independent infinite-variance random walks

## Finding

For two independent random walks with increments attracted to symmetric stable laws of respective indices \(0<\alpha,\beta<2\), the ordinary sample distance correlation of their paired time-indexed *levels* converges to the nonzero, nonunit random variable \(\mathcal R(L_\alpha,L_\beta)\) formed by sampling a **common uniform time** along two independent stable Lévy trajectories. In particular, first moments of increments are *not* needed. An unrestricted time-label permutation test rejects process independence with probability tending to one at every fixed nominal level.

## Assumptions and scope

Let \((\xi_j)_{j\ge1}\) and \((\eta_j)_{j\ge1}\) be mutually independent iid sequences; each innovation law is symmetric and belongs to the normal domain of attraction of a nondegenerate symmetric strictly stable law of respective index \(\alpha,\beta\in(0,2)\). Thus there are deterministic positive normalizers \(a_n,b_n\) such that \((a_n^{-1}\sum_{j\le\lfloor nt\rfloor}\xi_j,b_n^{-1}\sum_{j\le\lfloor nt\rfloor}\eta_j)\) converges in the product Skorokhod \(J_1\) topology to independent standard-scale symmetric stable Lévy motions \((L_\alpha,L_\beta)\); normalizers absorb each stable scale. Observe \(S_k=\sum_{j=1}^k\xi_j\) and \(T_k=\sum_{j=1}^k\eta_j\), \(1\le k\le n\). Construct distances \(a_{ij}=|S_i-S_j|\), \(b_{ij}=|T_i-T_j|\), their double-centered matrices \(A,B\), \(K_n=n^{-2}\sum_{ij} A_{ij}B_{ij}\), and \(R_n=\sqrt{K_n/\sqrt{K_n(S,S)K_n(T,T)}}\) when the denominator is positive (else zero). The stable limit's pathwise occupation law is \(\mu=\int_0^1\delta_{(L_\alpha(t),L_\beta(t))}\,dt\). Its distance correlation \(\mathcal R\) is defined by the same double-centered absolute-distance functional with Lebesgue-integral row and overall means.

## Proof

**1. Stable occupation limit.** The stable partial-sum functional limit theorem gives joint \(J_1\)-weak convergence of the independent rescaled walks. Under a Skorokhod coupling, each coordinate converges at every continuity time of its limiting path. Since any càdlàg path has at most countably many jumps, convergence holds for Lebesgue-almost every time simultaneously for the two paths. The \(J_1\) coupling also bounds the suprema of both sample paths eventually. Dominated convergence gives convergence in \(L^1([0,1])\), hence of the occupation measures and all first absolute-distance moments, and of their doubly centered distance kernels in \(L^2([0,1]^2)\). The end-time discrete Riemann sum has the same limit (the lone endpoint has weight \(1/n\)). Ordinary sample distance correlation is invariant under the two separate positive normalizations. Continuous mapping applies once the limiting distance variances are positive: a nonconstant càdlàg path's occupation law cannot be a point mass, because right-continuity makes any almost-everywhere constant path constant on \([0,1)\). A stable Lévy path is almost surely nonconstant.

**2. Strict positivity of the limit.** Let \(q(t)=L_\alpha(t)-\int_0^1L_\alpha(v)\,dv\), and condition on the first path. If the occupation distance covariance vanished, its zero-if-and-only-if-independence characterization would force its ordinary covariance to vanish; hence \(Z=\int_0^1q(t)L_\beta(t)\,dt=0\). Put \(Q(u)=\int_u^1q(t)\,dt\). Then \(Q\) is absolutely continuous, \(Q(0)=Q(1)=0\), and integration by parts for the semimartingale \(L_\beta\) gives \(Z=\int_0^1Q(u)\,dL_\beta(u)\). Conditionally on the first path, this is a symmetric stable variable of index \(\beta\) and scale proportional to \((\int_0^1|Q(u)|^\beta du)^{1/\beta}\). That scale is strictly positive whenever the first path is nonconstant: otherwise \(Q\equiv0\), hence \(q=0\) almost everywhere, hence the first càdlàg path is constant on \([0,1)\). Every nondegenerate stable variable has a continuous density; thus \(\Pr(Z=0\mid L_\alpha)=0\) almost surely. It follows that \(\mathcal R>0\) almost surely.

**3. Strict upper bound.** For any two nonconstant càdlàg paths \(f,g\), equality \(\mathcal R(f,g)=1\) in the distance-kernel Cauchy–Schwarz inequality forces the centered absolute-distance kernels to be proportional almost everywhere. Row means and diagonal-zero distances imply \(|g(s)-g(t)|=c|f(s)-f(t)|\) for almost every pair; right continuity upgrades this to every pair of interior continuity times. Hence \(g=a+bf\) for almost every time, with \(|b|=c>0\). Since both independent stable paths start at zero and are right continuous at zero, \(a=0\). At two fixed distinct interior times \(0<s<t<1\), this forces \(L_\alpha(s)L_\beta(t)-L_\alpha(t)L_\beta(s)=0\). The four evaluations have a nondegenerate joint Lebesgue density (stable independent increments in two independent coordinates); the displayed nonzero polynomial has a null zero set. Thus \(\mathcal R<1\) almost surely.

**4. Permutation failure.** Let \(\pi\) be a uniform permutation of the \(T\)-labels and let \(K_n^\pi\) be the squared distance covariance after permutation. Pure matrix double-centering gives the exact samplewise identity \(\mathbb E_\pi K_n^\pi=\bar a_{..}\bar b_{..}/(n-1)\), with \(\bar a_{..}=n^{-2}\sum_{ij}a_{ij}\) and analogously for \(\bar b_{..}\). Since \(K_n^\pi\ge0\), the exact conditional permutation \(p\)-value satisfies \(p_n\le\bar a_{..}\bar b_{..}/((n-1)K_n)\) whenever \(K_n>0\). On dividing by \(a_n b_n\), the numerator is tight and the denominator converges in distribution to a strictly positive random variable by steps 1–2. Consequently \(p_n\to0\) in probability. This requires no unconditional moment of any innovation and no exchangeability across observed time points.

## Verification

The included `CHECK.py` evaluates double-centered distances and verifies scale invariance, positivity on non-affine deterministic paths, exact expectation of the permuted squared statistic by exhaustive enumeration for small arrays, and nonzero correlations for a seeded collection of independent symmetric stable random-walk samples. Finite samples are diagnostic only. The mathematical proof rests on the stable functional limit theorem, conditional stable-integral nondegeneracy, and the exact permutation identity, not on these tests.

## Relationship to prior work

Zhu and Xie (2026) prove the Brownian-occupation analogue for centered finite-positive-variance increments, including their nonvanishing correlation and incorrect permutation calibration. The present result addresses an excluded **infinite-variance**, possibly **infinite-first-moment** regime; it replaces Gaussian conditional nondegeneracy with a stable stochastic-integral argument and works with càdlàg \(J_1\) paths rather than continuous Wiener paths. Basrak, Krizmanić and Segers (2010) document the stable partial-sum functional-limit background (including the iid case). Dehling, Matsui, Mikosch, Samorodnitsky and Tafakori (2018) consider distance covariance using *iid replicates of whole discretized stochastic processes*, including stable processes; that distinct sampling experiment does not furnish this result for *one pair of paths sampled repeatedly over time*.

## Limitations

Only independent iid symmetric stable-domain-of-attraction increments are covered. The limit distribution depends on the two stable indices, unlike the finite-variance Brownian universality class. We do **not** claim a closed form for its expectation, full support on \([0,1]\), analogous results for dependent increments, or validity of moment-based statistical calibration. The usual population distance correlation of fixed-time levels may be undefined when first moments fail; the bounded-path *conditional occupation* distance correlation remains well-defined almost surely.
