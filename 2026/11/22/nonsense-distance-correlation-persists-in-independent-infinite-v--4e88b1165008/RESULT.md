# Nonsense distance correlation persists in independent infinite-variance random walks

## Finding

For two independent random walks with iid symmetric innovations in the normal domains of attraction of nondegenerate symmetric stable laws of indices \(0<\alpha,\beta<2\), the ordinary sample distance correlation of the paired time-indexed **levels** converges in distribution to the distance correlation of the occupation law obtained by sampling a **common uniform time** along two independent stable Lévy trajectories. This limit is strictly between zero and one almost surely. No first moment of either innovation law is needed. The exact unrestricted time-label permutation \(p\)-value tends to zero in probability, so the test rejects process independence with probability tending to one at each fixed nominal level in \((0,1)\).

## Assumptions and scope

Write \(S_i=\sum_{j=1}^i\xi_j\) and \(T_i=\sum_{j=1}^i\eta_j\) for the levels of the two walks, with the two innovation sequences mutually independent and each sequence iid. Let \(a_n,b_n>0\) be the stable normalizers. In the product Skorokhod \(J_1\) topology, the two normalized partial-sum paths converge jointly to independent standard-scale symmetric strictly stable Lévy motions \((L_\alpha,L_\beta)\) on \([0,1]\). Here the scales of the innovation laws are absorbed in the normalizers.

Using indices \(1\le i,j\le n\), set \(a_{ij}=|S_i-S_j|\) and \(b_{ij}=|T_i-T_j|\). Let \(A,B\) be their double-centered matrices (subtract row and column means and add the grand mean). With \(K_n(S,T)=n^{-2}\sum_{ij}A_{ij}B_{ij}\), set \(R_n=\sqrt{K_n(S,T)/\sqrt{K_n(S,S)K_n(T,T)}}\) when the denominator is positive and zero otherwise. In the limit the occupation law is \(\mu=\int_0^1\delta_{(L_\alpha(t),L_\beta(t))}\,dt\) conditional on the two paths, and the same distance-correlation functional uses distance kernels with Lebesgue-integral means. The exact permutation test includes every permutation with equal weight and counts ties as at least as extreme as the observed statistic. It is not a finite Monte Carlo permutation approximation.

## Proof

**1. Stable occupation limit.** The iid stable functional limit theorem gives the stated coordinatewise \(J_1\)-weak limits; independence gives joint weak convergence in the product topology. Under a Skorokhod representation these paths converge almost surely pointwise at every simultaneous continuity time of the limit, and their suprema are eventually bounded (the \(J_1\) time changes are onto). Since the discontinuity sets are countable, dominated convergence gives convergence of absolute-distance kernels in \(L^2([0,1]^2)\) and also convergence of the grand distance means. Double centering is a bounded linear operation on these kernels. The grid from the paths \(a_n^{-1}S_{\lfloor nt\rfloor},b_n^{-1}T_{\lfloor nt\rfloor}\) uses indices 0 through \(n-1\) a.e.; replacing the one observation with index 0 by that with index \(n\) does not change the limit. Indeed, after relabeling cells, only one row and column of each raw kernel change and their combined area is at most \(2/n\), with eventually bounded normalized entries.

For either stable motion the occupation marginal is a.s. not a point mass: right continuity would make an a.e. constant path constant on \([0,1)\), while its value at a fixed interior time has a continuous nondegenerate distribution and its starting value is zero. For bounded random variables, the usual characterization of zero distance covariance as independence applies; in particular the distance variance of a nonconstant marginal is positive. The continuous mapping theorem therefore gives convergence of the normalized-kernel ratio. Separate positive rescalings of the two coordinates leave the distance correlation invariant.

**2. Strict positivity.** Conditional on the first path, let \(q(t)=L_\alpha(t)-\int_0^1L_\alpha(v)\,dv\) and \(Q(u)=\int_u^1q(t)\,dt\). These are bounded on the time interval; \(Q\) is absolutely continuous and \(Q(0)=Q(1)=0\). If the occupation distance covariance were zero, its two coordinates would be independent under this bounded occupation law. In particular their ordinary covariance \(Z=\int_0^1 q(t)L_\beta(t)\,dt\) would be zero. Conditional on the first path, integration by parts yields \(Z=\int_0^1Q(u)\,dL_\beta(u)\). For a deterministic step integrand the characteristic function of this integral is \(\exp(-|\theta|^\beta\int_0^1|Q|^\beta)\), by independence and scaling of stable increments. For the continuous \(Q\) above the same formula follows by step approximation: the left Riemann sums for the stochastic integral converge by summation by parts to \(-\int L_\beta\,dQ\) (the bounded path is continuous a.e. and \(Q\) is absolutely continuous), and the exponent integrals converge by uniform approximation. The integrand is conditionally nonzero: \(\int |Q|^\beta=0\) would imply \(Q\equiv 0\), hence \(q=0\) a.e. Thus conditionally \(Z\) is nondegenerate stable and has no atom at zero. This proves strict positivity almost surely.

**3. Strict upper bound.** If the limit were one, equality in kernel Cauchy–Schwarz would make the two double-centered kernels proportional a.e. with a factor \(c>0\). For the sample paths \(f,g\) define \(D(s,t)=|g(s)-g(t)|-c|f(s)-f(t)|\), \(r(s)=\int D(s,t)dt\), \(m=\int r(s)ds\). Then \(D(s,t)=r(s)+r(t)-m\) a.e. At every simultaneous continuity point of the paths the row mean is continuous in its time argument, by the absolute-distance triangle inequality. Approximating any pair of such points by pairs in the full-measure equality set extends the identity to these points, including the diagonal. Since \(D(s,s)=0\), there \(r(s)=m/2\) and \(D(s,t)=0\). On these times a scaled isometry of a nonconstant subset of the real line is affine: the distances to two distinct reference values determine each remaining value. Hence \(g=a+bf\) a.e. for some \(|b|=c\), and right continuity extends this to \([0,1)\); the starting values force \(a=0\). At fixed \(0<s<t<1\), the polynomial \(L_\alpha(s)L_\beta(t)-L_\alpha(t)L_\beta(s)\) would therefore vanish. The four evaluations have a joint Lebesgue density by independent nondegenerate stable increments in each coordinate and independence of the coordinates. The nonzero polynomial has a null zero set.

**4. Permutation failure.** For \(n\ge2\), a uniform permutation \(\pi\) gives the samplewise identity \(\mathbb E_\pi K_n^\pi=\bar a_{\cdot\cdot}\bar b_{\cdot\cdot}/(n-1)\), where the bars are grand means of the raw distance matrices. Indeed the sum of the diagonal entries of \(A\) is \(-n\bar a_{\cdot\cdot}\) and that of its off-diagonal entries is \(n\bar a_{\cdot\cdot}\); the mean permuted \(B\) entry is \(-\bar b_{\cdot\cdot}\) on the diagonal and \(\bar b_{\cdot\cdot}/(n-1)\) off it. When \(K_n(S,T)>0\), permutation leaves both marginal distance variances unchanged and \(K_n^\pi\ge0\); Markov's inequality gives \(p_n\le \bar a_{\cdot\cdot}\bar b_{\cdot\cdot}/[(n-1)K_n(S,T)]\). Divide the cross-covariance and the product of grand means by \(a_nb_n\). The latter normalized quantity is tight by step 1 and the former converges in law to a strictly positive variable by steps 1–2. Thus the bound tends to zero in probability and the event of zero observed cross-covariance has probability tending to zero. This argument uses no unconditional moment or exchangeability of levels over time.

## Verification

The included `CHECK.py` tests scale invariance and an exact permutation mean by exhaustion on a small array; it also gives deterministic and seeded stable-sample diagnostics for the bounds. These finite checks do not prove the weak convergence, strict bounds, or permutation conclusion, which follow from the arguments above.

## Relationship to prior work

Zhu and Xie prove the Brownian common-time occupation analogue for centered finite-positive-variance increments, including nonvanishing correlation and incorrect permutation calibration. The exact permutation identity is shared with that work. Here the novelty claim concerns the infinite-variance, possibly infinite-first-moment case, with conditional stable-integral nondegeneracy and càdlàg path convergence replacing the Gaussian continuous-path arguments. Basrak, Krizmanić and Segers discuss the stable partial-sum limit background, including the iid case. Dehling, Matsui, Mikosch, Samorodnitsky and Tafakori also consider stable processes, but sample iid replicates of entire discretized processes, not one pair of paths sampled at common times.

## Limitations

The theorem uses independent iid symmetric innovations in the stated stable domains of attraction. The limiting law is represented in terms of the two given stable indices; no comparison of the distributions at different index pairs is asserted. We do not claim a closed form for its expectation, full support on \([0,1]\), analogous conclusions for dependent innovations, or validity of moment-based calibration. Ordinary population distance correlation of fixed-time levels may be undefined when first moments fail; the bounded-path conditional occupation distance correlation remains well-defined almost surely.
