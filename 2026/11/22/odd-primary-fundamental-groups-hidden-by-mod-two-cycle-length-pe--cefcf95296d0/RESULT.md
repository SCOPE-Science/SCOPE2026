# Odd-primary fundamental groups hidden by mod-two cycle-length persistence

## Finding

For every integer \(q\ge3\) and \(d\ge q+1\), let \(\mathbf1=(1,\ldots,1)\in\mathbb Z^d\), let
\[
\Lambda_{q,d}=q^2\mathbb Z^d+q\mathbf1\mathbb Z,
\qquad
G_{q,d}=\operatorname{Cay}\bigl(\mathbb Z^d/\Lambda_{q,d},\{\pm e_1,\ldots,\pm e_d\}\bigr).
\]
The graph is finite, connected and simple. Define \(A_k(G)\), for integer \(k\ge2\), as the based fundamental group of the graph with a two-cell attached along every *simple* cycle having at most \(k\) edges; attach a cell for every such cycle, not for arbitrary closed walks.

With quotient maps as \(k\) increases, this entire group-valued persistence system is
\[
 A_k(G_{q,d})\cong
 \begin{cases}
 F_{(d-1)q^{2d-1}+1}, &2\le k\le3,\\
 \mathbb Z^d,&4\le k<q^2,\\
 \mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z,&q^2\le k<dq,\\
 1,&k\ge dq.
 \end{cases}
\]
Here \(F_r\) is the free group of rank \(r\). After the square-filling stage, the maps are the canonical quotients
\(\Lambda_{q,d}\twoheadrightarrow\Lambda_{q,d}/q^2\mathbb Z^d\cong\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z\twoheadrightarrow1\). The first transition sends graph loops to their displacement in \(\Lambda_{q,d}\).

For odd \(q\), the nontrivial intermediate group \(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z\) has zero first homology with \(\mathbb F_2\)-coefficients. Thus its vanishing mod-two cycle-length barcode does *not* certify triviality of the persistent fundamental group. The smallest stated instance is \(q=3,d=4\), a connected eight-regular graph with 2,187 vertices and transitions at lengths four, nine and twelve:
\[
 F_{6562}\ \longrightarrow\ \mathbb Z^4\ \longrightarrow\ \mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z\ \longrightarrow\ 1.
\]
The associated mod-two death multiset in this instance has 6,558 deaths at four and four deaths at nine; there is no mod-two death at twelve.

## Assumptions and scope

All graphs are undirected without loops or multiple edges, and \(\pm e_i\) are interpreted as distinct group elements. The base vertex is the zero coset. The integer cutoff is inclusive. The lattice \(\Lambda_{q,d}\) has index \(q^{2d-1}\); since its nonzero vectors have \(\ell_1\)-length at least \(q^2\ge9\), the graph is simple, with \(q^{2d-1}\) vertices and \(d q^{2d-1}\) edges. Consequently its ordinary graph fundamental group has rank \((d-1)q^{2d-1}+1\).

## Proof

**Step 1: Relations at length four.** A closed graph walk lifts to a nearest-neighbor walk in the standard integer grid \(\mathbb Z^d\). Its endpoint displacement belongs to \(\Lambda_{q,d}\). Every three-cycle or four-cycle in \(G_{q,d}\) has displacement zero, because its length is smaller than the minimum nonzero lattice norm \(q^2\). A simple four-cycle of the standard grid is an elementary unit square, so every four-cycle in the quotient is a projected coordinate square. Attaching all such squares gives precisely the two-skeleton of the standard cubical decomposition of \(\mathbb R^d/\Lambda_{q,d}\), a \(d\)-torus. The two-skeleton of a connected CW complex has the same fundamental group as the whole complex; since \(\mathbb R^d\) is the universal cover, the group is \(\Lambda_{q,d}\cong\mathbb Z^d\). No triangles occur, so the stages two and three have the free-group rank stated above.

**Step 2: Two length barriers.** Write any \(v\in\Lambda_{q,d}\) as \(v=q^2z+aq\mathbf1\) with \(z\in\mathbb Z^d\), and reduce \(a\) modulo \(q\). If \(a\equiv0\pmod q\), then \(v\in q^2\mathbb Z^d\), and every nonzero such vector has \(\ell_1\)-length at least \(q^2\). If \(a\not\equiv0\pmod q\), every coordinate of \(v\) is a nonzero multiple of \(q\), so \(\|v\|_1\ge dq\). Both bounds are attained: the coordinate vectors \(q^2e_i\) have length \(q^2\), and the diagonal \(u=q\mathbf1\) has length \(dq\).

Any cycle of length \(k\) has a lift with displacement \(v\) satisfying \(\|v\|_1\le k\). Therefore for \(4\le k<q^2\) all newly attached cycles have trivial displacement after the square fillings and impose no further group relations. For \(q^2\le k<dq\), every newly imposed displacement lies in \(q^2\mathbb Z^d\).

**Step 3: Exactness of the two thresholds.** Each coordinate walk of \(q^2\) consecutive positive \(e_i\)-steps projects to a *simple* cycle: no proper subwalk can have nonzero displacement in \(\Lambda_{q,d}\). These cycles impose all generators \(q^2e_i\) of \(q^2\mathbb Z^d\). Thus the intermediate group is exactly
\[
\Lambda_{q,d}/q^2\mathbb Z^d\cong\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z,
\]
generated by the coset of \(u=q\mathbf1\), which has exact order \(q\).

The monotone walk consisting of \(q\) positive steps in each of the \(d\) coordinate directions has length \(dq\), endpoint displacement \(u\), and projects to a simple cycle. Indeed, the coordinate differences between distinct partial sums all lie in \([0,q]\); any such difference belonging to the lattice must be zero or \(u\), the latter only for the two endpoints. Therefore this cycle kills the remaining generator at \(k=dq\), and all later groups are trivial. Every transition described is induced by the natural quotient map, proving the group-valued persistence claim.

**Step 4: Mod-two blindness.** For odd \(q\), the tensor product \((\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z)\otimes\mathbb F_2\) vanishes, although the group itself is nontrivial. In the example \(q=3,d=4\), ordinary graph rank is 6,562 and square filling leaves rank four, so 6,558 mod-two classes die at length four. The four remaining classes die at nine. No mod-two class detects the nontrivial group between nine and twelve.

## Verification

The included standalone standard-library checker reconstructs the 2,187 vertices of \(G_{3,4}\), checks degree eight, excludes triangles, verifies embedded coordinate and diagonal cycles, enumerates all nonzero lattice displacement vectors of \(\ell_1\)-length at most twelve, and verifies that the first noncoordinate displacement has length twelve. It also checks the exact count of first-shell coordinate vectors. The checker output is included. These finite checks support, but do not replace, the complete all-parameter proof above.

## Relationship to prior work

The cycle-relation filtration is due to Rajković, *Persistent A-homotopy of complex networks*, arXiv:2611.09967v1 (first public 29 October 2026), Sections 3.1–3.2. That paper gives the group construction, its mod-two summary and minimum-cycle-basis description, and computes the square-filled group of a rectangular periodic grid as \(\mathbb Z^2\) (Proposition 6(d)). It explicitly notes that mod-two abelianization cannot detect odd torsion. The present result supplies a specific infinite family of finite Cayley graphs and calculates the **entire group-valued filtration**, realizing cyclic odd torsion during an interval where the paper's mod-two summary is zero. The standard cubical covering-space description supplies the geometric input; neither covering-space theory alone nor the mod-two barcode implies this exact family and its two threshold lengths.

## Limitations

The theorem concerns this explicitly defined family of quotient-grid Cayley graphs and the relation-length filtration; it makes no statement about all graph families, higher homotopy groups, or an associated physical network mechanism. It does not imply that every zero mod-two barcode hides torsion. The 2026 paper cites a companion manuscript on algebraic aspects of the filtration; a complete public version of that manuscript was not identified in the targeted accessible sources. An unindexed overlapping construction remains a publication-priority risk. The mathematical proof and checker have not undergone independent external audit.
