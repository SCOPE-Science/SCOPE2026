# Review of Off-axis Lee–Yang density peaks induced by triangular decoration

## Correctness — PASS

The two hub-conditioned local polynomials are reconstructed directly from all four peripheral pair states, yielding an exact equation for all integers \(m\ge1\). The nonzero winding ellipse and strictly negative boundary-phase derivative give exactly \(2m+1\) simple circle zeros; the quantized root spacing gives the density and the first-root asymptotics. A sign-certified quadratic derivative proves precisely one angular interior maximum on each half circle. No finite experiment is used as a substitute for the all-size proof. The checker independently verifies 18 exact cut enumerations and 18 numerical polynomial-root/phase comparisons, plus derivative, normalization, peak and first-root cases.

## Originality — PASS (examined evidence; historical risk remains)

The closest actual published competitor is the arbitrary-coupling star Lee–Yang tomography result [4]. Its factorization into real linear leaf terms and its Poisson-kernel mixture give strict monotonicity toward the negative real axis, whereas the present triangular local factor has negative discriminant and produces two strict interior peaks. The full statement therefore cannot be obtained merely by replacing bond values in that theorem. General phase coexistence and Lee–Yang circle principles from [2] are used as known methods; [3] has a fixed degree hypothesis violated by the growing hub; [1] treats complete graphs and annealed scale-free models rather than the friendship geometry. The 2001 Cayley-tree and chain work [5] was checked at abstract level only; earlier unindexed decorated-star calculations remain possible. A failed search is not asserted to prove historical novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The result identifies a motivated qualitative transition in a basic graph decoration: correlated spin pairs in triangles produce a limiting angular Lee–Yang density with two off-axis maxima which **no distribution of independent ferromagnetic star couplings can reproduce**. The full-support density, explicit peak angles, all-finite-size simple circle roots and sharp leading-zero coefficient provide a complete, reproducible description of this topology-sensitive zero geometry. The finding is not marketed as an infinite-lattice phase transition.
