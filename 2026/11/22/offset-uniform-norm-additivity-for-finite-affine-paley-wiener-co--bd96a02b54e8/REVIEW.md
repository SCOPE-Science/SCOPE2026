# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS
The domain and target are specified by Fourier supports. Real affine composition has exact norm \(a^{-1/2}\), and its logarithmic-frequency action is the stated shift with phase \(e^{ib e^x/a}\). Test packets supported at frequencies approaching zero give offset-independent essential-norm lower bounds. Their finite-overlap formula tends to the Mellin multiplier symbol. Full-line Fourier diagonalization gives the exact zero-offset upper bound. Phase alignment is equivalent to a Kronecker orbit-closure condition; compact perturbations vanish on the weakly null packets. This proves both directions of the equivalence and flat approximation numbers. The resonance calculation is an exact concave-quadratic maximization, not a finite numerical experiment.

## Originality — PASS with residual risk
The 2026 Fock finite-sum paper establishes no-cancellation and bounded comparisons, not an exact offset-uniform Paley–Wiener norm formula. A 2026 Paley–Wiener paper establishes individual affine norms and two-summand no-cancellation, but does not give the stated finite-sum arithmetic criterion. The 2010 range paper and 2025 spectral paper concern individual compositions. Relevant published research records on tree-Hardy composition sums and disk-composition resonance have different spaces and invariants. No equivalent statement was found in the performed source and published-record searches. The main residual concern is whether classical general representations of affine groups or Wiener–Hopf operators already give the precise offset-uniform equivalence implicitly; literature absence is not proved.

## Mathematical value — PASS
This is a complete classification of **when the sharp triangle bound persists simultaneously for all real affine offsets** in a natural finite-sum operator family. It gives the exact essential norm and all approximation numbers without computing a noncommutative finite-sum norm for each set of offsets. The sharp rational-resonance counterexample shows that coefficient phases and multiplicative slope relations can create a genuine obstruction; this is not an isolated tiny constant improvement. The boundary is useful in quantitative signal-warp summation and noncompact approximation theory. The classical Mellin and Kronecker ingredients are credited rather than asserted novel.
