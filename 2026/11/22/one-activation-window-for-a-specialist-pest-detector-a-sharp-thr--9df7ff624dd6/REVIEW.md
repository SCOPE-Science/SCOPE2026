# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

For every budget and positive rate/weight parameter admitted by the claim, the weighted miss objective is strictly convex. Its derivative at zero has the sign of an explicitly defined transformed marginal. The latter is convex for every number of nonspecialist taxa, and strictly convex unless all their generalist rates coincide with the target's; the constant case is handled separately. A one-term nonspecialist expression is monotone, preventing two transitions with two pests. An exact three-pest construction has two positive roots and a strictly interior minimizing mixture between them. This is an analytic proof, not an inference from a finite grid. The accompanying executable checks pass 32,550 supplementary assertions.

## Originality — PASS on inspected evidence

The closest motivating source is Nguyen et al. (2024), https://doi.org/10.1002/eap.2955. The relevant publicly readable sections on the multi-trap detection law, costs, optimizations, selected tables and discussion were examined; supplementary appendices were not inspected. It uses a broader discounted total-cost model and computational optimization rather than the fixed-budget Poisson-weighted-miss theorem proved here; it does not report the sharp three-pest re-entry example. Moore and McCarthy (2016), https://doi.org/10.1111/2041-210X.12564, provide Poisson nondetection and survey allocation across sites and visits, not the claimed single-target specialist switching law. Relevant published mathematical research records concerning ecological trap choice, two-site survey order, and budget nesting have different objects and do not imply this theorem. Classical convexity itself is not new, and the literature search is not a proof of priority. The abstract of Kompas et al. (2019) was inspected but not its full text, and a historically equivalent niche result remains possible.

## Value — PASS

The explicitly motivated management decision is whether a single-target specialist belongs in a constrained multi-pest surveillance portfolio. A common monotone-intuition shortcut—retain a specialized device once it becomes worthwhile as the total budget rises—is mathematically false already for three species. The theorem supplies a sharp universal limit (one connected activation interval for any finite target count), a minimal biologically feasible counterexample and exact switching budgets. These are structural boundaries for decision models, not merely a numerical optimization or a small parameter perturbation. The idealizations limit practical use and the interior gain can be small.
