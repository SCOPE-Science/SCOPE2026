A detector specialized for a single pest can be optimal for an interval of surveillance budgets, but never for two separated budget intervals under a fixed-budget weighted-Poisson-miss model. Three pests are both necessary and sufficient for a specialist to be unused at low budgets, used in a strict mixture at intermediate budgets, and abandoned again at high budgets; an exact example switches at \(\log(4-2\sqrt2)\) and \(\log(4+2\sqrt2)\).
