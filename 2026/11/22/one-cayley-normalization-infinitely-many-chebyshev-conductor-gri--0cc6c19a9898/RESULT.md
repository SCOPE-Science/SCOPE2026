# One Cayley normalization, infinitely many Chebyshev conductor grids

## Finding

For all integers \(m,n\ge2\) over \(\mathbb C\), define the affine surface
\[
S_{m,n}:\quad x^2+T_m(y)^2+T_n(z)^2-2xT_m(y)T_n(z)-1=0,
\]
where \(T_j\) is the first-kind Chebyshev polynomial. Write \(U_j\) for the second-kind polynomial and put
\[
f=U_{m-1}(y)U_{n-1}(z),\quad u=x-T_m(y)T_n(z),\quad h=(y^2-1)(z^2-1).
\]
The normalization of **every** surface in this infinite family is the same affine four-nodal Cayley cubic:
\[
\widetilde S=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb C[y,z,w]/(w^2-h)
\cong\{a^2+y^2+z^2-2ayz-1=0\},\qquad a=yz+w,
\]
with normalization map \((y,z,w)\mapsto(x,y,z)\) given by \(x=T_m(y)T_n(z)+fw\). The conductor in the original coordinate ring is exactly \((f,u)\), and the quotient of the normalization by the original ring, as a module over \(\mathbb C[y,z]\), is \(\mathbb C[y,z]/(f)\).

The reduced singular locus consists of \(m-1\) vertical and \(n-1\) horizontal affine lines, whose pairwise intersections form the incidence graph \(K_{m-1,n-1}\), plus exactly four isolated ordinary nodes. It has \((m-1)(n-1)\) crossings of the two families and \(2(m+n-2)\) distinct pinch points. The complete formal local hypersurface forms, with new local coordinates \(u,v,w\), are

| Point stratum | Completed local equation | Number |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| General point of a double curve | \(u^2-v^2=0\) | Zariski-open along each curve |
| Crossings of double curves | \(u^2-v^2w^2=0\) | \((m-1)(n-1)\) |
| Pinch points | \(u^2-v^2w=0\) | \(2(m+n-2)\) |
| Isolated ordinary nodes | \(u^2-vw=0\) | \(4\) |

At each crossing and pinch point the intrinsic Jacobian singular subscheme differs from its reduced support by a length-one embedded structure, and there are no other nonreduced Jacobian contributions. Its total zero-dimensional excess length is \((m-1)(n-1)+2(m+n-2)\). Moreover \(S_{m,n}\cong S_{p,q}\) as abstract complex affine varieties **if and only if** \(\{m,n\}=\{p,q\}\).

## Assumptions and scope

The ground field is \(\mathbb C\) and \(m,n,p,q\) are integers at least \(2\), with **no coprimality assumption**. Define \(T_0=1\), \(T_1=t\), \(T_{j+1}=2tT_j-T_{j-1}\), and \(U_0=1\), \(U_1=2t\), \(U_{j+1}=2tU_j-U_{j-1}\). The *intrinsic Jacobian singular scheme* of an affine hypersurface is defined by its equation and its three first derivatives; its reduced support is the usual singular locus. Statements about analytic germs mean completed local rings at closed complex points.

## Proof

**1. Universal quadratic normal form.** The standard polynomial identity
\[
T_j(t)^2-1=(t^2-1)U_{j-1}(t)^2
\]
(for example by setting \(t=\cos\theta\), then using polynomial identity) implies
\[
F_{m,n}=(x-T_m(y)T_n(z))^2-(y^2-1)(z^2-1)U_{m-1}(y)^2U_{n-1}(z)^2=u^2-f^2h.
\]
Set \(A=\mathbb C[y,z]\), \(R=A[u]/(u^2-f^2h)\), \(B=A[w]/(w^2-h)\). Since \(y-1\) occurs to odd order in \(h\), the latter is nonsquare in \(\mathbb C(y,z)\). Thus \(R\) and \(B\) are domains. The substitution \(u=fw\) embeds \(R=A\oplus fAw\) in \(B=A\oplus Aw\) and gives \(\operatorname{Frac}R=\operatorname{Frac}B\), because \(w=u/f\). The element \(w\) satisfies the monic equation \(w^2-h=0\), so \(B\) is finite over \(R\).

**2. Normalization and exact conductor.** The hypersurface \(B\) is Cohen–Macaulay. Its Jacobian is generated by \(2w\), \(-2y(z^2-1)\), and \(-2z(y^2-1)\). On \(B\) all three vanish only when \(w=0\) and \(y,z\in\{\pm1\}\), four isolated surface nodes. Hence \(B\) is regular in codimension one and satisfies Serre \(S_2\), so it is integrally closed. The finite birational map from \(B\) is the normalization. The coordinate \(a=yz+w\) transforms \(w^2=h\) into \(a^2+y^2+z^2-2ayz-1=0\).

An arbitrary \(r=a_0+fb_0w\in R\) belongs to the conductor \((R:B)\) exactly when \(rw=a_0w+fb_0h\) belongs to \(R\), namely when \(a_0\in fA\). Therefore
\[
(R:B)=fA\oplus fAw=fB=(f,u)\subset R,
\qquad B/R\cong (A/fA)w.
\]
The two factors of \(f\) each have distinct simple roots: the roots of \(U_{j-1}\) are \(\cos(k\pi/j)\) for \(1\le k<j\), and none equals \(\pm1\). The support of the conductor is therefore a reduced grid of affine lines.

**3. Complete singular locus and incidence.** Where \(f\) is invertible, the normalization map is an isomorphism; its singular points are the four displayed \(A_1\) points. Where \(f=0\), the equation \(u^2-f^2h=0\) and all its derivatives vanish precisely when \(u=0\). Thus the remaining singular locus is \(V(u,f)\), a reduced union of the lines
\[
C_\alpha:\ y=\alpha,\ u=0,\quad U_{m-1}(\alpha)=0;\qquad
D_\beta:\ z=\beta,\ u=0,\quad U_{n-1}(\beta)=0.
\]
Each \(C_\alpha\) meets each \(D_\beta\) at one point; same-family lines do not meet. Exactly \((m-1)(n-1)\) intersection points occur. The four nodes have both \(y,z\in\{\pm1\}\), so do not lie on these lines.

**4. All formal singularity types.** In a completed local ring at any closed point, an invertible formal power series has a square root because the ground field is \(\mathbb C\). At a general point of one line, \(h\) and the other factor of \(f\) are units, while the vanishing factor of \(f\) is a regular local coordinate. Absorbing units gives \(u^2-v^2=0\). At a line intersection both factors of \(f\) vanish simply but \(h\) is a unit, yielding \(u^2-v^2w^2=0\). A vertical line meets \(z=\pm1\) and a horizontal line meets \(y=\pm1\); there a single factor of \(f\) and a single factor of \(h\) vanish simply, yielding \(u^2-v^2w=0\). These are exactly \(2(m-1)+2(n-1)\) distinct pinch points. Finally, at \(y,z\in\{\pm1\}\), \(f\) is a unit and both factors of \(h\) vanish simply, yielding \(u^2-vw=0\). The cases partition the full singular support, so the classification is exhaustive.

**5. The intrinsic Jacobian scheme.** Taking the three derivatives and the equation gives the following completed Jacobian ideals in the corresponding local coordinates:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{double curve}&(u,v)\\
\text{pinch}&(u,vw,v^2)\\
\text{crossing}&(u,vw^2,v^2w)\\
\text{node}&(u,v,w)
\end{array}
\]
For the pinch, the radical is \((u,v)\), with quotient \((u,v)/(u,vw,v^2)\) of length one at the point. At a crossing the radical is \((u,vw)\), with quotient \((u,vw)/(u,vw^2,v^2w)\) also of length one. The other two local Jacobian ideals are radical. This proves the exact embedded defect count. These computations concern complete local rings, so finite coordinate sample checks are not used to infer them.

**6. Isomorphism classification.** Every variety isomorphism preserves the singular locus, its irreducible curve components, and which components meet. Discarding the four isolated nodes, the component intersection graph is \(K_{m-1,n-1}\); its two bipartition sizes determine the unordered pair \(\{m-1,n-1\}\). Conversely swapping \(y,z\) identifies \(S_{m,n}\) with \(S_{n,m}\), and identical ordered indices give the identity. Thus the stated equivalence holds.

## Verification

The accompanying `chebyshev_checker.py` performs exact polynomial Chebyshev identities, checks simple-root and endpoint values, verifies each normal-form Jacobian ideal, and prints the combinatorial numbers for sample pairs \((2,2),(2,3),(3,5),(4,4),(5,6)\). The checker is a finite diagnostic, **not** an infinite proof. The complete proof is the ring-theoretic argument above. As a notable instance, the source's \((m,n)=(3,5)\) surface has six double curves, eight intersections, twelve pinch points, four additional isolated nodes, and twenty localized embedded length-one Jacobian defects.

## Relationship to prior work

Mascarin, *Intersections, Equations, and Singularities of Lissajous Varieties* (2026), gives the \((3,5)\) equation in Example 1.2 and identifies its six singular curves and four isolated points in Section 5. Bel-Afia, Meroni and Telen, *Chebyshev Varieties* (2024), Proposition 5.8 and examples, describe general singular supports for related cosine varieties. Mascarin and Telen, *Lissajous Varieties* (2025), develops defining equations and the Cayley cubic construction. None of the cited passages supplies this index-independent normalization together with the exact conductor ideal, all completed local types, embedded Jacobian excess or isomorphism classification. The new contribution is **not** the pre-existing singular-support description or the familiar Cayley cubic equation; it is the complete intrinsic normalization/conductor and local scheme theorem for this entire family.

## Limitations

The theorem is over \(\mathbb C\) only; it does not assert analogous forms in small positive characteristic. The normalization has four ordinary nodes; it is **not** a smooth resolution. Local types are completed-local descriptions, not a global claim that the double curves are split into two components. The conductor cover over each double line may be connected, and its ramification is the reason for the pinch points. The embedded-length calculation concerns the ordinary Jacobian singular scheme, not the full deformation theory. Literature novelty is a best-knowledge comparison and does not certify absolute priority.
