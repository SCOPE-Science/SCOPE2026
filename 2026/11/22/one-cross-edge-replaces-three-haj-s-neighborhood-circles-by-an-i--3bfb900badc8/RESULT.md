# One cross-edge replaces three Hajós neighborhood circles by an integral two-class

## Finding

Let \(H_{a,b}\) be the usual Hajós merge of two complete graphs with \(a,b\ge5\). There is a canonical orbit of *cross-edges*: each joins an ordinary neighbor of the merged endpoint in the first clique to one in the second. Adjoining any such edge produces \(G_{a,b}\). Write \(X=\mathcal N(H_{a,b})\) and \(Y=\mathcal N(G_{a,b})\), using **open** neighborhoods.

For every integer \(j\), their integral reduced homology groups satisfy
\[
\widetilde H_j(Y;\mathbb Z)\cong
\mathbb Z^{\mathbf1_{j=2}+\mathbf1_{j=a-3}+\mathbf1_{j=b-3}}.
\]
All groups are torsion-free. In contrast, \(X\simeq S^{a-3}\vee S^{b-3}\vee\bigvee^3 S^1\). The inclusion \(X\hookrightarrow Y\) kills all three integral first-homology generators and injects every higher homology group as a primitive summand, with one additional primitive degree-two class. Equivalently,
\[
H_j(Y,X;\mathbb Z)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z^4,&j=2,\\
0,&j\ne2,
\end{cases}
\]
and the boundary map \(H_2(Y,X;\mathbb Z)\to H_1(X;\mathbb Z)\) has rank three and saturated image. The new complex is simply connected. No stronger integral homotopy classification is claimed.

## Assumptions and scope

Fix integers \(a,b\ge5\). Name the vertices \(w,y,z\) and disjoint sets \(A,B\) of cardinalities \(a-2\) and \(b-2\). Put every edge within \(\{y\}\cup A\) and within \(\{z\}\cup B\); join \(w\) to every point of \(A\cup B\); and join \(y\) to \(z\). There are no other edges. This is the Hajós merge of \(K_a\) and \(K_b\), with the two removed edges incident to \(w\). Choose \(\alpha\in A\) and \(\beta\in B\), and adjoin exactly the edge \(\alpha\beta\). Every such choice is related by the natural permutations of \(A\) and \(B\).

For any finite loopless graph \(G\), \(\mathcal N(G)\) has faces precisely the vertex sets contained in the open neighborhood of some graph vertex. The geometric realization is implicit. All groups are integral singular (equivalently simplicial) homology, and relative groups refer to the natural inclusion. The two parameter lower bounds prevent one-dimensional sphere summands from the component clique complexes from interfering with the edge-change calculation.

## Proof

**1. The pre-addition decomposition.** For a finite vertex set \(S\), denote its full simplex by \(\Delta(S)\). Let \(T_A\) be the subcomplex on \(\{w,y\}\cup A\) generated by \(\Delta(A)\) and the simplices \(\Delta(\{w,y\}\cup(A\setminus\{u\}))\) for all \(u\in A\). Let \(T_B\) be the analogous subcomplex on \(\{w,z\}\cup B\). The union of the latter facets in \(T_A\) is
\[
C_A=\Delta(\{w,y\})*\partial\Delta(A),
\]
which is contractible, and \(C_A\cap\Delta(A)=\partial\Delta(A)\). The simplex-attachment cofibration therefore gives \(T_A\simeq S^{a-3}\); similarly \(T_B\simeq S^{b-3}\). They meet in exactly the vertex \(w\).

Every open neighborhood of \(H_{a,b}\) belongs to the following complete list of generating subcomplexes:
\[
X=T_A\cup T_B\cup U\cup P\cup Q,
\quad U=\Delta(A\cup B),\quad
P=\Delta(A\cup\{z\}),\quad
Q=\Delta(B\cup\{y\}).
\]
Attach the three last simplices in the displayed order. Each meets the complex built previously in exactly two disjoint nonempty contractible sets: respectively \(\Delta(A)\sqcup\Delta(B)\), then \(\Delta(A)\sqcup\{z\}\), then \(\Delta(B)\sqcup\{y\}\). Attaching a contractible simplex along two such sets adds one free circle to a connected complex. Consequently
\[
X\simeq S^{a-3}\vee S^{b-3}\vee\bigvee^3 S^1.
\]
For the circle basis, regard \(w\) as the original connector between the two pieces, and call the other three connectors \(U,P,Q\). The corresponding independent loops are \(\gamma_U,\gamma_P,\gamma_Q\), obtained by returning through \(w\).

**2. The new neighborhoods and relative groups.** Put \(A_0=A\setminus\{\alpha\}\) and \(B_0=B\setminus\{\beta\}\). Only the two open neighborhoods centered at \(\alpha,\beta\) change after inserting \(\alpha\beta\). Thus
\[
Y=X\cup E_A\cup E_B,
\quad E_A=\Delta(\{w,y,\beta\}\cup A_0),\quad
E_B=\Delta(\{w,z,\alpha\}\cup B_0).
\]
The two new simplices intersect each other in just \(\{w\}\subset X\). By direct inspection of the five generating families of \(X\),
\[
E_A\cap X=\Delta(\{w,y\}\cup A_0)
\cup\Delta(\{\beta\}\cup A_0)
\cup\Delta(\{\beta,w\})
\cup\Delta(\{\beta,y\}),
\]
and symmetrically
\[
E_B\cap X=\Delta(\{w,z\}\cup B_0)
\cup\Delta(\{\alpha\}\cup B_0)
\cup\Delta(\{\alpha,w\})
\cup\Delta(\{\alpha,z\}).
\]
In either intersection the first two simplices form a contractible union, while the final two edges each attach at their endpoints and add an independent circle. Hence both intersections are homotopy equivalent to \(S^1\vee S^1\). Excision for the two attachments yields \(H_2(Y,X;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^4\), and all other relative homology groups vanish.

**3. The integral attaching matrix.** Orient the two loop generators in \(E_A\cap X\) using the added edges \(\beta w\) and \(\beta y\), and those in \(E_B\cap X\) using \(\alpha w\) and \(\alpha z\). In the connector basis \((\gamma_U,\gamma_P,\gamma_Q)\), their images under the long-exact-sequence connecting map can be oriented to be
\[
M=\begin{pmatrix}
1&1&1&1\\
0&0&0&-1\\
0&-1&0&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Indeed the two routes to each newly attached endpoint go through the central \(U\)-connector or through one of the existing \(w,P,Q\) connectors, exactly as the four columns record. The three columns numbered one, two, and four give a minor of absolute determinant one. Hence the map is surjective over \(\mathbb Z\), with primitive image and kernel \(\mathbb Z\). The long exact sequence now proves \(\widetilde H_1(Y;\mathbb Z)=0\), an injective and split map on \(H_2(X;\mathbb Z)\) with free cokernel \(\mathbb Z\), and isomorphisms in higher degrees. Substituting the homology of \(X\) proves the formula in the Finding section and excludes all torsion. Finally, the same four attaching loops normally generate the free group \(\pi_1(X)\) on the three connector circles. Van Kampen therefore gives \(\pi_1(Y)=1\).

## Verification

The standalone Python verifier independently constructs the graph from its edge set, builds the complete open-neighborhood complex by enumerating all subsets of each actual neighborhood, and computes the full mod-two boundary ranks. It checks each parameter pair \(5\le a\le7\), \(a\le b\le8\), including the overlapping homology degrees when \(a=5\) or \(b=5\). It also checks exact set-theoretic identities for the two attachment intersections and verifies an integral unit minor of the stated matrix. All nine parameter-pair checks passed.

## Relationship to prior work

Braun and Vega introduced topology-sensitive analysis of Hajós operations and proved, in Theorem 3.3 of their 2018 preprint, the presence of a circle wedge summand for broad classes of Hajós merges. The four-connector argument also follows by specializing their construction to the unaugmented complete-graph merge. The new claim differs: it computes the **integral map** when a single previously missing cross-edge is inserted, proving that three first-homology generators die simultaneously while one primitive second-dimensional class appears, for all \(a,b\ge5\).

Related work on partially matched **disjoint** clique pairs concerns different graphs (there is no identified merged endpoint). A block-graph decomposition also does not apply: the graph here has additional cross-connections and does not decompose into clique blocks meeting at cut vertices. These statements distinguish the mathematical hypotheses rather than assert novelty solely from a lack of title matches.

## Limitations

The theorem treats exactly one added cross-edge of the specified orbit, not arbitrary new edges, several simultaneous additions, or arbitrary Hajós inputs. It calculates integral homology and the induced inclusion maps; aside from simple connectivity of the new complex, no unstable homotopy equivalence is claimed. Published or unindexed equivalent calculations may exist. The nearby indexed disjoint-clique result was available as an abstract rather than complete source text.
