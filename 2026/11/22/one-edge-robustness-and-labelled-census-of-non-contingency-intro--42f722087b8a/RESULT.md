# One-edge robustness and labelled census of non-contingency introspection on symmetric frames

## Finding
Let \(G\) be a finite simple undirected graph interpreted as an **irreflexive symmetric** Kripke frame by its adjacency relation. The non-contingency modality is \(\Delta\varphi:=\Box\varphi\lor\Box\neg\varphi\). A graph is called *valid* when the schema \(\Delta p\to\Delta\Delta p\) holds at every vertex under every Boolean valuation of \(p\).

**Structure and all single-edge edits.** A graph is valid if and only if each connected component is complete multipartite, with singleton isolated components allowed. For a valid graph \(G\), let \(k\) be its number of isolated vertices. For each nontrivial component \(C\), write \(a_{C,1},\ldots,a_{C,r_C}\) for the sizes of its uniquely defined nonadjacency classes (\(r_C\ge2\)), and let \(s_C\) and \(t_C\) count classes of size one and size two, respectively. Call a component a *star* when \(r_C=2\) and one part is a singleton (including \(K_2\)); set \(h_C=2\) for \(K_2\) and \(h_C=1\) for other stars. The exact numbers of individual edge removals and individual nonedge insertions that leave a valid graph are
\[
\begin{split}
 d(G)&=\sum_{C\text{ star}}|E(C)|+\sum_{C\text{ not star}}\binom{s_C}{2},\\
 a(G)&=\sum_C t_C+\binom{k}{2}+k\sum_{C\text{ star}}h_C.
\end{split}
\]
Both sums over \(C\) run over nontrivial components. These formulas classify the admissible edit **locations**, not just their totals: removable edges are either edges of star components or edges between two distinct singleton parts in nonstar components; insertable edges are either within a two-element part, between two isolated vertices, or from an isolated vertex to a star center (either endpoint of \(K_2\)).

**Exact labelled census.** Define \(B(x)=\exp(e^x-1)\), the exponential generating function of Bell numbers, and let \(\mathcal V_n\) denote all valid graphs on the fixed label set \([n]\). Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
 H(x)&:=\sum_{n\ge0}|\mathcal V_n|\frac{x^n}{n!}
   =\exp\bigl(x+B(x)-e^x\bigr),\\
 D(x)&:=\frac{x^2}{2}B(x)+x^2(e^x-1),\\
 \sum_{n\ge0}\left(\sum_{G\in\mathcal V_n}d(G)\right)\frac{x^n}{n!}
 &=\sum_{n\ge0}\left(\sum_{G\in\mathcal V_n}a(G)\right)\frac{x^n}{n!}
 =H(x)D(x).
\end{aligned}
\]
Here \(n=0\) is a formal combinatorial convention; actual nonempty Kripke frames correspond to \(n\ge1\). The first valid-graph counts for \(n=0,\ldots,10\) are
\(1,1,2,8,40,227,1484,11104,93195,862813,8715478\). The first counts of safe *directed edge toggles* (separately insertions and deletions summed across all valid graphs), for \(n=0,\ldots,9\), are \(0,0,1,12,72,450,3435,29946,283612,2897208\).

## Assumptions and scope
All graphs are finite, labelled when enumerated, simple, undirected, without loops or parallel edges. The accessibility relation is **exactly** the irreflexive adjacency relation, not its reflexive or transitive closure. An empty or singleton successor set satisfies \(\Delta\varphi\) vacuously. A valuation assigns \(p\) independently and arbitrarily to every vertex. A graph is *complete multipartite* when its vertices partition into nonempty independent classes with all possible edges between different classes. Every connected nontrivial complete multipartite graph has at least two classes, uniquely recovered as equivalence classes of nonadjacency. An *edge deletion* removes exactly one existing unordered edge; an *edge insertion* adds exactly one absent unordered edge on the unchanged vertex set. A safe edit is one whose resulting graph continues to validate the schema. Connected components of one vertex are treated only as isolated vertices, not as stars.

## Proof
**1. Necessity of complete multipartite components.** Let \(a,b\) be nonadjacent vertices with common neighbour \(w\). Suppose \(N(a)\ne N(b)\). Interchange \(a,b\) if necessary so that some \(v\in N(a)\setminus N(b)\) exists. Give \(p\) truth value true at \(b\) and false everywhere else. Since \(b\notin N(a)\) and \(b\notin N(v)\), non-contingency \(\Delta p\) is true at both \(a\) and \(v\). Since \(a,b\in N(w)\), \(\Delta p\) is false at \(w\). But \(v,w\in N(a)\), so \(\Delta\Delta p\) is false at \(a\), contradicting validity. Therefore every nonadjacent pair with a common neighbour has identical open neighbourhoods.

In any connected valid component, a shortest path of length at least three has initial vertices \(a,u,b,v\). Then \(a,b\) are nonadjacent with a common neighbour \(u\), so \(N(a)=N(b)\). Because \(v\in N(b)\), it follows that \(v\in N(a)\), contradicting shortestness. Thus every connected component has diameter at most two. Every nonadjacent pair in such a component therefore shares a common neighbour, and hence has equal open neighbourhoods. The relation of equality or nonadjacency is an equivalence relation: if \(N(a)=N(b)\) and \(N(b)=N(c)\), then \(a,c\) are nonadjacent because open neighbourhoods in loopless graphs cannot contain their own vertex, and the original relation is transitive. Its classes are independent, and distinct classes are completely joined. This is precisely complete multipartiteness.

**2. Sufficiency.** Fix a vertex \(w\) in part \(A\) of a connected complete multipartite component. If \(\Delta p(w)\) is true, every vertex outside \(A\) has the same \(p\)-value \(\varepsilon\). When there are two parts, all neighbours \(y\) of \(w\) have the identical successor set \(A\), so the truth of \(\Delta p(y)\) is constant among these neighbours. When there are at least three parts, each such \(y\) sees every vertex of \(A\) and at least one vertex outside \(A\) (in a third part), all those exterior vertices having value \(\varepsilon\). Consequently \(\Delta p(y)\) is true exactly when all vertices in \(A\) have value \(\varepsilon\), again independent of \(y\). Hence \(\Delta\Delta p(w)\) holds. An isolated vertex automatically satisfies the implication. Since the argument is valuation-independent, all such graphs are valid.

**3. Exact deletions.** A connected complete multipartite graph with a bridge must be a star: if at least three parts exist, every edge lies in a triangle, while in a complete bipartite graph with both parts of size at least two every edge lies in a cycle. Thus every edge of a star, and only such an edge in a bridged component, may be removed to split the component; its two resulting components are a smaller star or isolated vertices and remain complete multipartite. If a removed edge \(uv\) is not a bridge, the old connected component remains connected. In the new graph \(u,v\) are nonadjacent and must have identical neighbourhoods. Writing \(u,v\) in their original distinct parts, their remaining neighbourhoods can coincide exactly when both original parts had size one: any extra member of either part witnesses a mismatch, while all other vertices see both identically. Conversely when both parts were singletons, deleting \(uv\) merges these two parts and preserves complete multipartiteness. These cases are disjoint, giving the formula for \(d(G)\).

**4. Exact insertions.** Within an existing connected component, each nonedge \(uv\) lies in one independent part \(A\). To retain complete multipartiteness after adding \(uv\), the part must have size exactly two, when it splits into two singleton parts. If \(|A|\ge3\), an unchanged third member of \(A\) is nonadjacent to both endpoints but their open neighbourhoods differ, violating Step 1. Between distinct components, inserting one edge creates a bridge. By the bridge characterization, the merged component must be a star. This happens precisely for two isolated vertices, or when one component is an isolated vertex and it is attached to the center of an existing star (both endpoints are possible for \(K_2\)). This gives the formula for \(a(G)\).

**5. Labelled enumeration.** Every nontrivial connected valid graph on a labelled \(n\)-set corresponds bijectively to a set partition into at least two parts, hence there are \(\operatorname{Bell}(n)-1\) choices for \(n\ge2\), while a singleton component has one choice. Its connected exponential generating function is \(C(x)=x+B(x)-e^x\); assembling disjoint labelled components yields \(H(x)=e^{C(x)}\).

A connected valid graph with a **distinguished removable edge** is of exactly one of two types. First, its endpoints belong to two singleton parts: choose the unordered pair of labels (EGF \(x^2/2\)) and partition the remaining labels arbitrarily (EGF \(B(x)\)), giving \(x^2B(x)/2\). This includes \(K_2\). Second, the graph is a star on at least three vertices with its marked edge between the center and a leaf. Choose the labeled center, distinguished leaf and a nonempty labelled set of additional leaves, giving \(x^2(e^x-1)\). The two types are disjoint. They produce \(D(x)\). A globally marked removable edge belongs to one distinguished connected component with arbitrary remaining components, so labelled product gives \(H(x)D(x)\). Finally, removing a safe edge is a bijection, with inverse insertion, between (valid graph, safe removable edge) and (valid graph, safe insertable nonedge) at each fixed labelled vertex set. Their aggregate enumerators are therefore identical, proving both displayed generating functions.

## Verification
The standalone standard-library checker `check.py` independently evaluates the modal implication for **every** Boolean valuation on **every** labelled simple graph through six vertices. A separate graph-theoretic procedure identifies multipartite components by connected components in the graph and its complement restricted to each component; the semantics and structure classifications agree. For every valid graph in this exhaustive range, all individual edge toggles are checked against the independently implemented local formulas. A separate integer-labelled exponential-formula calculation checks both EGF coefficients. Running `python3 check.py` gives `VERIFY_OK graphs=33868 valuation_assignments=2131019 edit_checks=24796`. Finite exhaustive verification is corroboration only; Steps 1–5 establish all-size results.

## Relationship to prior work
Zolin (1999), *Completeness and Definability in the Logic of Non-contingency*, proves an exact **general-frame correspondence** for the introspection schema \(\Delta p\to\Delta\Delta p\) (Theorem 4.1); the frame condition itself is not claimed as new. The complete-multipartite structure on finite symmetric irreflexive frames is a specialization derived here, not a claimed general correspondence theorem. The new quantitative assertion is the classification of **all individual safe edge insertions/deletions**, their exact componentwise counts, and the joint labelled edge-edit exponential generating function. Sierra Miranda, Wang and Zenger (2026) study generalized path-condition proof calculi for non-contingency logic; they provide the contemporary logical setting but do not present these labelled graph edit enumerators. Existing exact modal counts for directed outward trees and one-variable semantics on complete irreflexive frames concern different graph structures or invariants. The Bell exponential formula and ordinary facts about complete multipartite graphs are classical tools and not asserted as discoveries.

## Limitations
The theorem applies only to finite **symmetric, loopless** frames and one edge edit at a time; it does not characterize arbitrary directed frames, reflexive frames, multiple simultaneous edits, or the number of inequivalent valuation theories. The unweighted labelled graph count follows from the classical correspondence between complete multipartite graphs and set partitions; the contribution concerns the valid-edit *location spectrum* and its marked enumerator. Although Zolin's general theorem and accessible related works were inspected, a full historical-priority guarantee against an equivalent graph-edit enumeration cannot be provided. No independent audit or proof-assistant formalization has been performed.
