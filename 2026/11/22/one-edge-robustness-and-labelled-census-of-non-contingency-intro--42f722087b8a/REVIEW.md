# Scientific assessment

**Correctness — PASS.** Direct finite-frame semantics give a short valuation witness whenever a connected graph contains two nonadjacent distance-two vertices with different open neighbourhoods. In connected valid graphs this forces the nonadjacency equivalence classes forming a complete multipartite partition. The converse is verified for every valuation, including the two-part and singleton boundaries. The bridge lemma and singleton-part argument enumerate exactly all individual valid edge deletions and insertions. Labelled assemblies and reversibility of edge toggling yield both equal generating functions. A distinct standard-library implementation checks all labelled graphs through six vertices (33,868 graphs; 2,131,019 graph-valuation assignments), 24,796 edit cases, and separate EGF counts. Finite tests do not substitute for the symbolic argument.

**Originality — PASS within inspected sources.** Zolin's 1999 Theorem 4.1 already proves the general non-contingency introspection frame correspondence; no novelty is claimed for that general correspondence. The 2026 Sierra Miranda–Wang–Zenger work treats proof systems for generalized modal path conditions; published results on outward-rooted trees and complete irreflexive cliques count different invariants. Inspected published modal edge-repair theorems concern either normal-box reduction or Church–Rosser axioms on sparse directed random graphs, not the symmetric-frame noncontingency property. The specifically checked material does not state the classification and exact all-component number of safe graph-edge edits or their marked Bell exponential enumerator. The unmarked complete-multipartite count is standard; the nontrivial proposed contribution is the full one-edge stability census, including bridges, singleton parts and the edit-reversal identity. Remaining risk: a graph-edit or reliability paper may contain an equivalent multipartite toggle classification even if it does not use modal-logic terminology.

**Value — PASS.** The question is a natural perturbation refinement of a classical modal frame-validity condition: how many one-edge changes preserve the condition, at which positions, and how many such changes exist over all labelled satisfying frames? The result determines these exact invariants for arbitrary component partitions and all labelled orders. It separates three qualitatively different insertion mechanisms from two deletion mechanisms; it is not merely a recurrence for the already-known Bell counts.

**Scope and risks.** Undirected irreflexive finite frames only; arbitrary directed relations and simultaneous edge edits are excluded. The general frame property and Bell partition facts are prior mathematics, not novel results. Historic completeness of the literature search, independent mathematical validation, and external priority certification are not asserted.
