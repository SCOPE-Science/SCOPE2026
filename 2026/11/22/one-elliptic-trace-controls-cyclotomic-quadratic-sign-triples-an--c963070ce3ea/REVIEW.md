# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS

For odd prime powers of characteristic at least five, the fractional-linear substitution \(x\mapsto-x/(x+1)\) proves that the quartic quadratic-character sum equals \(\chi_q(-1)U_q-1\), where \(U_q\) is the cubic character sum of a smooth elliptic curve. Reflection gives the second sum identity. Direct expansion of the eight indicator products, including all excluded zeros, gives the two claimed threefold-equality formulas. Hasse's bound proves positivity for every eligible field of order at least \(71\). Complete exact finite-field enumeration checks the nineteen remaining orders, and the characteristic-three obstruction is an identity. Computational verification is finite only; the infinite step is mathematical.

## Originality — PASS on inspected evidence

The closest inspected primary article, Wang–Fang (2023, DOI 10.1155/2023/5253261), studies arithmetic progressions of quadratic-residue signs and does not contain the cyclotomic triple or the rational symmetry and exact prime-power obstruction list. McDonald–Sahay–Wyman (2022, arXiv:2210.03789) establish broad asymptotic quadratic-residue shattering bounds, not this one-trace formula or sharp exception set. Previously published descriptions of triangular-number or consecutive-polynomial sign words concern distinct functions and do not imply this result by an affine or fractional-linear relabeling. Relevant character-sum and elliptic point-count techniques are classical. A pre-existing equivalent in specialized elliptic-character-sum literature is not completely excluded; independent priority verification remains outstanding.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The third cyclotomic polynomial is canonical rather than a freely chosen third shift; its fractional-linear stabilizer forces a single elliptic trace to govern eight counts. The result gives exact frequencies in every eligible finite field, a two-orbit structure, all exceptional small prime-power orders, and distinct sharp prime-versus-prime-power thresholds. In particular, extension-field failures at orders \(25\) and \(49\) are not predicted by the prime-field cutoff. These are complete, reusable arithmetic invariants of this natural quadratic-residue configuration, not merely a numerical lower bound.
