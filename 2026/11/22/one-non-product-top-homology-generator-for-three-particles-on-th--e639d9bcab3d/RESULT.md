# One non-product top-homology generator for three particles on the Nauru graph

## Finding
Let \(G=\operatorname{GP}(12,5)\) be the 24-vertex Nauru graph and \(B_3(G)=\operatorname{UConf}_3(|G|)\) the space of three unordered, pairwise distinct points in its geometric realization. Let \(T\subseteq H_3(B_3(G);\mathbb Z)\) be generated by the product three-tori obtained by moving one particle around each of three pairwise vertex-disjoint simple cycles.

The complete integral top-homology and product-torus quotient are
\[
H_3(B_3(G);\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{60},\qquad T\cong\mathbb Z^{59},\qquad H_3(B_3(G);\mathbb Z)/T\cong\mathbb Z.
\]
In particular, there is exactly one primitive non-product direction, and a 5,034-term signed integral cycle together with 59 geometric product tori forms an integral basis.

## Assumptions and scope
The generalized Petersen graph has vertex set \(\{u_i,v_i:i\in\mathbb Z/12\mathbb Z\}\) and edges \(u_iu_{i+1}\), \(u_iv_i\), and \(v_iv_{i+5}\), with subscripts modulo 12. Every edge is a unit interval; configurations exclude collisions anywhere on the geometric graph. A product torus requires three vertex-disjoint embedded circles; arbitrary loops supported in pairwise disjoint subgraphs yield integral sums of these simple-cycle products. No assertion is made about lower-degree homology or ring structure.

## Proof
Use the weight-three, fully reduced Świątkowski complex (An–Drummond-Cole–Knudsen, Theorem 4.5 and Proposition 4.9). At each trivalent vertex, choose the least-numbered incident edge as base half-edge \(h_0\); the other two degree-one generators are \(h_1-h_0\), \(h_2-h_0\), whose boundaries are \(e_1-e_0\), \(e_2-e_0\). The full complex is the tensor product of these local factors over the edge-polynomial ring. Its weight-three, homological-degree-three free group has
\[
\binom{24}{3}2^3=16,192
\]
generators. In each generator, three distinct vertices carry a half-edge difference, and the differential replaces one difference with the corresponding difference of edge variables, with Koszul sign. There are 39,600 distinct degree-two basis rows touched by this differential, out of 39,744 available basis rows.

The standalone exact calculation yields rank \(16,132\) for this differential over \(\mathbb F_3\). Therefore its rank over \(\mathbb Q\) is at least \(16,132\), so its integral kernel has rank at most 60. Because three particles permit no homological degree greater than three in this complex, the kernel is precisely \(H_3\).

Enumerate simple cycles through length 12 by placing the least vertex first and choosing the direction with the smaller second vertex. This yields respectively 12, 54, 108, and 246 unoriented simple cycles of lengths 6, 8, 10, and 12, and none shorter than six. Since the graph has only 24 vertices and girth six, each cycle in any disjoint triple has length at most 12. The exhaustive counts of unordered vertex-disjoint triples by length profile are 16 of type \((6,6,6)\), 72 of type \((6,6,8)\), 36 of type \((6,8,8)\), and 6 of type \((8,8,8)\), for a total of 130. The two orientations of each of the three factors give eight orientation choices for each geometric torus, not eight separate tori; each choice changes the chain at most by an overall sign. A product-torus chain is the signed product of the three one-particle cycles \(\sum_{v\in C}(h_{\mathrm{out}(v)}-h_{\mathrm{in}(v)})\), by the product on the complexes of disjoint subgraphs followed by inclusion in \(G\).

The accompanying certificate selects 59 such cycles and supplies one further 5,034-term integral cycle. Every selected cycle has zero integer boundary. On 60 explicitly recorded coordinate rows, their 60-by-60 coefficient minor has determinant \(-1\), calculated by exact rational elimination. Thus projection to those rows maps the 60 cycles to an integral basis of \(\mathbb Z^{60}\). Any integral cycle differs from an integer linear combination of the 60 cycles by a cycle whose projection is zero. The rational kernel has dimension at most 60 and the 60 projected cycles are independent, so such a difference is zero. This proves that the 60 cycles are an integral homology basis.

To test the span of all products, solve the transposed minor system for the linear functional on those 60 coordinates which takes value one on the last basis element and zero on the first 59. The verifier evaluates it on the chains for all 130 products and gets zero each time. Hence all product-torus classes are in the span of the first 59 elements of the basis, and these elements themselves are product classes. It follows that \(T\) is exactly their integral span and the quotient is \(\mathbb Z\), generated by the last basis element.

## Relationship to prior work
The graph-braid and discrete Morse framework of Farley–Sabalka explains the configuration space and topological viewpoint. The reduced Świątkowski complex and geometric product-cycle mechanisms are standard. The Nauru two-particle result gives \(H_2\cong\mathbb Z^{54}\) and concerns products of pairs of disjoint cycles. The Pappus and Desargues three-particle calculations each exhibit a primitive rank-one quotient by products but concern different graphs, with third Betti numbers 7 and 21 respectively. The claim here is the exact rank and integral product sublattice for the three-particle Nauru space, not the first occurrence of the rank-one obstruction or a general theorem for cubic graphs.

## Limitations
The classification concerns one specified finite graph and three unordered particles, not a family of generalized Petersen graphs. It identifies the top integral group and the sublattice generated by disjoint simple-cycle products; it does not classify the geometry of the non-product generator up to homotopy, lower-degree torsion, or the automorphism action. Literature overlap has been assessed against the accessible records and primary texts, but an unindexed equivalent calculation cannot be excluded without independent literature review.
