# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS

The proof identifies geometric flexes with the \(3\)-torsion torsor and Hesse triples with affine lines, so the relevant arithmetic action is \(v\mapsto Av+b\), with determinant forced by the Weil pairing. The exact verification enumerates every invertible two-by-two matrix over \(\mathbb F_3\), each of nine translations, and checks 432 point and twelve-line permutations. Its second fixed-line calculation uses the linear condition \((A-I)v+b\in H\), independently of the permutation-based computation, and yields ten distinct joint spectra separated by the pair of fixed counts within each determinant class. The supplied characteristic-seven curves are checked using group order and division polynomials. The result holds for all non-characteristic-three fields because the group-theoretic exhaustive classification is finite and uniform, not a finite-field sampling claim.

## Originality — PASS, with priority risk

Artebani–Dolgachev (arXiv:math/0611590, Section 2) give the geometric Hesse incidence and coset representation. Popov (arXiv:2408.16488, Lemma 6) gives the nine-flex/twelve-line geometry and geometrically acting special-affine group; the reviewed claim is not that geometry. Bruen–Hirschfeld–Wehlau (arXiv:1107.4387, Sections 3 and 6) give the classical possibilities for rational flexes and finite-field cubic classification. The inspected materials do not give the full determinant-stratified joint cycle census, the sharp single-field pair reconstruction, or the explicit three-way distinction over \(\mathbb F_7\). Related published mathematical-record searches did not identify an equivalent statement. An unindexed equivalent finite-group character calculation remains a genuine risk; absence of search matches alone is not treated as proof.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The finite étale schemes of nine flexes and twelve Hesse lines are intrinsic, classical geometric data of an arbitrary plane cubic. Their joint base-field rational-count pair is a natural, low-cost arithmetic observable; its determination of every extension count is a nontrivial and exact inverse theorem. The three explicit smooth curves over \(\mathbb F_7\) show why the second invariant is necessary, and the determinant-two collision shows why one must formulate recovery at the zeta-function level rather than claim recovery of a finer conjugacy class. The result is deliberately restricted and makes no claim about the cubic's Weil polynomial or all possible realizations.

## Residual limitations

Characteristic three is excluded; the geometric configuration degenerates there. Some theoretically allowable affine spectra may not be realized over a specific small finite field. This is a self-review only.
