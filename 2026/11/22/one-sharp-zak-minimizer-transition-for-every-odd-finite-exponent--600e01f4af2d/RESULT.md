# One sharp Zak-minimizer transition for every odd finite exponential Gabor cutoff

## Finding
Fix any odd integer \(L\ge 5\), any \(\lambda>0\), and let \(r=e^{-\lambda}\). The critical integer-lattice Gabor system generated by
\[
 g_{L,\lambda}(t)=e^{-\lambda t}\mathbf 1_{[0,L]}(t)
\]
is a Riesz basis of complex \(L^2(\mathbb R)\). Set \(T_L(x)=\cos(L\arccos x)\), and define
\[
 F_L(r,x)=\frac{1+r^{2L}-2r^L T_L(x)}{1+r^2-2rx},\qquad
 E_L(r,x)=1+r^{2L}-2r^L T_L(x)-r^{L-1}(1+r^2-2rx)T_L'(x).
\]
There is a **unique** \(r_L\in(0,1)\) satisfying
\[
 1+r_L^L=Lr_L^{(L-1)/2}(1+r_L).
\]
The unique minimizing cosine \(x_L(r)\) is completely characterized by
\[
 x_L(r)=\begin{cases}
 -1,&0<r\le r_L,\\
 \text{the unique zero of }E_L(r,\cdot)\text{ in }(-1,-\cos(\pi/L)),&r_L<r<1.
 \end{cases}
\]
The **optimal** Gabor frame bounds are
\[
 A_{L,\lambda}=e^{-2\lambda}F_L(r,x_L(r)),\qquad
 B_{L,\lambda}=\left(\frac{1-r^L}{1-r}\right)^2.
\]
The minimum of the Zak-frequency modulus occurs uniquely at frequency \(1/2\) modulo integers for \(r\le r_L\), and at exactly two conjugate frequencies \(\pm\arccos(x_L(r))/(2\pi)\) for \(r>r_L\). The two frequencies split from \(1/2\) with an **exact square-root law**. Put \(m=(L-1)/2\), \(\eta_L(r)=Lr^m(1+r)-(1+r^L)\), and
\[
 c_L=\frac{12(1+r_L^L)\eta_L'(r_L)}{r_L^{L-1}(1+r_L)^2L^2(L^2-1)}>0.
\]
As \(r\downarrow r_L\) from above, the two minimizing angles are \(\theta=\pi\pm\sqrt{c_L}\sqrt{r-r_L}+O_L((r-r_L)^{3/2})\) modulo \(2\pi\). Their transition damping satisfies
\[
 \lambda_L:=-\log r_L=\frac{2\log(2L)}{L}+O\!\left(\frac{(\log L)^2}{L^3}\right)
 \quad (L\to\infty\text{ through odd integers}).
\]
At the first new case \(L=5\), the transition is exactly \(r_5=(3-\sqrt5)/2\), with \(c_5=(5+3\sqrt5)/4\).

## Assumptions and scope
The time-frequency atoms are \(e^{2\pi i\ell t}g_{L,\lambda}(t-k)\) indexed by \(k,\ell\in\mathbb Z\). The optimal numbers \(A,B\) satisfy
\[
 A\|f\|_2^2\le \sum_{k,\ell\in\mathbb Z}|\langle f,e^{2\pi i\ell\cdot}g_{L,\lambda}(\cdot-k)\rangle|^2\le B\|f\|_2^2
\]
for every \(f\in L^2(\mathbb R)\). The scalar \(B/A\) is the **frame-operator** condition number, not the synthesis singular-value condition number. Changing cutoff endpoint conventions affects only null sets. The sharp transition theorem is asserted for odd integer \(L\ge5\), not arbitrary lengths or other Gabor lattices. The full argument also applies at \(L=3\), where its classification is previously known.

## Proof
**1. Exact Zak diagonalization.** The unitary Zak transform at the critical integer lattice is \(Zf(x,\xi)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}f(x+j)e^{-2\pi i j\xi}\), on \([0,1)^2\). The critical Gabor frame operator is multiplication by \(|Zg|^2\). For almost every \(0<x<1\), precisely \(j=0,\ldots,L-1\) contribute, so
\[
 Zg_{L,\lambda}(x,\xi)=e^{-\lambda x}\frac{1-r^L e^{-2\pi iL\xi}}{1-r e^{-2\pi i\xi}}.
\]
Its numerator and denominator do not vanish for \(0<r<1\). Consequently the Gabor family is a Riesz basis, with the sharp bounds obtained from the essential extrema of the displayed modulus. The triangle inequality shows its frequency maximum occurs uniquely at \(\xi=0\), and has squared value \(B=((1-r^L)/(1-r))^2\). Writing \(x=\cos(2\pi\xi)\), the squared frequency modulus becomes \(F_L(r,x)\). The essential minimum in the spatial Zak coordinate contributes \(e^{-2\lambda}\), proving the bound formulas once \(x_L\) is identified.

**2. A single candidate arc for the global minimum.** Since \(L\) is odd, \(T_L\) increases strictly from \(-1\) to \(+1\) on
\[
 I_L=[-1,-\cos(\pi/L)].
\]
Every \(x\in[-1,1]\) has a unique \(x_0\in I_L\) with \(T_L(x_0)=T_L(x)\); unless \(x\in I_L\), one has \(x_0<x\). For \(0<r<1\), the numerator of \(F_L(r,x)\) is strictly positive and is identical for \(x\) and \(x_0\), while the denominator \(1+r^2-2rx\) is strictly larger at \(x_0\). Hence \(F_L(r,x_0)<F_L(r,x)\) outside \(I_L\). In particular, **all** global minimizers lie in \(I_L\).

**3. Strict uniqueness within the candidate arc.** Direct differentiation gives
\[
 \partial_xF_L(r,x)=\frac{2rE_L(r,x)}{(1+r^2-2rx)^2},\qquad
 \partial_xE_L(r,x)=-r^{L-1}(1+r^2-2rx)T_L''(x).
\]
To check the crucial sign without assuming it, write \(L=2m+1\) and \(x=-\cos u\), \(0<u<\pi/L\). Then
\[
 T_L'(x)=L\frac{\sin(Lu)}{\sin u}
 =L\left(1+2\sum_{j=1}^{m}\cos(2ju)\right).
\]
Every \(\sin(2ju)>0\) in that interval, so differentiation with respect to \(u\) is strictly negative. Since \(dx/du=\sin u>0\), it follows that \(T_L''(x)<0\) on the interior of \(I_L\). Thus \(E_L(r,\cdot)\) is strictly increasing on \(I_L\). At the right endpoint \(T_L=1\) and \(T_L'=0\), whence
\[
 E_L(r,-\cos(\pi/L))=(1-r^L)^2>0.
\]
At the left endpoint, \(T_L(-1)=-1\) and \(T_L'(-1)=L^2\), yielding
\[
 E_L(r,-1)=(1+r^L)^2-L^2r^{L-1}(1+r)^2.
\]
It follows that the minimum is at \(-1\) when \(E_L(r,-1)\ge0\), and otherwise at the **single interior zero** of \(E_L\). There are no other minimizing frequencies, by Step 2 and strict monotonicity.

**4. Exactly one damping transition.** The sign of \(E_L(r,-1)\) is the sign of \(1+r^L-Lr^m(1+r)=-\eta_L(r)\). With \(r=e^{-\lambda}\), its zero equation becomes
\[
 \frac{\cosh(L\lambda/2)}{\cosh(\lambda/2)}=L.
\]
The left side is strictly increasing for \(\lambda>0\) because its logarithmic derivative is \((L\tanh(L\lambda/2)-\tanh(\lambda/2))/2>0\). It begins at \(1\) and tends to infinity. Thus exactly one positive \(\lambda_L\), hence one \(r_L\in(0,1)\), solves the equation. The endpoint sign gives the announced regimes. The \(L=5\) equation factors as \((1+r)^3(r^2-3r+1)=0\), which gives \(r_5=(3-\sqrt5)/2\).

**5. Square-root onset.** At \((r,x)=(r_L,-1)\), the derivatives obey
\[
 E_x=r_L^{L-1}(1+r_L)^2\frac{L^2(L^2-1)}{3}>0,\qquad
 E_r=-2(1+r_L^L)\eta_L'(r_L)<0.
\]
For the first identity, use the exact Chebyshev endpoint derivative \(T_L''(-1)=-L^2(L^2-1)/3\); for the second, differentiate the factorization \(E_L(r,-1)=-\eta_L(r)[1+r^L+Lr^m(1+r)]\). Its derivative is strictly negative at the crossing by Step 4. The implicit-function theorem applied to \(E_L(r,x)=0\) gives
\[
 x_L(r)+1=-\frac{E_r}{E_x}(r-r_L)+O_L((r-r_L)^2).
\]
Since \(\cos(\pi-v)=-1+v^2/2+O(v^4)\), the minimizing angles split by \(v=\sqrt{c_L}\sqrt{r-r_L}+O_L((r-r_L)^{3/2})\), where \(c_L=-2E_r/E_x\), exactly the constant above. Substitution at \(L=5\) simplifies it to \((5+3\sqrt5)/4\).

**6. Large-duration transition.** Put \(y=L\lambda_L/2\). The equation is \(\cosh y=L\cosh(y/L)\). The elementary inequalities \(e^y/2\le\cosh y\) and \(\cosh(y/L)\le e^{y/L}\) give \(y\le\log(2L)/(1-1/L)\), while \(\cosh y\ge L\) gives \(y\ge\operatorname{arcosh}L\). Hence \(y=O(\log L)\) and \(\cosh(y/L)=1+O((\log L)^2/L^2)\). Applying \(\operatorname{arcosh}\) and using \(\operatorname{arcosh}L=\log(2L)+O(L^{-2})\) yields \(y=\log(2L)+O((\log L)^2/L^2)\), proving the displayed \(\lambda_L\) asymptotic.

## Verification
The standalone `CHECK.py` reconstructs the frequency multiplier directly, evaluates the analytic Chebyshev formula and unique root for odd lengths \(3,5,7,9,11,15,21,41,81\), and compares it against independent bounded minimization and a complete fixed frequency grid at damping values spanning both regimes. It also cross-checks the critical equation and square-root coefficients for the first several lengths. Finite numerical tests supplement Steps 1–6 and **do not** certify the global-minimum or asymptotic claims without their analytic proof. Run `python3 CHECK.py` with Python 3 (standard library only).

## Relationship to prior work
Faulhuber and Shafkulovska, *Gabor frame bound optimizations* (arXiv:2204.02917, first submitted 2022-04-06), examine cutoff exponentials over lengths one and two and related lattice optimizations, not the odd-length global frequency-minimizer theorem here. An earlier published analysis of finite-duration exponential Gabor bases (published-finding corpus, 2026-11-16) established the all-even exact frame bounds, the complete odd length-three bifurcation, and the **weak-damping leading coefficient for every fixed odd length**. Those results are acknowledged as prior; neither their even case nor the length-three calculation entails the present all-odd finite-parameter unique-branch theorem and its large-duration transition. Kloos and Stöckler (arXiv:1311.7359) concern convolved exponential B-splines and totally positive functions, and Christensen, Kim and Kim (arXiv:1602.05717) concern continuous compactly supported windows. Pont et al. (arXiv:2608.04992) establish a qualitative frame-set result for continuous totally positive windows; discontinuous cutoff exponentials are outside its assumptions. The classical Zak criterion, the geometric series, and the earlier weak-damping coefficient are **not** claimed as new. The new structural mechanism is the strict concavity of the first Chebyshev arc, forcing a universal single symmetry-breaking transition across every odd cutoff length.

## Limitations
Only integer cutoff lengths \(L\ge5\) that are odd, unit critical time-frequency shifts, positive damping, and these particular discontinuous windows are covered. The exact interior minimum is characterized by a **unique algebraic root**, not a general elementary closed form. The large-duration threshold expansion does not assert a simultaneous weak-damping limit for optimal frame bounds. This is a mathematical derivation with self-review, not independent verification. A historical equivalent under finite-impulse-response or filter-bank terminology might remain undetected despite the searches described in the review; its appearance would alter the originality assessment, not the algebraic proof.
