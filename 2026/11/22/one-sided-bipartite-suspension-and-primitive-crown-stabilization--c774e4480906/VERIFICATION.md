# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

The two subcomplexes generated by neighborhoods centered on opposite shores intersect precisely in the graph: a common face can contain at most one vertex per shore, every vertex occurs on both sides by the nonisolation hypothesis, and common cross-pairs are edges. One universal vertex b suffices: all A-centered simplices contain b, while in the other half each simplex centered at a vertex other than b retracts relative to its nonempty base in the full A-simplex. They only meet the other pieces along their bases, so these retractions make the half contractible. Taking the homotopy pushout along the subcomplex inclusions gives the suspension, and the natural Mayer–Vietoris map identifies degree two integrally with the edge cycle lattice for shore-preserving maps. The rectangular graph has m(m+k-1) edges and 2m+k vertices. Its connected bipartition has different shore sizes when k>=1, and the omitted pairs and the full-neighborhood vertices determine precisely S_m times S_k. The oriented chain sequence gives the trace (f(f-1)+fe)-(2f+e)+1. Alternatively, its average is zero because there are two edge orbits, three vertex orbits and one connected component; exactness over Q gives zero invariant H_1. After barycentric subdivision there are no inversions, and rational coinvariant chains give the quotient homology; averaging identifies coinvariants and invariants. For an attachment, the relative graph boundary is the augmentation Z^m to Z, with kernel I_m and no relative H_2. Since the old graph is connected, the long exact sequence gives the stated primitive injection. At k=0 the hemispheres instead retract to boundary spheres of dimension m-2>=3, so degree-two Mayer–Vietoris is still an isomorphism, natural for the enlargement. The higher classes map to zero, not injectively.

## originality

PASS

Best of current knowledge, the one-sided suspension theorem and vertex-stabilization maps for rectangular crowns are distinct from the inspected both-shores edge-restoration theorem and the known balanced-crown homotopy and symmetry. The latter results, and the general notions of closed neighborhood, homotopy pushout, and graph cycle modules, are not credited as new.

## value

PASS

The new hypothesis reaches bipartite graphs and rectangular crowns inaccessible to the earlier both-shores cone argument. For this natural family it identifies the induced integral homology maps and the representation on each new layer, including the transition where the two higher classes vanish. These are useful distinctions beyond a sphere count or the known endpoint; the integral augmentation lattice is not recoverable from rank alone.
