# One-sided congruence lattice of the bi-infinite one-column quandle

## Finding
Let \(Q=\{\star\}\sqcup\mathbb Z\), where \(\star\notin\mathbb Z\), with
\[
x\triangleright y=\begin{cases}x+1,&x\in\mathbb Z,\ y=\star,\\x,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}
\]
Here \(\star\triangleright\star=\star\). The two right operations \(\triangleright\) and its inverse \(\triangleleft\) make \(Q\) a quandle. Call an equivalence relation a *forward congruence* if it respects \(\triangleright\), without assuming compatibility with \(\triangleleft\).

For integers \(c\) and positive integers \(d\), define \(H_{c,d}\) by keeping \(\star\) in a singleton class and, on \(\mathbb Z\), setting
\[
i\ H_{c,d}\ j\iff i=j\ \text{or}\ (i,j\ge c\ \text{and}\ d\mid(i-j)).
\]
Define \(P_d\) by keeping \(\star\) separate and identifying integers modulo \(d\). Write \(\Delta\) and \(\nabla\) for equality and the universal relation.

**Classification theorem.** The distinct forward congruences are exactly
\[
\Delta,\quad\nabla,\quad H_{c,d}\ (c\in\mathbb Z,\ d\ge1),\quad P_d\ (d\ge1).
\]
Among these, the genuine quandle congruences (those also respecting \(\triangleleft\)) are exactly \(\Delta,\nabla,P_d\). Consequently, every \(H_{c,d}\) is a half congruence.

Under inclusion, the forward-congruence lattice is distributive. With the convention that \(c=-\infty\) means \(H_{c,d}=P_d\), its proper nontrivial members obey
\[
H_{c,d}\wedge H_{e,f}=H_{\max(c,e),\operatorname{lcm}(d,f)},\qquad
H_{c,d}\vee H_{e,f}=H_{\min(c,e),\gcd(d,f)}.
\]
The usual least/top-element rules apply to \(\Delta\) and \(\nabla\).

**Finite-generation dichotomy.** Every \(H_{c,d}\) is a principal forward congruence, generated by the pair \((c,c+d)\). The universal congruence is also principal (generated by \((\star,0)\)), whereas \(\Delta\) is generated by the empty set. Every \(P_d\) is *not* finitely generated as a forward congruence. Thus every nontrivial proper finitely generated forward congruence is a half congruence, even though every finite quotient is a genuine quandle quotient.

## Assumptions and scope
The set of integers is bi-infinite; it is neither a finite cycle nor a one-sided ray. All quantifiers in the classification range over all equivalence relations on \(Q\), and all integer cutoffs and positive periods. An endomorphism/congruence is taken relative to the displayed right-operation convention. The assertion of finite generation refers to congruence generation **using only** \(\triangleright\); when the inverse operation is also admitted, the same pair \((c,c+d)\) generates \(P_d\) as a quandle congruence.

## Proof
**1. Quandle structure.** The right translation at \(\star\) fixes \(\star\) and shifts every integer by one. All other right translations are identity. They are bijective. Idempotence is immediate. The unique nontrivial right translation fixes its own index \(\star\) and preserves the collection of identity-indexed right translations, so right self-distributivity holds; alternatively, checking the two cases \(z=\star\) and \(z\ne\star\) in the quandle identity gives equality. The inverse operation decrements integers only when the right operand is \(\star\).

**2. Isolation of the distinguished element.** Let \(\sim\) respect \(\triangleright\). If \(\star\sim j\) for any integer \(j\), then for every integer \(i\), compatibility with the equivalent right operands \(\star\sim j\) gives \(i+1=i\triangleright\star\sim i\triangleright j=i\). Thus every integer lies in one class, and this class contains \(\star\): \(\sim=\nabla\). Hence every nonuniversal forward congruence isolates \(\star\). On \(\mathbb Z\), the remaining compatibility condition is exactly
\[
i\sim j\implies i+1\sim j+1.
\]
The converse follows from the operation definition and the isolation of \(\star\).

**3. Classification of shift-stable equivalences.** Let \(C_i\) denote the class of integer \(i\). The successor function \(S(C_i)=C_{i+1}\) is well-defined and surjective (every class has predecessor \(C_{i-1}\)). If all \(C_i\) are distinct, the relation is \(\Delta\). Otherwise choose \(i<j\) with \(C_i=C_j\). Then \(C_i\) lies on a finite directed cycle of \(S\), so \(C_t\) lies on that cycle for all \(t\ge i\). The set of indices \(t\) for which \(C_t\) lies on a directed cycle is nonempty and upward closed in \(\mathbb Z\). It is either all \(\mathbb Z\) or \(\{t:t\ge c\}\) for a unique integer \(c\). In the first case all classes lie on one directed cycle of some unique length \(d\), whence \(C_i=C_j\) exactly when \(d\mid(i-j)\), giving \(P_d\). In the second case all classes at indices \(t\ge c\) lie on one directed cycle of unique length \(d\), and satisfy that same modular criterion. No two indices below \(c\), nor one below and one at/above \(c\), can be equivalent: any such repetition would make the earlier index cyclic. Thus the relation is \(H_{c,d}\). All displayed relations are visibly forward stable; the parameters are unique because \(c\) is the first cyclic index and \(d\) its cycle length.

**4. Inverse compatibility.** On \(P_d\), shifting by minus one preserves equivalence, so it is a genuine quandle congruence; similarly for \(\Delta\) and \(\nabla\). For \(H_{c,d}\), the pair \(c\sim c+d\) is identified, but after right multiplication by \(\star\) using \(\triangleleft\), the images are \(c-1\) and \(c+d-1\), which are inequivalent: the former is below the cutoff. Hence every \(H_{c,d}\) fails the inverse-operation condition.

**5. Lattice operations.** For finite cutoffs, inclusion \(H_{c,d}\subseteq H_{e,f}\) is equivalent to \(c\ge e\) and \(f\mid d\). Also \(H_{c,d}\subseteq P_f\) precisely when \(f\mid d\), and \(P_d\subseteq P_f\) precisely when \(f\mid d\). No \(P_d\) is contained in an \(H_{c,f}\) with finite cutoff. These assertions follow by inspecting the first nontrivial identification and the periodic identifications beyond it. Therefore the greatest lower bound has cutoff \(\max(c,e)\) and period \(\operatorname{lcm}(d,f)\), and the least upper bound has cutoff \(\min(c,e)\) and period \(\gcd(d,f)\). They remain valid for the symbol \(-\infty\). The minimum/maximum lattice of cutoffs and the gcd/lcm lattice of positive integers are both distributive, and the displayed binary formulas, together with the external bottom and top, prove distributivity.

**6. Compactness and quotients.** Forward closure of \((c,c+d)\) gives exactly all pairs \((c+t,c+d+t)\) with \(t\ge0\) and their equivalence closure, namely \(H_{c,d}\). Identifying \(\star\) with any integer forces \(\nabla\) by step 2. Finally,
\[
P_d=\bigcup_{m\ge0}H_{-m,d},
\]
an increasing union of proper forward congruences. Every finite collection of identifying pairs drawn from \(P_d\) has all its integer coordinates bounded below, so its generated congruence is contained in some \(H_{c,d}\) and cannot equal \(P_d\). Equivalently, \(P_d\) is noncompact in the forward-congruence lattice. The quotient by \(H_{c,d}\) has a bi-infinite-indexed backward ray of distinct classes before the cutoff entering a directed \(d\)-cycle; its shift is surjective but not injective. Quotients by \(P_d\) have \(d+1\) elements and are quandles. All remaining proper quotients are infinite.

## Verification
A standalone program `check.py` checks many finite windows of the explicit relation formulas: forward stability, failure/preservation of inverse stability, intersections, and gcd/lcm joins computed as generated equivalence closures. It does not enumerate all infinite equivalence relations. The classification and finite-generation assertions rest on the preceding exact proof, not on those finite computations.

## Relationship to prior work
Cazet (2023) defined and studied the finite one-column permutation quandle family, primarily its automorphisms, polynomial invariants, cohomology and homomorphism quandles. Burrows and Tuffley (2023), building on a 2022 thesis, explained that a one-operation congruence on an infinite rack need not respect the inverse operation, proved a local-finiteness sufficient condition, and constructed infinite examples. Their countable explicit quandle example has two bi-infinite translation orbits as well as a distinguished fixed element. The theorem here treats the canonical *single* bi-infinite orbit with a fixed element and classifies **all** one-operation congruences and their full lattice, rather than exhibiting one example. It also describes a finitary-generation distinction between genuine and half congruences. This comparison does not rule out equivalent results elsewhere in unary-algebra or monoid-action language.

## Limitations
Only the indicated countable one-column quandle is classified; arbitrary infinite permutations with multiple orbits may have additional identifications between orbits. The lattice formula is a binary lattice statement and is not a claim that arbitrary infinite meets/joins can always be obtained by naive extrema and gcd/lcm. The proof is elementary and uses standard periodic-tail dynamics; historical priority relative to unindexed unary-algebra formulations remains unresolved. An independent audit has not been performed.
