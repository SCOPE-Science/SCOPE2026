# One thousand eighty Segre tangent cones on the Heisenberg-invariant theta fourfold

## Finding

Let \(k\) be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. In \(\mathbb P^8_k\), with homogeneous coordinates \(x_0,\ldots,x_8\), set \(\Sigma=V(f_0,f_1,f_2,f_3,f_4)\), where
\[
\begin{aligned}
f_0&=3(x_0x_1x_2+x_3x_4x_5+x_6x_7x_8),\\
f_1&=3(x_0x_3x_6+x_1x_4x_7+x_2x_5x_8),\\
f_2&=3(x_0x_4x_8+x_1x_5x_6+x_2x_3x_7),\\
f_3&=3(x_0x_5x_7+x_1x_3x_8+x_2x_4x_6),\\
f_4&=\sum_{j=0}^{8}x_j^3.
\end{aligned}
\]
Giovenzana–Rojas–Song prove that \(\Sigma\) is an integral fourfold of degree \(45\) with exactly \(1080\) geometric singular points (arXiv:2611.16593v1, §§2.3, 3). At **every one** of these points \(p\), the associated graded local algebra has the following isomorphism:
\[
\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak m_p}\mathcal O_{\Sigma,p}
\cong k[u_1,u_2,u_3,v_1,v_2,v_3]/I_2
\begin{pmatrix}u_1&u_2&u_3\\v_1&v_2&v_3\end{pmatrix},
\]
where \(I_2\) is the ideal generated by the three \(2\)-by-\(2\) minors. Thus each point has embedding dimension \(6\), Hilbert–Samuel multiplicity \(3\), and Hilbert series
\[
\frac{1+2t}{(1-t)^4}.
\]
Its projectivized tangent cone is the smooth Segre threefold \(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^2\subset\mathbb P^5\). In particular, blowing up the \(1080\) **reduced points** resolves these isolated singularities; each exceptional divisor is \(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^2\), with normal bundle \(\mathcal O(-1,-1)\). None of these singular local rings is a local complete intersection.

## Assumptions and scope

The base field is algebraically closed of characteristic zero, and the five cubics and their **scheme-theoretic** intersection are as displayed above. We use the established irreducibility, four-dimensionality and complete set-theoretic singular-point classification of \(\Sigma\), not any putative irreducibility of a general intersection of five cubics. We classify the tangent cones and ordinary blowup of these fixed singularities; no assertion of analytic equivalence with their tangent cones is made.

## Proof

**1. A representative and elimination of linear terms.** The point \(p=[1:-1:0:0:0:0:0:0:0]\) lies on all five cubics and belongs to their reported singular set. Work on \(x_0=1\), write \(x_1=-1+a\), \(x_2=b\), and denote \(x_3,\ldots,x_8\) by \(u_3,\ldots,u_8\). In the completed local ring, \(f_0=-3b+\text{terms of order at least two}\), whereas \(f_4=3a+\text{terms of order at least two}\). The formal implicit-function theorem eliminates \(a,b\) uniquely; in fact their solutions have order at least three in the six remaining variables, because the only nonlinearity of \(f_0\) not containing \(b\) and of \(f_4\) not containing \(a\) is cubic. The remaining three equations, each divided by \(3\), have quadratic initial forms
\[
q_1=u_3u_6-u_4u_7,\quad
q_2=u_4u_8-u_5u_6,\quad
q_3=u_5u_7-u_3u_8.
\]
These are, up to signs/order, precisely the maximal minors of
\[
M=\begin{pmatrix}u_3&u_4&u_5\\u_7&u_6&u_8\end{pmatrix}.
\]

**2. No concealed tangent equations.** Let \(S=k[u_3,u_4,u_5,u_6,u_7,u_8]\), \(J=I_2(M)\), and \(I\) the ideal of the three eliminated formal equations. By the determinantal rank-one parametrization, \(J\) is prime of height \(2\) and \(S/J\) has dimension \(4\). The initial-form ideal \(\operatorname{in}_{\mathfrak m}(I)\) contains \(J\). The completed local ring of the integral fourfold \(\Sigma\) has dimension \(4\), and the associated graded local ring has the same Krull dimension. A strict enlargement of a prime height-two ideal in this six-variable polynomial ring would have quotient dimension at most \(3\). Therefore \(\operatorname{in}_{\mathfrak m}(I)=J\) exactly; in particular there are no extra higher-order initial equations. This proves the claimed associated graded algebra at \(p\).

**3. Invariants and resolution.** The degree-\(d\) piece of the rank-one determinantal ring identifies with \(H^0(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^2,\mathcal O(d,d))\), of dimension \((d+1)\binom{d+2}{2}\). Summing gives \((1+2t)/(1-t)^4\), whose numerator at \(t=1\) gives multiplicity \(3\). The nonzero rank-one matrices form a smooth locus; the projectivization is the smooth Segre threefold. In each blowup chart at a point of this projectivization, the exceptional fiber is smooth, so the strict transform is regular there by the flat normal-cone deformation (equivalently the Jacobian criterion after division by the correct exceptional powers). Outside the exceptional locus the blowup is the identity and \(\Sigma\) is smooth. The exceptional Cartier divisor has normal bundle \(\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^5}(-1)\) restricted to the Segre embedding, namely \(\mathcal O(-1,-1)\). The original local ideal has height \(2\), but its three generators have linearly independent quadratic initial forms, so all three are minimal; consequently this local ring is not a complete intersection.

**4. Uniformity over all singular points.** The cited paper groups the \(1080\) points into \(40\) collections of \(27\), indexed by nontrivial cyclic subgroups of \((\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z)^4\). The projective Heisenberg group acts transitively on the \(27\) points with a prescribed stabilizer: for the first coordinate triple, cyclic permutations of the coordinates and diagonal cube-root scalings move transitively among the nine solutions of \(stu=0\) and \(s^3+t^3+u^3=0\), and the Heisenberg shifts move the three coordinate triples. The normalizer surjects onto \(\operatorname{Sp}(4,\mathbb F_3)\), which is transitive on the \(40\) one-dimensional subspaces. The normalizer therefore acts transitively on all \(1080\) singular points; since it preserves \(\Sigma\), the local calculations hold everywhere.

## Verification

The accompanying `VERIFY.py` checks, using exact rational symbolic arithmetic, that the representative satisfies all five cubics; that the linearization in \(a,b\) is invertible and the complete Jacobian has rank \(2\); that the three residual quadratic forms are exactly the three minors displayed; and that the projective determinantal variety has the expected smooth rank-two Jacobian at a nonzero rank-one representative. It also checks the Segre Hilbert-function coefficients against the stated rational generating function. The proof of equality of full initial ideals is dimension-theoretic and is not inferred from a finite truncation or computational search.

## Relationship to prior work

Giovenzana–Rojas–Song, arXiv:2611.16593v1, establish the ambient cubic equations, integrality, dimension, degree, exact set of \(1080\) singular points, and the tangent **space dimension six** and length-seven Jacobian singular scheme (their §3 and remark following Corollary 3.8). These are **not** claimed as new. The contribution here is the full **scheme-theoretic tangent cone**, its Segre geometry, Hilbert–Samuel multiplicity and ordinary blowup resolution. Gruson–Sam's arXiv:1301.5276v2 investigates degenerations of embedded abelian surfaces and the Coble–Shioda fivefold, a different locus, including Segre products that do not directly determine these \(1080\) local rings. Horadam's original 1957 account of this invariant locus could not be read in full; historical priority is therefore not certified.

## Limitations

The result classifies associated graded local algebras, not completed local rings up to formal isomorphism. It does not calculate the discrepancies of the exceptional divisors, prove crepancy, classify the nonreduced singularity subschemes afresh, or resolve the general hyperkähler family. The blowup statement concerns the reduced union of the \(1080\) points, **not** the blowup along the nonreduced Jacobian ideal. The historical Horadam paper and any unindexed treatments remain residual priority risks. Independent validation has not occurred.
