# Scientific assessment: optimal Legendre sensing of Brownian path energy

## Correctness — PASS

The objective is the unconditional squared error of the conditional mean of \(Q=\int_0^1B_t^2dt\) given exact normalized Legendre white-noise coordinates. Gaussian second-chaos orthogonality produces the exact information reward \(2\sum_{i,j\in S}G_{ij}^2\). Shifted-Legendre integration makes the Gram matrix diagonal except for a special \(0\)-\(1\) edge and the \(j\)-\((j+2)\) edges. Because all vertex and parity-chain edge squares decrease strictly, compressing each parity chain preserves the information budget and strictly increases reward unless already compressed. For compressed sets, strictly decreasing marginal rewards force the unique consecutive prefix. The infinite risk tail telescopes to the exact rational formula, and compact-operator min-max identifies the sharp unrestricted KL benchmark. The extracted standalone Python checker passed rational Gram, subset, telescoping and KL-bound tests. These finite computations are supplementary, not substitutes for the proofs.

## Originality — PASS, with qualified priority

Foster–Lyons–Oberhauser (arXiv:1904.06998) present the underlying polynomial Brownian representation, weighted-path optimality and the conditional quadratic-energy formula for two coefficients; the two-coefficient case is known and is not asserted novel. Kuznetsov (arXiv:1801.01079) supplies general Fourier methods for calculating mean-square errors of iterated Itô integrals; it can support error calculation but does not select the globally optimal subset of individual Legendre coordinates or quantify the exact efficiency loss against unrestricted linear measurements. Relevant published findings on OU endpoint Fourier mode bypass, Brownian mesh quadratic-control errors and stochastic Brownian kernel quadrature have distinct claims and do not imply this all-budget nonlinear-energy subset-selection theorem. Grebenkov (arXiv:1307.0185) optimizes the quadratic statistic itself, rather than the information subspace for a fixed stochastic functional. The distinct statement compared is the *unique global optimality of the initial Legendre-mode block for every budget*, together with the exact gap against all linear Gaussian sensing directions. Residual risk is an unlocated equivalent theorem in classical Gaussian quadratic-functional information theory or Fourier-mode selection.

## Value — PASS

A nonlinear positive path integral of Brownian motion is a natural SDE weak-output statistic, and finite independent Wiener-coordinate budgets arise in polynomial path simulation. Merely computing an additional small-budget conditional variance would have limited value: the supported contribution instead determines which modes to simulate under *any* budget and proves an all-budget exact error and asymptotically sharp multiplicative efficiency cost. The limiting factor \(3\pi^4/128\) measures the price of the prescribed polynomial-coordinate representation relative to optimal linear sensing. These results distinguish allocation of information from pathwise polynomial approximation. The theorem is confined to Brownian quadratic energy, noiseless nonadaptive Gaussian information and the shifted-Legendre bank.
