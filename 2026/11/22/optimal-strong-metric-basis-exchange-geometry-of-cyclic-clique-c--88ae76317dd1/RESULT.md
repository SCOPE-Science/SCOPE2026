# Optimal strong-metric-basis exchange geometry of cyclic clique cylinders

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge3\) and \(m\ge2\), set \(G=C_n\square K_m\), with labelled vertices \((i,a)\in\mathbb Z_n\times[m]\). A *strong resolving set* is a subset \(B\subseteq V(G)\) such that, for every distinct \(u,v\), some \(w\in B\) satisfies either \(d(w,u)=d(w,v)+d(u,v)\) or \(d(w,v)=d(w,u)+d(u,v)\). A *basis* is such a set of minimum cardinality. For each integer \(b\ge0\), put an edge between two distinct bases \(B,B'\) whenever \(|B\setminus B'|=|B'\setminus B|\le b\); call this graph \(\mathcal R_b\). Write \(Q_r\) for the \(r\)-dimensional Boolean cube; \(H^t\) denotes the graph joining distinct vertices at distance at most \(t\) in \(H\), with \(H^0\) edgeless. All graph products are simple undirected products.

The complete labelled basis families and their batch-exchange graphs are:

1. **Even \(n=2k\), \(m=2\).** There are exactly \(2^n\) bases: choose one vertex from each of the \(n\) disjoint mutually-maximally-distant vertex pairs (and take the complementary set as basis). The graph \(\mathcal R_b\) is \(Q_n^b\).
2. **Even \(n=2k\), \(m\ge3\).** There are exactly \(2^k\) bases: for each opposite pair of cycle fibers, select all \(m\) vertices in exactly one fiber for the basis. The graph \(\mathcal R_b\) is \(Q_k^{\lfloor b/m\rfloor}\).
3. **Odd \(n=2k+1\), \(m=2\).** There are exactly two bases, namely the two full color layers of size \(n\). They are adjacent in \(\mathcal R_b\) if and only if \(b\ge n\).
4. **Odd \(n=2k+1\), \(m\ge3\), except \((n,m)=(3,3)\).** There are exactly \(n\) bases: the complement of each cyclic interval of \(k\) consecutive full cycle fibers. The exchange distance between the bases indexed by \(s,t\in\mathbb Z_n\) is \(m\min(|s-t|,n-|s-t|)\). Consequently \(\mathcal R_b\cong C_n^{\lfloor b/m\rfloor}\).
5. **Exceptional \((n,m)=(3,3)\).** There are six bases, the complements of the three full cycle fibers and the three full clique-color layers. For \(0\le b<2\), \(\mathcal R_b=6K_1\); for \(b=2\), \(\mathcal R_b=K_{3,3}\); for \(b\ge3\), \(\mathcal R_b=K_6\).

In all five cases, **no two distinct bases communicate by a one-token slide along an edge of \(G\)**. In particular, the exact first connected batch threshold is \(1\) for even \(n\), \(m=2\); \(m\) for even \(n\), \(m\ge3\); \(n\) for odd \(n\), \(m=2\); \(m\) for odd \(n\), \(m\ge3\) outside the exception; and \(2\) for \((n,m)=(3,3)\).

## Assumptions and scope
For a graph \(H\), two vertices are *mutually maximally distant* if neither has a neighbor more distant from the other than their mutual distance. The *strong resolving graph* \(H_{SR}\) has these pairs as edges. The standard strong-resolving-graph theorem states that strong resolving sets are precisely vertex covers of \(H_{SR}\); hence minimum bases are complements of maximum independent sets of \(H_{SR}\). This characterization and previously known strong metric dimension values are background, **not** claimed as original here. We classify all labelled optimum bases and their complete batch-reconfiguration geometry. For \(m\ge2\), graph distances in \(G\) are sums of cycle distances and \(0\)/\(1\) clique distances.

## Proof
**Step 1 (strong resolving graph).** Let \(h=\lfloor n/2\rfloor\). From the Cartesian distance formula, a pair \((i,a),(j,c)\) is mutually maximally distant precisely if \(a\ne c\) and \(\operatorname{dist}_{C_n}(i,j)=h\). Indeed, unless the cycle displacement is maximal, a cycle-neighbor increases distance; unless the colors differ, changing to another clique color increases distance. If both conditions hold, all neighbors weakly decrease distance. Thus \(G_{SR}\) has exactly these edges. For even \(n=2k\), it is a disjoint union of \(k\) copies of the crown graph \(K_{m,m}\) with the pairwise corresponding edges removed (for \(m=2\), each copy is two disjoint edges). For odd \(n=2k+1\), the antipodal-step graph on cycle indices is itself a cycle: \(\gcd(k,2k+1)=1\). Thus \(G_{SR}\cong C_n\times K_m\), where \(\times\) denotes the categorical/direct product.

**Step 2 (even \(n\)).** If \(m=2\), there are \(n\) independent two-vertex components, each with two choices for its maximum independent set. Swapping one such choice exchanges precisely one basis vertex, giving Hamming distance on the Boolean cube \(Q_n\). If \(m\ge3\), a crown component has maximum independent sets exactly its two entire shores: an independent set meeting both shores may select only one matching-index pair, giving at most \(2<m\) vertices (when \(m=3\), still strict). Each shore choice contributes \(m\) basis vertices, and switching one component has batch cost \(m\). The claimed hypercube powers, counts and thresholds follow.

**Step 3 (odd \(n\), categorical product).** Index the strong-resolving graph's base cycle consecutively after the antipodal relabelling. An independent set is described by color subsets \(A_i\subseteq[m]\) at each cycle position. If adjacent subsets are both nonempty, then both are the same singleton; a multi-color subset is adjacent only to empty subsets. Let \(J\) be the positions carrying at least two colors and let \(j=|J|\). The positions in \(J\) are pairwise nonadjacent. If \(J\ne\varnothing\), its closed cycle-neighborhood has size at least \(2j+1\), since \(n\) is odd. Therefore the independent-set cardinality is at most
\[
 mj+\bigl(n-(2j+1)\bigr)=(m-2)j+2k\le mk.
\]
For \(m\ge3\), equality forces \(j=k\), with all \(k\) positions of \(J\) filled by all \(m\) colors and no further selected vertices. Such maximum index-independent sets are exactly the \(n\) cyclicly consecutive \(k\)-sets in the *original* cycle indices: two neighbors in the maximum independent set of the antipodal-step cycle differ by \(2k\equiv-1\pmod n\). If \(J=\varnothing\), connectedness forces all occupied adjacent singleton positions to have the same color, so the independent set has size at most \(n=2k+1\). This is strictly below \(mk\) unless \(k=1,m=3\), which is the stated exception. In that case there are also three monochromatic color layers, giving six maximum independent sets. For \(m=2\), the categorical product \(C_n\times K_2\) is a single even cycle of order \(2n\), with exactly two alternating maximum independent sets, corresponding to the two color layers.

**Step 4 (exchange metric).** In every case bases are complements of the maximum independent sets classified above, so the number replaced equals half their symmetric-difference size. For even cases this is ordinary Hamming distance times the stated block weight. For odd \(m=2\), the two color layers are disjoint, giving distance \(n\). For odd \(m\ge3\) outside the exception, two \(k\)-intervals of a \((2k+1)\)-cycle indexed by \(s,t\) differ in exactly \(\min(|s-t|,n-|s-t|)\) entire fibers on each side, giving the stated weighted cycle distance. In the exceptional \(3\times3\) case, two complements from the same row/column family exchange three vertices, while a row and a column exchange two; this yields the sharp \(K_{3,3}\) phase. These formulas specify every edge of every \(\mathcal R_b\) and all connectivity thresholds.

**Step 5 (sliding).** When a one-vertex exchange exists at all it is in the even \(n\), \(m=2\) case, where its two exchanged vertices form a mutually maximally distant pair: their cycle coordinates differ by \(n/2\ge2\), and their colors differ, so they are not adjacent in the original Cartesian product. All other families have smallest possible exchange size at least two. Thus no optimal one-token slide is legal.

## Verification
The standalone Python standard-library program `verify.py` separately constructs \(G\), computes all graph distances by breadth-first search, reconstructs every mutual-maximal-distance pair, and exhaustively enumerates the entire independent-set family of \(G_{SR}\). For fourteen parameter pairs from \((3,2)\) through \((8,2)\), including \((3,3)\), it tests the predicted maximum-independent-set classification and **every pairwise optimal exchange distance** against the formulas above. It also checks all one-token slide possibilities; for graphs with at most twelve vertices it additionally tests the strong-geodesic definition against the vertex-cover criterion on every subset. A separate transfer-matrix count also checks the complete independent-set counting polynomial as a diagnostic, without asserting that polynomial as a new result. The program returned `VERIFY_OK`.

## Relationship to prior work
González Yero [1] developed the simultaneous strong resolving graph viewpoint and strong metric dimension of graph families, providing the 2020 motivating literature and the primary graph-theoretic classification. Rodríguez-Velázquez et al. [2] established strong-resolving-graph product correspondences and strong metric dimensions for cycles crossed with cliques. These results determine minimum *sizes*, not the complete labelled optimum exchange geometry here. Antolín Camarena and Carnero Bravo [3] determine the homotopy type of independence complexes of certain categorical products, including \(C_{3r}\times K_m\). An indexed prior cyclic homology-character study [4] develops a three-state transfer description of those independent-set complexes, which is **not** newly claimed here and does not imply the optimum-basis exchange-distance statements. The present result's new content is the precise classification, pairwise cost, five batch phases, and universal slide obstruction.

## Limitations
The conclusions apply to ordinary finite simple \(C_n\square K_m\) with \(n\ge3,m\ge2\). The exchange move replaces several landmarks simultaneously while requiring only its endpoints to remain minimum bases; it does not require feasible intermediate subsets. The result does not determine analogous reconfiguration geometries for arbitrary Cartesian products. Full publication-priority clearance of unindexed or differently formulated sources is not asserted. Independent validation has not been performed; finite checks supplement the all-parameter argument.
