# Scientific assessment of the phase-alphabet criterion

**Correctness — PASS.** The obstruction for collinear phases is witnessed by conjugate antipodal signals, independently of amplitudes. For noncollinear phases the interleaved amplitude code uniquely identifies nonantipodal support pairs by their products and antipodal pairs by disjoint zero-lag energy intervals. The two antipodal correlation projections are independent exactly when the relative phase is not real. The two zero-lag energy equations and a relative complex product finish the decoder. Strictly positive amplitude budget preserves every ordering and product distinction. The proof, rather than the finite quadrature diagnostic, covers the full alphabet and contrast quantifiers.

**Originality — PASS for the phase-alphabet criterion, not for the unrestricted pattern count.** The closest published almost-flat construction already gives the unrestricted two-pattern minimum and bounded contrast. It requires a distinct-phase code across all sites to identify nonantipodal pairs, not the two prescribed phase settings allowed here. The known obstruction for real masks covers the collinear necessity after global phase normalization. The retained contribution is the matching sufficiency with a prescribed two-level alphabet and any positive amplitude contrast. The comparisons do not establish chronological priority or exhaust unindexed literature.

**Value — PASS.** Restricting phase control to two settings is materially different from allowing each site an independently encoded phase. The exact criterion says which pairs of phase settings work, how amplitude coding and phase diversity divide the identification task, and why neither constant amplitude nor a pair of purely real phase settings suffices. The result is about exact identifiability, not robustness under vanishing contrast or nearly collinear phases.
