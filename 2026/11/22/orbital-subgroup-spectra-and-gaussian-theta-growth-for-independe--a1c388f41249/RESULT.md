# Orbital subgroup spectra and Gaussian–theta growth for independently periodic monoids

## Finding

Let \(r\geq2\), and choose positive integers \(d_i,p_i\) for \(1\leq i\leq r\). Consider the finite pointed commutative monoid \(A\) with generators \(x_1,\ldots,x_r\), an absorbing basepoint \(*\), no mixed relations, and the independent relations
\[
x_i^{d_i+p_i}=x_i^{d_i}\qquad (1\leq i\leq r).
\]
For each \(I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,r\}\), put \(G_I=\prod_{i\in I}C_{p_i}\), with \(G_\varnothing\) trivial. All orbital pairs of \(A\) are indexed **without repetition** by \(I\) and a subgroup \(H\leq G_I\), and their quotient groups are \(G_I/H\). Consequently, using the orbital-pair splitting theorem of Sarazola–Shapiro–Zakharevich,
\[
K(A\text{-Sets})\simeq\bigvee_{I\subseteq [r]}\;\bigvee_{H\leq G_I}\Sigma^\infty_+B(G_I/H).\tag{1}
\]
In particular the spectrum is independent of every tail parameter \(d_i\), despite dependence of the monoid's cardinality on those parameters.

When all periods equal a **fixed prime** \(p\), let \(A_{r,p}\) denote any monoid of this form, and define
\[
{ k\brack s}_p=\prod_{u=0}^{s-1}\frac{p^{k-u}-1}{p^{s-u}-1},\qquad
m_{r,s}(p)=\sum_{k=s}^{r}\binom rk {k\brack s}_p\quad(0\le s\le r).
\]
Then the full stable splitting, with **each multiplicity explicitly evaluated**, is
\[
K(A_{r,p}\text{-Sets})\simeq\bigvee_{s=0}^r\left(\Sigma^\infty_+B(C_p^{\,s})\right)^{\vee m_{r,s}(p)}.\tag{2}
\]
In particular write
\[
R_r(p)=\sum_{s=0}^r m_{r,s}(p),\qquad
T_r(p)=\sum_{s=0}^r s\,m_{r,s}(p).
\]
These are the exact low-dimensional \(K\)-group invariants:
\[
K_0(A_{r,p}\text{-Sets})\cong\mathbb Z^{R_r(p)},\qquad
K_1(A_{r,p}\text{-Sets})\cong
\begin{cases}
(C_2)^{R_r(p)}\oplus(C_p)^{T_r(p)},&p\text{ odd},\\
(C_2)^{R_r(p)+T_r(p)},&p=2.
\end{cases}\tag{3}
\]
The asymptotic constants are **sharp**, distinguishing even and odd generator ranks. Set \(Q_p=\prod_{j=1}^\infty(1-p^{-j})\). For fixed prime \(p\), as \(m\to\infty\),
\[
\frac{R_{2m}(p)}{p^{m^2}}\longrightarrow
\frac{1}{Q_p}\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb Z}p^{-\ell^2},\qquad
\frac{R_{2m+1}(p)}{p^{m(m+1)}}\longrightarrow
\frac{1}{Q_p}\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb Z}p^{-\ell(\ell-1)},\qquad
\frac{T_r(p)}{rR_r(p)}\longrightarrow\frac12.\tag{4}
\]
For \(d_i=1\), the monoid has exactly \((p+1)^r+1\) elements, while \(\log_p R_r(p)\sim r^2/4\): its free \(K_0\)-rank grows superpolynomially in the monoid order.

## Assumptions and scope

The monoid \(A\) is the independent product of monogenic eventually periodic **nonzero** monoids with an additional absorbing basepoint, not the Cartesian product of independently pointed monoids (which would introduce mixed-zero products). Every nonzero monomial has a unique normal form \(\prod_i x_i^{e_i}\) with \(0\le e_i<d_i+p_i\). A nonzero product is always nonzero. Its support is the set of indices with positive exponents, and supports multiply by union. The spectrum \(K(A\text{-Sets})\) is the connective combinatorial algebraic \(K\)-theory of finite pointed \(A\)-sets with the cofibration sequences of the cited source; it is **not** algebraic \(K\)-theory of a monoid ring.

The orbital pairs have the definition in [1, Definition 2.8]: pairs of unpointed submonoids \(F\subseteq R\subseteq A\) for which \(R\) is saturated, \(R//F\) is a group, and \(af\in F\) with \(f\in F\) implies \(a\in F\). The original splitting theorem [1, Theorem 3.15] supplies the final \(K\)-theory equivalence. All classifications and asymptotics below are evaluations for this specified family, not a new general splitting theorem.

## Proof

**Step 1: saturated submonoids.** For each \(I\subseteq[r]\) define \(R_I\) to be the unpointed submonoid generated by \(\{x_i:i\in I\}\). Saturation means a nonzero product belongs to \(R\) only if both its factors belong to \(R\). Since the product support is a union, each \(R_I\) is saturated. Conversely, if a saturated submonoid \(R\) contains \(x_1^{e_1}\cdots x_r^{e_r}\), saturation of its successive factors forces \(x_i\in R\) whenever \(e_i>0\). Closure then forces exactly \(R=R_I\), where \(I\) is the collection of generators it contains. This identifies all \(2^r\) possible \(R\).

**Step 2: subgroup fibers are precisely the orbital stabilizers.** The group completion of \(R_I\) is \(G_I\): in a group the relation \(x_i^{d_i+p_i}=x_i^{d_i}\) reduces to \(x_i^{p_i}=1\), and the independent presentation imposes no further relations. Write \(\phi_I:R_I\twoheadrightarrow G_I\) for the canonical map. If \((R_I,F)\) is orbital, the quotient homomorphism \(R_I\to R_I//F\) factors through \(\phi_I\), and the kernel of the induced \(G_I\to R_I//F\) is a subgroup \(H\). The preimage of the identity of \(R_I//F\) is exactly \(F\): if \([a]=[1]\), there are \(f,f'\in F\) with \(af=f'\), so orbital axiom four implies \(a\in F\), whereas each \(f\in F\) is equivalent to \(1\). Thus \(F=\phi_I^{-1}(H)\), uniquely.

Conversely fix any \(H\leq G_I\) and set \(F=\phi_I^{-1}(H)\). This is an unpointed submonoid. For \(a\in A\), \(f\in F\), if \(af\in F\subseteq R_I\), support-union implies \(a\in R_I\); then \(\phi_I(af)\in H\) and \(\phi_I(f)\in H\) imply \(\phi_I(a)\in H\), so \(a\in F\). Every \(a\in R_I\) admits \(b\in R_I\) with \(\phi_I(ab)\in H\), so \([a]\) has an inverse in \(R_I//F\). To verify \(R_I//F\cong G_I/H\), it remains to prove the canonical surjection is injective. If \(\phi_I(a)H=\phi_I(b)H\), choose \(f\in F\) satisfying \(\phi_I(af)=\phi_I(b)\). Multiply both monomials \(af\) and \(b\) by a common \(t=\prod_{i\in I}x_i^{Np_i}\in F\), with \(N\) large enough to push all exponents past their thresholds \(d_i\). The resulting exponent coordinates have equal residues modulo \(p_i\), hence \(aft=bt\) in \(R_I\). Therefore \(a\sim_F b\), as needed. This proves the orbital-pair classification and quotient labels.

**Step 3: spectrum and low homotopy groups.** Apply [1, Theorem 3.15] to Step 2, giving (1). When all periods equal prime \(p\), each \(G_I\) is the vector space \(\mathbb F_p^{|I|}\). For fixed \(k=|I|\), subgroups with quotient dimension \(s\) are subspaces of dimension \(k-s\), counted by the Gaussian coefficient \({k\brack s}_p\). There are \(\binom rk\) choices of \(I\); this proves (2). Every \(\Sigma^\infty_+ BG\) has \(\pi_0\cong\mathbb Z\) and \(\pi_1\cong\mathbb Z/2\oplus G\) when \(G\) is a finite abelian group: split off the sphere spectrum, then use the first positive-degree stable Hurewicz isomorphism \(\pi_1(\Sigma^\infty BG)\cong H_1(BG;\mathbb Z)=G\). Summing these groups over (2) gives (3). Gaussian symmetry also gives
\[
T_r(p)=\frac12\sum_{k=0}^r k\binom rk\sum_{j=0}^k {k\brack j}_p.\tag{5}
\]

**Step 4: sharp Gaussian–theta asymptotics.** Let \(a_k(p)=\sum_{j=0}^k {k\brack j}_p\). The standard product formula gives, for \(0\le j\le k\),
\[
p^{j(k-j)}\le {k\brack j}_p\le Q_p^{-1}p^{j(k-j)}.\tag{6}
\]
Consequently \(p^{\lfloor k^2/4\rfloor}\le a_k(p)\le(k+1)Q_p^{-1}p^{\lfloor k^2/4\rfloor}\). Since \(R_r(p)=\sum_{k=0}^r\binom rk a_k(p)\), terms with \(k<r\) contribute \(o(a_r(p))\): writing \(d=r-k\), terms \(d\le r/2\) are bounded, up to a polynomial factor, by the binomial expansion of \((1+p^{-3r/8})^r-1\), while for \(d>r/2\) their total is at most a polynomial factor times \(2^r p^{-3r^2/16}\). Both tend to zero. Therefore \(R_r(p)\sim a_r(p)\).

For even \(r=2m\), set \(j=m+\ell\); for each fixed integer \(\ell\), the Gaussian product ratio tends to \(Q_p^{-1}\) and \(j(2m-j)=m^2-\ell^2\). For odd \(r=2m+1\), the exponent is \(m(m+1)-\ell(\ell-1)\). The bounds (6) are summable in \(\ell\), so dominated convergence proves both constants in (4). Finally (5), the top-layer dominance, and \(0\le k/r\le1\) imply \(T_r(p)/(rR_r(p))\to1/2\). The cardinality and superpolynomial-rank assertion follows directly for \(d_i=1\).

## Verification

The included standalone `verify_orbits.py` enumerates every saturated unpointed submonoid, every qualifying stabilizer, and every subgroup preimage **as actual element sets** for six small monoids. It finds exact equality of the two sets of orbital pairs, not merely equal total counts. The cases include two distinct tail lengths and both equal and unequal periods. It also checks the Gaussian multiplicity formulas for \((r,p)=(2,2),(2,3),(3,2),(3,3)\), and prints `MULTIGENERATOR_ORBITS_VERIFY_OK` on success. These finite checks do **not** prove the statements for arbitrary ranks or periods; the proof above supplies the uniform argument.

Illustrative exact ranks: for \((r,p)=(2,2)\), \((R_r,T_r)=(10,7)\), hence \(K_1\cong(C_2)^{17}\); for \((r,p)=(2,3)\), \((R_r,T_r)=(11,8)\), hence \(K_1\cong(C_2)^{11}\oplus(C_3)^8\). The diagonal subgroups of \(C_p^2\) contribute mixed, genuinely two-generator quotient summands.

## Relationship to prior work

Sarazola, Shapiro and Zakharevich [1] already prove the general orbital-pair splitting theorem and explicitly compute **one-generator** eventually periodic monoids (Proposition 5.4). Equation (1) is obtained by applying their theorem, not by reproving or extending its general scope. The new evaluated content is the precise multi-generator orbital subgroup classification, all quotient multiplicities, integral low-\(K\)-group ranks, and parity-dependent sharp rank-growth constants. Weissmann [2] develops a strong Burnside ring decomposition into maximal-subgroup Burnside rings in a broad class of finite monoids; this is relevant to the additive orbit-rank side, so no claim is made that subgroup-indexed Grothendieck-group structure in itself is unknown. The explicit spectral multiplicities and stable \(K_1\) calculations use [1] and are not asserted by [2]. The Gaussian coefficients and theta-series asymptotics of subspace counts are classical; the asserted evaluation concerns their mixed Boolean/Gaussian transform arising from this monoid \(K\)-theory problem, not discovery of Gaussian asymptotics.

## Limitations

This theorem is restricted to independent eventually periodic generators with all \(d_i,p_i\ge1\); it does not cover mixed annihilation relations, noncommutative monoids, or arbitrary finite abelian monoids. The stated low-degree groups do not determine the higher stable homotopy groups of the classifying-space summands. The asymptotic is for a **fixed prime** as the number of generators grows; no uniform joint \(p,r\) limit is claimed. A general pre-existing splitting theorem and classical subgroup-count asymptotics are indispensable ingredients, and further unindexed precedent may exist. No independent mathematical audit is claimed.
