# Review of orbital subgroup spectra and Gaussian–theta growth

## Correctness — PASS

The claim concerns the explicitly presented finite commutative monoid with independent eventually periodic generators and a single absorbing basepoint. Factorization preserves coordinate supports; this forces all saturated unpointed submonoids to be coordinate-subset submonoids. Their group completions are independent cyclic products, and the fourth orbital-pair axiom identifies stabilizers with preimages of subgroups. A large common periodic multiple proves the reverse quotient-congruence implication, so there are no extra or missing pairs. The stable splitting is an explicitly credited application of Sarazola–Shapiro–Zakharevich's Theorem 3.15. Gaussian subspace counting and the degree-one stable Hurewicz theorem produce the low-degree formulas. The theta constants follow by a dominated Gaussian limit and a quantitative top-layer bound, not by extrapolation from computations. Actual packaged verification independently enumerates complete pairs in six finite instances. Remaining limit: the checker samples only finitely many parameter values.

## Originality — PASS, with priority qualifications

The closest full-text reference, Sarazola–Shapiro–Zakharevich (2026), proves the encompassing general splitting theorem and explicitly computes one-generator monoids, but does not provide this multigenerator subgroup-fiber classification, Gaussian quotient-rank polynomial, \(K_1\) multiplicities or the weighted-subspace theta asymptotic. Weissmann (2025) already gives a broad maximal-subgroup Burnside ring decomposition and thus anticipates an abstract additive orbit-ranking mechanism. Classical Gaussian coefficients and subgroup-count theta constants are also acknowledged. What is distinguished here is their full spectral and degree-one evaluation for the natural independent-product monoid family, including a sharp parity-dependent growth law for its mixed Boolean–Gaussian transform. Searches of relevant published findings and the accessible published mathematical literature showed no identical or dominating explicit evaluation. These are best-knowledge comparisons, not a proof of historical priority; unindexed literature is a residual risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The generating relations define the first natural multigenerator family following the one-generator examples of the source splitting theorem. Their new quotient summands include diagonal subgroups not present in a Cartesian juxtaposition of one-generator calculations. The complete stable multiplicity polynomial describes all higher classifying-space summands, while the exact \(K_1\) torsion and the sharp Gaussian–theta free-rank asymptotic expose a substantial transition: the number of summands grows superpolynomially in monoid order. The result is a motivated infinite classification and asymptotic law, not an arbitrary finite slice or a routine restatement of the general splitting theorem.
