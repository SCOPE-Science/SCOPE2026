# Orientability and a connected-sum classification of asymmetric simplex-skeleton deleted products

## Finding

For integers \(p,q\geq1\), let \(N=p+q+3\), let \([N]=\{1,\ldots,N\}\), and form the finite regular polytopal complex
\[
X_{p,q}=\bigcup_{\substack{\varnothing\ne A,B\subseteq[N]\\A\cap B=\varnothing\\|A|\le p+1,\ |B|\le q+1}}\Delta(A)\times\Delta(B),
\]
where \(\Delta(A)\) is the simplex on the indicated vertex set. Then:

1. **Every \(X_{p,q}\) is an orientable closed connected PL manifold of dimension \(p+q\).** More precisely, ordering the vertices of each top-dimensional cell increasingly in each factor, the coefficient \(\operatorname{sgn}(A,B,w)\), with \(\{w\}=[N]\setminus(A\cup B)\), gives a cellular integral fundamental cycle. The sign means the sign of the permutation whose displayed word is the increasing list of \(A\), followed by the increasing list of \(B\), followed by \(w\).
2. **For every \(q\ge2\), the first-coordinate projection induces an isomorphism**
   \[
   \pi_1(X_{1,q})\cong\pi_1(K_{q+4})\cong F_{\binom{q+3}{2}},
   \]
   where \(F_r\) denotes the free group of rank \(r\).
3. **At the first asymmetric three-dimensional case,**
   \[
   X_{1,2}\cong_{\mathrm{TOP}}\mathop{\#}_{10}(S^1\times S^2).
   \]
   In particular this case of the connected-sum conjecture in González--González--Pérez-Morales is true at the stronger homeomorphism level.

## Assumptions and scope

All vertex sets are finite; the two simplex factors are *ordered*, not identified under particle interchange. Only nonempty disjoint vertex subsets define cells, with product cell orientations and the usual integral cellular boundary. The notation \(K_m\) in the fundamental-group statement means the complete graph regarded as a one-dimensional CW complex, rather than its clique complex. The result uses the existing theorem that \(X_{p,q}\) is a connected closed combinatorial manifold for \(p,q>0\), and the classical classification of closed orientable three-manifolds with free fundamental group. It does not require an identification of the higher-dimensional manifolds with connected sums.

## Proof

**1. The integral fundamental cycle.** Top-dimensional cells have \(|A|=p+1\), \(|B|=q+1\) and precisely one unused vertex \(w\). Write \([A]\times[B]\) for the product orientation obtained by listing each subset increasingly. Define the top chain
\[
Z_{p,q}=\sum_{\substack{A\cap B=\varnothing\\|A|=p+1,\ |B|=q+1\\[N]\setminus(A\cup B)=\{w\}}}\operatorname{sgn}(A,B,w)\,[A]\times[B].
\]
Its cellular boundary vanishes integrally. Indeed, each codimension-one cell arises by deleting one vertex either from \(A\) or from \(B\); its complement in \([N]\) then consists of two vertices \(u,v\). Exactly two top cells contain that ridge, by allocating either \(u\) or \(v\) to the deficient factor and leaving the other as \(w\). Their coefficients in the boundary are opposite: swapping the chosen vertex and the unused vertex reverses the sign of the ordered concatenation, after accounting for the sign of the deletion in that factor and the standard product-boundary factor \((-1)^p\) when a vertex of \(B\) is deleted. This is a literal sign cancellation, and does not depend on the parity of \(p\) or \(q\). Every facet appears with coefficient \(\pm1\), so this is a nowhere-zero integral orientation cycle. The cited manifold theorem now upgrades it to a fundamental cycle for the connected closed \((p+q)\)-manifold \(X_{p,q}\).

**2. A graph of simply connected spaces.** Set \(p=1\), \(q\ge2\), and \(N=q+4\). Project \(X_{1,q}\) to the first coordinate, which lies in \(K_N\). For a first-coordinate vertex \(v\), the fiber is the \(q\)-skeleton of the simplex on the remaining \(N-1=q+3\) vertices. For the interior of an edge \(e=uv\), its fiber is the \(q\)-skeleton on the remaining \(N-2=q+2\) vertices. Thus \(X_{1,q}\) is *exactly* a graph of spaces over \(K_N\): a vertex space \(A_v\) over each \(v\), and a cylinder \(B_e\times[0,1]\) for each edge, attached at its two ends by the natural inclusions \(B_e\hookrightarrow A_u,A_v\). This is an equality of regular product-cell descriptions, not an assumption of a locally trivial bundle.

Both \(A_v\) and \(B_e\) are path-connected and simply connected, since \(q\ge2\) and each contains the entire two-skeleton of a simplex on at least four vertices. The two-skeleton of a simplex is simply connected because every loop in its complete one-skeleton is generated by triangular loops, and all those triangles are filled. Applying Seifert--van Kampen successively to a maximal tree of vertex and edge spaces, then attaching the remaining edge cylinders, gives a free group with one generator per edge outside that tree. Since \(K_N\) has \(N\) vertices and \(\binom N2\) edges, the rank is \(\binom N2-N+1=\binom{N-1}{2}=\binom{q+3}{2}\). The projection induces the stated isomorphism.

**3. The three-manifold consequence.** At \((p,q)=(1,2)\), the cited manifold theorem gives a connected closed PL three-manifold. Part 1 establishes orientability; Part 2 gives fundamental group \(F_{10}\). The prime-decomposition theorem for orientable three-manifolds, free-product decomposition of fundamental groups, and the classification of orientable prime three-manifolds imply that a closed connected orientable three-manifold with free fundamental group of rank \(r\) is homeomorphic to the connected sum of \(r\) copies of \(S^1\times S^2\). Any simply connected prime factor is \(S^3\), by the three-dimensional Poincaré theorem, so contributes no additional summand. Taking \(r=10\) gives the asserted homeomorphism.

## Verification

The accompanying standalone `check_cells.py` enumerates the genuine product cells (not their barycentric subdivision), assigns product-boundary signs, and checks \(\partial Z_{p,q}=0\) with every ridge incident to exactly two top cells for all \(1\le p,q\le5\). For \((p,q)=(1,2)\), it obtains cell numbers \((30,120,150,60)\), ranks \((29,81,59)\) of the three cellular boundary operators over both \(\mathbb F_2\) and \(\mathbb F_3\), and Betti numbers \((1,10,10,1)\) over those fields. This is independent finite consistency checking of the combinatorial construction. The three assertions are established for their stated infinite ranges by the proofs above, not by extrapolation from the finite checks. Run `python3 check_cells.py` with Python 3.8 or later; `check_output.txt` records the expected output.

## Relationship to prior work

González, González and Pérez-Morales (arXiv:2611.12847v1, first made public 9 November 2026) prove in Theorem 1.3 that every \(X_{p,q}\) is a closed combinatorial manifold, connected when \(p,q>0\). Their Remark 1.5 explicitly regards orientability for the asymmetric family as a hoped-for consequence of a stronger connected-sum prediction, and Conjecture 1.6 predicts the homotopy type \(\#_{\binom{p+q+2}{p+1}}(S^p\times S^q)\). A prediction is not a proof. The present result supplies a direct integral orientation and establishes the previously conjectured \((p,q)=(1,2)\) case by an independent fundamental-group argument.

The earlier González--González paper (arXiv:2307.14454; later Selecta Mathematica 31 (2025), article 60) computes homology and Morse structures for the *symmetric* two-coordinate deleted product of a single skeleton, but does not furnish the asymmetric orientation or the \(p=1\) graph-of-spaces calculation. Previously known classifications of ordinary two-point configuration spaces of complete graphs concern the different case where both coordinates have dimension one. The 2026 paper also explains why the symmetric \((2,2)\) case is a different four-dimensional problem.

## Limitations

Only the \((1,2)\) asymmetric case is classified up to homeomorphism here. The fundamental-group calculation does not establish the higher-dimensional connected-sum conjecture for \(q\ge3\), nor the cases \(p,q\ge2\). The orientability assertion is independent of those unknown global homotopy types. No diffeomorphism, equivariant decomposition, or unordered-particle quotient is asserted. Standard three-manifold classification theorems are used as cited mathematical inputs. Although the relevant public preprints and indexed comparison records were checked, an unindexed equivalent argument remains a residual priority risk; independent validation has not been performed.
