# Scientific review

## Correctness

**PASS.** The precise regular product-cell model is the one in González--González--Pérez-Morales, Theorem 1.3. The new orientation cycle has an explicit sign for every top cell and opposite incidence signs along both kinds of ridges, proving integral orientability for all positive dimensions. The graph-of-spaces decomposition for the first one-dimensional skeleton is exact, and all vertex and edge spaces are simply connected for the stated range. Van Kampen therefore gives the asserted free fundamental group with the correct rank. The closed, orientable, connected three-dimensional special case is classified using the classical 3-manifold prime-decomposition theorem and the Poincaré theorem. Independent finite cellular orientation and boundary-rank computations agree with the formulas. These finite computations are checks, not the source of the infinite proof.

## Originality

**PASS, with the explicitly listed priority risk.** The 2026 paper's Theorem 1.3 supplies manifold recognition, whereas Remark 1.5 and Conjecture 1.6 explicitly place the connected-sum identification in the status of a prediction; they do not prove an integral global orientation for arbitrary asymmetric parameters or the free fundamental-group formula. The 2023/2025 González--González work treats equal simplex skeleta in both coordinates, not the asymmetric mixed skeleton complex. The published complete-graph graph-braid calculations concern the different symmetric one-dimensional case. Semantic database searches of asymmetric deleted products, orientability, the graph-of-spaces projection and the predicted ten-fold connected sum did not uncover a theorem implying the new claim. A symmetric deleted-product homology result found in indexed research records does not include this asymmetric family. Lack of an indexed match cannot establish uniqueness, and an uncatalogued result remains possible.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** The source explicitly motivates determining which new deleted-product manifolds realize predicted connected sums. The theorem gives an integral orientation for the entire asymmetric manifold family and resolves the first asymmetric three-dimensional instance at the homeomorphism level, while also obtaining the fundamental group of an infinite higher-dimensional subfamily. The result is not merely a finite census or a change of notation: it uses graph-of-spaces topology and three-manifold classification to settle an explicit conjectural case that the cited source leaves open.
