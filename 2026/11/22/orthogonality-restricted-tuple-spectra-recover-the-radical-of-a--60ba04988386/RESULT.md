# Orthogonality-restricted tuple spectra recover the radical of a finite symplectic parameter span

## Finding
Fix a prime power \(q\). Let \((V,\beta)\) be a countably infinite-dimensional nondegenerate alternating space over \(\mathbb F_q\), with the field and bilinear form named in its language. Let \(A\subset V\) be a finite individually named parameter set, \(U=\langle A\rangle\), \(d=\dim U\), and \(\rho=\dim\operatorname{rad}(\beta|_U)\). For every integer \(r\ge0\), let \(T_r^\perp(A)\) be the number of complete types (equivalently, pointwise-stabilizer orbits) of ordered \(r\)-tuples \((x_1,\ldots,x_r)\in V^r\) satisfying \(\beta(x_i,x_j)=0\) for all \(i,j\). Tuples may have zero coordinates, repeated coordinates and arbitrary linear dependencies; their coordinates may lie in \(U\).

Write \({n\brack k}_q\) for the Gaussian binomial coefficient, with value zero outside \(0\le k\le n\), and set \(m=(d-\rho)/2\). Define \(J_t(d,\rho;q)\), the number of totally isotropic \(t\)-subspaces of \(U\), by
\[
J_t(d,\rho;q)=\sum_{j=\max(0,t-m)}^{\min(t,\rho)}{\rho\brack j}_q q^{(t-j)(\rho-j)}\prod_{i=0}^{t-j-1}\frac{q^{2m-i}-q^i}{q^{t-j}-q^i}.
\]
Empty products are one; \(J_0=1\). The exact all-arity spectrum is
\[
\boxed{T_r^\perp(A)=\sum_{h=0}^{r}{r\brack h}_q\sum_{t=0}^{\min(h,d)}J_t(d,\rho;q)\left(\prod_{i=0}^{t-1}(q^h-q^i)\right)q^{(r-h)(d-t)}.}
\]
For \(d\ge1\) the first two counts simplify to
\[
\boxed{T_1^\perp=2q^d,\quad T_2^\perp=2q^{2d}-(q^d-q^\rho)(q^d-q^{d-1})+(q+1)\left[q^d+(q^d-1)q^{d-1}\right].}
\]
The first count recovers \(d\) for fixed \(q\); since \(q^d-q^{d-1}>0\), the second then recovers \(q^\rho\), hence \(\rho\). In the class of finite alternating spaces, \(d\) and \(\rho\) determine the abstract isometry class. Therefore these two scalar type counts determine every subsequent orthogonality-restricted type count. They do not determine which individually named parameter vectors realize the form.

Unary data are insufficient. Over \(\mathbb F_2\), two-dimensional parameter spaces with zero form and with one hyperbolic pair both have \(T_1^\perp=8\), but their orthogonal pair counts are respectively \(62\) and \(56\). This makes arity two sharp. For completeness, when \(d=0\), one has \(T_1^\perp=2\) and \(T_2^\perp=5\), directly from the all-arity formula.

## Assumptions and scope
The ambient alternating form is nondegenerate and has unbounded hyperbolic rank; finite parameter spans may be degenerate. The word *orthogonal* means vanishing bilinear pairing, including when the characteristic is two. The finite field \(\mathbb F_q\) is named, not varied inside one structure. Complete types are over the pointwise-named set \(A\); its linear span is definable over \(A\). All tuple coordinates are vector variables, and their linear dependence or equality is part of their complete type. No assumption that the new coordinates lie outside the span is imposed.

## Proof
**Orbit normal form.** A tuple \(x=(x_i)_{1\le i\le r}\) is determined up to the pointwise stabilizer of \(A\) by the kernel of its linear map into \(V/U\), the induced map into \(U\) on that kernel, the pairing of the remaining independent cosets with \(U\), and all pairwise pairings among the new vectors. Indeed these data define a linear isometry on the finite-dimensional sum of the parameter span and tuple span, fixing the parameter span. Finite-dimensional alternating-space Witt extension (after embedding this finite sum in a finite nondegenerate ambient subspace) extends this isometry to the whole countable space. Conversely the data are invariant under the pointwise stabilizer. Every compatible finite datum embeds in the ambient space: add dual vectors for the desired functionals and enough new hyperbolic pairs to meet the required pairings. The imposed new-vector pairings are all zero, so no extra Gram matrix is to be counted.

**Counting the kernel and its image.** Put \(W=\mathbb F_q^r\), let \(L:W\to V\) send the standard basis to the tuple, and let \(H=\ker(W\to V/U)\) have dimension \(h\). There are \({r\brack h}_q\) choices of \(H\). The induced map \(a=L|_H:H\to U\) has totally isotropic image \(S\), since \(\beta(Lu,Lv)=0\) for all \(u,v\in W\). If \(\dim S=t\), then after choosing \(S\), the number of surjections \(H\to S\) is \(\prod_{i=0}^{t-1}(q^h-q^i)\). Choose a complement \(W_0\) to \(H\) solely to count coordinates. Its \(r-h\) basis vectors have independent images modulo \(U\), and their pairings with the \(d\)-dimensional space \(U\) must vanish on \(S\), leaving \((r-h)(d-t)\) independent field values. Their mutual pairings are fixed at zero. Thus each \((H,S,a)\) contributes \(q^{(r-h)(d-t)}\) types, independently of the complement chosen. This proves the displayed expression once \(J_t\) is evaluated.

**The isotropic-space factor.** Let \(R=\operatorname{rad}(U)\), \(\dim R=\rho\), and note that \(U/R\) is nondegenerate symplectic of dimension \(2m=d-\rho\). For an isotropic \(t\)-subspace \(S\), put \(j=\dim(S\cap R)\); then its projection in \(U/R\) is an isotropic \((t-j)\)-subspace. There are \({\rho\brack j}_q\) choices of intersection with \(R\), and each projected isotropic space has \(q^{(t-j)(\rho-j)}\) lifts. The number of isotropic \(s\)-subspaces in a \(2m\)-dimensional nondegenerate symplectic space equals
\[
\prod_{i=0}^{s-1}\frac{q^{2m-i}-q^i}{q^s-q^i},
\]
obtained by counting ordered mutually orthogonal independent frames and dividing by the number of ordered bases of an \(s\)-space. Necessarily \(0\le s\le m\). Summing over allowable \(j\) proves the asserted \(J_t\) formula without any nonstandard classification lemma.

**First two arities and sharp recovery.** For one coordinate, there are exactly \(q^d\) algebraic types from vectors in \(U\), and exactly \(q^d\) independent-coset types, indexed by the unrestricted pairing functional on \(U\). This gives \(2q^d\). For two orthogonal coordinates, split according to the dimension of their joint image in \(V/U\). If this dimension is two, there are \(q^{2d}\) types; their mutual pairing is specified to be zero. If it is zero, there are exactly as many types as ordered orthogonal pairs of elements of \(U\). An element of \(R\) has all \(q^d\) partners, while each of the other \(q^d-q^\rho\) elements has \(q^{d-1}\) orthogonal partners, yielding \(q^{2d}-(q^d-q^\rho)(q^d-q^{d-1})\). If the quotient image has dimension one, its kernel is one of the \(q+1\) lines in \(\mathbb F_q^2\). The kernel vector may map to zero in \(U\), giving \(q^d\) possible pairings for the remaining quotient generator, or to any of \(q^d-1\) nonzero vectors, giving \(q^{d-1}\) pairings per vector. The sum of these three cases is the stated two-arity formula. At fixed \(d\) its coefficient of \(q^\rho\) is \(q^d-q^{d-1}>0\), establishing unique recovery for every prime power and every finite parameter span of positive dimension.

## Verification
`verify.cpp` independently constructs finite nondegenerate symplectic ambient spaces over \(\mathbb F_2\) and \(\mathbb F_3\), exhaustively enumerates admissible ordered pairs, and groups them by linear-relation kernels and full pairings to a fixed parameter-span basis. It checks eight finite cases, including both radical options in dimension two and the three-dimensional totally isotropic base in ambient dimension ten. Its recorded output ends `FINITE_SYMPLECTIC_SIGNATURES_PASS`.

`verify_general.py` independently enumerates small finite linear maps and isotropic image strata rather than relying on Gaussian-binomial coefficients, testing the all-arity formula through arity four in eight base/field cases; its recorded output ends `GAUSSIAN_ISOTROPIC_SPECTRUM_PASS`. The finite verifiers corroborate the arithmetic and orbit normal form; unbounded-arithmetic and all-arity claims follow from the symbolic proof above, not from finite experimentation.

## Relationship to prior work
Liu's 2013 study classifies finite singular-symplectic subspace orbit geometries and gives polynomial counts for subspace lattices. It motivates finite degenerate symplectic parameters and supplies context for isotropic-subspace enumeration but does not state a complete orthogonality-restricted **named-parameter tuple-type** spectrum or recovery of the radical from two orbit counts. Barbina's 2004 thesis develops automorphism groups of countable symplectic spaces and the finite-subspace isometry-extension machinery used here, but its inspected material does not supply the displayed counting formulas. The published 2026 published-finding corpus finding *Exact product tuple-orbit profiles for homogeneous finite-field bilinear spaces* computes **unmarked unrestricted** tuple orbit counts, including \(\prod_{i=0}^{r-1}(1+q^i)\) for alternating spaces, by counting kernels and arbitrary descended forms. Those data do not distinguish the radical of a **named base**, and restricting the new Gram matrix to zero together with tracking intersection with the base is not covered by that theorem. Searches for pointed symplectic-stabilizer orbit counts, orthogonal pair-type counts, finite-field Gaussian-isotropic formulas and radical reconstruction did not reveal an equivalent statement.

## Limitations
This result concerns the specified infinite-dimensional nondegenerate alternating space over a **fixed** finite field and orthogonality-restricted complete ordered vector types, with repetitions and algebraic vectors permitted. It is not a classification of all tuple orbits of arbitrary reducts, nor a reconstruction of the actual parameter labelling from two numbers. The calculation uses familiar Witt-extension and symplectic Gaussian counting techniques; its potential equivalence to unpublished or older folklore is a real historical-priority risk. A 1998 article of Simon Thomas about groups acting on infinite-dimensional symplectic spaces was located but only its abstract was accessible; its stated focus is overgroup rigidity rather than numerical stabilizer orbit spectra. Independent mathematical validation has not been performed.
