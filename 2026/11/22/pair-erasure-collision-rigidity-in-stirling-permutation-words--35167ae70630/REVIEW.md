# Review of pair-erasure collision rigidity in Stirling permutations

## Correctness
**PASS.** The order-standardizing erasure is explicitly defined. A three-case insertion-cancellation lemma identifies precisely the colliding pairs of label erasures, and a persistent-gap argument proves the full pairwise count. The consecutive-label collision classes imply the distinct-output mean. Exhaustive exact checks cover all \(146598\) Stirling permutations of orders two through seven (\(3+15+105+945+10395+135135\)), including the pairwise structural criterion, count formula, mean and three minimum-diversity sources. The proof, not finite enumeration, establishes the arbitrary-order statement.

## Originality
**PASS, qualified.** The nearest full texts are Bóna's 2007 Stirling-permutation insertion/descent analysis and Janson's 2008 recursive-tree correspondence; neither contains the claimed erasure collision classification or its census. A separate indexed result on standardized two-deletion ambiguity for ordinary permutations studies a different source and deletion model. Searches of related source terms, equivalent tree-pruning descriptions and indexed findings identified no dominating result. Earlier unindexed equivalent work cannot be ruled out; the related generalized-Stirling manuscript was reviewed at abstract level only.

## Mathematical value
**PASS.** The exact distribution of *which* label erasures coincide under order-standardization is directly relevant to one-read reconstruction in increasing-tree/Stirling source families. The theorem gives every pairwise collision count, all equality patterns, the complete one-output extremizers, and the exact typical number of distinct outputs; it does not merely restate the classical \((2n-1)!!\) enumeration, nor the ascent/plateau statistics. The correction \(7/6\) to the naive \(n\)-output count has a structural nesting/sibling decomposition.

## Scope and limitations
Only deletion of both copies of one label and subsequent order-standardization is considered. Higher multiplicities, arbitrary two-position deletions and code capacity are outside scope. The finite checker cannot certify the inductive cancellation lemma independently of the mathematical proof. Publication-priority uncertainty remains for research not indexed or accessible at review time.
