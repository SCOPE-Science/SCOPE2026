# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The domain is fixed to the intrinsic Euclidean metric on the unit-weight Loday associahedron. The original interval inequalities and established nested-complex facet incidence determine exactly which pairs meet in codimension two; projection of interval indicators onto the sum-zero hyperplane gives the signed normal products, from which the entire multiset and both count identities follow. The four-endpoint construction with a negligible boundary-sharing population proves the Dirichlet weak limit. A fresh finite verifier independently checks actual affine ranks, angle levels, and formulas for dimensions indexed by \(3\le n\le8\); it is corroboration only, not an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, priority provisional
The 2002 realization and 2023 survey already contain the facet equations and combinatorial nested-tube compatibility. A realization-comparison paper and low-dimensional projection calculations concern different or lower-dimensional geometry. Compared published work concerning metric angles of other polytopes and edge lengths of Loday associahedra does not imply the claimed all-dimensional ridge-angle histogram or the five-spacing limit. Searches cannot rule out unindexed or unexamined formulas; the closest known ingredients are credited rather than presented as discoveries.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The question asks for intrinsic Euclidean ridge geometry of a canonical Catalan polytope family, an invariant not determined by the face lattice or by edge lengths. The result reveals an exact reflection pairing of angle supports despite unequal counts, a sharp \(2/n\) acute-proportion deficit, and a nondegenerate Dirichlet limiting law. It unifies the small-dimensional angle examples into an infinite-family metric theorem.

## Limits and source comparisons
Loday (arXiv:math/0212126) proves the vertex/facet realization, and Pilaud–Santos–Ziegler (arXiv:2305.08471, especially the realization and nested-complex sections) survey the relevant combinatorial data; these are essential established inputs. Ceballos–Santos–Ziegler (arXiv:1109.5544) compare different realizations; such equivalence does not preserve Euclidean angles. Rost's projection note gives low-dimensional examples, which do not settle the higher-dimensional multiplicities or limit. General statements about metric angle spectra of other convex polytopes have different objects and cannot imply this claim merely from a shared angle formula shape. A more general unpublished or unindexed Euclidean angle classification is a remaining historical-priority risk. The finite checker does not establish results for arbitrary \(n\), and no independent audit has been carried out.
