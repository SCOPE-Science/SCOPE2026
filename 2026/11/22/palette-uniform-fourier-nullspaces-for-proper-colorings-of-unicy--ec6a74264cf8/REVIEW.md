# Scientific review

## Correctness

**PASS.** The theorem's normalization is unnormalized counting convolution on the vertex-color group. The dual coboundary identifies the balanced character space and its edge-character quotient; the one-cycle character filter yields the complete signed spectrum. The smallest-exponent congruence argument proves the zero classification for all \(q\ge3\), and the free cycle action proves its exact multiplicity. Stirling's formula gives the stated fixed-palette asymptotic. The standalone exact checker confirms 5,209 independently enumerated Fourier values and four selected further cases. These tests support but do not replace the all-parameter proof.

## Originality

**PASS under bounded comparison.** Goodall's 2008 full-text Sections 3–5 give the general graph-tension Fourier framework and chromatic enumerator identities, but do not state a palette-uniform unicyclic Fourier-zero classification. A published study of first-degree Fourier sampling on proper colorings of cycles gives a Gram spectrum, not the full character array. An earlier ternary cycle calculation covers the restricted \(q=3\), cycle-only case, which is explicitly credited. The broader exact integer zero test, rank formula for every unicyclic graph and palette-order asymptotic were not found in the searched sources. The chief residual risk is an unindexed specialization of complete weight enumerators or the Potts transfer matrix equivalent to this result.

## Value

**PASS.** Exact Fourier recovery on constrained graph colorings is a natural invariant. The result replaces generic duality by a precise arithmetic if-and-only-if cancellation criterion, identifies a congruence transition at every palette order, proves that all pendant-tree shapes share the same rank when their sizes agree, and quantifies the vanishing fraction of additional losses. These are exact infinite-family statements, not arbitrarily selected small-case enumerations.

## Scope and limits

The palette has at least three elements and the graph is connected, simple and unicyclic. Neither multiple-cycle graphs nor general weighted color models are settled. This review is not independent certification; an equivalent older result under different terms remains possible.
