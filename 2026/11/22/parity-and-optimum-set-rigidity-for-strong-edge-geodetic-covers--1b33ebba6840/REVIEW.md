# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The exact path-length capacity bound excludes fewer than the stated number of terminals and forces one entire bipartition shore at cardinality \(n\). Near-one-factorizations give edge-covering path assignments for odd \(n\). For even \(n\), counting edges between omitted opposite-shore vertices excludes all mixed \(n+1\)-sets with at least two selected vertices on each side; an explicit near-one-factorization and derangement constructs every surviving full-shore-plus-one case. The independently computed finite examples agree. No finite check is used as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS (provisional)
The 2023 preprint/2024 article by Klavžar and Zmazek solves the complete bipartite strong edge geodetic minimum, not its matching-deleted crown analogue or the exact optimal-set rigidity and count. The 2020 Cartesian product paper studies a different graph operation, while ordinary edge-geodetic bounds permit multiple paths per terminal pair. Primary source statements and research-record comparisons uncovered no covering result, though unindexed earlier work remains a risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The graph is a canonical perfect-matching deletion of a biclique, and the deleted edges change the strong edge geodetic optimum by two in odd order while leaving even order unchanged. The proof reveals near-one-factorization geometry and classifies every minimizer of an infinite family, including a sharp parity change in optimizer multiplicity.
