# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The vector-pair identity (1) holds after choosing opposite cyclic gaps below half the circumference, which is always possible for four distinct vertices. If those gaps differ, strict concavity of cosine gives the minimum at gaps one and two; if they agree, odd parity forces unequal intervening gaps and gives exactly the same bound. Equality conditions agree, and an independent elementary argument classifies every even-order zero. The finite checker enumerates 80,730 four-subsets for orders 4 through 26, supplementing but not replacing the all-order proof. The primitive-frequency deduction is a residue permutation.

**Originality — PASS relative to inspected evidence.** Barber (arXiv:2104.15057, Proposition 3(c)) determines the unrestricted four-root minimum allowing repetitions; his minimizing construction repeats a root and does not attain the sharp value for distinct supports. Kalogirou (arXiv:2607.06098) advances general term-count upper bounds without the distinct-four-point coefficient classification. Cheng (arXiv:2610.27231) treats symmetric five-term exceptional sets and conditional prime-order separation, not this exact universal formula. The published four-point cyclic Wiener record optimizes an aggregate Fourier \(\ell^1\) quantity for bounded moduli, so does not imply a single-frequency sharp minimum. Relevant searches did not locate the statement; this is not proof of absolute novelty. An older distinct-root extremal geometry result remains the principal bibliographic risk.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** Indicator Fourier transforms, unlike unrestricted sparse exponential sums, forbid repeated phase atoms at primitive frequencies. This restriction changes the optimal cancellation scale by an exact factor of three asymptotically, creates a unique translation orbit of worst four-site masks at every odd order, and supplies an all-modulus, exact nonvanishing threshold for Fourier-zero stability of binary four-site data. This is a natural sharp invariant, not a preselected numerical slice; the even-order parity boundary and all equality configurations are part of the theorem. It does not solve general separation for longer masks.
