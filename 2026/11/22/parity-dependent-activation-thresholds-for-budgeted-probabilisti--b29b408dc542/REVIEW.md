# Scientific assessment: budgeted probabilistic firebreaks

**Correctness: PASS.** For independent bond transmission on a path with a uniformly selected single ignition, the mean loss reduces exactly to a sum of exponentials over all contiguous edge intervals. That sum is strictly convex because length-one intervals give a strictly positive Hessian in every nonzero direction. Explicit central-edge gradient comparisons give \((m-k)(mq-k)\) in the even case and \((m-k)(q+mq^2-k)\) in the odd case, so the sharp budget boundaries follow from necessary and sufficient simplex optimality. At equality strict convexity leaves no second optimizer. The four-patch continuation reduces to a strictly convex one-variable function with the stated endpoints. The standard-library checker passed 6952 finite/exact checks; this is corroboration, not the proof.

**Originality: PASS on the assessed record, with priority uncertainty.** The closest published mathematical comparison, *Exact staircase of optimal two-firebreak gaps on a uniformly ignited cycle* (2026), uses exactly two perfect edge deletions on a cycle and independent ignitions throughout, optimizing a discrete gap. Finney (2001) analyzes geometric fuel-treatment pattern design, Wei–Rideout–Kirsch (2008) use discrete spatial treatment optimization, and Demange et al. (2021) analyze combinatorial graph cuts. None of their inspected statements yields the exponential-budget parity threshold by direct specialization or stronger implication. General convex optimization and the elementary pair-connectivity formula are known methods, not novelty claims. An unrestricted historical priority guarantee is unavailable; generic continuous network interdiction literature and inaccessible relevant older text remain residual risk.

**Value: PASS.** The exact dimension-dependent first-activation point answers when a minimal model should concentrate all firebreak strengthening at the middle versus also strengthen additional gaps. Parity doubles the first-activation budget for a matched half-size and produces a sharp, testable benchmark for numerical spatial-intervention optimizers. It also isolates the critical modeling assumptions under which such a recommendation holds. The result is more than a numerical optimum for one arbitrarily chosen small network, while making no ecological or engineering effectiveness claim without calibration.
