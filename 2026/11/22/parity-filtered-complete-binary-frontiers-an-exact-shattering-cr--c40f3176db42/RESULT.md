# Parity-filtered complete binary frontiers: an exact shattering criterion and polynomial

## Finding
Let \(T_h\) be the perfect rooted binary tree of edge-height \(h\ge2\). A *frontier* is a maximal antichain of its ancestor order, equivalently a complete prefix code meeting each root-to-leaf path once. For \(p\in\{0,1\}\), let \(\mathcal F_{h,p}\) consist of exactly those frontiers of cardinality congruent to \(p\) modulo \(2\), queried by vertex membership.

**Exact criterion.** A set \(S\) of tree vertices is shattered by \(\mathcal F_{h,p}\) if and only if it is an ancestor antichain without two sibling leaves, and either (a) some depth-\(h-1\) vertex is incomparable with every member of \(S\), or (b) \(S\) itself is a complete frontier using only vertices of depth at most \(h-2\), with \(|S|\equiv p\pmod2\).

Define polynomials \(A_1(z)=1+3z\), \(B_1(z)=3z\), and, for \(j\ge2\),
\[
 A_j(z)=z+A_{j-1}(z)^2,\qquad B_j(z)=z+B_{j-1}(z)^2.
\]
Define \(K_0(z)=z\) and \(K_{j+1}(z)=z+K_j(z)^2\). Then the complete shattering polynomial (including the empty support) is
\[
 \boxed{\Phi_{h,p}(z)=A_h(z)-B_h(z)+\tfrac12\left(K_{h-2}(z)+(-1)^pK_{h-2}(-z)\right).}
\]
For every \(h\ge2\), \(\operatorname{VCdim}(\mathcal F_{h,p})=2^{h-1}-1\). For \(h\ge3\) the number of maximum-cardinality shattered supports is exactly \(2^{h-1}3^{2^{h-1}-1}\), for either parity. At height two there are six maximal singletons for even parity and seven for odd parity. For \(h\ge3\) both shattering complexes are nonpure. At height three the full polynomials are
\[
 \Phi_{3,0}(z)=1+14z+67z^2+108z^3,\qquad
 \Phi_{3,1}(z)=1+15z+66z^2+108z^3.
\]

## Assumptions and scope
The root has depth zero and each nonleaf has two children. Tree vertices are distinctly labeled by their binary addresses. Shattering means that every assignment of zeros and ones to all queries in a fixed support is realized by a frontier of the *specified parity*. The empty support is shattered because each parity family is nonempty for \(h\ge2\). The claim concerns neither all downsets nor variable-size unconstrained frontiers, and it makes no assertion about the Littlestone dimension.

## Proof
**Step 1: Unrestricted extension and pair obstructions.** A shattered support must be an ancestor antichain: the all-one assignment otherwise requires comparable vertices in a frontier. It cannot contain two sibling terminal leaves, since these always occur together in any frontier. Conversely, given an ancestor antichain \(S\) with no sibling leaves, and any positive-label subset \(P\subseteq S\), build the antichain \(Q\) from \(P\) together with the parents of the negatively labeled terminal leaves. No two members of \(Q\) can be comparable: distinct negatively labeled leaves cannot be siblings, and any selected positive vertex comparable with such a parent would contradict either the antichain condition or the absent sibling-leaf pair. Complete \(Q\) to a frontier by adding the original terminal leaf on each as-yet-uncovered root-to-leaf path. This frontier includes exactly the queried positives and avoids every queried negative, including negative internal vertices. Thus the two pair conditions exactly characterize unrestricted shattering.

**Step 2: A free bottom parent provides both parities.** Suppose a depth-\(h-1\) vertex \(w\) is incomparable with all elements of \(S\). For each chosen labeling the construction in Step 1 may be modified entirely inside the descendant branch of \(w\) to use either \(w\) or its two terminal children. Neither option changes the query trace, while the cardinalities differ by one. Hence the labeling is realized with either parity. This proves sufficiency in alternative (a).

**Step 3: Exhaustion when all bottom parents are touched.** Assume every depth-\(h-1\) vertex is comparable to some queried vertex. Under the pair conditions, each bottom parent is either below exactly one queried internal ancestor, is itself queried, or has exactly one queried terminal child. A frontier containing all queries is consequently forced uniquely: choose the queried internal vertices, and for any queried terminal child choose its sibling. Its cardinality parity is fixed. If this forced frontier has the wrong parity, the all-one labeling fails. If any queried terminal child occurs, changing that query alone to zero forces selecting its parent rather than its two children, changing parity while all other covered bottom-parent blocks are fixed. The same parity-flip argument applies when a queried bottom parent is changed from one to zero: its two children become forced. Therefore shattering is impossible if any queried vertex has depth at least \(h-1\). If all queries have depth at most \(h-2\), covering every bottom parent makes \(S\) itself a complete upper-level frontier. For any chosen labeling, retain the positively labeled members of \(S\); replace every negatively labeled vertex by three descendant vertices, obtained by splitting it once and then splitting one of its two children. Two levels are available, and this raises the frontier size by exactly two per negative query. Consequently every labeling has parity \(|S|\), and all are realized exactly when \(|S|\equiv p\pmod2\). This proves necessity and sufficiency in (b).

**Step 4: Enumerating.** For a height-one binary subtree, permissible ancestor antichains without both sibling leaves are the empty set and any one of its three vertices, so their polynomial is \(A_1=1+3z\). Among these, the supports touching its sole bottom parent are the three singletons, giving \(B_1=3z\). At higher heights an admissible antichain either consists of the root, or is a disjoint union of two admissible child-subtree antichains. To touch every bottom parent, the same recursion applies, but both child-subtree antichains must touch all their bottom parents. Thus \(A_j=z+A_{j-1}^2\) and \(B_j=z+B_{j-1}^2\). The difference counts precisely those admissible supports with a free bottom parent. Complete upper-level frontiers are exactly the ordinary frontiers of a perfect height-\(h-2\) tree, counted by \(K_{h-2}\) through root-or-two-subfrontiers decomposition. Extracting the terms of cardinality congruent to \(p\) gives the displayed parity projection. The two support classes are disjoint.

**Step 5: Extremal supports and nonpurity.** Put \(m=2^{h-1}\), the number of bottom parents. Each permissible antichain has at most one query assigned to any given bottom-parent block: a query above the block occupies all descendant blocks, while a bottom parent or one of its children occupies one block. These assigned nonempty block sets are pairwise disjoint, hence \(|S|\le m\). A support with a free bottom parent has at most \(m-1\) queries; the exceptional upper-level frontier has at most \(m/2\) vertices. Conversely, choosing one free bottom parent and independently choosing one vertex from each other triple (bottom parent or either terminal child) produces \(m-1\) shattered queries. For \(h\ge3\), the exceptional upper-level fronts cannot reach \(m-1\), and any support of size \(m-1\) must have precisely one free block and one selected vertex from every other bottom block. This gives \(m3^{m-1}\) distinct maximum supports. For \(h=2\) there are six free-block singleton supports; additionally the root is an odd upper-level frontier. Finally, for odd parity the root singleton is shattered by alternative (b) but cannot extend to a larger ancestor antichain; for even parity the two children of the root form a shattered, inclusion-maximal upper-level frontier. Their sizes \(1\) or \(2\) are strictly below \(m-1\) when \(h\ge3\) except at \(h=3\), where \(m-1=3\). Nonpurity follows.

## Verification
Two standalone, independently written programs `verify_parity_independent.py` and `verify_parity_cpp.cpp` enumerate complete frontiers directly by root/child recursion, then test every possible Boolean trace on every ancestor antichain at heights two and three; Python additionally does the exhaustive height-four trace census for both parity families. These are independent of the symbolic polynomial recurrence. The measured height-three coefficients are \((1,14,67,108)\) and \((1,15,66,108)\); height-four supports number \(73{,}901\) and \(73{,}902\) respectively. The C++ checker independently expands the polynomial recurrence to height five and tests its degree and top coefficients. Reproducible commands and exact outputs are supplied in `VERIFICATION.md` and companion files. Finite tests corroborate, but do not replace, the all-height proof above.

## Relationship to prior work
Misanantenaina and Wagner, *A polynomial associated with rooted trees and specific posets* (2018, Section 2.2 and Theorem 2.8), counts maximal antichain **hypotheses** and describes their branch recursion; it does not characterize shattered query supports after a parity constraint. The published work *Exact static and online shattering of complete hierarchical frontiers* gives the unrestricted frontier dimensions, explicitly without fixed-frontier-cardinality claims; it does not imply this parity-sensitive polynomial. The published *Flag shattering complexes of maximal rooted-tree antichains and an unbounded online gap* identifies the **unrestricted** pair-obstruction graph; it does not supply the free-bottom-parent criterion or the exceptional parity-matched upper-level fronts. These exact pointwise statements are compared here rather than only titles or parameters. Elsholtz–Heuberger–Krenn (2019) count compact Huffman codes by equivalence/shape rather than parity-filtered Boolean query traces. The unrestricted structural lemma is credited as prior work; only the parity refinement, its polynomial, and the resulting extremal laws are advanced here.

## Limitations
The theorem uses complete binary trees of height at least two, vertex-membership queries, and the parity of frontier cardinality. It does not cover arbitrary irregular rooted trees, multi-valued queries or general modular constraints. No Littlestone dimension or independently validated historical uniqueness is claimed. Unindexed literature on complete prefix codes may contain equivalent results; independent mathematical audit has not been performed.
