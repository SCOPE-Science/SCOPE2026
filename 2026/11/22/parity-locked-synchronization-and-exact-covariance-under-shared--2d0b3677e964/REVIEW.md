# Scientific review: shared-noise sign-gradient synchronization

## Correctness — PASS
The gradient's uniform sign probabilities yield the exact finite-state map, which is closed at both endpoints. The interval between two sign thresholds proves the autonomous two-step pure-death gap process; independent geometric holding times follow from strong Markov restarting at each level. Extremal-order preservation proves global within-class synchronization, and the product of a binomial lower index with a fair orientation satisfies all detailed-balance equations. The stationary covariance and mean-average variance then follow by explicit arithmetic. An independent exact-rational enumeration in the supplied `verify.py` checks every two-coordinate state through eleven noise-resolution levels and all three-coordinate parity classes through six levels (11,056 assertions). Finite checks corroborate, but do not replace, the proof. Critical caveat: unique stationary law is not ordinary-time convergence because of period two.

## Originality — PASS (historical priority provisional)
The signSGD defining paper (Bernstein et al., arXiv:1802.04434, full paper inspected), the sign-gradient geometry paper (Balles et al., arXiv:2002.08056, full HTML inspected), and the public scalar additive-noise Ehrenfest result (full result inspected) do not contain the particular multidimensional common-noise coupling and its full covariance consequences. The scalar marginal binomial result and Markov-chain pure-death/coupling tools are *not* new. The searched literature on Ehrenfest and common-random-number couplings did not reveal an exactly equivalent multivariate theorem. Standard Markov-chain material from Porod's text (sections 11.2.4 and 11.3 inspected) establishes adjacent background machinery but not this exact simultaneous-sign threshold dynamics. Residual risk: an older generic monotone coupling of the Ehrenfest chain might implicitly imply portions of the theorem; this remains a historical-priority limitation. No claim of established absence from all literature is made.

## Value — PASS
The relevant phenomenon is algorithmic, not a random low-dimensional arithmetic exercise: a perfectly shared noise source can turn a coordinate-separable optimization into almost-sure near-consensus, reducing an arbitrary-dimensional process to at most two parity clusters. The exact coalescence-time law provides an interpretable finite-time scale, and the invariant covariance gives an exact, dimension-independent variance floor for averaging nominally separate coordinates. This identifies a concrete consequence of using a common noisy minibatch signal versus independent coordinate perturbations. The result is intentionally limited to the precisely specified isotropic lattice model; it does not establish conclusions for general noisy training or practical data distributions.
