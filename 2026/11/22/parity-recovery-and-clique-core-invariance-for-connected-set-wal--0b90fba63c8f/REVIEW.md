# Review of connected-set Walsh convolution on complete split graphs

## Correctness
**PASS.** Every nonempty connected vertex set of \(K_s\vee\overline{K_m}\) either contains a core vertex or is exactly one independent-side singleton. Splitting the signed sum gives the stated two-term Walsh multiplier. The \(k=0\) exception and all endpoints \(s\ge1,m\ge2\) were checked. The null locus, exact rank, parity-specific spectral gap and clique-core invariance follow with no generic-position assumptions. Translation averaging proves minimum norm over *all* real or complex projections; the unique invariant projection has the explicitly evaluated central-Krawtchouk complement kernel. An exhaustive exact checker independently enumerates connected masks, transforms them, and checks 44,936 characters; 16 all-size combinatorial Krawtchouk samples supplement an algebraic proof valid for every dimension. Computations are corroboration, not the infinite proof.

## Originality
**PASS, qualified by the recorded priority risks.** Jerrum–Meeks and Liao–Hou concern counting connected induced subgraphs and the subgraph-component polynomial, not the signed Walsh convolution operator. Polyanskiy studies spherical averaging and homogeneous Fourier layer operators, not the connected-set recovery dichotomy. Defant et al. establish the general Fourier minimum-projection identity and analyze different spectral families; that principle is explicitly credited, and their theorem does not imply the new exact split-graph zero set without a separate computation. published-finding corpus comparisons searched connected vertex-set Fourier transforms, star and split graph aliases, central Hamming layers, and exact binomial endpoints. The nearest indexed findings concern *independent sets* of clique coronas or windmills and *spanning trees* of bipartite graphs, mathematically distinct indicator families. An unindexed equivalent graph-specific result remains possible.

## Value
**PASS.** Complete split graphs are a natural two-parameter family interpolating between stars and graphs with arbitrarily large universal clique cores. The result describes exactly which connected-set measurements are blind to which Fourier characters; hub-clique growth leaves the lost-mode geometry and optimal endpoint amplification unchanged. At even leaf sizes the lost fraction decays like an inverse square root, but the globally minimal endpoint projection norm grows exponentially, whereas odd sizes permit complete recovery. The complete algebraic structure and sharp constants address a natural recovery/stability invariant, beyond a routine table of small orders.

## Scope and limitations
The theorem does not classify arbitrary split graphs, and the norm is a range-projection norm, not a general inverse norm. Exact connectivity characterization, Krawtchouk duality and translation averaging are standard inputs, not claimed innovations. Literature comparison is bounded and an independent audit has not been performed.
