# Scientific review: two-edge spider sink polynomials

## Correctness — PASS

Every edge orientation of a tree is acyclic. Conditioning on the direction of each hub edge gives the peripheral-sink factor \(1+3z\) per two-edge arm; the all-inward hub orientations require the correction \((z-1)(1+z)^m\). Expansion gives exact nonnegative integer coefficients and a simple zero at the origin. The proof turns each negative-root question into a strictly monotone scalar equation on the disjoint intervals \((1/3,1)\) and \((1,\infty)\), with parity controlling whether the first interval is feasible. Its derivative estimates, endpoint limits, and uniform asymptotic bounds establish all-order root counts, simplicity, and the two limits. The independent edge-by-edge census matches all \(21,844\) orientations across \(m=1,\ldots,7\); those checks are supporting evidence, not an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, provisional historical priority

Alexandersson–Maia Leite (arXiv:2610.41502v1, Proposition 2.4 and Theorems 3.1 and 4.3) provides a general sink-set expansion and claw-free real-rootedness results; two-edge spiders with at least three arms are not claw-free, and their real-root census is not stated there. Hwang–Jung–Lee–Oh–Yu (arXiv:1910.10920) gives the foundational acyclic-orientation polynomial framework, not these zero counts. The nearest examined published examples are square bouquets and clique bouquets. Their different underlying block graphs and factor polynomials do not logically provide a theorem for two-edge-arm trees. Searches using aliases, the explicit polynomial, and the parity/real-root statement found no directly covering record. Unindexed or unexamined historical material could still cover the result; priority is not independently certified.

## Value — PASS

The October 2026 work gives strong real-rootedness theorems under claw-free hypotheses. The natural claw-containing depth-two spider family exhibits a sharp exact boundary: only its two path cases are real-rooted, while every larger member has a degree-growing polynomial with only two or three simple real zeros. The complete negative-root intervals and exponential escape rate \(y_m\sim3^m\) describe mathematically meaningful root geometry not supplied by an orientation count alone. The classification applies to infinitely many finite trees, without an artificial parameter cutoff.

## Closest sources and limitations

The cited full-text sources are arXiv:2610.41502v1 and arXiv:1910.10920, together with the published square-bouquet and clique-bouquet results. The classification does not extend to arbitrary arm lengths, and no location theorem is asserted for the nonreal roots. Finite verification is not independent mathematical audit. Historical priority is best-knowledge rather than exhaustive.
