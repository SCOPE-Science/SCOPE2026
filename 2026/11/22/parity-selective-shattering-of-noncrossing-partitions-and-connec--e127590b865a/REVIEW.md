# Scientific review

## Correctness

PASS. The canonical positive-chord closure groups endpoints by crossing connectivity, with all negative endpoints singleton. Its block count is exactly \(2t+r\). With a negative query present, at least \(2t\) cyclic block-boundary transitions exist but at most \(t\) can be forbidden negative matching edges; an adjacent-block merger therefore toggles parity without changing the trace. The only obstruction is the all-positive connected-crossing matching, which forces the one-block partition. A spanning-tree parity obstruction and a boundary-path-with-free-vertex construction prove the exact VC value. The standalone exhaustive trace checker confirms every matching through eight points and the six-point face-count contrast.

## Originality

PASS, with bounded historical priority. The closest unrestricted noncrossing-partition theorem has no block-parity classification; connected-chord literature enumerates crossing-connected matchings but does not determine shattering by odd/even block-count strata. Fixed-block and fixed-rank partition results do not supply the parity-switch argument. Primary sources on rank-refined noncrossing partitions and connected chord diagrams were checked at accessible levels, together with the full 2024 paper on chord intersections and the published unrestricted result. A 2016 connected-chord paper and 2019 rank-enumeration preprint could harbor undiscovered equivalent formulations; neither abstract supports this exact learning-theory claim. Historical uniqueness is not asserted.

## Value

PASS. This is an all-parameter structural classification of a natural, highly symmetric support layer for two equinumerous rank-parity subclasses of a Catalan lattice. It identifies the exact missing-face sequence with the connected chord diagrams studied independently in enumerative combinatorics. The six-point equality of concept counts but inequality of three-face counts illustrates an actual structural distinction; the result is not a recomputation of the Narayana numbers or an arbitrary isolated support sample.

## Limitations

The theorem does not give the full parity-restricted shattering polynomial, and its matching classification is restricted to supports that are perfect matchings. The complete three-query parity count is verified only for six points. Source coverage is substantial but not exhaustive, and an independent mathematical audit has not been performed.
