# Parity-selective spectral relaxation of Markov-label riffle shuffles

## Finding

Consider a random permutation \(\Pi_n\) generated by sorting a stationary symmetric binary Markov word \(B_1,\ldots,B_n\) stably, placing all zeros before all ones. This is the inverse form of the Markov-label riffle shuffle. Put \(p=\Pr(B_{i+1}=B_i)\), \(q=1-p\), and \(\rho_n(p)=\mathbb E[\operatorname{sgn}(\Pi_n)]\). For every integer \(n\ge1\) and \(0\le p\le1\), its exact sign character satisfies

\[
\rho_{n+4}=\frac{(1+p)^2}{2}\rho_{n+2}-p^2\rho_n,
\]

with initial data \(\rho_1=1\), \(\rho_2=(1+p)/2\), \(\rho_3=(1+2p-p^2)/2\), and \(\rho_4=(1+3p-p^2+p^3)/4\). In particular, for each fixed integer \(m\ge1\) and \(q\downarrow0\),

\[
\rho_{2m}(1-q)=1-\frac{m}{2}q+O_m(q^2),\qquad
\rho_{2m+1}(1-q)=1-\frac{m(m+1)}4q^2+O_m(q^3).
\]

Thus the first nontrivial decay of the alternating-sign representation is **linear in adhesion loss for even decks but quadratic for odd decks**. For every fixed \(n\ge2\), the sign observable after \(t\) independent shuffles has expectation \(\rho_n(p)^t\). Consequently, for each \(s>0\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\lim_{q\downarrow0}\mathbb E[\operatorname{sgn}(\Pi_{2m}^{(\lfloor s/q\rfloor)})]&=e^{-ms/2},\\
\lim_{q\downarrow0}\mathbb E[\operatorname{sgn}(\Pi_{2m+1}^{(\lfloor s/q^2\rfloor)})]&=e^{-m(m+1)s/4}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The difference is also visible when the deck grows and \(nq\to\xi\in[0,\infty)\). Define \(a=1-1/\sqrt2\), \(b=1+1/\sqrt2\). Along even deck sizes,

\[
\rho_n(1-q)\longrightarrow
\left(\frac12+\frac1{2\sqrt2}\right)e^{-a\xi/2}+
\left(\frac12-\frac1{2\sqrt2}\right)e^{-b\xi/2};
\]

along odd deck sizes,

\[
\rho_n(1-q)\longrightarrow
\left(\frac12+\frac1{\sqrt2}\right)e^{-a\xi/2}+
\left(\frac12-\frac1{\sqrt2}\right)e^{-b\xi/2}.
\]

The convergence holds along all subsequences with the indicated parity and scaling. In particular, the odd profile has zero first derivative at \(\xi=0\), whereas the even profile has first derivative \(-1/4\).

## Assumptions and scope

The initial bit is fair, and conditional on the current bit, the next equals it with probability \(p\) and differs with probability \(q\). The stable sorting convention means that the inversion number of \(\Pi_n\) is exactly \(I_n=\sum_{i<j}\mathbf1_{\{B_i=1,B_j=0\}}\), hence \(\operatorname{sgn}(\Pi_n)=(-1)^{I_n}\). The labels are independently regenerated at successive shuffle rounds. Results concern all integers \(n\ge2\); \(n=1\) appears only in the recurrence initialization. Error constants in the fixed-deck expansions may depend on \(n\). For the growing-deck limit, \(q\in[0,1]\) may depend on \(n\) with \(nq\to\xi\); the endpoints are included by continuity.

## Proof

**Exact signed transfer.** Track the pair \((b,z)\), where \(b\) is the most recent bit and \(z\in\{0,1\}\) is the parity of the number of ones already observed. Appending a one flips \(z\) but contributes no new inversions; appending a zero leaves \(z\) unchanged and multiplies the accumulated inversion sign by \((-1)^z\). In the ordered basis \((0,0),(0,1),(1,0),(1,1)\), the signed transition matrix is

\[
T=\begin{pmatrix}
(1+p)/2&0&0&(1-p)/2\\
0&-(1+p)/2&(1-p)/2&0\\
(1-p)/2&0&0&(1+p)/2\\
0&-(1-p)/2&(1+p)/2&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

With the initial row \(v=(1/2,0,0,1/2)\) and the all-ones column \(\mathbf1\), direct conditioning gives \(\rho_n=vT^{n-1}\mathbf1\). Elementary determinant expansion yields

\[
\det(\lambda I-T)=\lambda^4-\frac{(1+p)^2}{2}\lambda^2+p^2.
\]

Cayley–Hamilton proves the asserted recurrence. Computing the first four products proves all four initial conditions, including the boundary values \(p=0\) and \(p=1\).

**Rare-switch parity.** The number of changes in the binary word is exactly \(\operatorname{Bin}(n-1,q/2)\). Conditional on the change locations, the initial bit remains fair. With no changes, the sorted permutation is the identity. With one change after position \(k\), a word beginning in zero is already sorted, whereas one beginning in one has inversion count \(k(n-k)\). For even \(n=2m\), precisely the \(m\) odd values of \(k\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}\) create odd permutations in the latter case. Thus \(\Pr(\operatorname{sgn}\Pi_n=-1)=mq/4+O_m(q^2)\), yielding \(\rho_{2m}=1-mq/2+O_m(q^2)\).

For odd \(n=2m+1\), \(k(n-k)\) is always even, so a single label change never gives an odd permutation. With exactly two changes, after positions \(1\le k<\ell\le n-1\), an initially zero word has inversion number \((\ell-k)(n-\ell)\), and an initially one word has inversion number \(k(\ell-k)\). Since \(n\) is odd, either is odd precisely when \(k\) is odd and \(\ell\) is even. There are \(\sum_{j=1}^{m}j=m(m+1)/2\) such pairs. Their total probability is \(m(m+1)q^2/8+O_m(q^3)\); three or more changes contribute only \(O_m(q^3)\). Hence \(\rho_{2m+1}=1-m(m+1)q^2/4+O_m(q^3)\).

**Repeated shuffles.** The sign is a multiplicative character of the symmetric group. Since shuffle increments are independent, the expected sign after \(t\) rounds is exactly \(\rho_n^t\). The expansions and \((1-cq^j+o(q^j))^{\lfloor s/q^j\rfloor}\to e^{-cs}\) establish the two fixed-deck relaxation laws. Because a uniform permutation on \(S_n\), \(n\ge2\), has mean sign zero, its total-variation distance from the \(t\)-step shuffle satisfies the rigorous but generally nonsharp bound \(d_{\mathrm{TV}}(t)\ge|\rho_n|^t/2\).

**Growing-deck crossover.** The two distinct roots for the subsequence recurrence are

\[
r_\pm(p)=\frac{(1+p)^2\pm(1-p)\sqrt{p^2+6p+1}}4.
\]

For \(q=1-p\downarrow0\), these satisfy \(r_+=1-aq+O(q^2)\), \(r_-=1-bq+O(q^2)\), with \(a=1-1/\sqrt2\), \(b=1+1/\sqrt2\). For even \(n=2m\), solve the two-root recurrence using the values \(\rho_2,\rho_4\); the coefficients of \(r_+^{m-1},r_-^{m-1}\) converge respectively to \(1/2+1/(2\sqrt2)\) and \(1/2-1/(2\sqrt2)\). For odd \(n=2m+1\), the coefficients of \(r_+^m,r_-^m\), solved using \(\rho_1,\rho_3\), converge respectively to \(1/2+1/\sqrt2\) and \(1/2-1/\sqrt2\). As \(mq\to\xi/2\), powers converge to \(e^{-a\xi/2}\) and \(e^{-b\xi/2}\). At \(q=0\), both limits equal one and extend continuously. This proves both profiles with no unproved limit interchange.

## Relationship to prior work

Jiang's 2026 paper defines the four adhesive shuffle families and proves fixed-size mixing-time scales \(q^{-1}\) for the three cut-first shuffles and \(q^{-2}\) for the Markov-label shuffle. Those full-deck results are **not** claimed anew here. Jonasson and Morris proved polylogarithmic mixing for fixed positive switching probability in the related Markovian riffle model. Palomares reported finite-deck spectral computations for the equivalent geometric model, but no all-size sign character law in the inspected analysis. The published three-scale inversion study describes inversion-count weak limits and zero-inversion probabilities; those do not determine oscillatory sign expectations because parity is not a continuous function of the normalized inversion statistic. The present result identifies the exact alternating-sign Fourier coefficient and an even/odd contrast in its strong-adhesion relaxation.

## Limitations

The sign representation is one group character, not the full permutation spectrum. The even-deck \(q^{-1}\) sign relaxation does not contradict full-deck \(q^{-2}\) mixing, and no cutoff or sharp total-variation profile is asserted. The large-deck crossover fixes \(nq\) and is not a joint time-and-size mixing theorem. Published or unpublished special-character calculations not located in the inspected material could imply the parity formula; mathematical priority remains subject to independent checking. The mathematical result and the finite checker were assessed without independent validation.
