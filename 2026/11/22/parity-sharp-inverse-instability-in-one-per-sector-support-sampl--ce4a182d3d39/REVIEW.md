# Scientific review

## Correctness: PASS

The sine-coordinate interpolation is exact for open vertex normal cones. A cyclic-bidiagonal determinant has two and only two terms; the sign differs by parity. The even singular set is regular because every partial derivative of the determinant is negative there, and the matrix has rank exactly one less than full. Perturbations of a regular polygon along its kernel remain geometrically admissible for small parameters. On compact angular support, coarea gives a sharp positive \(t^{-1}\) inverse-norm tail with the stated surface-integral constant, hence the moment and squared-error conclusions. The augmented row eliminates the sole singular direction. The supplied numeric checker independently checks finite determinant and kernel cases, but finite data are not offered as proof of the infinite statements.

## Originality: PASS (historical priority provisional)

The foundational 1992 interpolation equation and 2022 rank framework are expressly treated as antecedents. In particular, Dostert–Jochemko Example 3.4 already contains the equal-midsector hexagon's alternating null vector; the present result does not claim that example. The new checked claim combines an all-order parity criterion, a smooth nonidentifiable design hypersurface, a sharp random-design inverse-norm tail coefficient, exact moment threshold, and its statistical consequence. The nearby 1990 and 1992 reconstruction articles address constrained noise-aware algorithms and interpolation, respectively; the 2022 article establishes generic identifiability and many-sample convergence, not the one-per-sector random inverse-risk law. A relevant earlier unindexed treatment could still contain an equivalent risk asymptotic.

## Mathematical value: PASS

Exactly one support measurement per cone is the natural minimal design in a fixed-fan polygon inverse problem. The sharp contrast between almost-sure identifiability and infinite unconditional mean-squared error, controlled solely by parity under interior random sampling, is a substantive stability boundary. The exact coefficient permits quantitative analysis of design laws and the single calibrated facet measurement gives a provably robust remedy. The result is not merely the known hexagonal rank-deficiency example, nor an arbitrary finite computation.

## Scope and residual risks

The conclusion about error applies to the unconstrained linear support-parameter inverse, not to the convexity-constrained estimator of prior literature. The sampled directions occupy prescribed sectors with an independent compactly supported smooth density; more general models require separate work. The surface-integral tail coefficient is exact but is not evaluated in closed elementary form. An unpublished or poorly indexed equivalent may exist.
