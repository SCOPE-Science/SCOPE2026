# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The zonogon support-width formula is exact. Between edge-normal breakpoints its positive sinusoidal profile is strictly concave, so the minimum occurs at one of the explicitly calculated facet normals. Strict sine concavity and the cyclic-gap sum give the optimal global width with uniqueness. The active-constraint derivative formula follows by differentiating finitely many smooth candidate widths. The circulant Fourier eigenvalues vanish only in the constant mode, and additionally in the alternating mode for even half-side count. A direct pairing of odd sine increments yields the exact alternating-family width deficit. Boundary cases include the four-sided case and all permitted strict convexity angle ranges. The standalone numerical checker passed 9,317 comparisons; its finite scope is not substituted for proof.

**Originality — PASS, provisional priority.** The global Jensen step belongs to the classical toolbox and is not claimed as a standalone original theorem. Audet–Hansen–Messine (2009), and Fejes Tóth (2022), address broader polygonal width optimization, with different sharp constants and no asserted nonsmooth tangent-spectrum classification in the inspected scientific text. An accessible full theorem on equiangular polygons controls Wigner-corrected area deficit under fixed normal directions; another on equal angular vertex rays proves area rigidity, not variable-edge-angle width stability. Neither statement implies the parity-dependent nullspace and explicit nonlinear quadratic loss for equilateral zonogons. Search noncoverage is not definitive proof of priority. Older classical Fourier or zonogon stability literature could contain an equivalent statement not located in the inspected sources.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** Width is a central geometric invariant in the finite isoperimetric literature. The theorem supplies a sharp exact optimizer in a natural nontrivial constrained class, then distinguishes two regimes for every even side count: linear response in every nonrotational tangent when the half-side count is odd, versus precisely one nontrivial neutral tangent and a fully integrable quadratic loss when it is even. This identifies a structural singularity rather than a renaming or a single numeric case, while explicitly not claiming to resolve the unrestricted open problem.

Messine, *Isoperimetric Polygons of Maximum Width* (2009); C. Audet and J. Ninin, *Maximal perimeter, diameter and area of equilateral unit-width convex polygons* (2010). Compared published results on equiangular parallel-opposite polygons and on equilateral radial-grid polygons have different hypotheses and conclusions. An equivalent classical stability theorem may be unindexed. No claim of independent assessment is made.
