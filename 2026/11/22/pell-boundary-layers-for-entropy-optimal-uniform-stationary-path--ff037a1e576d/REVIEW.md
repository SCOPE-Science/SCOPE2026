The optimum exists and is unique by compactness and strict entropy concavity. Interpolating toward a supported-interior stochastic matrix rules out zero permitted entries. ## Originality — PASS on inspected evidence, with priority qualifications

The explicit half-line sequence and its limiting surface entropy constant are not stated in the relevant reviewed published treatments. Van der Straeten (2009) gives general maximum-entropy equations for reversible chains but assumes positive transitions between all states and studies spin models. Chliamovitch–Dupuis–Chopard (2015) develops two- and three-state inference and numerical entropy estimation. Diaconis–Wood (2012) studies *uniformly sampled matrices* from the tridiagonal doubly stochastic polytope, not entropy maximizers. Boyd–Diaconis–Sun–Xiao (2006) addresses spectral mixing, a different optimization objective. The later Bolzern–Colaneri–De Nicolao (2026) abstract describes general Luce-score characterizations but does not state the rational-Pell half-line limit or boundary entropy correction. General stationary-score equations can motivate the calculation but do not by themselves supply this explicit boundary classification.

Access limitations: the Diaconis–Wood full paper was not available via its journal page or the attempted institutional route, so its comparison rests on the abstract and bibliographic contents; the later 2026 item was inspected only through its abstract. Other equivalent matrix-scaling treatments might exist outside the searched literature. Publication priority therefore remains provisional.

## Value — PASS

Uniform-stationary birth–death transitions supported on paths are a canonical maximum-entropy design problem. Their entropy-optimal transitions exhibit persistent, alternating near-boundary departures from the uniform bulk probabilities even though the bulk converges rapidly. The exact first three edge probabilities and the positive, computable \(1/n\) entropy loss quantify this boundary effect for *all* path lengths in the limiting regime; the result distinguishes maximum entropy from the better-known spectral fastest-mixing design. This is a structural asymptotic classification, not a small numerical improvement or arbitrary finite table.
