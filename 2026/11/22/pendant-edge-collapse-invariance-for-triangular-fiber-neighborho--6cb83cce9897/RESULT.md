# Pendant-edge collapse invariance for triangular-fiber neighborhood complexes

## Finding
For every finite simple undirected graph \(H\), and every degree-one vertex \(w\) with unique neighbor \(v\), the ordinary **open** Lovász neighborhood complex satisfies the explicit elementary simplicial collapse
\[
\mathcal N(K_3\square H)\ \searrow\ \mathcal N\bigl(K_3\square(H-w)\bigr).
\]
The smaller complex is understood as the induced subcomplex on the three-color fibers over \(V(H)\setminus\{w\}\); thus the statement specifies a genuine collapse, not just an abstract homotopy equivalence. Writing \(d=\deg_{H-w}(v)\), the collapse uses exactly
\[
3+3\cdot 2^{d+1}
\]
elementary free-face pairs, all of which are described below. In particular, grafting or pruning any finite pendant tree preserves the simple-homotopy type and all integral homology and cohomology operations of \(\mathcal N(K_3\square H)\). Every nonempty tree \(T\) satisfies
\[
\mathcal N(K_3\square T)\ \searrow\ \partial\Delta^2\ \cong S^1.
\]
For a graph obtained by attaching arbitrary trees to a cycle \(C_n\) with \(n\ge5\), the established triangular-cycle calculation combines with the present collapse to give \(\mathcal N(K_3\square H)\simeq S^1\times S^1\), retaining its nonzero integral cup product. The cycle type itself is existing work [3], not a newly computed result.

## Assumptions and scope
A finite simple undirected graph is allowed to be disconnected. The product \(K_3\square H\) has vertices \((i,u)\), where \(i\in\{0,1,2\}\) and \(u\in V(H)\). Two vertices are adjacent exactly when either their base vertices agree and their colors differ, or their colors agree and their base vertices are adjacent. The *open neighborhood* of a vertex consists of its adjacent vertices, not the vertex itself. The complex \(\mathcal N(G)\) is generated by simplices on the open neighborhoods of all vertices of \(G\).

Fix a leaf \(w\) and its unique neighbor \(v\). Put \(H_0=H-w\), \(x_i=(i,w)\), \(y_i=(i,v)\), and \(Z_i=\{(i,z):z\in N_{H_0}(v)\}\). Set
\[
L_i=\{y_j:j\ne i\}\cup Z_i,\qquad |L_i|=d+2.
\]
Each \(L_i\) is the open neighborhood of \(y_i\) in \(K_3\square H_0\), and hence is a simplex of the smaller neighborhood complex. The larger complex has the smaller as an actual subcomplex. No planarity, girth, connectedness, degree bound, or triangle-free hypothesis is imposed on \(H\).

## Proof
**1. Classify every new face.** Every face containing any vertex \(x_i\) is a subset of at least one of exactly six neighborhood simplices:
\[
A_i=\{x_j:j\ne i\}\cup\{y_i\},\qquad
B_i=\{x_i\}\cup L_i\quad (i=0,1,2).
\]
Indeed, a common neighbor of a face containing an \(x_i\) must be adjacent to that vertex; the only such centers are the other two vertices in the leaf's triangular fiber or \(y_i\). The neighborhood centered at the leaf-color vertex \(x_i\) is precisely \(A_i\), and the neighborhood centered at \(y_i\) is precisely \(B_i\). Every face avoiding all three \(x_i\) already occurs in \(\mathcal N(K_3\square H_0)\): centers in the old graph contribute their old-neighborhood faces, and a center at \(w\) contributes at most one old vertex. Thus these six simplices describe all new faces and no old face is lost.

**2. Three triangular free-face collapses.** For each \(i\), let \(\{j,k\}=\{0,1,2\}\setminus\{i\}\). The edge \(\{x_j,x_k\}\) is contained in the unique maximal simplex \(A_i=\{x_j,x_k,y_i\}\): no \(B_\ell\) contains two leaf-fiber vertices, while among the \(A_\ell\) only \(A_i\) contains that pair. Therefore it is a free edge, and the pair \((\{x_j,x_k\},A_i)\) is an elementary collapse. Execute these three pairs in any order. All remaining faces involving a new vertex now lie in one of the cones \(B_i\). In particular the surviving edges \(\{x_j,y_i\}\) from an \(A_i\) are also edges in \(B_j\), because \(i\ne j\).

**3. Collapse each remaining cone relative to its base.** For each \(i\), choose one of the two old vertices in \(L_i\) having color different from \(i\), and call it \(h_i\). For every \(S\subseteq L_i\setminus\{h_i\}\), pair
\[
\{x_i\}\cup S\quad\text{with}\quad\{x_i,h_i\}\cup S.
\]
Within a fixed \(i\), process these pairs in descending order of \(|S|\). Each lower face then has exactly one remaining coface: any other coface involving \(x_i\) must lie in the same simplex \(B_i\), and every extension using another vertex of \(L_i\) has already been removed. There are no residual faces with \(x_i\) outside \(B_i\) by Step 2, and the pairs for different \(i\) have disjoint new-vertex supports. Each pair is consequently an elementary free-face collapse. There are exactly \(2^{|L_i|-1}=2^{d+1}\) such pairs for each \(i\), and every face involving the leaf's fiber disappears.

The resulting complex is exactly \(\mathcal N(K_3\square H_0)\), proving the collapse and its exact count \(3+3\cdot2^{d+1}\).

**4. Consequences.** Repeated leaf deletion preserves a complex by an actual sequence of simplicial collapses. Every finite nonempty tree can be reduced to one vertex without removing that final vertex. Its product with the three-vertex complete graph is \(K_3\), whose open-neighborhood complex is precisely the triangle boundary. Graphs formed by attaching finite trees to an arbitrary base can be pruned back to that base, leaving the neighborhood complex unchanged up to simple homotopy. For a cycle core of length at least five, the torus conclusion follows from the published cycle calculation [3], together with the new pruning theorem.

## Verification
The standard-library Python script `verify_leaf_collapse.py` independently constructs each neighborhood complex from graph adjacency, enumerates every face, and checks the exact elementary free-face condition for **every single prescribed collapse**. It verifies final equality with the target complex, not only a comparison of Betti numbers. Fixed-seed tests cover 320 graph-and-leaf cases with path, cycle, clique, and random base cores up to eight old vertices, for a total of 22,080 checked free-face pairs; the largest original complex has 11,305 faces. A separate reduced mod-two chain computation checks eleven paths. A four-color boundary test confirms \(\widetilde H_2(\mathcal N(K_4))\cong\mathbb Z\) versus rank seven over \(\mathbb F_2\) for \(\widetilde H_2(\mathcal N(K_4\square K_2))\), so the analogous four-color leaf invariance does not hold. The finite checks are corroboration, not the proof of the universal assertion. Run `python3 verify_leaf_collapse.py` alongside this document; `verification_output.txt` records the actual output.

## Relationship to prior work
The open-neighborhood construction, graph folding, and basic homotopy methods were developed in the literature [1,2]. Shukla [2] provides broad results for maximum-degree-at-most-three graphs and four-regular circulants, but neither the all-graph triangular-product pruning theorem nor a relative explicit collapse count is stated there. In particular arbitrary base graphs here can have unbounded degree.

The published open-neighborhood classification of \(K_m\square H\) for \(m\ge4\) and short-cycle-free \(H\) [4] does **not** imply the present \(m=3\) theorem; indeed its calculation for \(m=4\) already distinguishes a one-vertex base and an edge base. The published \(K_3\square C_n\) torus theorem [3] computes the cycle core but does not treat arbitrary trees attached to it. The new result is the exact graph-operation collapse under leaf addition, not a re-claim of those previously published homotopy types. Equivalence to a broader, unindexed graph-homotopy result remains a bibliographic risk.

## Limitations
The theorem concerns the ordinary **open** neighborhood complex with a triangle factor; no claim is made for closed neighborhoods, a categorical rather than Cartesian product, deletion of a base vertex of degree greater than one, or an arbitrary complete-graph factor. The actual collapse proof works for all finite simple base graphs and does not rely on finite experiments. It does not classify the neighborhood homotopy type for arbitrary leafless cores: their topology must be determined separately. Novelty screening, however extensive, cannot establish global priority, and no independent scientific audit has been performed.
