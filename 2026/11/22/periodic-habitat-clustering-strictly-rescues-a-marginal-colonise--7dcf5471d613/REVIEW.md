# Scientific assessment: periodic habitat forcing and coloniser invasion

## Correctness — PASS

The resident's reciprocal ratio obeys an affine linear periodic equation with a contracting period map whenever \(K\overline b>\mu\). Two independently reconstructed, exact logarithmic derivative identities yield the closed invasion exponent and its strict arithmetic-minus-harmonic mean gain. When the exponent is positive, the coloniser's reciprocal logistic equation has a unique positive periodic solution. Exponential convergence of the resident gives attraction in the full physical state space. A rational habitat-autocorrelation family realizes the constant-forcing marginal-invasion boundary exactly; perturbation algebra establishes its \(3\eta^2/340\) onset coefficient. The independently executable `check.py` verifies exact parameters and selected numerical period integrals. Numerical quadrature is corroboration rather than a universal proof.

## Originality — PASS, with bounded historical-priority risk

The 2026 starting paper supplies the static two-species model, its landing factors and equilibrium conditions; its main methods and results do not allow time-periodic habitat autocorrelation. Peniston et al. already explain lower mean resident occupancy under seasonal colonisation in a single-species Levins model, and that general effect is **not new**. Burkart et al. already establish seasonally enabled coexistence in a periodically driven chemostat; that general phenomenon is **not new**. What is compared here is the precise two-species invasion identity, its strict sign under every nonconstant positive waveform at equal effective-competition mean, and existence/attraction of coexistence at the exact static threshold for the 2026 hierarchical habitat-clustering model. Closest accessible comparisons do not state this object-level theorem or a stronger result covering it. Classical competition-colonisation results may contain an unindexed equivalent proof; absolute priority is not established.

## Value — PASS

The originating model explicitly treats habitat autocorrelation as an ecological control on persistence but evaluates static values. A changing habitat clustering pattern is a motivated extension. The exact formula proves a directional change in an *invasion threshold*, not merely a change in resident mean density: a strictly excluded-at-threshold coloniser persists on a unique positive seasonal orbit for every nonzero admissible perturbation. The sign is independent of forcing frequency and shape, and the sharp small-amplitude prefactor quantifies sensitivity. These facts delimit when temporally changing landscape structure can support coexistence even though its mean static structure cannot.

## Scope and risks

The inferior species must have globally dispersed propagules (constant effective colonisation), the resident's mean colonisation must permit persistence, and the model is deterministic. The result does not generalize automatically to seasonally varying coloniser rates, individual-based spatial randomness or arbitrary exogenous disturbances. A close generic occupancy principle is longstanding; specialist unindexed prior work remains the main originality risk.
