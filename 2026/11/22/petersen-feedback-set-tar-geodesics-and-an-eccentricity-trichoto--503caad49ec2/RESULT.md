# Petersen feedback-set TAR geodesics and an eccentricity trichotomy

## Finding
Let \(P=\operatorname{KG}(5,2)\) be the Petersen graph: its ten vertices are the two-element subsets of \([5]\), and distinct vertices are adjacent when the corresponding pairs are disjoint. A *feedback vertex set* (FVS) is a vertex set \(S\) for which \(P-S\) is a forest. For an integer budget \(b\), let \(\mathcal R_b(P)\) have as vertices all FVSs of cardinality at most \(b\), with an edge when exactly one graph vertex is added or removed. Then:

1. The minimum FVS cardinality is three. The twenty minimum FVSs are exactly the three-edge stars on the auxiliary five-point complete graph. Their fixed-cardinality single-exchange graph is \(5K_4\), a previously established fact.
2. The budget-four graph has \(160\) states and \(140\) transitions, and its components are \(5\) components of order \(29\) together with \(15\) isolated vertices. The fifteen isolated states are exactly the four-edge cycles on four auxiliary ground points.
3. The budget-five graph has \(400\) states, \(980\) transitions, and is connected. For **every** pair \(S,T\) of minimum FVSs, its shortest-path metric is precisely
   \[
   d_{\mathcal R_5(P)}(S,T)=|S\triangle T|=2(3-|S\cap T|).
   \]
   Its \(190\) unordered pairs of minimum FVSs have distance distribution \(30\) at distance two, \(90\) at distance four, and \(70\) at distance six. Thus the smallest budget allowing transformations between every pair of minimum FVSs is five, and at that budget all such paths can attain the symmetric-difference lower bound without a detour.
4. For the complete budget-five reconfiguration graph, the eccentricity of a state depends **only** on its cardinality: eight for all \(20\) size-three states, nine for all \(140\) size-four states, and ten for all \(240\) size-five states. Hence its radius is eight, its diameter ten, and exactly \(120\) unordered state pairs attain the diameter. These last finite metric assertions are established by an exhaustive reproducible enumeration and two distinct graph implementations, as specified below.

The central contributions are the exact budget-four component profile, the geodesic metric between optimal deletion sets at budget five, and the complete budget-five eccentricity profile; the optimum-set classification, fixed-cardinality optimum exchange components, and general existence of a budget-five connection have antecedents expressly distinguished under prior work.

## Assumptions and scope
The vertex ground set \([5]=\{1,2,3,4,5\}\) is labelled. An FVS is a set of vertices of \(P\), each vertex itself being a two-subset of \([5]\). Equivalently, represent a candidate FVS as an ordinary set \(S\) of edges of the auxiliary \(K_5\). All graphs are finite, simple and undirected. A move in \(\mathcal R_b(P)\) adds or removes exactly one vertex while preserving feedback-vertex-set feasibility and cardinality at most \(b\); it is neither a fixed-cardinality exchange nor a physical token slide. The graph includes all feasible states down to the true optimum cardinality. Distance means edge-distance in the named reconfiguration graph, and the eccentricity of a state is its maximum finite distance to any other state.

## Proof
**1. Minimum sets.** The Petersen graph has ten vertices, fifteen edges and degree three. For a deletion set \(S\) of size \(s\), write \(e_P(S)\) for the number of edges induced by \(S\) in \(P\). The remaining graph has \(10-s\) vertices and exactly
\[
15-3s+e_P(S)
\]
edges. For \(s\le2\), this is more than the maximum \(9-s\) edges in a forest on \(10-s\) vertices, so no FVS of size at most two exists. For \(s=3\), acyclicity forces \(e_P(S)=0\): the three auxiliary \(K_5\)-edges must pairwise intersect. Every such three-edge family is either a three-edge star or a triangle. If it is a star centered at \(x\), the remaining Petersen graph is a subdivided \(K_{1,3}\), with the one retained auxiliary edge through \(x\) at its center and the six edges among the other four labels in three pendant pairs. It is a tree. If the removed auxiliary edges form a triangle, the remaining Petersen graph contains a six-cycle (on the six auxiliary edges joining the triangle's three points to the two other points). Thus precisely the three-edge stars are minimum FVSs, in \(5\binom43=20\) choices. Two stars exchange just one Petersen vertex if and only if their centers agree; this yields \(5K_4\).

**2. Four-vertex deletion sets.** Any four-edge auxiliary set containing a three-edge star is feasible by monotonicity. For a fixed star center there are \(1+4\binom42=25\) such sets: the full four-edge star, or three chosen spokes and one of the six off-center edges. They are disjoint as families over the five possible centers, since four edges cannot contain three spokes at two different centers. There are therefore \(125\) of these sets.

Now suppose no three-edge star is contained. The auxiliary four-edge graph has maximum degree at most two. Up to isomorphism its only possibilities on five available labels are a five-vertex path, a four-cycle with an isolated fifth label, or a triangle disjoint from a single edge. The path and the disjoint triangle-plus-edge each contain three pairs of disjoint auxiliary edges. Consequently \(e_P(S)=3\), leaving \(3+e_P(S)=6\) edges on six remaining vertices, which cannot be a forest. For a four-cycle, exactly two pairs of auxiliary edges are disjoint. Its complement in the ten vertices of \(P\) consists of the two diagonals of the auxiliary quadrilateral and the four edges to the fifth point. Their induced Petersen subgraph is the six-vertex double-star (two adjacent three-valent centers, each with two leaves), hence is a tree. There are \(5\cdot3=15\) labelled auxiliary four-cycles. Thus there are exactly \(125+15=140\) feasible four-vertex deletion sets.

In \(\mathcal R_4(P)\), every three-edge star has exactly seven feasible four-edge supersets. Each four-edge set containing a minimum star has exactly one star center; the four three-edge stars of a fixed center and their \(25\) distinct four-edge supersets form a connected component of order \(29\). No such component contains another star center. The fifteen four-cycle states have no feasible three-edge subsets and are isolated. Hence the component multiset is \(\{29,29,29,29,29,1^{15}\}\), with \(20\cdot7=140\) edges.

**3. Five-vertex deletion sets and connectivity.** A five-edge auxiliary graph avoiding any three-edge star has maximum degree at most two, but its degree sum is ten across five labels, forcing every degree to be two. Therefore it is a five-cycle. The complementary five auxiliary edges are also a five-cycle, and under Kneser disjointness they induce a five-cycle in \(P\). Hence such a deletion set is not an FVS. Conversely every five-edge set containing a three-star is an FVS. Exactly \((5-1)!/2=12\) auxiliary five-cycles exist, so the number of feasible size-five sets is \(\binom{10}{5}-12=240\). Total states are \(20+140+240=400\). Every feasible four-set has six feasible size-five supersets, giving \(140+6\cdot140=980\) TAR edges.

Each feasible four-set containing a three-star connects downward to a three-star; each feasible five-set connects downward through a four-set containing a three-star. Every four-cycle can be extended by any one of the six remaining auxiliary edges to a feasible five-set containing a three-star. Finally for any distinct ground labels \(x,y\), choose three-spoke stars centered at \(x\) and \(y\), both including the auxiliary edge \(xy\). Their union has five edges, so a two-addition/two-removal path connects them. Stars sharing a center connect through their four-edge union. These connections link all five centers. It follows that \(\mathcal R_5(P)\) is connected.

**4. Exact shortest paths between minima.** Every TAR path from \(S\) to \(T\) must delete each vertex of \(S\setminus T\) and add each of \(T\setminus S\), so its length is at least \(|S\triangle T|\). Suppose \(S,T\) are minimum stars. If they have the same center, they differ in exactly one auxiliary edge and their union is a feasible four-set: distance two. If their centers differ and their stars share an edge, they share only the edge joining their two centers; their union has five edges, is feasible, and supplies a two-addition/two-removal path of length four.

It remains to consider disjoint stars with distinct centers \(x,y\). Among the three other ground labels, there are two distinct \(u,v\) for which \(xu,xv\in S\) and \(yu,yv\in T\). Write \(s\) for the third edge of \(S\) and \(t\) for the third edge of \(T\). All six edges are distinct. Then the following six legal steps convert \(S\) into \(T\):
\[
\begin{aligned}
S&\longrightarrow S\cup\{yu\}\longrightarrow S\cup\{yu,yv\}\\
&\longrightarrow \{xu,xv,yu,yv\}\\
&\longrightarrow \{xu,xv,yu,yv,t\}\\
&\longrightarrow \{xv,yu,yv,t\}\longrightarrow T.
\end{aligned}
\]
The initial added sets contain \(S\); the central four-edge set is an auxiliary four-cycle, hence a valid FVS; the subsequent sets contain \(T\). No set exceeds five edges. Thus the lower bound is always attained. Within a center there are \(5\binom42=30\) unordered pairs at distance two. For each of the \(\binom52=10\) pairs of distinct centers, exactly \(3^2=9\) star pairs both contain the edge joining the centers and hence are at distance four, giving \(90\). The remaining \(10(16-9)=70\) star pairs are disjoint and have distance six.

**5. Complete metric and eccentricity stratification.** These final fixed-graph assertions are verified by complete enumeration rather than by extrapolating small-parameter experiments. `verify_kneser.py` independently enumerates all \(638\) candidate deletion sets of size at most five in the ten-vertex graph, tests acyclicity with disjointness adjacency and union-find, constructs every valid addition/removal edge, and uses breadth-first search from each of the \(400\) valid budget-five states. It checks all \(\binom{400}{2}=79{,}800\) unordered distances and the exact eccentricity distribution. `verify_petersen.py` independently builds the standard wheel-and-spokes Petersen graph, uses a different forest predicate and shortest-path implementation, and reconstructs the same complete metric. Both source programs are embedded intact. Their assertions establish the eccentricity distribution stated above and show that exactly \(120\) pairs attain distance ten. The enumeration is finite and exhaustive for this fixed graph; it does not purport to prove an infinite-family metric statement.

## Verification
From a directory containing the two files, execute `python3 verify_kneser.py` and `python3 verify_petersen.py`. The first requires only Python 3's standard library. The second requires the `networkx` Python library; it generates the Petersen graph independently rather than importing data from the first verifier. Both successfully ran from the exact source files embedded in this finding. The asserted output includes the complete counts \(20,140,240\), the budget-four components \(5\times29+15\times1\), the budget-five \(400\) states and \(980\) edges, all minimum-pair distances \(30,90,70\), the size-dependent eccentricities \(8,9,10\), and \(120\) diameter pairs. A program's unsuccessful execution would invalidate its computational evidence rather than imply that a claimed configuration is impossible.

## Relationship to prior work
The previously published *Sharp induced-forest threshold and optimum-exchange components of two-subset Kneser graphs* already proves the minimum FVS classification of \(\operatorname{KG}(n,2)\), including its \(n=5\) case and fixed-size \(5K_4\) optimum exchange graph. None of those baseline facts is asserted here as new. Other publicly available Petersen induced-forest filtration work computes face counts and topological invariants, not TAR shortest paths or eccentricities; the baseline set counts may be independently recovered from its full face enumeration.

Bong, Carlson, Curtis, Haas and Hogben (2022) provide the general token-addition/removal framework and definitions, but their zero-forcing and other \(X\)-set theorems do not yield the displayed Petersen-specific metric. Bousquet, Hommelsheim, Kobayashi, Mühlenthaler and Suzuki (2023), Theorem 4, prove that all *nonminimum, fixed-cardinality* feedback vertex sets of a subcubic graph can be exchanged. Applied to the Petersen graph at cardinality four, their theorem **already implies that budget-five TAR is connected** (a four-set exchange may be simulated by one addition and one removal). Connectivity alone is therefore **not a new contribution**. Their available theorem statements, however, do not determine the budget-four \(29\)-state components and isolated four-cycles, the exact geodesic equality for every optimum pair, or the \(400\)-state eccentricity trichotomy. The detailed full paper was not accessible in this run and remains a residual source-inspection risk.

## Limitations
The theorem is specific to the labelled Petersen graph; no bound or formula is asserted for general cubic or pair-Kneser graphs. The radius, diameter and eccentricity counts are obtained by explicitly exhaustive, replayable finite computation; their evidence is the supplied enumerator and independent second implementation, not the prose output alone. The structural minimum-pair path theorem has an independent analytic proof. The 2023 full-text access limitation and possible unpublished alternative derivations preclude a claim of universal priority. No independent audit or external validation has been performed.
