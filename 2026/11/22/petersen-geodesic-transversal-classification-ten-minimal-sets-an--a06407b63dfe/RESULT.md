# Petersen geodesic-transversal classification: ten minimal sets and a three-spare connectivity threshold

## Finding
Let \(P=KG(5,2)\) be the Petersen graph, with vertices the ten two-element subsets of \([5]=\{1,2,3,4,5\}\); two vertices are adjacent when their two-subsets are disjoint. For \(v\in[5]\), let \(A_v\) be the four graph vertices corresponding to the edges incident with \(v\) in \(K_5\), and let \(B_v\) be the six graph vertices corresponding to the edges of the induced \(K_4\) on \([5]\setminus\{v\}\).

**Complete classification.** A subset \(S\subseteq V(P)\) meets every inclusion-maximal shortest path of \(P\) if and only if it contains some \(A_v\) or some \(B_v\). Its inclusion-minimal members are precisely the five \(A_v\) and five \(B_v\), in two orbits under the natural \(S_5\) action. Thus the minimum cardinality, previously established to be four, has exactly five solutions; five additional inclusion-minimal solutions have cardinality six. Equivalently, the simplicial complex of vertex subsets containing no maximal geodesic has ten facets, five of cardinality six and five of cardinality four, and is nonpure.

The full cardinality generating polynomial is
\[
T_P(z)=5z^4+30z^5+80z^6+90z^7+45z^8+10z^9+z^{10}.
\]
Equivalently, writing \(q_j=\binom{5-j}{2}\),
\[
T_P(z)=\sum_{j=1}^{5}(-1)^{j+1}\binom5j z^{10-q_j}(1+z)^{q_j}+5z^6.
\]
Hence exactly \(261\) vertex subsets are geodesic transversals.

**Reconfiguration.** Let \(\mathcal R_b\) have as vertices the geodesic transversals of size at most \(b\), with an edge when exactly one graph vertex is added or removed. At budgets \(b=4,5,6\), respectively, its component-size multisets are \(\{1^5\}\), \(\{7^5\}\), and \(\{22^5,1^5\}\). At \(b=7\) it is connected, with \(205\) vertices. Consequently, reconfiguring between arbitrary minimum four-vertex transversals requires and suffices to allow **three spare vertices**. The shortest distance between any distinct two minima in \(\mathcal R_7\) is exactly six elementary changes.

## Assumptions and scope
Graphs are finite, undirected, simple, unweighted. A geodesic is a shortest vertex path; it is *maximal* if not contained as a consecutive subpath of any longer geodesic. A geodesic transversal intersects every such maximal geodesic. The ten graph vertices are distinguished by the edges of a fixed labelled \(K_5\); all enumerations count distinct labelled vertex subsets, not orbits, unless otherwise stated. In reconfiguration, each intermediate state must itself be a geodesic transversal and obey the inclusive upper cardinality bound. There is no imposed adjacency requirement between the graph vertices added and removed.

## Proof
**Step 1: turn the path condition into a five-vertex forbidden subgraph.** The graph \(KG(5,2)\) is triangle-free, has diameter two, and any two nonadjacent vertices have exactly one common neighbor. Every one-edge geodesic extends to a two-edge geodesic, since each endpoint has a neighbor not adjacent to the other. Hence the maximal geodesics are exactly its thirty two-edge geodesics. For overlapping two-subsets \(\{a,b\}\) and \(\{a,c\}\), the unique common neighbor is the complementary pair \(\{d,e\}=[5]\setminus\{a,b,c\}\). Regard all ten Petersen vertices as edges of \(K_5\). A maximal geodesic now corresponds precisely to the three edges \(\{a,b\},\{a,c\},\{d,e\}\), i.e. a spanning copy of the disjoint union \(P_3\sqcup K_2\) in \(K_5\).

Set \(F=E(K_5)\setminus S\). Then \(S\) is a geodesic transversal if and only if \(F\) contains no spanning \(P_3\sqcup K_2\).

**Step 2: forbidden-pattern lemma.** Suppose \(F\) has no isolated vertex. If \(F\) contains two disjoint edges, the fifth ground vertex has a neighbor. Its incident edge, together with the matched edge touching that neighbor and the other disjoint matched edge, gives a spanning \(P_3\sqcup K_2\). Therefore a forbidden-pattern-free \(F\) without isolated vertices has no two disjoint edges. A simple family of pairwise intersecting edges is either a star or a subgraph of a triangle: if three edges do not have a common endpoint, they are the three edges of a triangle, and no further edge can intersect all three. The triangle alternative has two isolated vertices on a five-vertex ground set, which is excluded. Since \(F\) has no isolated vertices, the star must contain all four edges at its center. Conversely, a graph with an isolated vertex cannot contain a spanning \(P_3\sqcup K_2\), and a full four-edge star cannot contain it because every pair of distinct edges intersects. Thus the only allowable \(F\) are graphs having at least one isolated vertex and the five full four-edge stars.

If \(F\) has an isolated ground vertex \(v\), all four edges at \(v\) belong to \(S\), that is \(A_v\subseteq S\). If \(F\) is the full four-star centered at \(v\), then \(S=B_v\). Conversely, any \(S\) containing \(A_v\) or \(B_v\) hits all the forbidden triples, proving the characterization.

**Step 3: minimality and polynomial.** Each \(A_v\) is minimal because an omitted member leaves fewer than four selected vertices, and the preceding classification admits no transversal of smaller size. Each \(B_v\) is minimal because deleting an edge introduces into its complement a four-star plus one edge among its leaves, which has no isolated ground vertex and is not itself a star. No \(B_v\) contains an \(A_u\), since it omits the edge \(\{u,v\}\) whenever \(u\ne v\), and the same statement is evident for \(u=v\). Thus these ten sets are exactly the inclusion-minimal transversals. Natural permutations of \([5]\) act transitively on the five members of each family, with no size mixing.

The supersets containing at least one \(A_v\) are counted by inclusion-exclusion. Choosing \(j\) prescribed star centers forces all edges touching those centers into \(S\), leaving \(q_j=\binom{5-j}{2}\) free edges; the polynomial for this intersection is \(z^{10-q_j}(1+z)^{q_j}\). Every proper superset of \(B_v\) contains some \(A_u\): adding one missing edge \(\{u,v\}\) completes the star \(A_u\), for \(u\ne v\). Therefore the only transversals missed by star inclusion-exclusion are the five \(B_v\) themselves. Add \(5z^6\) and expand to obtain the polynomial above.

**Step 4: exact reconfiguration threshold.** Distinct \(A_u,A_v\) meet in the single edge \(\{u,v\}\), so their union has seven vertices. Up to budget six, a transversal containing an \(A_v\) can belong to exactly one such star interval. This interval has one state of size four, six states of size five, and fifteen states of size six. It is connected through additions/removals to its \(A_v\) and has \(22\) states at budget six; at budget five it has \(7\). Each of the five \(B_v\) is a size-six isolated state at budget six because it has no feasible deletion and additions would exceed the budget. These exhaust all transversals by Step 2, giving the stated components.

At budget seven, every pair \(A_u,A_v\) is connected via \(A_u\cup A_v\): add the three vertices of \(A_v\setminus A_u\) and then remove the three of \(A_u\setminus A_v\). Every intermediate set contains one of the two stars. Each \(B_v\) connects by adding a single edge \(\{u,v\}\) to a seven-vertex set containing \(A_u\). Any larger transversal can be reduced one vertex at a time to one of these minimal sets by retaining a contained minimal set. Hence \(\mathcal R_7\) is connected. The distinct minima differ in six vertices, giving a lower bound of six moves, achieved by the displayed route. The polynomial gives \(5+30+80+90=205\) vertices at budget seven.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify_petersen.py` using the accompanying standalone Python 3 program. It constructs graph distances by breadth-first search, enumerates all thirty length-two maximal geodesics, tests each of the \(2^{10}=1024\) vertex subsets directly against those paths, and checks equality with a separately formulated star/complement criterion. It independently expands the inclusion-exclusion polynomial, classifies all inclusion-minimal sets, checks all \(120\) label permutations, and constructs the one-vertex-change graphs at cardinality budgets four through seven. The checked output is `PETERSEN_GEODESIC_VERIFY_OK`. The finite checks corroborate, but do not substitute for, the combinatorial proof above.

## Relationship to prior work
Manuel, Brešar and Klavžar (2021) introduced the geodesic-transversal problem and explicitly established \(\operatorname{gt}(P)=4\) for the Petersen graph (Section 3, Figure 1). That scalar result is prior art and is not asserted here as new. The contribution is the complete characterization of *all* transversals, their full polynomial and two minimal symmetry classes, together with the sharp three-spare reconfiguration threshold and exact optimum-to-optimum distance. Published records on geodesic transversals of triangular graphs concern \(L(K_n)\), not its complement \(KG(5,2)\); complementation changes shortest paths and does not imply this statement. Other records address crown graphs and wheels rather than the ten-vertex Petersen graph. The elementary edge-set lemma is proved here directly, independently of any priority claim for that lemma in graph extremal theory.

## Limitations
This is a complete finite theorem for the labelled Petersen graph, not an all-order classification of generalized Petersen or Kneser graphs. The earlier scalar optimum is already known. The comparison to literature is a best-effort priority check, not a guarantee against unindexed or inaccessible work. Reconfiguration uses one-vertex additions and removals, not an adjacency-constrained token slide. No independent scholarly audit has been performed.
