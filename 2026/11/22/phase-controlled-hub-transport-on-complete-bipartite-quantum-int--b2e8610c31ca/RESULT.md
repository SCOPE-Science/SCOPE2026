# Phase-controlled hub transport on complete bipartite quantum interferometers

## Finding
Fix an integer \(m\geq2\). Consider the Hermitian one-excitation Hamiltonian on the complete bipartite graph \(K_{2,m}\) with two distinguished hubs \(u,v\), leaves \(w_1,\dots,w_m\), zero onsite energies, and unit-modulus hoppings
\[
H_{uw_j}=H_{w_ju}=1,\qquad H_{vw_j}=z_j,\qquad H_{w_jv}=\overline z_j,\qquad |z_j|=1.
\]
Put \(S=\sum_{j=1}^m z_j\) and \(s=|S|\). The exact hub-to-hub transition amplitude at each real time \(t\) is zero if \(s=0\); otherwise it is
\[
\langle v|e^{-itH}|u\rangle
=\frac{S}{2s}\left[\cos\bigl(t\sqrt{m+s}\bigr)-\cos\bigl(t\sqrt{m-s}\bigr)\right].
\]
The following conditions form a complete classification of transport between the two hubs.

1. If \(s=0\), the transfer probability is identically zero.
2. If \(s=m\), perfect state transfer occurs at \(t=\pi/\sqrt{2m}\).
3. If \(0<s<m\), put \(R=\sqrt{(m+s)/(m-s)}>1\). Perfect state transfer occurs if and only if \(R=a/b\) in lowest terms for positive integers \(a>b\) of opposite parity. In that case \(t=\pi b/\sqrt{m-s}\) is a transfer time.
4. If \(0<s<m\) and \(R\) is irrational, pretty good but not perfect state transfer occurs: for every \(\varepsilon>0\), a positive time has transition probability greater than \(1-\varepsilon\).
5. If \(0<s<m\) and \(R=a/b\) in lowest terms with \(a,b\) both odd, neither perfect nor pretty good state transfer occurs. For the resonance \(R=3\), the exact maximum transfer probability is \(16/27\).

For every \(m\geq2\), perfect-transfer radii and non-pretty-good-transfer radii are both countable dense subsets of \((0,m)\); irrational-ratio radii form a dense, full-Lebesgue-measure subset. These statements lift to dense sets of phase assignments on the \(m\)-torus. Thus arbitrarily small magnetic-phase changes can turn a non-pretty-good-transfer device into one with arbitrarily accurate transport, while preserving every hopping magnitude.

## Assumptions and scope
The dynamics is a continuous-time, closed-system, single-excitation quantum walk generated by the fixed Hermitian matrix \(H\) above; its propagator is \(e^{-itH}\). No decoherence, sinks, interactions, onsite potential, or non-unit hopping magnitude is permitted. Perfect transfer means \(|\langle v|e^{-itH}|u\rangle|=1\) at a finite positive time. Pretty good transfer means that the supremum of this magnitude over positive times equals one; by unitarity this is equivalent to approximate transfer of the full state. The labels are fixed, and all assertions hold for every integer \(m\geq2\) and every vector of unit complex hopping phases. The combination \(S\) changes by a global phase under a gauge change and its modulus \(s\) is gauge invariant.

## Proof
Order the basis with hubs first. Write
\[
H=\begin{pmatrix}0&B\\B^*&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad B=\begin{pmatrix}1&\cdots&1\\z_1&\cdots&z_m\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The hub block of even powers of \(H\) equals powers of the Hermitian Gram matrix
\[
BB^*=\begin{pmatrix}m&\overline S\\S&m\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Odd powers have vanishing hub-to-hub block. In particular the full characteristic polynomial is \(x^{m-2}[x^2-(m+s)][x^2-(m-s)]\) (including boundary multiplicities). Therefore the hub block of the propagator is \(\cos(t\sqrt{BB^*})\). If \(s>0\), write \(BB^*=mI+sT\), where \(T\) is a Hermitian involution with off-diagonal entry \(T_{vu}=S/s\). Its two spectral projectors are \((I\pm T)/2\), of eigenvalues \(m\pm s\). Taking the \((v,u)\) entry gives the stated cosine-difference formula. For \(s=0\), \(BB^*=mI\), so that entry vanishes at every time. For \(s=m\), its two frequencies are \(\sqrt{2m}\) and zero, so a time with cosine equal to \(-1\) transfers perfectly.

Now fix \(0<s<m\), with positive frequencies \(\lambda_+=\sqrt{m+s}\) and \(\lambda_-=\sqrt{m-s}\). By the triangle inequality for real cosines,
\[
\frac12|\cos(\lambda_+t)-\cos(\lambda_-t)|\le1.
\]
Equality occurs precisely if the two cosines are \(+1\) and \(-1\), in some order. Thus there must be integers of opposite parity \(a,b\) such that \(\lambda_+t=a\pi\) and \(\lambda_-t=b\pi\). As the frequencies are positive, such a time exists exactly when the reduced rational frequency ratio \(R\) has numerator and denominator of opposite parity; the stated transfer time then works. An irrational ratio precludes finite equality. However the continuous flow \(t\mapsto(\lambda_+t,\lambda_-t)\pmod{2\pi}\) is dense in the two-torus when \(R\) is irrational. It comes arbitrarily close to phases \((0,\pi)\), proving arbitrarily accurate transfer with fixed output phase \(S/s\). For both-odd reduced rational ratios, the orbit is periodic and cannot reach a pair of opposite cosine extrema; compactness gives a strict maximum below one.

At \(R=3\), let \(x=\lambda_-t\). Then \(\cos(3x)-\cos x=-4\cos x(1-\cos^2x)\), so
\[
|\langle v|e^{-itH}|u\rangle|^2
=4y(1-y)^2,\qquad y=\cos^2x\in[0,1].
\]
Differentiating shows its unique interior maximum occurs at \(y=1/3\), giving \(16/27\). More generally, any \(R>1\) is realized by
\[
\frac{s}{m}=\frac{R^2-1}{R^2+1}.
\]
The map from \(R\in(1,\infty)\) to \(s\in(0,m)\) is continuous and strictly increasing. Reduced rationals with opposite parity and reduced rationals with both entries odd are each dense, as are irrationals, with the latter of full measure. All \(s\in[0,m]\) are attainable as lengths of sums of \(m\ge2\) unit phasors (the polygonal resultant principle, or induction adding one unit segment). Moreover the real-analytic map from the phase torus to \(s^2\) is nowhere locally constant and hence has measure-zero preimages of individual fixed values. These facts prove density of both resonant phase classes and almost-everywhere pretty good transfer.

## Relationship to prior work
Christandl, Datta, Ekert and Landahl introduced exact quantum-state transfer in spin networks [1]. Fan and Godsil characterized pretty good state transfer in ordinary real-weight double-star graphs using spectral frequency arithmetic [2]. Cameron and collaborators proved broad spectral results and constructions concerning universal state transfer on complex-weighted graphs [3]. These are foundational or broader general techniques, but their inspected statements do not directly classify pairwise hub transport under arbitrary unit hopping phases on \(K_{2,m}\) by the single gauge-invariant resultant \(s\). The direct contribution is the exact all-phase classification, its dense three-regime transport geometry, and the sharp \(3:1\) non-transfer ceiling. Published results on other quantum transport architectures show similar frequency-parity mechanisms; such mechanisms are acknowledged rather than claimed new in isolation.

## Limitations
This is not a classification of arbitrary complex-weighted bipartite graphs: it requires equal absolute coupling strengths and exactly two hubs. It describes single-excitation coherent dynamics, not noise, many-body interactions, or robustness on bounded observation horizons. The dense near-perfect-transfer regime may require very long times and is not an engineering noise guarantee. The arithmetic and spectral techniques are established; the assessed novelty is their exact combination on this phase-controlled network. Historical priority cannot exclude unindexed or inaccessible specialized equivalents. Independent scientific audit has not been performed.
