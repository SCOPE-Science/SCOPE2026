# Review: Poisson attractor selection on Boolean cubes

## Correctness — PASS

The state convention, missing self-loops and all dimension and probability ranges are explicit. The fundamental NOR identity \(T^3=T\) is proved for undirected graphs by polarity. Exact defect moments follow from the special two-neighbor overlap at cube distance two; all other distinct neighborhoods are disjoint. The published local-dependence theorem is applied with explicit values \(b_1,b_2,b_3\), so the probabilistic limit does not rely on finite simulation. A union bound makes sparse defect locations separated with probability tending to one; separation gives exactly \(|U|\) versus \(2^d-d|U|\) sites and a primitive two-cycle. The universal-basin event includes the exceptional initially all-zero configuration. The fixed-density lower regime follows from the explicitly calculated variance and Chebyshev's inequality. The independent finite checker confirms all states through dimension four and the separate neighborhood-union histogram identity; this is corroboration, not the infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk

The 1990 Arratia–Goldstein–Gordon theorem supplies general Poisson tools and the 2015 Aledo literature already treats the generic period-one/two restriction. Neither states the cube NOR-specific random attraction-basin law, exact output covariance, \(1/d\) transition window, or sparse two-phase code geometry. Relevant published result records on cube excitation defects and NOR on Kneser, leaf-corona and ladder graphs were compared by their claims and implications: they have different update rules or underlying graphs and do not determine the claimed probability. An informal discussion of isolated vertices in random induced cube subgraphs concerns a distinct event including the site's own state; it does not directly provide the NOR-attractor law. The required Poisson specialization is elementary once the correct dependency geometry is recognized, so related probabilistic work may independently contain equivalent estimates. Full-text access to some older domination literature and repository packages was limited; see the structured source comparisons.

## Value — PASS

The asymptotic fraction of initial configurations attracted to the universal NOR two-cycle is a natural dynamical basin invariant. The sharp Gumbel-shaped density window, uniform finite-dimensional total-variation estimate, exact variance correction and complete limiting phase-defect distribution together quantify an otherwise invisible selection transition on exponentially large phase spaces. The coding interpretation explains how infinitely many distinct isolated-site two-cycles coexist with the universal orbit. No assertion of a new general Poisson theorem or a complete finite-cube orbit classification is needed.
