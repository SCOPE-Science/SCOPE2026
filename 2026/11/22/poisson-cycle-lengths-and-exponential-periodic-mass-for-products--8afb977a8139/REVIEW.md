# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
Conditional on fixed sets of roots, the product has an exact alternating-cycle representation between their two exclusive parts. Counting disjoint prescribed cycles proves the factorial-moment formula for every feasible finite configuration. The logarithmic root-rank law follows from a monotone consecutive-weight ratio; hypergeometric concentration gives the rare survival factor \(q_n=\exp(-(2+o_{\Pr}(1))/\log n)\). Uniform factorial corrections for bounded multiples of \(\log n\), plus geometric tail domination, yield the marked Poisson limit, largest-cycle law, and exponential periodic-mass limit. All relevant quantifiers, the exclusion of common fixed points, and the cutoff arguments are explicit. Exact enumeration through five vertices checks the finite identity in every parameter stratum; it is not used to prove the asymptotics.

## Originality — PASS, historically qualified
The classical idempotent enumeration and the theory of unconditioned bipartite random mappings are acknowledged. The latter establishes Poisson–Dirichlet component sizes for fully alternating maps without absorbing common roots, whereas this result concerns a killed alternating model induced by two random retractions and has length scale \(\log n\), not macroscopic component size. Brown–Higgins treat unrestricted random transformations and Dalal–Schmutz unrestricted compositions, neither the product of uniform idempotents. Searches of related results on retractions, products, random mappings, cycle counts, and equivalent absorbing bipartite models did not identify a matching theorem. An unlocated broad result for killed bipartite maps is a residual priority risk; negative searches do not establish uniqueness.

## Value — PASS
Combining two natural uniformly sampled retractions, neither of which has a nontrivial cycle, produces a full population of new cycles on a distinct logarithmic scale. A single exact finite-size factorial-moment law yields the microscopic Poisson cycle counts, a nonhomogeneous mesoscopic point process, an explicit extremal distribution, and a rate-two exponential law for periodic mass. This is a structural probabilistic phenomenon in products of finite random transformations, not a cosmetic change of parameters or a precomputed enumeration.

## Limitations
The transient component geometry is not characterized. Short-cycle counts and the limiting point process have different scopes: the latter excludes zero and is not a statement about the total number of cycles. Existing general killed-mapping results might potentially overlap, although no such covering theorem was located. Finite exhaustive tests do not constitute an infinite proof, which is analytical.
