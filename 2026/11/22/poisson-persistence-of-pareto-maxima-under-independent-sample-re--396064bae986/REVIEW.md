Independent record indicators prove the planar generating function and variance; two complementary joint-maximality bounds control refreshed labels. The fixed-dimensional Poisson limit uses the published single-sample maxima mean/variance estimates, while planar total-variation control follows from finite-population Bernoulli coupling and the Poisson–Stein bound. **Originality — PASS, qualified.** Barndorff-Nielsen–Sobel study maxima within one random vector sample; Bai–Devroye–Hwang–Tsai supply one-sample moment and CLT inputs; Hwang–Tsai–Chen study a different \(k\)-dominance relation. General marked-record/overlap theory might cover parts of this result and historical priority is not independently established.

**Value — PASS.** Random partial refresh is a natural durability test for a Pareto frontier: a fixed fraction of the data is retained, but the surviving-frontier fraction is a nontrivial \(q/(2-q)\). The critical retention scale \((\log n)^{1-d}\) and the exact finite and asymptotic distributions quantify when any old frontier labels survive after a batch update. This is an interpretable statistical robustness invariant, not an arbitrary parameter slice.

**Residual limitations.** The result assumes fixed dimension and independent continuous coordinates. Full-vector replacements are essential to the refreshed-label bound; nonuniform adversarial updates and coordinatewise refresh remain outside scope. The historical search is substantial but not exhaustive, particularly for broadly stated marked-record thinning theorems.
