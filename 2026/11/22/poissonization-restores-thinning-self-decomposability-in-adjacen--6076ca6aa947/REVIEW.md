# Review of the adjacent-gamma Poissonization result

## Correctness (C): PASS
The theorem identifies both laws explicitly, including the shape–rate convention, \(r>0\), \(0\le s\le1\) and \(\theta>0\). For the continuous mixture, the Lévy density \(x^{-1}[(r+1)e^{-\theta x}-e^{-\theta x/s}]\) is positive for \(s>0\); its monotonicity is decided exactly by the derivative at the origin. The pure-gamma endpoint \(s=0\) is treated separately. For the count, its logarithmic generating-function coefficients \(\lambda_n=[(r+1)q^n-a^n]/n\) are positive for *every* \(n\ge1\). The explicit quotient for thinning innovations yields coefficients \(\nu_n(c)\), and a uniform all-index inequality proves their positivity precisely in the claimed region. Outside it, the coefficient at degree one is negative for retentions approaching one. The factorial-Palm identity follows by exact differentiation of the published PNXL generating function. The standalone exact-rational checker passed on representative rational parameters; finite cases are corroboration, not the universal proof.

## Originality (O): PASS, with stated priority risk
Irshad–Aswathy–Maya–Nadarajah (2023) give the PNXL law, its PGF and an INAR model, but the accessible mathematical sections do not derive the continuous-versus-discrete self-decomposability split or the factorial-Palm hierarchy. Pakes (2022), Section 2, supplies the classical Lévy/canonical criteria; the present work makes the model-specific nontrivial sharp comparison. Kella–Löpker (2022) gives general binomial-thinning theory, not the adjacent-shape gamma phase boundary. The published two-parameter Poisson generalized Lindley family with generating function \(\theta^\alpha(\theta+2-z)/[(\theta+1)(\theta+1-z)^\alpha]\) is mathematically equivalent to the curve \(r=\alpha-1,\ s=\theta/(\theta+1)\) inside the present three-parameter family; that curve and its established distributional properties are not claimed as new. The new statement treats all independent mixing weights and the continuous/discrete distinction and Palm hierarchy, which do not follow from identifying that curve. Ong–Sim–Liu–Srivastava (2023) construct an NB-plus-*shifted*-NB law, whose Bernoulli-factor generating function differs from the mixture of adjacent NB **shapes**. No covering equivalent or stronger statement was located by the specified scientific searches or relevant published research-record comparisons. The first paper's indexed sections 1, 2.1, 2.2 and 6 were available, but the direct PDF/full rendering was not; uninspected sections and older specialized literature remain residual priority risks. Search absence is not a claim of absolute novelty.

## Value (V): PASS
Mixed Poisson count laws are used as INAR innovations and as candidate stationary marginals. The result fully classifies which adjacent-gamma mixtures can serve as stationary marginals of every-retention Bernoulli-thinning autoregression and constructs their compound-Poisson innovations. The exact separation between continuous mixing self-decomposability and discrete thinning self-decomposability is meaningful: the latter can hold while the former fails, with explicit rational witnesses. The all-order critical factorial-Palm hierarchy of a published count model gives a natural closure property useful for length-biased/event-sampled counts; the statement is not limited to an arbitrary handful of indices.

## Closest literature and limitations
- Irshad, Aswathy, Maya and Nadarajah, *Mathematics* 12 (2024), 81, https://doi.org/10.3390/math12010081: PNXL definition, generating function and innovation model, not the stated self-decomposability comparison in the material read.
- Pakes, *Axioms* 11 (2022), 584, https://doi.org/10.3390/axioms11110584: classical continuous and discrete self-decomposability criteria; acknowledged rather than renamed as a new theorem.
- Kella and Löpker, arXiv:2202.10296, https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.10296: general binomial thinning and mixing principles.
- Irshad, D'cruz, Maya and Khan, *Journal of Biopharmaceutical Statistics* 33 (2023), 335–356, https://doi.org/10.1080/10543406.2022.2152832: prior two-parameter Poisson-generalized Lindley law occupying a parameter curve.
- Papageorgiou and Vardaki, *Journal of Statistical Theory and Practice* 16 (2022), 30, https://doi.org/10.1007/s42519-022-00261-z: broad bivariate/general Poisson–Lindley framework, metadata and abstract only.
- Ong et al., *Stats* 6 (2023), 942–955, https://doi.org/10.3390/stats6030059: shifted negative-binomial mixture; same vocabulary but distinct PGF and no implication of the present rate threshold.

The argument does not cover arbitrary multi-component gamma mixtures, arbitrary thinning operators or an independent priority audit. The original PNXL full publisher PDF was not retrievable, which limits the exhaustive literature comparison.
