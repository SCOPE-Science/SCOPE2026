# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The precise first-group absorption rule stops the process at the first zero hit; after every surviving rate-
\(r\) reset all particles coincide at their current maximum. The exact renewal equation holds without assuming independence of the first-cycle survival mark and terminal maximum. The union bound gives \(N\exp(-x\sqrt{r/D})\); the two-particle maximum transform is derived from independent Gaussian mean and difference, integrated explicitly. At \(\kappa=\sqrt{r/D}/2\), its Laplace moment is strictly less than one, giving a positive exponential supersolution for the probability of *eventual* absorption. Iteration, nonexplosion, and a positive-probability excursion to an adequately distant position yield strict nonabsorption from each initial \(x>0\). The reflection principle proves the zero-reset tail and finite-mean threshold; two limiting reset-rate assertions follow from bounded convergence and the supersolution. Auxiliary deterministic diagnostics replay the transform and inequalities. Risks: the capture bound is not optimal, and simulations or quadratures are not relied upon for infinite-time claims.

## Originality — PASS (best-knowledge, qualified)
Lee–Yang–Lizana (arXiv:2604.24406) analyze the same group rule with **nonzero harmonic confinement**, and do not claim this \(k=0\), all-positive-reset-rate nonabsorption result. Lee–Yang–Park–Lizana (arXiv:2412.02524) give a group-resetting framework and CM avoidance in a potential, rather than exact first-particle nonabsorption without confinement. Majumdar–Sabhapandit–Schehr (arXiv:1509.04516) use a single lattice walk reset to its *historical* maximum, which does not imply the claimed \(N\ge2\) collective-current-maximum statement. Relevant searchable indexed claims concern confined rightmost-reset OU groups or different origin/threshold reset rules and do not cover the current statement. Six precise searches of the research index found no dominating result. Remaining risk: inaccessible or unindexed work may have an equivalent result; a negative search is not proof of absolute priority.

## Value — PASS
For a concrete, recent, motivated physical collective-selection protocol, the theorem establishes a sharp qualitative distinction between \(N=1\) and \(N\ge2\), and a discontinuous finite-mean/infinite-mean outcome at \(r=0\) for \(N\ge3\). Positive eternal survival rather than merely a very long censored hitting time has implications for interpreting simulations and designing confinement. It is an exact, all-parameter qualitative classification with an explicit escape bound, not an arbitrary numerical slice. Bound sharpness and extension to weak confinement remain open.
