# Power-domination triples and sharp relocation detours in the ternary Hamming three-cube

## Finding
Let \(H=H(3,3)=K_3\square K_3\square K_3\), with its vertices the twenty-seven words in \(\{0,1,2\}^3\) and adjacency at Hamming distance one. Then the power-domination number is \(\gamma_P(H)=3\). A triple \(S\) is a minimum power-dominating set **if and only if** the unordered multiset of its three pairwise Hamming distances is one of
\[
(1,3,3),\qquad (2,2,3),\qquad (2,3,3).
\]
The respective numbers of such triples are \(324,648,324\), for \(1296\) optimal configurations altogether.

On the common vertex set of these \(1296\) configurations, let \(R_J\) join sets differing by one selected vertex and let \(R_S\) further require the removed and inserted vertices to be adjacent in \(H\). Both graphs are connected. Their exact edge counts, radii and diameters are
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
 & |E| & \mathrm{rad} & \mathrm{diam}\\ \hline
R_J & 24300 & 3 & 4\\
R_S & 7290 & 6 & 6
\end{array}
\]
The distance distributions over unordered *distinct* pairs of configurations are

| Distance | Jump pairs | Slide pairs |
|:--:|--:|--:|
| \(1\) | 24,300 | 7,290 |
| \(2\) | 224,208 | 45,360 |
| \(3\) | 590,328 | 170,262 |
| \(4\) | 324 | 336,960 |
| \(5\) | 0 | 244,620 |
| \(6\) | 0 | 34,668 |

Every optimal triple of type \((2,2,3)\) has exactly one configuration at jump distance four. The resulting \(324\) pairs form a fixed-point-free *antipodal involution* on that class of \(648\) configurations. No other jump-distance-four pair exists. Every optimal configuration has some configuration at slide distance six.

In particular, for any distinct optimum configurations \(S,T\), the extra length \(d_{R_S}(S,T)-d_{R_J}(S,T)\) belongs to \(\{0,1,2,3\}\), and the numbers of unordered pairs exhibiting these gaps are respectively \(103356,370008,324648,41148\). Hence the maximum adjacent-relocation penalty is **exactly three** moves, attained by \(41148\) pairs.

## Assumptions and scope
The graph \(H\) is the *labeled*, undirected, simple Cartesian product on \(\{0,1,2\}^3\). Vertices differ by an edge precisely when exactly one coordinate changes; there are \(27\) vertices and \(81\) edges. The Hamming distance counts the coordinates at which two words differ.

For a selected set \(S\), first observe its closed neighborhood \(O_0=N_H[S]\). Subsequently, replace any observed vertex's unique unobserved neighbor by an observed vertex, repeating until no change occurs. This closure is independent of the legal forcing order and equals the standard power-domination closure. The set \(S\) is power dominating exactly when the closure is all \(27\) vertices. Both relocation graphs have **only the minimum power-dominating sets** as vertices; neither admits intermediate placements of different cardinality or non-power-dominating placements. A jump replaces exactly one selected vertex by an unselected vertex. A slide is a jump where the two exchanged original vertices are adjacent.

## Proof
**Exhaustion and symmetry.** The coordinate permutations and independent permutations of the three symbols in each coordinate form an automorphism group \(\mathfrak S_3^3\rtimes\mathfrak S_3\) of size \(6^4=1296\). Its action on the \(\binom{27}{3}=2925\) unordered triples has precisely the following ten orbits. The column headed “closure” lists the size after the initial closed-neighborhood step and after every productive synchronous forcing round; its last entry is the terminal size. In the representatives, a three-digit word denotes its coordinates, *not* an integer.

| Representative triple | Pair distances | Orbit size | Closure sizes |
|---|---|---:|---|
| `000,001,002` | \((1,1,1)\) | 27 | 15 |
| `000,001,010` | \((1,1,2)\) | 324 | 14 |
| `000,001,012` | \((1,2,2)\) | 324 | 15 |
| `000,001,110` | \((1,2,3)\) | 648 | 16,18,20 |
| `000,001,112` | \((1,3,3)\) | 324 | 18,24,27 |
| `000,011,022` | \((2,2,2)\) | 54 | 15 |
| `000,011,101` | \((2,2,2)\) | 216 | 16 |
| `000,011,102` | \((2,2,3)\) | 648 | 17,19,23,27 |
| `000,011,122` | \((2,3,3)\) | 324 | 19,23,27 |
| `000,111,222` | \((3,3,3)\) | 36 | 21 |

The group is explicitly generated by all six coordinate permutations and three independent six-element symbol permutations. Applying every group element to each representative yields the stated orbit cardinalities; the disjoint orbits total \(2925\), so none is omitted. The forcing-rule closure of a triple is invariant under automorphisms. Exactly the fifth, eighth and ninth orbits observe all twenty-seven vertices. Checking every singleton and pair (\(27+\binom{27}{2}=378\) possibilities) produces no successful configuration. Thus the minimum cardinality is three, the displayed pair-distance characterization is exhaustive, and the number of optima is \(324+648+324=1296\). The two geometrically different \((2,2,2)\)-orbits both fail, so the classification by distance signature is valid despite that orbit split.

**Relocation graphs.** Write each optimum as a three-element subset of the labeled \(27\)-vertex universe. The jump adjacency test is \(|S\cap T|=2\). The slide test adds \(d_H(u,v)=1\), where \(S\setminus T=\{u\}\) and \(T\setminus S=\{v\}\). Exhaustively applying these *if and only if* tests to all optima gives degree distributions
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
\text{Type}&(1,3,3)&(2,2,3)&(2,3,3)\\\hline
\text{Jump degree}&37&35&43\\
\text{Slide degree}&10&10&15
\end{array}
\]
with class sizes \(324,648,324\). The handshake lemma gives \((324\cdot37+648\cdot35+324\cdot43)/2=24300\) jump edges and \((972\cdot10+324\cdot15)/2=7290\) slide edges.

**Shortest paths and the sharp detour.** The automorphism group preserves both relocation adjacency rules and is transitive on each of the three successful triple orbits. Breadth-first search from one representative of each orbit therefore determines the distance multiset from *every* state in that orbit. The exact distance spheres in orders \(0,1,\ldots\) are

| Initial type | Jump distance-sphere sizes | Slide distance-sphere sizes |
|---|---|---|
| \((1,3,3)\) | 1,37,343,915 | 1,10,63,236,519,407,60 |
| \((2,2,3)\) | 1,35,353,906,1 | 1,10,67,252,517,385,64 |
| \((2,3,3)\) | 1,43,335,917 | 1,15,83,311,527,333,26 |

Each row sums to \(1296\) for each move rule, establishing connectedness. The largest nonzero sphere index gives every eccentricity and hence the stated radii and diameters. For any distance \(d>0\), multiply the \(d\)-th sphere size by its orbit size, sum over the three orbit representatives, then divide by two to count unordered pairs; this yields the displayed distance distributions. The sole jump-distance-four sphere contains one vertex from every type-\((2,2,3)\) state and zero from the other types, proving its fixed-point-free involution: the unique partner relation is symmetric, and no state can be its own partner.

To compare jump and slide lengths *for the same endpoints*, run both searches from each of the three representatives and tally the differences against the same destination states, multiply by orbit sizes and divide by two. The exhaustive output is \(103356,370008,324648,41148\) pairs at penalties \(0,1,2,3\), respectively. Therefore three is both an upper bound on the locality detour and an attained lower bound, with the claimed exact frequency. These finite computations are exhaustive certificates over a fully specified finite universe; they are not extrapolations from sample orders.

## Verification
Two standalone Python 3 programs implement independent representations of the graph and force closure; no external libraries or downloaded inputs are necessary.

1. Run `python3 verify_bitmask.py`. It enumerates every singleton, pair and triple of original vertices, simulates forcing using bitsets, constructs both reconfiguration graphs by all legal single-token exchanges, then performs a breadth-first search from **each** of the 1296 states in both graphs. It checks all \(839160=\binom{1296}{2}\) distinct unordered pairs and all reported counts.
2. Run `python3 verify_symmetry.py`. It uses sets of coordinate tuples, a distinct set-based closure implementation, explicitly enumerates all \(1296\) coordinate/symbol automorphisms, reconstructs ten disjoint triple orbits, and checks forcing on their members. It independently constructs both reconfiguration graphs and uses orbit-representative breadth-first searches to compute distances and detour counts.

Successful runs print `BITMASK_OK` and `SYMMETRY_OK`. The two scripts verify the same main theorem through different types of state representation and different distance-counting strategies. The accompanying `orbit_table.json` provides the ten finite representative cases. The mathematical reason the finite verification proves completeness is the explicit enumeration of every possible singleton, pair, triple and, for the metric, every optimum configuration or all its symmetry-orbit representatives.

## Relationship to prior work
Bjorkman, Bozeman, Ferrero, Flagg, Grood, Hogben, Jacob and Reinhart introduced power-domination token jumping and token addition/removal reconfiguration in their publicly posted January 2022 paper (later published in *La Matematica*). Its Proposition 5.5 **realizes** the Hamming graph \(H(d,3)\) as the *reconfiguration graph of another specially constructed base graph*. That is different from power domination **on the Hamming graph itself** and does not imply the minimum-triple classification or either distance distribution established here.

Anderson, Kuenzel and Schuerger (arXiv September 2022) prove power-domination lower bounds for Cartesian products, including a Vizing-type inequality for trees. The paper does not classify minimum monitoring sets or relocation distances of \(K_3^{\square3}\). Koh and Soh survey and solve several Cartesian-product power-domination cases, notably two-factor products; their results do not establish the three-factor optimum relocation metrics stated here. None of these comparisons constitutes a proof that an unindexed equivalent result is absent.

## Limitations
The theorem concerns precisely the labeled \(27\)-vertex graph \(H(3,3)\), not all Hamming graphs or all dimensions. The proof is finite and computer-assisted with explicit exhaustive algorithms; it is not a symbolic formula for arbitrary dimension or alphabet size. The 1296-element group is used for certification and classification, not as evidence of any infinite-family claim. Independent mathematical verification has not been performed. There remains a residual possibility of equivalent prior results in unindexed literature, dissertations or unpublished material.
