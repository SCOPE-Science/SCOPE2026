# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The normal form and product are documented by Jones–Lawson (2011), Section 1.1, and Lawson (2007), Section 3. Within the balanced finite submonoid, commutation with a shorter element requires either two zero products or one compatible common-prefix idempotent; with a longer element it requires two zero products or idempotence of the fixed element. These mutually exclusive cases yield the complete centralizer formula. Each possible longest common prefix has the stated exact word-pair count. Summing level-pair counts and then two convergent geometric series proves the sharp commutation deficit. Direct exhaustive independent word multiplication passed at seven parameter points up to 92 elements and every ordered pair. Scope excludes the full untruncated polycyclic monoid. A finite check is not used in lieu of the general proof.

## Originality — PASS, with priority risk
The archival primary literature establishes the inverse-monoid normal form and orthogonal completion, not this centralizer and inter-level counting law. Published Brandt-semigroup commutation formulas apply to a single level plus zero, but not cross-level multiplication. Existing finite acyclic graph inverse-semigroup centralizer counts do not cover a rose with loops; the bicyclic result is a different zero-free one-generator setting. Direct searches for the alias "balanced/gauge-zero prefix-replacement monoid", longest-common-prefix centralizers, commutation probability and power-law cutoff produced no implication covering the full claim. Search incompleteness and older rooted-tree partial-isomorphism work remain material residual risks; no definitive priority claim is made.

## Value — PASS
The finite degree-zero depth filtration is a canonical sequence of finite inverse submonoids of a foundational inverse monoid. The complete exact element-centralizer multiset provides more information than a single commuting probability, and exhibits a genuine prefix-depth invariant; the noncommuting deficit has a positive sharp exponential coefficient. The cross-level phenomenon is absent from isolated Brandt blocks and differs qualitatively from commutativity in a bicyclic word-length ball. This is a natural full-parameter enumeration and asymptotic of an algebraic invariant, not an arbitrary isolated numerical slice.
