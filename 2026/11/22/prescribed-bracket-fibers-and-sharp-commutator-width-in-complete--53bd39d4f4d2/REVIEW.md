# Scientific review

## Correctness: PASS

For \(a,b\ge2\) over \(\mathbb F_q\), the bracket is exactly the product of an \(a\)-by-two and a two-by-\(b\) matrix. Rank-stratification of the left factor yields distinct zero-, rank-one and rank-two target fibers, and higher ranks are impossible. The freely chosen pair of central inputs explains the global factor \(q^{2ab}\). A matrix-rank lower bound and an explicit pairing of rank-one terms prove the exact minimal number of brackets over any field. No finite experiment is substituted for any of these general proof steps.

The saved checker independently evaluates all target matrices in seven configurations, including extension-field arithmetic over \(\mathbb F_4\), and verifies both targetwise counts and the normalization of all ordered vertex-coordinate pairs. Exact output and code are provided. The proof does not rely on an unverified computational lemma.

## Originality: PASS, with residual priority risk

Mainkar (2013), building on Dani and Mainkar (2004), defines the graphical Lie-algebra construction and studies graph reconstruction, not these fibers. The complete-bipartite ideal and abelian-subalgebra enumerations concern different invariants. A directly relevant published finite-field bracket-fiber formula applies only to forests; \(K_{a,b}\) with \(a,b\ge2\) contains a four-cycle. The triangle-with-leaves bracket theorem has a different graph and target stratification. Their full accessible result texts were checked, as were alternate matrix-product formulations and related records. These statements do not imply the complete rank-specific fiber formula and its sharp width classification without additional work.

The key equivalence with rectangular matrix factorization and rank decomposition uses *standard* linear algebra. Priority uncertainty is real: a sufficiently general historical count of factorizations \(AB=M\) could imply the numerical part under this identification. Such a dominating source was not found in the searched corpus or primary texts, but absence from an index is not proof of uniqueness. A 2004 foundational paper was partly accessible through searchable text; a page-image inspection of its PDF was blocked by source access restrictions.

## Value: PASS

For a natural infinite family of graph Lie algebras, every derived target is classified by its **individual bracket fiber size**, and the exact number of additive brackets required is determined. The case \(\min(a,b)=3\) is a sharp and conceptually meaningful threshold: the derived ideal is generated by brackets but the bracket map is no longer surjective. The commutator width grows as \(\lceil\min(a,b)/2\rceil\); this is not an arbitrary finite-table slice. The reduction to familiar determinantal rank is transparent and enhances interpretability, while not being misrepresented as new.

## Scope and remaining limitations

The result is restricted to complete-bipartite, centrally edge-labeled two-step Lie algebras. No claims about arbitrary graphs, three-step graph Lie algebras, integral forms or scheme-level singularities are made. Historical equivalence with a general matrix-product fiber theorem remains the main originality risk. No independent mathematical validation was performed.
