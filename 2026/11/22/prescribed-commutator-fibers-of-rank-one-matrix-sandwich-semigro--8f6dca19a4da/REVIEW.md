# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

Over every finite field and with a fixed rank-one sandwich matrix, independent basis changes reduce to \(A=E_{11}\). A commutator then factors as \(UJV\) with two shared distinguished coordinates; all other entries contribute a uniform affine factor \(q^{2(m-1)(n-1)}\). The rank-two classification is proved by factorization rigidity and the identity \(J^{-1}T^{-1}J=T^{\mathsf T}/\det T\). Rank-one cases are exhausted by whether either distinguished axis vanishes. Target-stratum counts are proved by ordinary matrix factorizations. The standalone exact-field verifier independently checked each individual fiber across eight exhaustive finite cases, including \(\mathbb F_4\); its finite tests are not substituted for the general proof. The already published zero-target formula is credited.

## Originality — PASS relative to inspected sources

The closest result, *Commutator-rank census of finite rectangular-band semigroup algebras* (2026-11-02), is mathematically the same rank-one sandwich algebra. Its global rank histogram and commuting-pair count are **prior coverage**, not novel components. Crucially its full mathematical statement gives only the **aggregate** counts by commutator rank, while explicitly listing prescribed nonzero target fibers as a limitation. The present statement solves exactly that finer nonzero-target problem, including the split into \(\operatorname{GL}_2\) and determinant-one fibers and all impossible target types. The published commuting-idempotent spectrum counts only the zero target and restricted operands. The 2015 matrix-sandwich study and 2024 transformation-variant congruence study have different enumerative objectives. Semantic searches were performed for rectangular-band algebras, rank-one Munn/homotope variants, annihilator predicates, and prescribed Lie brackets; no stronger statement was identified. Residual risk: undiscovered older equivalent formulations or inaccessible literature.

## Value — PASS

The result is a complete, dimension-uniform description of fibers of a natural bilinear map on finite semigroup algebras, not just a repetition of its global rank polynomial. It determines which prescribed matrices can be expressed as a single commutator and how nonzero fibers vary on different rank-two geometric loci. The determinant-one versus general-linear splitting makes a structural distinction invisible to the prior histogram, and the row includes exact counts of targets in every locus. These fiber data can be used to investigate commutator width, conditional distributions, and representation geometry.

Priority beyond the materially inspected literature has not been established. No scheme structure, higher sandwich rank, or independent validation is asserted. The zero-target and rank histogram results already known in the rectangular-band semigroup algebra are not novel claims.
