# Prescribed cubic fibers of two-pole quiver radicals and a matching-number law

## Finding

Let \(G=(U\sqcup V,E)\) be any nonempty finite **simple bipartite** graph, without isolated vertices. Write \(w=|U|+|V|\) and \(e=|E|\). Make an acyclic quiver \(Q_G\) by introducing two new vertices \(s,t\), and the arrows \(s\to u\) for every \(u\in U\), \(u\to v\) for every edge \(uv\in E\), and \(v\to t\) for every \(v\in V\). Over an arbitrary finite field \(k=\mathbb F_q\), let \(J\) denote the Jacobson radical of the path algebra \(kQ_G\). Then \(\dim_kJ=w+4e\), and \(J^3\) has the length-three path basis indexed by \(E\).

For any prescribed \(z=\sum_{uv\in E}z_{uv}(s\to u\to v\to t)\in J^3\), put \(F(z)=\{uv\in E:z_{uv}\ne0\}\), \(W=U\sqcup V\), and \(W_F=\{\text{endpoints of edges in }F(z)\}\). For \(S\subseteq W\), put \(e_G(S)=|\{uv\in E:u,v\in S\}|\). The **complete cubic fiber formula** is

\[
\boxed{\#\{x\in J:x^3=z\}=q^{3e}\sum_{S\supseteq W_F}(q-1)^{|S|}q^{e-e_G(S)}.}\tag{1}
\]

Every \(z\in J^3\) is a cube, and all targets with the same support set have the same fiber size. In particular, when \(F(z)\) meets every vertex, the fiber has size \(q^{3e}(q-1)^w\).

Let \(\nu(G)\) be the maximum matching size. Define the positive integer
\[
c_G=\#\{S\subseteq W:|S|-e_G(S)=w-\nu(G)\}.
\]
Then the proportion of \(x\in J\) with \(x^3=0\) obeys the **sharp, characteristic-independent large-field law**
\[
\boxed{\frac{\#\{x\in J:x^3=0\}}{|J|}=c_Gq^{-\nu(G)}+O_G(q^{-\nu(G)-1})\quad(q\to\infty\text{ through prime powers}).}\tag{2}
\]

Over any field, the reduced cubic-zero scheme is the coordinate subspace arrangement defined by the squarefree monomial ideal \((a_u b_{uv}c_v:uv\in E)\), times an affine space of dimension \(3e\). Its codimension in \(J\) is **exactly \(\nu(G)\)**, and \(c_G\) is its exact number of top-dimensional geometrically irreducible components.

At the same time, for a uniformly chosen \(x\in J\), the total variation distance from the distribution of \(x^3\) on \(J^3\) to the uniform distribution is at most \(1-(1-q^{-1})^w\le w/q\). Thus the whole cube law is nearly uniform for large fields, even though the zero fiber has a matching-controlled exceptional probability when \(\nu(G)<e\).

## Assumptions and scope

The quiver has no paths longer than three. The path algebra has its ordinary concatenation product, and the radical consists of the positive-length paths; **the cube map is on \(J\), not on the unit group or on the full path algebra**. Each middle-layer edge appears exactly once, and there are no parallel arrows. The graph is fixed in the \(q\to\infty\) assertion. The zero root is included. No assumptions on the characteristic or algebraic closure of the finite field are needed; the geometric statement holds over any field. The assumption that \(G\) has no isolated vertices is used only for the simple full-support fiber expression, not for formula (1) or the matching-number law.

## Proof

**Path coordinates.** The arrow coefficients of an arbitrary \(x\in J\) are \(a_u\) on \(s\to u\), \(b_{uv}\) on \(u\to v\), and \(c_v\) on \(v\to t\). There are \(w+e\) arrows, exactly \(2e\) distinct two-arrow paths, and exactly \(e\) distinct three-arrow paths. Their total is \(w+4e\). The \(3e\) length-two and length-three coefficients of \(x\) are completely free when \(x^3\) is specified, because three positive-length factors can contribute only three arrows. Moreover, the coefficient of the length-three path indexed by \(uv\) in \(x^3\) is precisely \(a_u b_{uv}c_v\). Each path has only one decomposition as a sequence of its three constituent arrows; there is neither a binomial coefficient nor a possible characteristic-dependent cancellation. Hence \(x^3=z\) is equivalent to the independent edge equations
\[
a_u b_{uv}c_v=z_{uv}\qquad(uv\in E).\tag{3}\]

**All prescribed fibers.** Choose \(S\subseteq W\), the set of vertices whose coefficients \(a_u\) or \(c_v\) are nonzero. These vertex coefficients have \((q-1)^{|S|}\) assignments. Equation (3) forces \(S\) to contain the endpoints of every edge with nonzero \(z_{uv}\). For each edge induced by \(S\), the middle coefficient \(b_{uv}\) is uniquely determined (including its forced zero value when \(z_{uv}=0\)). For each of the remaining \(e-e_G(S)\) edges, the prescribed target must be zero and \(b_{uv}\) can be chosen arbitrarily in \(q\) ways. The independent choices, multiplied by the \(q^{3e}\) free higher-path coordinates, give formula (1). Choosing every vertex coefficient equal to one proves surjectivity of \(x\mapsto x^3\), without restricting the prescribed target.

**Matching number and top-dimensional components.** Over any field the cubic-zero equations are the squarefree monomials in (3). Their coordinate ring is reduced: the monomial ideal generated by products of distinct variables is radical. Its irreducible components are the coordinate subspaces indexed by inclusion-minimal sets of variables meeting every triple \(\{a_u,b_{uv},c_v\}\). Every matching of size \(\nu(G)\) yields \(\nu(G)\) pairwise-disjoint triples, so such a variable transversal must have at least \(\nu(G)\) variables. Conversely, Kőnig's bipartite matching theorem gives a vertex cover \(C\subseteq U\sqcup V\) of size \(\nu(G)\). Setting to zero the \(a_u\) or \(c_v\) associated with \(C\) meets all triples. Thus the minimum transversal cardinality is exactly \(\nu(G)\); the free higher-path factor does not change codimension.

**Asymptotic count and coefficient.** At \(z=0\), divide (1) by \(|J|=q^{w+4e}\):
\[
\Pr(x^3=0)=\sum_{S\subseteq W}(1-q^{-1})^{|S|}q^{|S|-w-e_G(S)}.\tag{4}
\]
Alternatively the number of roots is the polynomial \(q^{3e}\sum_S(q-1)^{|S|}q^{e-e_G(S)}\). Its degree is \(3e+e+\max_S(|S|-e_G(S))\). The elementary identity \(\max_S(|S|-e_G(S))=\alpha(G)=w-\nu(G)\) follows because every induced edge can be eliminated from \(S\) by deleting at most one vertex per edge, producing an independent subset of size at least \(|S|-e_G(S)\), while any maximum independent set attains equality. The final equality is Kőnig's theorem. Therefore the degree is \(w+4e-\nu(G)\), and its leading coefficient is exactly the positive integer \(c_G\) displayed above. This proves (2). Geometrically the same coefficient counts the top-dimensional coordinate components: their distinct intersections have strictly lower dimension, so their highest-degree point-count contributions do not overlap.

**Near uniformity.** The event that all \(w\) outer arrow coefficients are nonzero has probability \((1-q^{-1})^w\). Conditional on this event, multiplication by the nonzero scalars \(a_u c_v\) is a bijection of each independent uniformly distributed \(b_{uv}\), so the entire cubic target is exactly uniform on \(J^3\). Decompose the unconditional law into this uniform part and its complement and apply the convexity bound for total variation distance. This gives \(\|\mathcal L(x^3)-\mathrm{Unif}(J^3)\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\le1-(1-q^{-1})^w\le w/q\), as claimed.

## Verification

A self-contained Python 3 checker, `check.py`, implements two separate tests: exhaustive enumeration of the three arrow-coordinate families and the independent induced-edge support formula, followed by literal path-basis multiplication of every radical element in three small algebras. All prescribed targets were checked over \(\mathbb F_2\) for six graphs and over \(\mathbb F_3\) for five graphs. The full path-basis replay checked \(64\), \(729\), and \(2048\) radical elements for the one-edge and two-edge cases, including all higher-path coordinates. The checker also verifies the matching/independence identity and leading coefficient for each graph; the recorded output is `checker_output.txt`. These finite checks do not replace the proof for arbitrary graphs or prime powers.

Examples of the leading constants are \(c_G=3^r\) for a matching of \(r\) disjoint edges, \(c_G=1\) for a star with at least two leaves, and \(c_G=2\) for the four-cycle \(K_{2,2}\). Each example follows by evaluating the explicit subset count, and the checker covers small instances.

## Relationship to prior work

Kadison and Young, arXiv:1302.1730 (first posted 2013-02-07), Section 4, explicitly give the positive-length path basis and the radical grading of an acyclic quiver algebra. Their theorem concerns subalgebra depth, not cubic-power fibers. Bouchat, Hà and O'Keefe, arXiv:1008.4829 (first posted 2010-08-28), study monomial path ideals of directed rooted trees, their Betti numbers, and resolutions; general squarefree-monomial geometry and Kőnig's theorem are treated as background here. The monomial-ideal interpretation, as an abstract technique, is not claimed new.

The published results *Complete power-map fibers in radical-square-zero quiver unit groups* (published-finding corpus, 2026-10-25) concern powers in **units of a quotient with \(J^2=0\)** and hence have neither the current cubic radical map nor its nonzero third radical layer. *Pairwise-annihilating tuple varieties of radical-square-zero quiver algebras* (published-finding corpus, 2026-11-02) count pairwise products of **different** elements when the radical square is zero, not prescribed cubes in an algebra with nonzero third layer. *Commuting-pair polynomial for the three-step multiarrow line quiver* (published-finding corpus, 2026-11-02) counts commuting **pairs**, not cubic roots. A prior exact cubic-fiber result for \(N_5(k)\) covers a different single chain algebra and does not furnish equation (1) for arbitrary graphs. The matching-number consequence also uses classical facts and is not asserted to be a new theorem about matching itself.

## Limitations

This is an exact count for a four-layer, two-pole quiver family, not an assertion about all acyclic quivers, general relations on paths, parallel arrows, or arbitrary power exponents. The large-field limit fixes the graph; it does not claim a uniform bound when graph size grows with \(q\). The coefficient \(c_G\) is not generally determined by the matching number alone. Exact historical priority of the combined prescribed-cube count is not proved by database search, and squarefree-monomial formulations outside the checked literature might imply special cases. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
