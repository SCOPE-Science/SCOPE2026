# Scientific review

## Correctness

PASS. The two-pin annihilator is exactly the set of binomials \(b(z^d-(-1)^d)\) divisible by \((1+z)^t\) modulo \(p^r\). For \(d=p^a m\), \(p\nmid m\), Lucas' digit criterion and the elementary binomial identity give \(\nu_p\binom{d}{j}=a-\nu_p(j)\) for \(1\le j\le p^a\). This proves the precise index and full-image cutoff. The quotient-with-remainder argument gives an explicit decoder, and Haar conditional measures prove the information formula. Finite checks support, but do not replace, the proof. Remaining limits: no claim for finite rings, noisy observations, or arbitrary cellular rules.

## Originality

PASS within available evidence. Nowak-Kępczyk (arXiv:2511.17389) examines prime-field Frobenius replica recovery on a two-dimensional lattice, not images of two-pin cylinders modulo prime powers. Castronuovo et al. (arXiv:2611.16746) give global group-CA entropy methods, not the finite-coordinate conditional-image stratification. Akın et al. (arXiv:2306.10838, §§3–5) provide the general duality formalism for modular linear cellular automata and global entropy, but not two-site conditional images, their indices or the information staircase. Existing finite-field mask-surjectivity results determine only a collapse time and do not entail the intermediate prime-power-ring image indices. Exact searches for the prime-adic staircase, alternative dual and reconstruction formulations, and relevant modular-automaton literature found no matching statement. A residual historical-priority risk remains because not every older specialized full text was available.

## Value

PASS. A surjective, Haar-preserving dynamics can retain a quantized amount of information about two specific input coordinates while forgetting them altogether at a sharp distance-dependent time. The complete classification for every prime, ring exponent, distance and positive time includes explicit decoders and distinguishes nonreduced rings from their finite-field reductions. This is a motivated structural local-information invariant, not a numerical sample or a global-entropy restatement.
