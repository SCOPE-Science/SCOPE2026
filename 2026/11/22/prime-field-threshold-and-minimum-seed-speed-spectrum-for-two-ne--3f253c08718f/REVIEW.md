# Scientific review — two-neighbor projective-plane bootstrap seeds

## Correctness — PASS
The graph process is equivalent to closure under pairs of point joins and pairs of line meets, not to the line-filling process used in related geometric percolation work. The symbolic proof classifies every four-vertex composition, rules out the balanced two-point/two-line case with an explicit closed ten-vertex container, normalizes every nondegenerate four-seed to a prime-subplane generating configuration, and constructs a five-point generating seed for every proper finite-field extension. For prime orders, ordered projective frames and avoiding triangle–line flags give the displayed exact count. Two standalone exhaustive programs derive the \(q=2,3,4\) finite census from separate finite-field implementations and independently reproduce every labeled seed and synchronous round count. Computation is not used as evidence for untested infinite orders.

## Originality — PASS, with specified partial overlap
Morris (2009) establishes the standard graph bootstrap framework on grids, not projective-plane incidence graphs. Gerbner et al. (2016) establish results for a different *line-filling* percolation rule, not vertex-level two-neighbor activation. The published subfield/colour-refinement result (2026) already gives a coordinate-generation argument and a five-point individualization threshold, and therefore provides substantive pre-existing coverage of the subfield mechanism and point-only threshold; these ingredients are credited and not claimed independently new. The prior Heawood \(P_3\)-convexity census covers the binary case. The narrower mathematical contribution assessed as original is the complete mixed point/line minimum-seed classification and prime-order count, together with the independently determined full quaternary seed composition-by-completion-time spectrum. Searches of mathematical research records and primary literature did not locate an exact earlier statement or stronger theorem implying that combined classification. This does not establish absolute priority.

## Value — PASS
The object is the graph's natural irreversible two-neighbor process, with an exact field-extension obstruction and sharp minimum infection size. The mixed-seed classification distinguishes feasible projective frames and nonincident triangle–line placements from balanced placements, a distinction that neither the basic coordinate-generation lemma nor all-point individualization specifies. At the smallest proper extension, the 42-vertex incidence graph admits 334,656 minimal five-vertex seeds in six point/line compositions, but only three possible completion times. The exact joint table is a reproducible natural finite benchmark for infection dynamics and explains how minimum set size and propagation time differ. These are not arbitrary graph slices or a mere recovery of the previously known prime-field coordinate lemma.
