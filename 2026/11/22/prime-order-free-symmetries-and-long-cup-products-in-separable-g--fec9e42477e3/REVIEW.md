# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. A fixed geometric point is equivalent to an invariant carrier simplex. At prime order every edge orbit is a connected spanning cycle, forbidden by non-two-connectivity; at composite order a divisor-step edge orbit is a disconnected fixed face. Babson et al.'s Theorem 3.1 supplies the connectivity of the cover. The group homology and cohomology spectral sequences give the exact stated degree range, and a classifying-map pullback gives every nonzero cup power. The finite enumeration reproduces the expected smallest instances. Higher integral homology and exact LS category are not asserted.

## Originality
PASS relative to the checked records. The primary 1997 source gives the cover's wedge homotopy and top rational symmetric-group character, including a cyclic-invariant table, but does not state the integral low-degree cohomology ring of the ordinary cyclic quotient. Kahn–Saks–Sturtevant supplies adjacent graph-property group-action context, not this computation in material inspected. Related directed multipath and chessboard quotients are not equivalent complexes. Searches cannot establish absolute novelty; the result is a derived application of the known wedge theorem, and an unindexed equivalent corollary may exist.

## Value
PASS. The graph complex arises naturally from separability and knot-invariant topology. The prime/composite freeness boundary explains which label rotations lead to genuine cyclic coverings, while the unbounded family of nonzero integral cup products and lower LS-category bounds reveals topology invisible to the prior rational top-homology character. The result is an all-prime structural family, not a chosen small-parameter recomputation.
