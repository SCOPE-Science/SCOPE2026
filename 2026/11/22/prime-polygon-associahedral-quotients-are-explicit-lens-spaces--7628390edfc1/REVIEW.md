# Mathematical review: Prime polygon associahedral quotients are explicit lens spaces

## Correctness — PASS
The stated object is exactly the simplicial complex of noncrossing diagonals of an \(n\)-gon. The regular GKZ associahedron is an \((n-3)\)-dimensional simple polytope, with dual boundary precisely this complex (Ceballos–Santos–Ziegler, Section 3.1). For its GKZ vectors, the area and first-moment identities give the three affine constraints explicitly. The GKZ dimension theorem makes these the entire affine direction. The rotation-invariant interior barycenter permits equivariant polar duality and radial projection to the sphere of the Fourier subrepresentation with harmonics zero and one deleted. For prime \(p\), every surviving nonzero frequency is faithful; for composite \(n\), the divisor-frequency construction gives a genuine geometric fixed point. Consequently the free prime action yields *the stated weighted lens space*, not just a homology analogue. The fundamental group, homology, and nonvanishing cup powers follow from the standard lens-space calculation. The standalone enumerator and weight verifier passed for the documented finite cases. The proof is uniform; computations are neither substituted for nor claimed to certify the infinite theorem.

## Originality — PASS, conditional on accessible published literature
The three closely relevant primary sources were materially inspected: the 2004 cyclic-sieving theorem for polygon dissections concerns fixed **finite faces**, the 2011 GKZ realization exposition gives polytope dimensions/faces, and the 2014 standard-associahedron exposition proves dihedral isometries. None of the inspected relevant sections states an orbit-space homeomorphism or the Fourier lens weights. Additional searches for associahedral rotation quotients, prime-polygon lens spaces, cluster complexes, and alternate formulations did not locate a covering statement. Related indexed findings about lens-space quotients of cyclic *nonmatching* complexes or Rips complexes act on different simplicial objects; no implication to the associahedral quotient was established. A plausible unindexed derivation or later full-text source remains a publication-priority risk, so this is not a claim of global uniqueness.

## Value — PASS
The standard associahedron is a canonical parameter space for triangulations, so understanding the effect of its natural cyclic symmetry is a motivated topological question. The exact weights identify a full homeomorphism type and give odd-degree integral torsion in arbitrarily many degrees as the prime grows. Neither the cyclic-sieving fixed-face enumeration nor the existence of an isometric polygon action alone determines these invariants. The sharp composite/prime geometric freeness criterion distinguishes the full action on the sphere from action on vertices of the associahedron. The result is an all-prime natural classification rather than a finite table, an arbitrary slice, or a restatement of a pre-existing homology computation.
