An idealized two-transit exoplanet with candidate periods \(\Delta/k\), \(1\le k\le N\), needs exactly one predetermined follow-up probe for each prime power at most \(N\) to identify every alias, and this optimum survives sufficiently short transit windows. Restricting the same probes to an exact regular grid requires a denominator divisible by \(\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,N)\), revealing a sharp difference between flexible and rigid schedules.
