# Prime-power torsion in cyclic quotients of friendship-graph independence complexes

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge4\), let \(F_n\) denote the friendship graph (\(n\) triangles meeting at one common vertex). Let \(C_n=\langle r\rangle\) act by moving each triangle to the next while preserving its two noncentral vertex types. Let \(Y_n\) be the positive-dimensional connected component of the geometric quotient \(|\operatorname{Ind}(F_n)|/C_n\). Then
\[
H_2(Y_n;\mathbb Z)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z,&n=p^a\quad(p\text{ prime},\ a\ge1),\\
0,&n\text{ has at least two distinct prime factors}.
\end{cases}
\]
The full geometric quotient has one additional isolated-point component. The connected component \(Y_n\) is simply connected. Thus the appearance of second integral torsion detects exactly prime-power numbers of petals.

More precisely, put \(W_n=\{x\in\mathbb R^n:\sum_i x_i=0\}\), with \(C_n\) permuting coordinates. There is a natural homeomorphism
\[
Y_n\cong\Sigma\bigl(S(W_n)/C_n\bigr).
\]
For odd primes \(p\ge5\), the latter is the suspension of the lens space \(L(p;1,2,\ldots,(p-1)/2)\). In particular \(H_{2j}(Y_p;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z\) for \(1\le j\le(p-3)/2\) and \(H_{p-1}(Y_p;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z\), with all other positive integral homology vanishing. By contrast, allowing all \(n!\) petal permutations yields a closed \((n-1)\)-disk as the positive-dimensional quotient component.

## Assumptions and scope
All graphs are finite, simple and unweighted. Index the noncentral vertices by \(a_i,b_i\), \(i\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\), with edges \(a_ib_i\) and every \(a_i,b_i\) adjacent to the common center \(c\). The cyclic generator sends \(a_i\mapsto a_{i+1}\), \(b_i\mapsto b_{i+1}\), \(c\mapsto c\). A face of \(\operatorname{Ind}(F_n)\) is an independent vertex set. Geometric quotient means the actual topological orbit space of the realization, **not** the naive simplicial quotient on vertex orbits. Write \(S(W_n)\) for the unit sphere in \(W_n\), and \(\Sigma\) for unreduced suspension. Only the second integral homology of general composite orders is classified; at prime orders the full integral homology is also given.

## Proof
**(1) Identify the covering complex exactly.** Any independent set containing \(c\) is the singleton \(\{c\}\). Any other independent set chooses at most one element from each pair \(\{a_i,b_i\}\). These latter faces form the join \((S^0)^{*n}\), the boundary of the standard \(n\)-cross-polytope on vertices \(\{\pm e_i\}\). Consequently its geometric realization is \(C_n\)-equivariantly homeomorphic, by radial normalization, to the sphere \(S(\mathbb R^n)\); the center contributes a distinct isolated point.

**(2) Isolate the fixed axis.** Orthogonally and equivariantly, \(\mathbb R^n=\mathbb R(1,\ldots,1)\oplus W_n\). Therefore \(S(\mathbb R^n)\) is the join \(S^0*S(W_n)\). The group fixes the \(S^0\) factor, so taking the quotient commutes with that join and gives \(Y_n\cong\Sigma(S(W_n)/C_n)\). Since \(n\ge4\), \(S(W_n)\cong S^{n-2}\) is connected and simply connected.

**(3) Determine every fixed-point subgroup.** A power \(r^k\) has precisely \(d=\gcd(n,k)\) coordinate cycles. Its invariant subspace in \(\mathbb R^n\) has dimension \(d\), and the zero-sum condition cuts its dimension to \(d-1\). A nonzero fixed point on \(S(W_n)\) therefore exists exactly when \(\gcd(n,k)>1\). No numerical search is used in this equivalence.

**(4) Compute the orbit fundamental group.** Armstrong's orbit-space theorem for a finite simplicial group action on a simply connected polyhedron gives \(\pi_1(S(W_n)/C_n)\cong C_n/N\), where \(N\) is generated by powers having fixed points. After equivariant triangulation, the theorem applies to the finite orthogonal action here. If \(n=p^a\), the eligible exponents are exactly the multiples of \(p\), and hence \(N=\langle r^p\rangle\), giving quotient \(C_p\). If distinct primes \(p,q\) divide \(n\), then \(r^p,r^q\in N\), and because \(\gcd(p,q)=1\) these generate \(C_n\), giving the trivial quotient. This exhausts all \(n\ge4\).

**(5) Transfer fundamental groups to homology.** The Hurewicz identification in degree one gives \(H_1(S(W_n)/C_n;\mathbb Z)\) equal to the abelianization of the preceding cyclic fundamental group, hence exactly \(\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z\) or zero. The suspension isomorphism gives \(H_2(Y_n;\mathbb Z)\cong\widetilde H_1(S(W_n)/C_n;\mathbb Z)\). The suspension of a connected space is simply connected, proving the remaining claim about \(Y_n\).

**(6) Prime-order and full-symmetry refinements.** For odd prime \(p\), the Fourier decomposition \(W_p\cong\bigoplus_{k=1}^{(p-1)/2}\mathbb C_k\) gives complex weight \(k\) for the coordinate shift (with a consistent Fourier orientation). Every nonidentity rotation acts without a fixed point on \(S(W_p)\); its quotient is, by definition, \(L(p;1,2,\ldots,(p-1)/2)\). The standard one-cell-per-dimension lens-space cellular chain complex has alternating boundaries \(0\) and multiplication by \(p\), giving the stated integral homology after suspension. Finally, \(S(W_n)/S_n\) is a spherical fundamental chamber for coordinate order, hence a closed \((n-2)\)-ball. Its suspension is a closed \((n-1)\)-ball. The isolated apex point remains separate.

## Verification
The accompanying `checker.py` independently enumerates all petal-compatible faces for \(4\le n\le8\), verifies their cross-polytope face numbers and Euler characteristics, enumerates coordinate cycles for every nonidentity rotation for \(4\le n\le50\), and checks the prime-power subgroup order against exact integer factorization. These finite tests are a reproducibility check, not a substitute for the argument covering all \(n\ge4\). Run `python3 checker.py` from the directory containing this document and script; the final line must be `PASS: all exact tests`.

## Relationship to prior work
The nonequivariant sphere-wedge and cross-polytopal structure of independence complexes of chordal graphs is classical, including Adamaszek (2015) and the later discrete-Morse treatment by Barik, Mondal and Mukherjee (first public 14 April 2026). Armstrong's orbit-space fundamental-group theorem and the integral homology of lens spaces are used as established tools, not claimed as new. The new scope is the exact prime-power-versus-mixed-prime torsion dichotomy for the **geometric cyclic quotient** of this natural graph family, including the prime-order lens-space and full-symmetric disk contrast. Related higher-independence results for windmills concern different threshold complexes and primarily homology representations, not the integral second homology of the ordinary cyclic geometric quotient.

## Limitations
No formula is asserted for integral homology above degree two when \(n\) is composite, nor for arbitrary subgroups of the full friendship-graph automorphism group. The result relies on the established Armstrong theorem and standard lens-space homology, so its originality consists in the precise graph-symmetry realization and arithmetic threshold, not in those general theorems. Publication priority cannot be guaranteed against unindexed equivalent work. Independent mathematical validation has not been performed.
