# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The face classification is exact, including the isolated apex component. An equivariant radial identification realizes the ordinary independence sphere as the coordinate permutation sphere; the diagonal splitting gives an actual suspension orbit-space model. For every rotation power the fixed subspace in the zero-sum representation has dimension \(\gcd(n,k)-1\). The original Armstrong (1965) theorem identifies the orbit fundamental group with the group modulo its fixed-point elements, from which the prime-power criterion follows by elementary cyclic-subgroup arithmetic. Suspension and the degree-one Hurewicz theorem give the claimed second integral homology. Standard Fourier decomposition and lens-space cellular homology prove the prime refinement. The exact checker supports the combinatorial step on small instances and the arithmetic step through order 50; the all-order proof does not depend on extrapolation.

## Originality — PASS, with priority risk
The April 2026 source develops nonequivariant Morse reductions for chordal-graph independence complexes; its full HTML paper contains neither this cyclic quotient nor a prime-power integral torsion law. Adamaszek's 2015 full-text paper characterizes chordal independence complexes and cross-polytope cores, but does not calculate these symmetry quotients. Armstrong's 1965 theorem is general and supplies the orbit fundamental-group tool, not the exact fixed-element computation for petal rotations. Hatcher's classical lens-space calculation is explicitly credited. Indexed nearby results concerning higher-independence windmills and suspended lens quotients of graph associahedra have different complexes and do not state this specific arithmetic classification. However, the argument is a fairly direct application of powerful classical machinery, so an unindexed explicit specialization remains a meaningful priority risk. One particularly relevant windmill archive was not accessible in full; its indexed claim concerns threshold-two and higher complexes, rather than this ordinary-independence orbit space.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The prime-power dichotomy is a complete, natural, all-parameter classification of the first torsion group in a highly structured family: connected planar chordal graphs have torsion-free covering-space homology, yet their genuine cyclic symmetry quotients have arbitrarily large prime torsion in the same fixed low degree. The arithmetic contrast with the two-distinct-prime case and the total disappearance of this torsion after full petal symmetrization are structural boundary phenomena, not arbitrary finite enumerations. The proof records explicit group actions and topological homeomorphisms that can be reused when comparing other graph-independence orbit spaces; it does not claim a new general theory of lens spaces.

## Closest literature and limitations
Barik–Mondal–Mukherjee (arXiv:2604.12606), Adamaszek (arXiv:1508.02426), and Armstrong (1965) provide important components. Integral higher homology at composite orders and arbitrary subgroup quotients remain outside the claim. Full review of all potentially unindexed work has not been performed.
