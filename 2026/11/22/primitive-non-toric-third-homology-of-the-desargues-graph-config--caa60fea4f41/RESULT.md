# Primitive non-toric third homology of the Desargues graph configuration space

## Finding
Let \(D\) be the bipartite graph on the two-element and three-element subsets of \(\{0,1,2,3,4\}\), with an edge for each inclusion of a two-set into a three-set. Thus \(D\) is the twenty-vertex, thirty-edge Desargues graph (equivalently the bipartite Kneser graph \(H(5,2)\), or \(\operatorname{GP}(10,3)\)). Let \(B_3(D)\) be the continuous space of three **unordered, pairwise distinct** particles on the geometric realization \(|D|\).

For every unordered triple of pairwise vertex-disjoint simple cycles in \(D\), the independently moving particles give an embedded three-torus in \(B_3(D)\). Let \(L\) be the subgroup of integral degree-three homology generated by all these geometric product-torus classes. Then
\[
\boxed{H_3(B_3(D);\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{21},\qquad
L\cong\mathbb Z^{20},\qquad
H_3(B_3(D);\mathbb Z)/L\cong\mathbb Z.}
\]
The graph has **exactly twenty** unordered triples of pairwise vertex-disjoint simple cycles, and every such triple consists of three hexagons. Their twenty torus classes are independent and form a **primitive direct summand**, with precisely one complementary integral homology generator not generated by disjoint-cycle product tori.

An associated exact torsion consequence is
\[
\boxed{H_2(B_3(D);\mathbb Z)[2]\cong(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^5,}
\]
where the bracket denotes elements annihilated by multiplication by two; no assertion is made about the exponents of the full two-primary torsion subgroup.

## Assumptions and scope
The space is the ordinary unordered collision-free **continuous** graph configuration space, not the unsubdivided deleted-cell subcomplex. All abelian groups use integral singular homology unless a coefficient field is stated. The twenty tori are precisely products of three *vertex-disjoint simple graph cycles*, each used by one particle; other possible geometric three-cycles are not assumed to have this form. The result concerns exactly the incidence graph above and particle number three, not all bipartite cubic graphs or all generalized Petersen graphs.

## Proof
**1. An exact integral chain model.** The reduced Świątkowski comparison theorem and all-vertex reduction [2] identify the homology of \(B_3(D)\) with that of the particle-weight-three reduced chain complex \(\widetilde S(D)_3\). At every vertex \(v\), lexicographically order its three incident graph edges as \(e_0(v),e_1(v),e_2(v)\), and use local degree-one generators \(a_i(v)=h_i(v)-h_0(v)\) for \(i=1,2\), with \(\partial a_i(v)=e_i(v)-e_0(v)\). Edge variables have chain degree zero and particle weight one. Local generators are exterior-ordered by vertex and carry degree and weight one.

In particle weight three, the chain group in degree three is generated by triples of distinct vertices, with two local-generator choices per vertex; in degree two there are two local generators at distinct vertices and one edge variable. Consequently,
\[
\operatorname{rank} C_3=\binom{20}{3}2^3=9120,\qquad
\operatorname{rank} C_2=\binom{20}{2}2^2\cdot30=22800,
\qquad C_4=0.
\]
For an ordered degree-three monomial \(a_{i_1}(v_1)a_{i_2}(v_2)a_{i_3}(v_3)\), the differential is exactly
\[
\partial_3=\sum_{j=1}^{3}(-1)^{j-1}
\bigl(e_{i_j}(v_j)-e_0(v_j)\bigr)
\prod_{k\ne j}a_{i_k}(v_k).
\]
This is an explicitly defined \(22800\)-by-\(9120\) signed integer matrix, with at most six nonzero terms per column.

**2. An integral kernel basis, with a finite exact upper bound.** Use lexicographically ordered two-subsets followed by lexicographically ordered three-subsets as the twenty graph vertices, increasing-edge indices, and lexicographic chain bases. The supplied `desargues_certificate.json` records twenty-one distinct free column indices:

`[8895,8897,8898,8987,8997,8998,9045,9048,9051,9055,9056,9059,9060,9095,9098,9103,9104,9105,9109,9114,9117]`.

The standard-library program `VERIFY.py` constructs every signed column of \(\partial_3\) and verifies that **the other 9,099 columns are independent modulo three**. Thus their rank over \(\mathbb Q\) is at least 9,099. The certificate supplies twenty-one integer columns \(z_1,\ldots,z_{21}\), each with exactly zero integral boundary, whose coefficients on the twenty-one excluded chain positions form the identity matrix. They are therefore independent over \(\mathbb Z\). These opposing rank inequalities force
\[
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Q}\partial_3=9099,
\qquad\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Q}\ker\partial_3=21.
\]
More strongly, the twenty-one recorded integral cycles form a **full integral basis**. Indeed, any integral kernel element has twenty-one integral coordinates on the excluded positions; subtracting the same integer combination of the \(z_i\) yields a kernel element supported entirely on the 9,099 independent columns, hence zero over \(\mathbb Q\) and therefore zero over \(\mathbb Z\). Since \(C_4=0\), this proves the asserted \(H_3\) exactly.

**3. Exhaustive geometric torus classes and primitivity.** Every simple cycle is enumerated once by beginning at its least vertex and selecting only the orientation in which the second vertex has smaller index than the last. The exhaustive enumeration yields 1,456 simple cycles of all lengths and exactly twenty unordered triples with disjoint vertex sets; each triple consists of three six-cycles. For an oriented simple cycle \(c\), the reduced-chain loop representative is the sum of the incoming-minus-outgoing half-edge differences over all its vertices. The signed exterior product of three such representatives gives its product three-torus class, and the verifier checks every integral boundary is zero.

Express the twenty product chains in the integral kernel basis from step 2 by reading their twenty-one excluded chain coordinates. The resulting integer \(21\)-by-\(20\) matrix, recorded in the certificate, has the determinant \(+1\) after omitting its zero-based row number seven. `VERIFY.py` recomputes this exact minor using division-exact Bareiss elimination. Consequently the twenty torus classes generate a primitive rank-twenty sublattice of \(H_3\cong\mathbb Z^{21}\). Its quotient is free of rank one, and no disjoint-simple-cycle product three-torus represents the missing generator.

**4. Two-torsion detected, without guessing its exponent.** A separate exhaustive Gaussian elimination of \(\partial_3\) over \(\mathbb F_2\) has rank 9,094. Hence \(\dim_{\mathbb F_2}H_3(B_3(D);\mathbb F_2)=9120-9094=26\). The universal coefficient exact sequence in degree three identifies the additional five mod-two dimensions, beyond the twenty-one integral free generators, with \(\operatorname{Tor}(H_2(B_3(D);\mathbb Z),\mathbb F_2)\). For a finitely generated abelian group this equals its subgroup of elements of order at most two. Therefore that subgroup is \((\mathbb Z/2)^5\), exactly as claimed. This does not distinguish possible cyclic summands of orders two, four, eight, and so forth.

## Verification
Place `VERIFY.py` beside `desargues_certificate.json` and run `python3 VERIFY.py` with Python 3.10 or later. Only the standard library is required. The program independently reconstructs the incidence graph, all reduced chain bases, all signed degree-three boundary columns, the rank-9,099 characteristic-three witness, the rank-9,094 characteristic-two calculation, the twenty-one integral null cycles, every simple graph cycle, every disjoint triple, all twenty signed product torus chains, and the determinant-one primitivity minor. It succeeds with `DESARGUES_GRAPH_THREE_POINT_TOPOLOGY_CERTIFICATE_OK`. The certificate is finite and exhaustive for the stated twenty-vertex graph; no numerical or stochastic rank estimate is used.

## Relationship to prior work
Abrams [1] initiated the discrete graph-configuration approach, while An, Drummond-Cole and Knudsen [2], Theorem 4.5 and Proposition 4.9, supply the exact reduced homology-computing chain model, **not** the present evaluated kernel or torus sublattice. Maciążek and Sawicki [3] describe product and nonproduct homology cycles on graph configuration spaces, but their inspected complete article gives no Desargues three-particle computation. The available Desargues-specific two-particle classification [4] gives \(H_2(B_2(D);\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{35}\) and hexagon-pair generators; changing particle number does not imply the three-particle result. The available three-particle dodecahedral and Pappus calculations [5,6] concern different graphs (with ranks twenty-six and seven, respectively). The statements, rather than just titles or shared chain methods, were compared; none of these accessible results establishes this rank-twenty primitive triple-hexagon torus lattice or its rank-one complement.

## Limitations
No full integral \(H_1\) or \(H_2\), two-primary exponents, cup products, fundamental-group presentation, automorphism representation, or complete homotopy type is claimed. This is a finite exact topological computation, not a general parameter theorem. A mathematical-record and literature search cannot establish absolute publication priority: unindexed equivalent calculations or a stronger theorem not surfaced in the searches remain possible. The proof relies on the published comparison theorem and on independently checkable finite certificates, rather than on a computer proof of the comparison theorem. Independent mathematical review has not been performed.
