# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS
Axiomatic uniqueness of point joins and line intersections gives the precise new distance-two nonedges after any chosen star of \(t\le q\) incidences is removed. The point-shore clique complex is two simplices with a nonempty simplex intersection; the line-shore complex has a cone vertex. Every mixed clique has at most one vertex on one shore, because the incidence graph has no quadrilateral. Thus two contractible cover pieces intersect in the current connected incidence graph. Their suspension homotopy pushout yields exactly \(q^3-t\) integral two-spheres. Natural Mayer–Vietoris maps and graph spanning-tree fundamental cycles give the split, primitive inclusion on homology. The endpoint leaf is dominated. Exact finite checks for both small projective planes and every permitted fault stage agree, but are not used to prove the infinite family.

## Value — PASS
Incidence loss at one vertex is a natural fault model in projective-plane geometry. The theorem gives a full all-order stability interpolation, not just a finite Betti table: one and only one primitive integral two-sphere disappears per faulty incidence and every comparison map splits, without torsion. The last connected stage is proved to collapse onto the earlier vertex-puncture topology. This identifies a sharp per-edge law and its endpoint mechanism.

## Limitations
Only a single star of at most \(q\) missing flags and inclusive radius two are included. Arbitrary failures and larger scales are open here. The finite verifier tests orders two and three only; correctness for any finite plane rests on the geometric proof. Bibliographic priority is qualified and no independent audit has occurred.
