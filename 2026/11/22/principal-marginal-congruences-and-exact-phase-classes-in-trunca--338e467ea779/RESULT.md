# Principal marginal congruences and exact phase classes in truncated monomial inverse semigroups

## Finding

Let integers \(m>n\ge2\) be fixed. Let \(\mu_n\) be the complex roots of unity of order dividing \(n\), and let \(S_{m,n}\) be the finite inverse semigroup introduced by Duan and Zhang: its elements are zero and all partial monomial matrices \(A(I,J;\zeta)\) of rank at most \(n\), where \(I,J\subseteq[m]\) are nonempty subsets of the same size, the support map is the unique increasing bijection \(J\to I\), the coefficient on its first arrow is \(\zeta\in\mu_n\), and every other coefficient is one. Let \(\pi(A)\) be the undecorated support map. For \(\omega(x)=x^n\), write \(A\sim B\) when \((AX)^n=(BX)^n\) and \((XA)^n=(XB)^n\) for every \(X\in S_{m,n}\), and write \(\equiv\) for the inverse-semigroup congruence generated by \(\sim\).

**Theorem.** Put \(t=\binom mn\) and \(h=\binom{m-2}{n-2}\). For all \(A,B\in S_{m,n}\), the following hold.

1. The marginal congruence is exactly the support kernel:
   \[
   A\equiv B\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \pi(A)=\pi(B).
   \]
   Thus \(S_{m,n}/\!\equiv\) is the inverse semigroup of all increasing partial bijections of \([m]\) with rank at most \(n\), and its cardinality is \(1+\sum_{r=1}^n\binom mr^2\). This is also the greatest idempotent-separating congruence of \(S_{m,n}\).
2. This congruence is **principal**: choose any rank-\(n\) set \(I\subseteq[m]\) containing both endpoints and any primitive \(n\)-th root \(\zeta\). Then it is generated by the single marginally equivalent pair \((d_I(\zeta),P_I)\), where \(P_I\) is the diagonal projection supported on \(I\) and \(d_I(\zeta)=A(I,I;\zeta)\).
3. The marginal equivalence classes are exact. If \(\pi(A)\ne\pi(B)\), then \(A\not\sim B\). If the supports agree and have rank \(r<n\), then \(A\sim B\), regardless of phase. If their common support has rank \(n\), distinct phases are marginally equivalent **if and only if** both the domain and the image contain \(1\) and \(m\). Hence the number of \(\sim\)-classes is
   \[
   1+\sum_{r=1}^{n-1}\binom mr^2+h^2+n(t^2-h^2)
   =1+\sum_{r=1}^{n}\binom mr^2+(n-1)(t^2-h^2).
   \]
4. Among uniformly sampled ordered pairs conditioned on \(A\equiv B\), the proportion that also satisfy \(A\sim B\) tends, for each fixed \(n\), to \(1/n\) as \(m\to\infty\), with error \(O_n(m^{-2})\).

## Assumptions and scope

All products are ordinary complex matrix products. Composition and inversion stay within \(S_{m,n}\), as proved in Duan--Zhang, Proposition 2.1. When \(A\ne0\), the domain of \(\pi(A)\) consists of the columns containing a nonzero entry, and its image consists of the occupied rows. Rank is the common cardinality of these two sets. The zero matrix has empty support and forms a singleton class for both relations. The symbol \(d_I(\zeta)\) denotes the diagonal matrix whose first occupied diagonal position has value \(\zeta\), with other occupied positions equal to one. The classification is for all integers \(m>n\ge2\), with no assumption that \(n\) is odd.

## Proof

**Support separation.** The map \(\pi\) is an inverse-semigroup homomorphism. If \(\pi(A)\ne\pi(B)\), choose an arrow \(j\mapsto i\) of \(\pi(A)\) absent from \(\pi(B)\) (interchange the matrices if necessary). Set \(X=E_{ji}\). Then \(AX=\xi E_{ii}\) for some \(\xi\in\mu_n\), so \((AX)^n=E_{ii}\), whereas \(BX\) is zero or a non-diagonal rank-one matrix unit times a scalar, and \((BX)^n=0\). Hence \(A\not\sim B\). Since \(\ker\pi\) is a congruence, this proves \(\equiv\subseteq\ker\pi\).

**Marginal equivalence inside a support.** Suppose \(A=A(I,J;\alpha)\) and \(B=A(I,J;\beta)\) have equal nonempty support. Put \(\delta=\alpha\beta^{-1}\), \(c_R=AB^*=d_I(\delta)\), and \(c_D=B^*A=d_J(\delta)\). Here \(A^*=A^{-1}\). If \(A\sim B\), substitute \(B^*X\) for \(X\) in the left marginal-equivalence identity, obtaining \((c_RX)^n=(P_IX)^n\). Interchange \(A\) and \(B\), make the analogous substitution, then take adjoints to get \((Xc_R)^n=(XP_I)^n\). Thus \(c_R\) is marginal. Applying the same argument to \(A^*\sim B^*\), which follows by taking adjoints in the two identities defining \(\sim\), shows that \(c_D\) is marginal as well. Conversely, if \(c_R,c_D\) are marginal, use \(A=c_RB=Bc_D\) and the marginal insertion identities to obtain \((AX)^n=(BX)^n\) and \((XA)^n=(XB)^n\) for every \(X\). Thus
\[
A\sim B\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad d_I(\delta),d_J(\delta)\in M_{x^n}(S_{m,n}).
\]
By Duan--Zhang, Theorem 2.4, for nontrivial \(\delta\) a diagonal \(d_K(\delta)\) is marginal precisely when \(|K|<n\), or \(|K|=n\) and \(\{1,m\}\subseteq K\). This gives all asserted classes. There are \(\binom mr^2\) supports of rank \(r\); at rank \(n\), exactly \(h^2\) supports have both their domain and range containing both endpoints. The class-count formula follows.

**Generation of the full phase kernel by one pair.** Choose a rank-\(n\) subset \(I\) containing \(\{1,m\}\) and a primitive \(n\)-th root \(\zeta\). Theorem 2.4 gives \(d_I(\zeta)\sim P_I\). Let \(K\) be any nonempty subset of \([m]\) with \(|K|=r\le n\). Choose an \(r\)-element subset \(J\subseteq I\) containing \(\min I=1\) and let \(X=A(K,J;1)\). Then direct multiplication yields
\[
Xd_I(\zeta)X^*=d_K(\zeta),\qquad XP_IX^*=P_K.
\]
Therefore \(d_K(\zeta)\equiv P_K\) for every \(K\) of size at most \(n\). Taking powers, every \(d_K(\zeta^j)\equiv P_K\) for \(0\le j<n\). For any nonzero \(A=A(K,J;\eta)\), write \(A=d_K(\eta)A(K,J;1)\). Thus \(A\equiv A(K,J;1)\). Any two elements with equal support are congruent, proving \(\ker\pi\subseteq\equiv\). The construction uses only the one generating pair and its compatible products and inverses, so the congruence is principal.

**Maximal idempotent separation.** The support kernel separates all idempotents, since the idempotents are exactly the projections \(P_K\). Conversely, in any idempotent-separating congruence \(\rho\), if \(A\mathrel\rho B\), then the idempotents \(AE_{jj}A^*\) and \(BE_{jj}B^*\) are \(\rho\)-equivalent and hence equal, for every \(j\). Their support projections specify the values of the underlying partial maps on every singleton. Therefore \(\pi(A)=\pi(B)\) and \(\rho\subseteq\ker\pi\). This shows maximality.

**Quantitative difference.** The number of ordered pairs in \(\equiv\) is
\[
C_{m,n}=1+n^2\sum_{r=1}^n\binom mr^2,
\]
whereas the number in \(\sim\) is
\[
R_{m,n}=1+n^2\sum_{r=1}^{n-1}\binom mr^2+n^2h^2+n(t^2-h^2).
\]
For fixed \(n\), \(\sum_{r<n}\binom mr^2=O_n(m^{2n-2})\), \(h^2=O_n(m^{2n-4})\), and \(t^2=\Theta_n(m^{2n})\). Consequently \(R_{m,n}/C_{m,n}=1/n+O_n(m^{-2})\).

## Verification

The standalone exact integer program `check_margins.py` constructs every decorated increasing partial bijection of ranks at most \(n\). It implements matrix multiplication by composing the sparse coefficient-decorated partial maps, and independently evaluates the defining power-word equations for all multipliers. It checks every within-support phase pair against the theorem, separates all differing supports by rank-one multipliers, and constructs the least congruence generated by the theorem's single specified pair by closure under multiplication and inversion. It verifies that the resulting partition is exactly the support partition, including all class counts. Its displayed checks cover \((m,n)=(1,2),(2,2),(3,2),(3,3),(4,2),(4,3),(5,2)\), up to 251 elements; these boundary and small-parameter tests corroborate, but do not establish, the theorem for arbitrary parameters. Running `python check_margins.py` prints `VERIFY_OK` and `PRINCIPAL_OK`.

## Relationship to prior work

Duan--Zhang (2026) defined the specific finite semigroups, proved the explicit margin formula (Theorem 2.4), and determined exactly when the marginal equivalence is itself a congruence (Theorem 3.1). In the case \(m\le n\), their proof already identifies equivalence with equality of support maps, which is not claimed as new. In the nonnormal case \(m>n\), they prove strict inclusion \(\sim\subsetneq\equiv\), but do not state the full generated congruence, a principal generating pair, or the precise equivalence-class spectrum. The present result determines these objects using their margin theorem as an essential input. It should be read as a concrete structural completion of their example, not as a new general marginal theory.

Araújo--Araújo--Kinyon (2025) introduced inverse-semigroup marginal relations and their congruence question. Brookes (2020) classified congruences in the larger unrestricted partial wreath product and described greatest idempotent-separating congruences, but did not compute the power-word marginal equivalence on the restricted increasing first-phase family. A related published record on full partial monomial matrices with odd power words determines global-scalar equivalence classes, a different semigroup with a normal margin; that statement does not cover the nonnormal examples considered here.

## Limitations

The result depends on the explicit margin formula of Duan--Zhang and their finite semigroup construction; it does not claim an independent derivation of that formula. The theorem is specific to the rank cutoff equal to the word exponent and to the restricted increasing, first-position phase decoration; no extension to arbitrary partial monomial matrices, different rank cutoffs, or arbitrary inverse semigroups follows. Historical priority is not exhaustively certified; the conclusion is a short consequence of known structural inputs, and alternative presentations of the maximal idempotent-separating congruence could make portions of it familiar. The finite checker is not an infinite proof. Independent audit is not performed.
