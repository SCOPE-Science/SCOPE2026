# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

Grading and class three make all higher-layer choices of the two inputs invisible, giving q^(2(D−d)) lifts per generating pair. In the tensor model F(x,y)=xxy−2xyx+yxx. For nonzero x, a basis with x=e₁ shows that F(x,e_j), j≠1, have distinct multidegrees and a coefficient equal to 1. Thus the kernel is exactly the line ℓ of x and every vector of W_ℓ has q second inputs for that x, including in characteristics two and three. For two distinct lines, put their representatives in one basis as e₁,e₂; their spanning multidegrees have different repeated generators, so the intersection is exactly zero. Changing x within its line rescales the induced isomorphism from V/ℓ to W_ℓ by a nonzero scalar, making the projective identification with planes P containing ℓ canonical. Distinct W_ℓ have disjoint nonzero vectors; this gives both the image size and q(q−1) generating pairs for every nonzero attained target. The zero count is q^d+(q^d−1)q, which yields the displayed probability. Degree three has one basis vector for each multidegree with one repeated and one distinct generator and two for each triple of distinct generators (by Jacobi and independent tensor coefficients), hence dimension d(d−1)+2 binom(d,3)=(d³−d)/3. At d=2 the image beats no dimension bound and has the full q² values; at d≥3 the target dimension exceeds 2d, so unattained targets exist. The three displayed fiber cases are correctly specified; the empty case is not realized in rank two, as the Finding explicitly states.

## originality

PASS

Credit the standard incidence flag embedding and the free-Lie structure, not a new abstract flag variety. The closest representation-theoretic formulation already identifies the complex projective geometry after a standard change of model, but does not establish the ordinary free-Lie word-map result over every finite field, particularly characteristic three, or classify the prescribed affine fibers. Inspected Engel-probability and same-day fiber theorems treat different Lie algebras.
