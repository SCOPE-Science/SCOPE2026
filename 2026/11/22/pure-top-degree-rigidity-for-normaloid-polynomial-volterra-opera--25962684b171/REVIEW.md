# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS
For any real \(\alpha>2\), monomial radial integration produces the explicit gamma-ratio coefficient in (2); its power-law exponent proves polynomial boundedness and compactness. In the critical integer case, compact perturbation of \(a_dS^d\) plus absence of nonzero eigenvectors determines the entire spectral disk. The maximal lower-degree coefficient produces an asymptotically unique collision of two monomial images, with exponent \(q/d-1\), which dominates both diagonal errors and proves a strict norm gap. Exact beta-product moments prove subnormality of the pure critical operator for every integer degree at least three. Numerical beta-moment and witness checks reinforce but do not replace these infinite-dimensional proofs.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)
The new statement concerns **all polynomial symbols** in every super-Gaussian power-Fock Hilbert space and gives a positive asymptotic mixed-symbol norm obstruction. Earlier monomial-only work supplies the pure critical case, and Constantin–Persson (2015) already establishes the exact generalized-Fock spectral disk for all polynomial symbols; neither is claimed as new. Mengestie (2026) settles a formally analogous question for the classical Gaussian exponent two, but cannot imply the result for the faster weights where the critical degree can be any integer three or greater. Mengestie–Ueki (2018), Yang–Zhou (2022), and Mengestie–Takele (2023) provide boundedness and compactness context; their available inspected material does not give this mixed-symbol classification. The 2022 text was not fully accessible, and older weighted-shift normaloid theory remains a priority risk; no failed search proves novelty by itself.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Beyond the known spectrum and compactness classification, this identifies exactly when bounded operators are normaloid, hyponormal, or subnormal and shows that arbitrarily small lower-degree perturbations destroy even the weakest of the three operator-normality properties. A quantified two-monomial witness explains the mechanism and identifies which lower polynomial degree controls the obstruction. This removes a natural ambiguity left by previously known pure monomial and classical Gaussian cases, rather than merely renaming a known classification.

## Scope and status
The theorem is restricted to complex polynomial symbols and real power exponents greater than two. The norm gap is an asymptotic detection result, not a globally sharp numerical norm. The underlying monomial subnormality is previously established; its proof is reproduced for self-containment. The scientific statements have not been independently audited.
