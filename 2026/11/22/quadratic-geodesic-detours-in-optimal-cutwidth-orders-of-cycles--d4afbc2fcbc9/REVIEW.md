**Correctness — PASS.** Each nontrivial prefix has cut boundary twice its number of cyclic components. This yields the binary growth encoding and a complete three-case classification of all legal adjacent swaps. Counting binary switches establishes the degree distribution and edge count. A root-fixed insertion argument supplies optimal Kendall-geodesic paths. Most importantly, conserved particle flow through all \(n-1\) interfaces yields a lower bound on rotated clockwise orders that is matched by an explicit vacancy-transfer schedule. Independent implementations directly enumerate all permutations for \(3\le n\le8\) and construct a binary-state transition graph for \(3\le n\le14\). The proof, not finite testing, supports all \(n\ge3\).

Published adjacent-swap detours for pattern-avoiding permutations concern a different order constraint and do not imply this claim. Searches for equivalent cycle-layout, connected-prefix, clockwise-rotation and particle-flux formulations did not reveal stronger prior coverage; historical unindexed equivalence remains possible. Search absence alone is not treated as proof of novelty.

**Value — PASS.** The example answers a natural algorithmic question exactly at the optimum cutwidth, where the general width-doubling theorem does not apply. It shows an unbounded quadratic gap between unrestricted shortest adjacent-swap distance and feasible optimal-layout distance, while locating a large isometric subclass and giving the full configuration and degree census. This is an infinite family with constructive paths and lower-bound certificates, not a numerological finite slice.
