# Quadratic rigidity of all thirteen Archimedean solids

## Finding
Every one of the thirteen classical Archimedean solids is **perfect** in the sense of quadratic interpolation: if a real polynomial \(q:\mathbb R^3\to\mathbb R\) of total degree at most two vanishes at every vertex of an Archimedean solid \(P\), then
\[
q(x)=c(\|x\|_2^2-R^2)
\]
for some real \(c\), where the origin is the circumcenter and \(R>0\) is the circumradius. Equivalently, the evaluation map on the nine-dimensional space of restrictions of quadratic polynomials to the circumsphere has rank nine. This proves Conjecture 1 explicitly posed by Berestovskiĭ and Nikonorov (2023).

A stronger orbit statement holds. Every orbit of a nonzero vector under the orientation-preserving icosahedral rotation group is perfect. An orbit of a nonzero vector \(v=(a,b,c)\) under the orientation-preserving octahedral rotation group is perfect precisely when at least two of \(a,b,c\) are nonzero and \(|a|,|b|,|c|\) are not all equal. The two excluded orbit types are the coordinate-axis octahedron and the cube. The regular truncated tetrahedron is perfect separately.

## Assumptions and scope
A real quadratic polynomial means a polynomial of degree at most two, including constant and linear terms. The kernel being generated by a sphere equation is independent of translating and uniformly rescaling the vertex configuration. A perfect orbit means that the only such polynomial vanishing at *all distinct orbit points* is the circumsphere equation. The thirteen classical Archimedean solids here exclude prisms, antiprisms and the pseudorhombicuboctahedron: one truncated tetrahedron with tetrahedral rotation symmetry; six with octahedral rotation symmetry (cuboctahedron, truncated cube, truncated octahedron, rhombicuboctahedron, truncated cuboctahedron, snub cube); and six with icosahedral rotation symmetry (icosidodecahedron, truncated dodecahedron, truncated icosahedron, rhombicosidodecahedron, truncated icosidodecahedron, snub dodecahedron). Each is vertex-transitive under its stated *proper* rotation group, including the chiral snubs.

For a sphere \(S_R^2\), let \(\mathcal H_j\) be the space of real homogeneous harmonic polynomials of degree \(j\), restricted to the sphere. Because \(\|x\|^2=R^2\) on this sphere, restriction identifies degree-at-most-two polynomials modulo the sphere equation with
\[
\mathcal H_0\oplus\mathcal H_1\oplus\mathcal H_2,
\qquad \dim(\mathcal H_0,\mathcal H_1,\mathcal H_2)=(1,3,5).
\]
The statement concerns Euclidean quadratic interpolation, not perfection in graph theory or projective algebraic geometry.

## Proof
**A representation lemma.** Let a finite group \(G\) act orthogonally on \(\mathbb R^3\), and suppose \(W\) is a finite direct sum of pairwise nonisomorphic real irreducible \(G\)-modules. For any point \(v\), evaluation on its finite orbit is \(G\)-equivariant from \(W\) to the functions on \(Gv\). Its kernel is a \(G\)-submodule. Orthogonal complete reducibility and pairwise inequivalence imply that the kernel is the direct sum of exactly those irreducible summands on which evaluation at \(v\) is identically zero. Indeed, by the orthogonal projection onto a given invariant summand, a nonzero vector in the kernel would yield a nonzero irreducible submodule of the same type; conversely an irreducible summand on which the evaluation map is nonzero has zero kernel. This is a standard consequence of Schur's lemma and complete reducibility. All the representations used below are real orthogonal and irreducible, so no complex-type subtlety arises.

**Icosahedral rotations.** The proper icosahedral group \(I\), isomorphic to \(A_5\), has five rotation classes of sizes \(1,15,20,12,12\), with angles \(0,\pi,2\pi/3,2\pi/5,4\pi/5\). The character on \(\mathcal H_j\) at a rotation through angle \(\theta\) is \(1+2\sum_{k=1}^j\cos(k\theta)\). Set \(\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2\). For \(j=1\) and \(j=2\) this gives respectively
\[
\chi_1=(3,-1,0,\varphi,1-\varphi),\qquad
\chi_2=(5,1,-1,0,0).
\]
Their weighted class-character inner products satisfy
\[
\frac1{60}\sum_{c}|c|\,\chi_j(c)\chi_k(c)=\delta_{jk}\quad (j,k\in\{1,2\}),
\]
and their averages over the group are zero. Thus \(\mathcal H_0\), \(\mathcal H_1\), and \(\mathcal H_2\) are three inequivalent irreducible real representations. At every \(v\ne0\), a constant is nonzero, at least one linear form is nonzero, and the trace-free harmonic quadratic \(x\mapsto(v\cdot x)^2-\|v\|^2\|x\|^2/3\) is nonzero at \(x=v\). Every summand therefore evaluates nontrivially. The lemma yields rank nine on every nonzero icosahedral orbit. This proves all six icosahedral Archimedean cases.

**Octahedral rotations.** The proper octahedral group \(O\) is the group of signed coordinate-permutation matrices with determinant \(+1\). Under this group the harmonic pieces split into four pairwise inequivalent irreducibles:
\[
\mathcal H_0=\langle1\rangle,\quad
\mathcal H_1=\langle x,y,z\rangle,\quad
E=\langle x^2-y^2,x^2+y^2-2z^2\rangle,\quad
T=\langle xy,yz,zx\rangle.
\]
For the five conjugacy classes (identity, quarter-turn about a coordinate axis, half-turn about a coordinate axis, third-turn about a body diagonal, half-turn about an edge axis), of sizes \(1,6,3,8,6\), the three nontrivial characters are
\[
\chi_{\mathcal H_1}=(3,1,-1,0,-1),\quad
\chi_E=(2,0,2,-1,0),\quad
\chi_T=(3,-1,-1,0,1).
\]
Their class-weighted inner products, normalized by \(24\), form the \(3\times3\) identity, and each has mean zero, proving irreducibility and inequivalence, including inequivalence to constants. The nonzero constant and linear blocks evaluate nontrivially at every nonzero point \(v=(a,b,c)\). Evaluation on \(E\) vanishes identically exactly if \(a^2=b^2=c^2\); evaluation on \(T\) vanishes identically exactly if \(ab=bc=ca=0\). In the first case the orbit has eight points, the vertices of a cube, and in the second it has six points, the vertices of an octahedron. In all other nonzero cases the lemma gives full rank nine. Every octahedrally symmetric Archimedean solid on the six-element list above has at least twelve vertices and hence is neither exceptional orbit. This proves all six.

**Truncated tetrahedron.** The twelve vertices, after uniform scaling and a choice of axes, are the coordinate permutations of \((3,1,1)\) with all sign patterns \((\epsilon_1,\epsilon_2,\epsilon_3)\in\{\pm1\}^3\) satisfying \(\epsilon_1\epsilon_2\epsilon_3=1\). Every vertex lies on the sphere \(\|x\|^2=11\). Write a putative vanishing polynomial as
\[
q(x)=C+\sum_{i=1}^3 A_i x_i^2+\sum_{i=1}^3 B_i x_i+D_{12}x_1x_2+D_{13}x_1x_3+D_{23}x_2x_3.
\]
For one assignment \((a,b,c)\) of the unsigned coordinates \((3,1,1)\), the four even-sign evaluations have independent sign characters \(1,\epsilon_1,\epsilon_2,\epsilon_3\). Their zero Fourier coefficients yield
\[
C+A_1a^2+A_2b^2+A_3c^2=0,
\quad aB_1+bcD_{23}=0,
\quad bB_2+acD_{13}=0,
\quad cB_3+abD_{12}=0.
\]
Comparing the three placements of the entry \(3\) in the constant equation gives \(A_1=A_2=A_3=A\) and \(C=-11A\). For each \(i\), comparing placements where its unsigned coordinate is \(3\) and \(1\) gives the two equations \(3B_i+D_{jk}=0\) and \(B_i+3D_{jk}=0\), so \(B_i=D_{jk}=0\). Thus \(q=A(\|x\|^2-11)\), proving uniqueness and completing the thirteen-solid classification.

## Verification
The standalone `verify_archimedean.py` performs exact symbolic rational/number-field computations. It checks rank nine on the twelve truncated-tetrahedron vertices and the one-dimensional nullspace containing the sphere equation, character orthogonality for the octahedral and icosahedral class data, rank nine for six illustrative octahedral orbit representatives, and the two exceptional cube/octahedron ranks. It also verifies rank nine for the twelve vertices of a regular icosahedron over \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)\). Execute `python3 verify_archimedean.py` with SymPy installed; the successful output ends in `ARCHIMEDEAN_QUADRATIC_UNIQUENESS_CHECKS_PASS`. These finite checks corroborate the displayed exact deductions, but the group-character and orbit arguments prove the infinite families, not the tested finite sample alone.

## Relationship to prior work
Berestovskiĭ and Nikonorov, *Perfect and almost perfect homogeneous polytopes* (first public arXiv version April 24, 2023), explicitly conjecture that every Archimedean solid is perfect (Conjecture 1) and establish the terminology and background rigidity theory. The proof above resolves that stated finite classification, strengthening it to arbitrary icosahedral rotation orbits and an exact octahedral orbit exception criterion. Their earlier 2021 work addresses transitive orthogonal orbit descriptions of finite homogeneous configurations rather than a complete quadratic kernel classification. Previously published exact harmonic interpolation and erasure results for individual cuboctahedral, truncated-tetrahedral and icosahedral vertex sets establish the nine-dimensional kernel rank for those particular sets, so that aspect is credited; they do not imply the claim for all Archimedean solids or the stated general orbit classification. Classical finite-group character methods underlie the proof and are not proposed as new.

## Limitations
The statement is about real quadrics and the thirteen **classical** Archimedean solids, not all homogeneous or uniform polyhedra, higher-degree equations, or all finite rotation-group orbits (for example, tetrahedral orbits beyond the one explicit case). The orbit proof assumes that vertices form a single orbit of the stated proper rotation group, a standard classification fact. Searches cannot establish complete scholarly priority; an unindexed proof of the conjecture or a dominating invariant-theoretic theorem may exist. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
