# Scientific review of the cubic Brownian sampling-site classification

## Correctness
PASS. The covariance kernel is \(\min(s,t)\), and Wick pairing gives \(\operatorname{Var}(\int W_t^3dt)=9/5\). Gaussian conditional regression supplies both full-value polynomial coefficients, whose normal moments give the full-reading explained variance. The sign bit has exactly two equiprobable outcomes, making the conditional mean automatically the globally optimal decoder in that sensor class. The sign objective has a single interior stationary point, and the full-reading quartic derivative has exactly one interior zero. Exact positive evaluation of the full derivative at the sign optimum proves a strict change of sampling site. The standalone checker passed 27 symbolic/numerical assertions; its finite tests corroborate, rather than replace, the proof.

## Originality
PASS on a qualified best-knowledge comparison. The full texts of Foster–Lyons–Oberhauser (arXiv:1904.06998v1), Guo–Kostina (arXiv:1909.01317v1) and Fesl–Koller–Utschick (arXiv:2212.04470v1) were inspected for their actual targets and results. The first studies polynomial path approximation and quadratic Brownian functionals, the second causal trajectory tracking, and the third Gaussian channel estimation under one-bit quantization. Public results on cubic Gaussian Hermite moment extremality, Gaussian cubic regression integration and Brownian-bridge kernel quadrature were inspected in full: none provides an implication to the new Brownian path-integral site identities. Related comparator results concern quadratic path energy, linear path integrals, or other observation models; none supplies this cubic sign/full pair of optima. Residual risk: an earlier equivalent specific Gaussian-moment optimization may exist under alternate terminology, and there has been no independent priority audit.

## Value
PASS for a natural low-information stochastic quadrature benchmark. The signed cubic time integral is a canonical nonlinear Brownian path functional appearing in polynomial path approximations and moment-based simulation. Restricting a single sensor from a full-valued reading to its zero-threshold sign shifts the unique optimal location backward by approximately \(0.07851\) of the horizon and drops the sharp explained-variance fraction from about \(89.23\%\) to \(21.07\%\). These are unknown-in-the-inspected-literature, non-implied exact design quantities for a specific hardware budget, rather than an arbitrary mesh slice. They can benchmark third-order path-functional estimation under sign-only observation. Scope limitation: unrestricted one-bit encoders and higher-resolution or adaptive sensors are not covered; no general numerical method convergence theorem is asserted.
