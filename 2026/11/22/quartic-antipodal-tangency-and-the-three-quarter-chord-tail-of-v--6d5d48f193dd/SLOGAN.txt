A unique antipodal pair with parallel tangents and noncancelling curvature vectors on a spherical space curve produces a sharp \(\varepsilon^{3/4}\) extreme-chord law and a uniform quartic-ellipse conditional endpoint limit. For the classical Viviani sphere–cylinder intersection, the leading probability constant is exactly \(8^{3/4}\mathrm B(1/4,3/2)/(32E(-1)^2)\approx0.1424415734\) after scaling the distance deficit by the cylinder radius.
