# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The object is a labelled length-\(n\) word in the finite abelian coordinate group \(A^n\), primitive for cyclic rotation, not an unbordered word or an orbit class. The period-divisor Möbius identity is pointwise. Orthogonality on each period subgroup gives the integer coefficient formula. Because every surviving Möbius divisor is a multiple of \(b=n/\operatorname{rad}(n)\), frequencies outside \(V_b^\perp\) vanish. Within it the highest power \(q^d\) in the signed divisor sum strictly dominates every lower distinct power for \(q\ge2\), so cancellation is impossible. A character with opposite nontrivial labels at positions \(0,b\) proves that \(q^b\) is attained, matching divisibility; the trivial character maximizes magnitude. Complete annihilator support determines the exact translation stabilizer and projection. All quantifiers and boundaries include \(n=2\) and all finite abelian groups, including Klein four, and exclude \(n=1\) for the witness. Exact computations checked 8,188 binary, 1,089 ternary, 336 cyclic-four, and 336 Klein-four frequencies.

## Originality — PASS under bounded comparison
Moree's first publicly dated 2003 paper supplies classical primitive-word Möbius enumeration and sign-twisted fixed-content formulas. Its examined Sections 1–4 do not establish arbitrary position-wise character transforms, exact full-support rigidity or the sharp spectral gap. The directly inspected published primitive-necklace root-ratio theorem addresses asymptotic/counting inequalities, and the directly inspected onto-word first-band theorem concerns a different word set and a sampled degree-one Gram matrix. Related restricted-degree sampling calculations cannot imply noncancellation at every character of a full annihilator. The claim is stronger than standard primitive-word cardinality but makes no historical priority guarantee: a hidden equivalent group-algebra treatment would cover it. No such coverage was located in the bounded comparisons.

## Value — PASS
Primitive or aperiodic words are a canonical family for cyclic coding and combinatorics. Their **additive** convolution is natural for recovery of signals indexed by word configurations, but its kernel and stability are not determined by the classical necklace count. The result completely classifies the recoverable quotient, reveals a squarefree-kernel dependence that strongly changes rank at prime powers, and proves sharp minimum and maximum multiplier magnitudes over all alphabets/lengths. These are exact all-parameter invariants for a nontrivial natural family, rather than an arbitrary finite slice or a small numerical improvement.

## Closest comparisons and limitations
P. Moree, *The formal series Witt transform*, arXiv:math/0311194v1, first public 2003-11-12, establishes the period-counting prerequisites. The published work *Exact first-band Fourier sampling spectra on surjective words* studies a different sampling problem; *Sharp root-ratio thresholds for primitive-necklace counts* concerns arithmetic growth of counts. The extension to arbitrary weighted words, nonabelian alphabets and a full multiplicity census of distinct nonzero coefficients is not proved. The novelty conclusion is bounded by search and access coverage.
