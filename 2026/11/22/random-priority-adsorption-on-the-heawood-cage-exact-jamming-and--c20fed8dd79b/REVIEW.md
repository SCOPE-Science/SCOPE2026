# Scientific review: exact Heawood adsorption and kinetic occupancy

## Correctness: PASS
The claim is restricted to the fourteen-vertex Fano incidence graph and a specified independent uniform arrival order (or independent first-arrival exponential clocks). The graph is defined explicitly. The five classes of maximal independent sets follow from point–line incidence constraints, and an exhaustive independent-set polynomial check reproduces the known graph polynomial. The output-set event has a direct equivalence to inequalities of independent uniform arrival times. Integrating those inequalities over all order regions yields each of the thirty-seven output probabilities, without relying on simulation. The independently implemented residual-set recursion gives the same terminal law, and conditioning on which sites arrive before time \(t\) proves the exact finite-time Bernstein formula. Every coefficient, fraction and normalization is verified by the standalone exact-rational script. The result asserts no general graph extremality.

## Originality: PASS, qualified
The closest primary literature is the Fano-incidence independent-set extremality theorem of Perarnau and Perkins (arXiv:1611.01474, first public November 4, 2016), which establishes the entire equilibrium independent-set polynomial but not random-priority selection probabilities. Brightwell, Janson and Luczak (arXiv:1510.05560, first public October 19, 2015) study greedy independent sets on random degree-specified graphs at asymptotic scale, rather than the exact output measure on this fixed graph. Pippenger (1989), DOI:10.1137/0402034, establishes the classical graph adsorption framework and cubic-tree asymptotic. Its accessible abstract does not give the present finite exact law, but its full text was not available, which is a concrete historical-priority risk. Other published finite-graph adsorption examples concern different graphs; their summary-level comparisons do not certify absence of all possible equivalent formulations. The standard finite-graph recursion is not claimed as new: the evaluated Fano-incidence output distribution, its full kinetically averaged mean and their precise equilibrium/kinetic contrast are the assessed result.

## Value: PASS
The Heawood graph is not an arbitrary finite sample: it is the point–line graph of the Fano plane, the cubic girth-six cage, and the equality graph for a sharp independent-set partition-function extremality result. It is therefore a principled finite test case for the difference between equilibrium independent-set measures and random irreversible adsorption. The exact probabilities reveal an unexpected absent cardinality six, a probability \(12/35\) of attaining either optimal side, an amplification factor \(222/35\) relative to uniform saturated sets, and a finite-girth density excess \(103/5880\) over the infinite cubic-tree limit. These form a complete small-model benchmark with a precisely reproducible full kinetic mean, not an arbitrary parameter substitution or a claimed universal theorem.

## Limitations and closest literature
The exact law concerns one finite graph only. The strongest historical-priority uncertainty is the inaccessible full 1989 adsorption article, together with possible unindexed evaluations of the same graph. Earlier exact calculations for other finite cages and earlier exact calculations on other small graphs do not logically determine this Fano-incidence distribution.
