# Scientific review: partial-automorphism extension spectra of asymmetric bicliques

## Correctness — PASS

The claim is a full-size, all-positive-integer theorem for \(a>b\ge1\), not an interpolation from small cases. Graph degrees force the global automorphism group to preserve the unequal sides. Every partial induced-subgraph isomorphism either preserves these sides or swaps occupied portions; the latter have exactly two one-sided classes and one two-sided class. The first yields the product of two rook polynomials, and the latter is counted without overlap by \(2C+C^2\). Strict positivity of every coefficient of \(C\) proves the complete nonextendible-rank support, and the highest coefficient proves the boundary population. Independently enumerating all partial injections reproduced the complete vectors for eight labeled graph sizes, including balanced control examples. The finite check is not the general proof.

## Originality — PASS, with historical-priority risk

The 2015 induced-subgraph graph inverse semigroup article proves semilattice and ideal structure, not the rank-refined extension-obstruction enumeration. The 2018 partial graph-automorphism article proves structural membership and representation results, not this all-rank formula. Prior homogeneous-graph rank spectra cover balanced bicliques but not unequal ones. A nearby square-zero partial-isometry census counts only maps whose square vanishes; it neither supplies the unrestricted extension count nor the sharp cutoff. Dedicated mathematical-record and bibliographic searches found no statement implying the asymmetric factorization. This remains a best-knowledge priority assessment, not a formal uniqueness certificate; an unlocated general rook-monoid enumeration might imply the result.

## Value — PASS

Extension of partial induced-subgraph symmetries to global automorphisms is a central natural question in the structure of partial-automorphism inverse monoids. The theorem quantifies the entire failure of extension for every asymmetric complete bipartite graph, gives a clean factorization from cross-class matching counts, and locates the exact rank at which *all* local symmetries become global restrictions. The boundary coefficient counts the largest nonextendible maps and distinguishes every unequal biclique from the homogeneous equal-class case. This is a complete canonical family-level invariant rather than a single small census or an arbitrary slice.

## Prior literature and residual limits

Chih and Plessas, arXiv:1506.02093 (2015); Jajcay et al., arXiv:1809.04422 (2018); classical homogeneous graph classification; published homogeneous rank spectra (2026); published square-zero multipartite partial-isometry census (2026). The last two cover different scopes, and only the balanced case and classical rook coefficients are credited as existing. No comparison of all papers on partial isomorphism monoids is possible, so historical originality has residual risk. The result excludes general multipartite graphs and does not certify independent proof checking.
