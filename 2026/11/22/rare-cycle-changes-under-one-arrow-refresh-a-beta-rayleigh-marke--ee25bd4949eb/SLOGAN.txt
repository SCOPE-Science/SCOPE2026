Replacing one uniformly selected arrow of a uniform random mapping changes its number of cycles with probability asymptotic to \(\sqrt{\pi/(2n)}\), with exactly balanced births and deaths. Conditional on a change, the affected sink-basin occupies a \(\operatorname{Beta}(1/2,3/2)\) fraction of vertices, and the created/destroyed cycle length divided by \(\sqrt n\) converges to \(\sqrt U\,R\), with independent beta \(U\) and Rayleigh \(R\).
