# Mathematical review

**Correctness — PASS.** The normalized exponential representation is distributionally exact for uniform circular spacings; deleting a uniformly chosen site merges two exchangeable adjacent gaps. A gamma--beta integral establishes the exact harmonic change probability, and an independent memoryless calculation gives the finite binomial tail formula. The conditioned joint density of the competing maximum and merged-sum excess converges pointwise to a normalized product density, hence in total variation by Scheffé's lemma. The error from a constituent gap exceeding the remaining maximum is bounded by \(2/H_{n-1}\); exponential concentration controls the random denominator after rare-event conditioning. **Originality — PASS, with historical-priority risk.** Schlemm (2014, arXiv:1405.6114) treats the unconditional largest circular spacing; Mijatović and Vysotsky (2015, arXiv:1508.00944) treat maxima over all fixed-length consecutive spacing sums. Neither full-text theorem or proof states the law of a single uniformly selected deletion conditional on it changing the old maximum, nor the three independent limit marks. A separate independent-thinning result for maximal gaps gives a limiting joint law when \(\varepsilon_n\log n\) converges and does not imply the order \((\log n)/n\) success probability or conditional marks for one forced deletion. **Value — PASS.** This is a natural leave-one-out robustness question for circle coverage and circular load balancing. The exact finite failure rate distinguishes random-site deletion from worst-case deletion, and the rare-event conditional theorem identifies the old worst gap's size-bias, the scale of extra exposed arc, and the failed site's relative location. These quantities are not supplied by an unconditioned maximal-gap limit or a maximal two-spacing theorem.
