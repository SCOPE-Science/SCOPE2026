# Rational homology of every dihedral quotient of the second tolerance complex of a polygon

## Finding
For each integer \(n\ge4\), give the unfilled polygon \(C_n\) its one-dimensional edge complex, and let \(X_n=\mathcal T_2(C_n)\). The *geometric* orbit space of the natural polygonal dihedral action is \(Q_n=|X_n|/D_n\). Its complete reduced rational homology is
\[
\widetilde H_j(Q_n;\mathbb Q)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Q^{B(n)},&j=3,\\
0,&j\ne3,
\end{cases}
\]
where \(B(4)=0\), while for \(n\ge5\)
\[
B(n)=\frac{1}{8}\left(\binom{n-1}{3}-\binom{n-5}{3}+4n-16-2\mathbf1_{2\mid n}\right)
-\left\lfloor\frac{(n-3)^2+6}{12}\right\rfloor.
\]
We take \(\binom m3=0\) when \(m<3\). In particular, \(B(n)\) grows quadratically, and \(B(4),\ldots,B(13)\) are \(0,1,1,3,4,6,8,11,13,17\).

## Assumptions and scope
The tolerance complex \(\mathcal T_2(K)\) consists of all \(\sigma\) for which some face \(\tau\in K\) satisfies \(|\sigma\setminus\tau|\le2\). Every subset of \(C_n\)'s vertices of size at most three belongs to \(X_n\); a four-element subset belongs exactly when it contains one of the polygon's edges; no subset of size five belongs. For \(n=4\), the ordinary square with distinct consecutive edges is meant. The quotient is that of geometric realizations; it is not a quotient of simplices identifying their vertices without subdivision.

## Proof
**Vanishing upstairs.** The entire two-skeleton of the \((n-1)\)-simplex lies in \(X_n\), so \(|X_n|\) is simply connected and has no reduced degree-zero or degree-one homology. It also has zero integral second homology. Indeed, the integral two-cycle group of the full two-skeleton is generated by tetrahedral boundaries \(\partial[v_0v_1v_2v_3]\) on four-element subsets. The boundary of every tetrahedron with a cyclic edge is already filled in \(X_n\). If a four-subset \(A\) has no cyclic edge, choose two consecutive elements in its cyclic order; move the first one a single position forward along the cycle toward the second. Such a move changes the four-subset to \(A'\), and the five-element set \(A\cup A'\) has four other tetrahedral faces, each containing the newly traversed cycle edge. The formal simplicial boundary-squared identity in the ambient simplex implies that \(\partial[A]\) and \(\partial[A']\) differ up to a sign by a linear combination of boundaries of these admitted tetrahedra. Repeating until the chosen two vertices become consecutive shows that \(\partial[A]\) lies in the submodule generated by admitted tetrahedral boundaries. Thus all reduced rational homology of \(X_n\) is concentrated in degree three (and the integral homology groups in lower degrees vanish).

**Orbit chains versus invariant chains.** Barycentrically subdivide before quotienting so that a group element stabilizing a simplex fixes it vertexwise. Over \(\mathbb Q\), quotient cellular chains are the invariant chains, by averaging across each face orbit. The invariant functor for finite groups is exact in characteristic zero. Hence \(\widetilde H_*(Q_n;\mathbb Q)=\widetilde H_*(X_n;\mathbb Q)^{D_n}\). The remaining task is its degree-three dimension.

**Signed face orbits.** For \(k\in\{1,2,3,4\}\), let \(a_k\) be the number of dihedral orbits of \(k\)-element faces whose setwise stabilizer induces only even permutations on the \(k\) vertices. Each such orbit contributes one invariant oriented \((k-1)\)-chain; if an odd stabilizer exists, its invariant contribution is zero. From the reduced invariant-chain Euler characteristic and the homology concentration,
\[
B(n)=a_4-a_3+a_2-a_1+1.
\]
The action is vertex-transitive, so \(a_1=1\). Every pair of vertices is interchanged by a polygon reflection, so \(a_2=0\). Therefore \(B(n)=a_4-a_3\).

Every three-element face has three positive cyclic gaps summing to \(n\). Passing to the dihedral group permutes the gaps by all of \(S_3\). Its stabilizer contains an odd vertex permutation precisely if at least two gaps coincide. Consequently, \(a_3\) is the number of partitions of \(n\) into three distinct positive integers. The elementary three-part partition count gives
\[
a_3=\left\lfloor\frac{(n-3)^2+6}{12}\right\rfloor.
\]

For four-element faces, a cyclic gap word \((a,b,c,d)\) comprises four positive integers summing to \(n\), at least one of which is \(1\) (equivalently the face contains a cycle edge). Rotation and reflection act on these gap words as \(D_4\). Counting the signs of the induced permutations of the four selected vertices gives a signed Burnside average. For \(n\ge5\), the identity fixes
\[
E_n=\binom{n-1}{3}-\binom{n-5}{3}
\]
allowed gap words. The half-turn fixes \(R_n=2\mathbf1_{2\mid n}\) gap words, because any equal half-period gap value must include a unit. Quarter-turns fix none (they would require four equal gaps, all equal to \(1\) and \(n=4\)). Each of the two even reflections fixes \(2n-8\) allowed gap words; each of the two odd reflections fixes \(R_n\). Induced orientation signs are positive for identity, half-turn and even reflections and negative for quarter-turns and odd reflections. Therefore
\[
a_4=\frac{E_n+R_n+2(2n-8)-2R_n}{8}
=\frac{\binom{n-1}{3}-\binom{n-5}{3}+4n-16-2\mathbf1_{2\mid n}}{8}.
\]
For clarity, the even-reflection count follows by setting the opposite gaps equal: when the repeated gap is \(1\), there are \(n-3\) choices for the remaining two positive gaps; when it is larger than \(1\), require at least one of the remaining gaps to be \(1\), yielding \(n-5\) additional possibilities. At \(n=4\), the unique four-element face has an odd reflection stabilizer, so \(a_4=0\); also \(a_3=0\). Substitution completes the result.

## Verification
The accompanying self-contained Python file `check.py` verifies the signed-orbit formula exhaustively for every order \(4\le n\le30\). It independently constructs genuine barycentric-quotient flag chain complexes through \(n=13\) and checks their rational-characteristic and characteristic-two Betti numbers against the formula. These are finite arithmetic checks; the argument above proves the all-order assertion. Reproduce with `python3 check.py` (standard Python library only).

## Relationship to prior work
Kim and Lew established foundational Leray-number theory of tolerance complexes (2021 preprint, published 2023). Selvaraja's 2026 preprint studies tolerance-complex collapsing properties, particularly under chordality and clique hypotheses. A general nonequivariant sphere-wedge computation for tolerance complexes of graph edge complexes already determines the underlying homotopy groups of \(X_n\); the present claim is instead the complete **geometric dihedral-orbit rational homology**. Invariant oriented face orbits and their stabilizers must be considered, so ordinary Betti-number formulas do not determine this quotient. Different literature on dihedral actions on two-independence cycle complexes concerns a distinct simplicial functor.

## Limitations
The computation is over \(\mathbb Q\); it neither establishes the integral torsion of \(Q_n\) nor claims its complete homotopy type. The result applies to unfilled polygon edges and tolerance index two, not to filled polygonal triangulations or higher indices. The historical literature search cannot rule out unindexed equivalent formulations; no independent mathematical audit has been performed.
