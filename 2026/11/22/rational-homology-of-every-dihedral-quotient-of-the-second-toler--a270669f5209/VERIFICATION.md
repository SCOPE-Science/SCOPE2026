The faces are exactly the full two-skeleton and tetrahedra containing a polygon edge. In the ambient simplex all integral two-cycles are generated by boundaries of tetrahedra. For an edge-free four-set, move a vertex one step toward the next selected vertex. The union with the original set has five vertices, and its three tetrahedral faces other than the original and moved sets contain the traversed edge. Applying boundary squared to the union equates the original and moved boundaries up to sign modulo admitted boundaries; iterating reaches an admitted face. Thus reduced homology upstairs can occur only in degree three. After barycentric subdivision stabilizers fix their simplices pointwise; over Q projection sends each invariant orbit sum to a nonzero orbit-size multiple of the associated quotient cell, giving a chain isomorphism. Exactness of invariants then identifies quotient homology with invariant homology. In the original oriented chains each orbit contributes one dimension precisely when the stabilizer acts by even permutations. Hence a1=1, a2=0 by reflection, and the reduced Euler formula is B=a4-a3. Three gaps give distinct-part partitions, equivalently the signed S3 average [binom(n-1,2)-3*floor((n-1)/2)+2*1_(3|n)]/6, which gives the stated floor. For four gaps containing a 1, the identity fixes the difference of the two composition counts. The half-turn and each odd reflection fix 2*1_(2|n) words for n>=5, whereas the quarter-turns fix none. Each even reflection repeats one opposite gap pair: if the repeated value is 1 there are n-3 choices, and if larger than 1 there are n-5 choices by placing the required 1 among the two singles without counting a pair of ones twice. Applying the induced vertex signs yields the claimed a4. At n=4 the full tetrahedron contracts equivariantly to its barycenter, so its quotient has zero reduced homology.
