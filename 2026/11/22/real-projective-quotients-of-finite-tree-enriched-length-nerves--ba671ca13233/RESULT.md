# Real-projective quotients of finite-tree enriched length nerves

## Finding

Let \(T\) be a finite unweighted tree with \(n\ge 2\) vertices and \(e=n-1\) edges, equipped with its shortest-path metric. For real \(r\ge0\), define the ordered length nerve \(N_r(T)\) to be the simplicial set whose \(k\)-simplices are tuples \((x_0,\ldots,x_k)\) of tree vertices satisfying
\[
\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}d_T(x_i,x_{i+1})\le r.
\]
Its degeneracies repeat a vertex and its faces delete a vertex. Write \(X_L=|N_L(T)|\) for any integer \(L\ge1\).

**Theorem.** For every finite tree \(T\) and every integer \(L\ge1\):

1. \(X_L\simeq\bigvee^{e}S^L\), integrally. If an automorphism \(h\) fixes \(p(h)\) edges endpointwise and reverses \(q(h)\) edges setwise, then on top integral homology its trace is \(p(h)+(-1)^{L+1}q(h)\). More precisely, \(H_L(X_L;\mathbb Z)\) is the free signed edge-permutation lattice: a setwise edge reversal has sign \((-1)^{L+1}\), while other edges are transported with their chosen orientations. No other reduced homology survives.
2. If \(g\) is an automorphism with \(g^2=1\) and **no fixed vertex**, then it reverses a unique edge, \(e=2s+1\), and acts freely on \(X_L\). Its **ordinary geometric orbit space** satisfies
\[
\boxed{X_L/\langle g\rangle\simeq \mathbb{RP}^{L}\vee\bigvee^{s}S^L},\qquad s=(e-1)/2=(n-2)/2.
\]
The wedge formula holds for all \(L\ge1\), without assuming a path-shaped tree.

For \(L\ge2\), the quotient has fundamental group \(C_2\). Its positive-degree integral homology has \(\mathbb Z/2\) in each odd degree strictly between \(0\) and \(L\), and in degree \(L\) it has \(\mathbb Z^{s+1}\) for odd \(L\) and \(\mathbb Z^s\) for even \(L\); all other positive-degree integral groups vanish. At \(L=1\) its fundamental group is free of rank \(s+1\).

A further nonequivariant persistence consequence is that, over every field, the reduced degree-zero barcode consists of \(n-1\) intervals \([0,1)\), and in every degree \(k\ge1\) there are precisely \(e\) intervals \([k,k+1)\), with no others. The unreduced degree-zero barcode also has one \([0,\infty)\) interval.

## Assumptions and scope

The vertex set is finite, the graph is simple and connected with no cycles, every edge has length one, and all nerve stages use the inclusive **sum of successive distances**. This is an **ordered simplicial set**, not an unordered or symmetrized Vietoris–Rips complex. The group acts by applying its vertex permutation to each coordinate without permuting coordinates. The quotient means the geometric realization followed by the ordinary topological orbit quotient, not a homotopy orbit space. A fixed-point-free involution is required only for the projective-space conclusion; the first assertion applies to all tree automorphisms. Since every nondegenerate \(k\)-simplex has length at least \(k\), each \(X_L\) is an \(L\)-dimensional finite CW complex.

## Proof

**1. Integral length spectral sequence and explicit generators.** The cellular chain complex of \(X_L\) consists of tuples with no equal consecutive vertices, with the usual alternating deletion boundary; faces with repeated consecutive vertices vanish in the normalized complex. Filter by integer total length \(0,1,\ldots,L\). The associated graded is the integral magnitude chain complex, where only deletions preserving total length remain. By the tree magnitude-homology theorem of Hepworth–Willerton, as proved again by Asao–Izumihara and Gu, the associated graded homology is concentrated in \((k,\ell)=(0,0)\) and \((k,\ell)=(k,k)\) for \(k\ge1\). The former is \(\mathbb Z^n\), while the latter is \(\mathbb Z^{2e}\), freely generated by the two alternating \(k\)-step sequences on every edge.

Fix an orientation \(u\to v\) of each edge. Let \(A_{a,k}\) and \(B_{a,k}\) be the two \((k+1)\)-tuples alternating between its endpoints, starting respectively at \(u\) and at \(v\). The first length-lowering differential takes, for \(k\ge2\),
\[
 d^1A_{a,k}=B_{a,k-1}+(-1)^k A_{a,k-1},\qquad
 d^1B_{a,k}=A_{a,k-1}+(-1)^k B_{a,k-1}.
\]
For \(k=1\), these are the two opposite oriented edge boundaries in \(\mathbb Z^V\). As \(T\) has no graph cycles, the \(k=1\) differential has image rank \(e=n-1\) and kernel precisely the span of \(A_{a,1}+B_{a,1}\), one per edge. For \(k\ge2\), the displayed two-by-two matrix has image and kernel rank one separately for each edge. Thus the first-page differential has zero homology in every strictly positive degree below \(L\), while its top kernel has free rank \(e\). No later spectral-sequence differential is possible: the first page is supported only in the homological-degree-equals-length diagonal. The filtration therefore gives
\[
H_0(X_L;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z,\quad H_i(X_L;\mathbb Z)=0\ (0<i<L),\quad H_L(X_L;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z^e.
\]
Explicit integral cycles representing a basis are
\[
 z_{a,L}=A_{a,L}+(-1)^{L+1}B_{a,L}.
\]
They are literal cycles for the full boundary, not only associated-graded cycles: every interior deletion is degenerate and the two endpoint terms cancel. The chosen-edge basis makes the automorphism sign formula immediate, including at \(L=1\).

**2. From homology to homotopy.** For \(L=1\) the nerve has \(n\) vertices and both directed copies of each tree edge, hence is a connected graph of cycle rank \(e\). At \(L=2\), the two-cells \((u,v,u)\) identify opposite directed edges, while \((u,v,w)\), for every tree path of two edges, identifies a length-two one-cell with the corresponding two-edge path. Its fundamental group therefore reduces to that of the underlying tree and is trivial. Attaching cells of dimensions at least three does not change \(\pi_1\). For \(L\ge2\), the homology calculation and the inductive Hurewicz theorem show that \(X_L\) is \((L-1)\)-connected. Representing a basis of its top integral homology by \(L\)-spheres and applying the simply connected homology Whitehead theorem gives \(X_L\simeq\bigvee^e S^L\).

**3. The canonical projective subspace.** A fixed-point-free automorphism of order two of a finite tree exchanges the endpoints of a unique edge \(a=\{u,v\}\), and all other edges occur in interchanged pairs. The sub-nerve \(Y_L=|N_L(\{u,v\})|\subset X_L\) has in dimension \(k\le L\) exactly two nondegenerate cells, the alternating tuples, exchanged freely by \(g\). This is the \(L\)-skeleton of the two-object chaotic-category bar construction \(E C_2\); its quotient is the corresponding \(L\)-skeleton of \(BC_2\), the standard real-projective cell model. Equivalently, the boundary of each of its two \(k\)-cells has the two endpoint faces \(B_{k-1}+(-1)^k A_{k-1}\). Inductively each pair of top cells forms two equivariantly exchanged cones over the preceding homotopy sphere (each cone attaching map has degree \(\pm1\)), so \(Y_L\) is equivariantly homotopy equivalent to the antipodal \(S^L\), with \(Y_L/\langle g\rangle\simeq\mathbb{RP}^L\).

**4. Splitting the quotient.** The top-homology inclusion \(H_L(Y_L;\mathbb Z)\to H_L(X_L;\mathbb Z)\) identifies the primitive summand generated by the central-edge cycle \(z_{a,L}\). By the basis in step 1 the relative homology \(H_*(X_L,Y_L;\mathbb Z)\) vanishes away from degree \(L\), while
\[
H_L(X_L,Y_L;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z[C_2]^s
\]
with the \(s\) basis orbit-pairs provided by noncentral edges (possible orientation signs do not change the regular-module isomorphism). For \(L\ge2\), \(Y_L\) and \(X_L\) are \((L-1)\)-connected, so the relative Hurewicz theorem and surjection of \(\pi_L(X_L)\) onto relative \(H_L(X_L,Y_L)\) supply \(s\) based \(L\)-sphere maps. Their \(g\)-translates represent the full regular summands, with attachment points paired by \(g\). Attaching those equivariant sphere orbits to \(Y_L\) gives a \(C_2\)-equivariant map to \(X_L\) inducing an integral homology isomorphism between simply connected universal covers. The homology Whitehead theorem makes it an equivariant homotopy equivalence. Passing to free quotients gives \(X_L/C_2\simeq(Y_L/C_2)\vee\bigvee^s S^L\). For \(L=1\), the quotient is a connected graph: \(Y_1/C_2\) is a circle and each noncentral paired-edge orbit contributes one further independent cycle, giving the same formula. The stated integral groups follow from the usual integral homology of \(\mathbb{RP}^L\).

**5. Persistence.** Stages are constant for noninteger \(r\), since every tuple has integral length. At stage \(k\) all reduced homology occurs in dimension \(k\) (at \(k=0\), in dimension zero), so every positive-dimensional class dies upon inclusion in the next stage; every listed class is born at its stage. This yields the indicated bars over every field.

## Verification

The accompanying standard-library-only Python checker `verify_tree_enriched.py` enumerates all nondegenerate tuples of length at most \(L\), forms the normalized signed cellular boundary, and independently computes ordinary and orbit-space Betti numbers over primes \(2,3,5\). It checks \(138\) finite cases across paths, a star, and a symmetric branching double-star, with dimensions through four, and verifies all tested predictions. To reproduce, run `python3 verify_tree_enriched.py` in the directory containing the included file. The proof is integral and uniform; finite-field computations are supporting falsification tests, not an infinite or integral certificate.

## Relationship to prior work

Melodia's 21 August 2026 preprint identifies persistence from the filtered length nerve and its magnitude associated graded; no exact tree free-symmetry orbit theorem is evident from its accessible abstract. The filtered-nerves framework predates this work. Ivanov–Xu distinguish the ordered \(\ell^1\) simplicial set from the symmetrized \(\ell^1\) complex. Hepworth–Willerton and Asao–Izumihara establish the crucial diagonal tree **magnitude homology**, and Gu provides an algebraic-Morse proof; these are explicitly invoked in step 1 rather than claimed as new. The nonequivariant homology is consequently accessible via known spectral-sequence machinery. The additional conclusion here is the **integral equivariant basis and exact ordinary quotient homotopy type for all fixed-vertex-free involutive tree symmetries**, not implied solely by known magnitude ranks or by homology of the covering space. Comparisons with previously published symmetry quotients of independence and matching complexes involve different filtrations and objects.

## Limitations

The projective-space splitting requires a free involution on the vertex set of a finite unweighted tree. An involution fixing a tree vertex generally has geometric fixed points and is not covered by the quotient formula. Weighted trees, non-tree graphs, general finite groups, unordered length–Rips complexes, and full equivariant higher homotopy or multiplicative structures are not classified. The theorem asserts homotopy equivalence of the orbit spaces, not a homeomorphism and not a compatible equivalence for all filtration inclusion maps. Independent mathematical validation and exhaustive priority verification remain unperformed; an unindexed equivalent result remains possible.
