Deleting one row of a square independent continuous cost matrix destroys a reciprocal row-column minimum with asymptotic probability \(1/2\) and creates an asymptotically \(\operatorname{Poisson}(1/4)\) number of new ones. The two effects are positively dependent: the asymptotic expected number of births is \(11/24\) conditional on a loss but only \(1/24\) conditional on no loss, and their full joint generating function is explicit.
