# Rectangular-closure orders and an exponential gap in extended fibered persistence

## Finding

For a finite **one-critical** bifiltration, consider only the restrictions to coordinatewise nondecreasing continuous paths on which all distinct noninitial simplex entrance grades occur at distinct times. The possible strict grade orders have the following exact characterization: **every prefix must be closed under taking the coordinatewise maximum of its entrance grades and retaining all grades dominated by that maximum**. This gives an exhaustive finite enumeration rule, not merely a necessary precedence condition.

For the antichain of grades \(p_i=(i,n+1-i)\), \(1\le i\le n\), with \(n\ge1\), there are exactly \(2^{n-1}\) such strict orders, precisely those built by enlarging a consecutive index interval one endpoint at a time. The same exact count occurs as **distinct degree-labelled combinatorial persistence barcodes** of an explicit one-critical wedge-of-spheres bifiltration constructed below.

For that family, the number of strict orders obtainable by restricting to **positive-slope affine lines** is at most
\[
1+\binom n2+\binom{\binom n2}{2}.
\]
At \(n=6\), exact rational chamber enumeration gives 30 affine-line orders versus 32 monotone-path orders; the missing order \((3,4,5,2,1,6)\) is proved impossible for every positive-slope affine line. Thus the distinction already holds at six grades. At \(n=13\), the line bound is 3082 versus 4096 monotone-path orders, and the contrast is exponentially versus at most quartically growing.

## Assumptions and scope

Let \(P=\{p_1,\dots,p_n\}\subset(0,L)^2\) be a set of **distinct** grades, and let \(F\) be a finite one-critical bifiltration over \([0,L]^2\), meaning that the support of every simplex is the principal coordinatewise upper set generated by a single grade. Assume \(P\) is exactly the set of distinct noninitial simplex grades; simplices may share a grade, but every grade labels at least one simplex. All grade events are assumed distinct along the restricted path. We regard the initial complex as present from \((0,0)\), and compare filtrations by their indexed subcomplexes (simplicial equivalence), not by numerical event times.

For \(A\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}\), define \(Q(A)=(\max_{i\in A}p_i^{(1)},\max_{i\in A}p_i^{(2)})\), and put
\[
\operatorname{cl}(A)=\{i:p_i\preceq Q(A)\},\qquad \operatorname{cl}(\varnothing)=\varnothing.
\]
Here \(\preceq\) is coordinatewise comparison. For \(A=\varnothing\) we start at \((0,0)\). A *strict entrance order* is a permutation of the \(n\) grades that is actually realized by first-entry times of a continuous nondecreasing path from \((0,0)\) to \((L,L)\).

## Proof

**1. Necessity of rectangular closure.** Let \(\gamma\) realize strict order \(\pi\) and let \(A_k=\{\pi_1,\dots,\pi_k\}\) be the grade set present immediately after its \(k\)-th entrance. At that time, the path parameter \(u\) dominates every grade in \(A_k\), hence dominates \(Q(A_k)\). If another grade were dominated by \(Q(A_k)\), its simplex would already be present. Therefore \(\operatorname{cl}(A_k)=A_k\), for every \(k\).

**2. Sufficiency and explicit construction.** Conversely assume \(\operatorname{cl}(A_k)=A_k\) for all prefixes. Define \(q_0=(0,0)\) and \(q_k=Q(A_k)\). The \(q_k\) form a coordinatewise nondecreasing sequence. Since \(\pi_k\notin A_{k-1}=\operatorname{cl}(A_{k-1})\), the grade \(p_{\pi_k}\) raises at least one coordinate maximum. Join \(q_{k-1}\) to \(q_k\) by an affine segment. Before reaching \(q_k\), the new grade \(p_{\pi_k}\) is absent because one of its coordinates is the newly raised maximum, attained only at the segment endpoint. No other unentered grade can enter during the segment, because any such grade would be dominated by \(q_k\), contradicting \(\operatorname{cl}(A_k)=A_k\). Concatenate these segments, followed by a monotone segment to \((L,L)\). The grade events occur precisely in order \(\pi\), strictly separated. This proves the general characterization.

**3. Exact antichain count.** For \(p_i=(i,n+1-i)\), nonempty \(A\) has rectangular closure
\[
\operatorname{cl}(A)=\{i:\min(A)\le i\le\max(A)\}.
\]
Thus each allowable prefix is an index interval. After choosing its first index \(k\), the next index must be the as-yet-unused immediate left or right neighbor. There are \(\binom{n-1}{k-1}\) possible interleavings of the \(k-1\) left and \(n-k\) right expansions. Summing over \(k\) gives \(\sum_k\binom{n-1}{k-1}=2^{n-1}\).

**4. Genuine persistence barcode realization.** Let the initial subcomplex be a single vertex \(*\) at grade \((0,0)\). For each \(i\), choose an integer dimension \(d_i=i\) and a separate simplicial \(d_i\)-sphere \(S_i\), modeled as the boundary of a \((d_i+1)\)-simplex, whose only vertex in common with any other \(S_j\) is \(*\). Give every noninitial simplex of \(S_i\) the unique entrance grade \(p_i\). This defines a finite one-critical bifiltration: the complex at \(u\) is exactly the wedge of those \(S_i\) with \(p_i\preceq u\), together with the basepoint. Along any path with strict events, the graded persistent homology over **any field** has a unique infinite positive-degree interval born at the entrance of \(p_i\) in dimension \(d_i\). All dimensions are distinct, so the degree-labelled combinatorial barcode records precisely the order permutation. Consequently there are exactly \(2^{n-1}\) distinct *strict-event* combinatorial barcode types among these monotone paths.

**5. The affine-line upper bound.** Every positive-slope affine line has equation \(y=m x+c\), with \(m>0\). Parameterize it by increasing \(x\), and set \(w=1/m>0\), \(z=(n+1-c)/m\). Grade \(p_i\) enters at coordinate
\[
t_i=\max\{i,z-wi\}.
\]
For \(i<j\), a strict order swap can occur only when \(t_i=t_j\). The increasing branch \(i\) and decreasing branch \(z-wi\) imply that this equality is possible only across the two branches, exactly on \(z=j+wi\). There are at most \(M=\binom n2\) such lines in the \((w,z)\)-parameter half-plane. On each open chamber of their arrangement every \(t_i\) has a fixed strict order. An arrangement of \(M\) affine lines has at most \(1+M+\binom M2\) open regions. This is the announced quartic upper bound, valid for all positive-slope affine lines regardless of intercept. Lines with simultaneous grade events cannot realize a strict-event barcode order.

**6. A six-grade obstruction without computation.** For \(n=6\), the interval-prefix order \((3,4,5,2,1,6)\) is realized by the successive segment endpoints
\[
(0,0),(3,4),(4,4),(5,4),(5,5),(5,6),(6,6).
\]
Suppose an affine line realizes it with \(t_i=\max(i,z-wi)\), \(w>0\). The required inequalities \(t_3<t_4<t_5\) force \(t_4=4\) and \(t_5=5\) (the decreasing branch cannot increase with the index). Since \(t_2>t_5\), we have \(z>5+2w\). Since \(t_3<t_4\), we have \(z<4+3w\), and hence \(w>1\). Moreover \(t_5=5\) forces \(t_6=6\), while \(t_1<t_6\) forces \(z<6+w\), hence \(w<1\). Contradiction. The exact finite count 30 comes from the reproducible rational chamber enumerator in `CHECK.py`; `LINE_WITNESSES.csv` gives 30 line parameters, one per realized strict order. Exhaustive chamber checking for \(n\le5\) recovers all \(2^{n-1}\) orders, so six is the first gap *within this specified antichain family*.

## Verification

Run `python3 CHECK.py` from the directory containing this file and `LINE_WITNESSES.csv` (the script itself uses only Python's standard library). It independently enumerates all unit north/east lattice paths for antichains of size through eight and 45 further arbitrary generic grade sets, compares their event orders with the rectangular-closure permutation test, and checks the exact count \(2^{n-1}\). It also enumerates every affine-line order chamber using rational intersections and samples for \(2\le n\le13\); this gives 30 at six grades and 638 at thirteen grades. Every finite test is exact. The arbitrary-size results follow from the mathematical arguments above, not from finite extrapolation. In the six-grade example, the 30 supplied rational line witnesses independently certify all attainable strict orders.

## Relationship to prior work

Belton, Brooks, Fajstrup, Fasy and Vidaurre (2026) introduce the extended fibered barcode, define simplicial equivalence and identify counting its classes as an open direction (their Section 7, Conjecture 7.1). The result here gives an exact order criterion for the one-critical strict-event subcase, an exponential count for a canonical antichain and a six-grade obstruction to affine-line completeness. It does **not** solve the general multi-critical or tied-event counting problem. Bapat and collaborators (2022/2025) partition positive-slope line restrictions by critical-value data for matching-distance computation; their arrangement machinery is compatible with, but does not supply, the antichain monotone-path count or the explicit strict-barcode gap proved here.

## Limitations

The complete order criterion requires one-critical entrance supports and **strict, separated grade-entry times**. The full set of weak orders with simultaneous entries, the general \(k\)-critical case for \(k>1\), and classes of directed homotopies in the entrance subcomplex are not classified. The wedge-of-spheres construction is a witness exhibiting genuine distinct **graded** combinatorial barcodes, not a claim that every bifiltration has this many barcode types. The affine-line bound counts strict order types and is not an upper bound on numerical (unreparameterized) barcodes. Exact priority against unavailable or unindexed publications cannot be guaranteed, and independent mathematical audit has not occurred.
