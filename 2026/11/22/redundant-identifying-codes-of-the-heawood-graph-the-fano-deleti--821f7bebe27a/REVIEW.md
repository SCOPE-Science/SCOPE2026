# Scientific review: redundant identifying codes of the Heawood graph

## Correctness — PASS

The two-domination and pairwise two-distinguishing criteria for fault tolerance are necessary and sufficient. The Fano incidence analysis reduces every possible omitted set of size at most four to explicit closed-neighborhood and four-set constraints. The proof excludes all larger omitted sets by hereditary obstructions and classifies all valid omitted triples and quadruples; every permitted smaller deletion extends to a quadruple. Two distinct standalone enumerators examine every one of 16,384 masks, one by the exact symmetric-difference criterion in a cyclic Fano model, the other by removing each detector from codes built with an independent binary-vector model. Both produce coefficients \(98,224,91,14,1\); the geometric enumerator also verifies the size-14 and size-84 orbits under all 336 graph automorphisms. The proof is finite and exhaustive, not an inference from sampled cases.

## Originality — PASS, provisional

Jean and Seo, arXiv:2112.01685v1 (2021), supply the general redundant-identification condition and investigate cubic graphs. That criterion and possible scalar lower-bound information are known; the claim is the distinct, Fano-specific complete code polynomial, exact structural deletion classification, all inclusion-minimal states and two automorphism orbits. Their complete preprint was inspected with emphasis on the criterion and cubic-graph section; no covering Heawood configuration classification was identified. Balbuena–Foucaud–Hansberg, arXiv:1407.7263, treat ordinary identifying codes and Heawood locating domination instead. The complete graph-specific ordinary-identification token-slide theorem in the public record was read, and its 268 ordinary seven-detector codes have a different feasibility requirement. A separate locating-domination result has 448 six-detector codes, also not a substitute. Indexed-record comparisons did not identify a matching redundant-Heawood result. Absence from those searches cannot establish historical uniqueness; an unpublished census or stronger omitted theorem is a residual priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Redundant identification is a motivated sensor-failure requirement. The Heawood graph is the canonical cubic incidence graph of the smallest projective plane, so the nontrivial distinction between ordinary and fault-tolerant sensors is structural rather than an arbitrary numerical slice. The exact complete polynomial, min-cost placements, and two geometric orbit types tell which Fano configurations can be left uninstrumented, show that every minimal code is optimum, and offer a concrete benchmark for redundancy on sparse highly symmetric networks. The contribution is finite and graph-specific, with no claimed general-projective-plane formula.

Publication-priority clearance is provisional; independent mathematical verification has not been performed. The checkers reproduce a full finite examination, and no generalization to all incidence planes or simultaneous failures is established.
