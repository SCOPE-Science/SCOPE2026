## Originality — PASS, with residual publication risk

The 2014 publicly posted Fitch–Leonard work establishes finite-gain joint centrality and a general performance formula for two noisy leaders. The inspected path-specialization section gives only numerical examples for 51 and 101 vertices, not a scaled-feedback continuum law, algebraic optimum overshoot, or explicit finite reentrance. The corresponding 2016 dissertation section was also read. Patterson–McGlohon–Dyagilev address fixed/noise-free leader states and an exact algorithm for a different grounded follower matrix; their result does not imply the full finite-gain stationary-covariance transition. The 2017 resistance-distance paper develops general equivalent Green-function evaluation, but its listed noisy two-leader closed-form solution is on cycles, not the path gain-crossover. The exact reduction may also follow from earlier Green-function formulas; novelty is claimed for the sharp nonmonotone macroscopic law and certified finite case, not for matrix inverses or generic leader selection. Unindexed equivalent asymptotic work remains possible.

## Value — PASS

Optimal two-leader placement in noisy formation control is a motivated design question, and earlier path examples indicate shifts between weak and strong feedback. The result shows that simple monotone spreading is false: with rising gain, the optimal leaders temporarily become farther apart than under arbitrarily strong pinning. The universal limiting peak and an exact finite counterexample make this a substantive boundary for design heuristics, not an arbitrary numeric table or a textbook specialization. The reduction also makes exact finite designs computable through a one-dimensional rational search.

## Scope and limitations

Uniform edge strengths, equal pinning gains, independent equal-intensity disturbances and the stationary full-state inverse trace are essential. The continuum result takes gain proportional to inverse path size. Finite exact optimizers need not vary monotonically with strength. Nonlinear networks, alternative noise models and general graphs are outside scope. Publication-priority confidence is limited to the inspected source material; separate expert review remains desirable.
