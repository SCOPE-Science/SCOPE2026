# Scientific assessment

**Correctness — PASS.** Exact diagonalization by the stationary projection gives the scalar identity for every positive step and every number of repeats. The one-positive-root derivative argument proves the unique minimum and two-crossing positivity interval. Taylor's theorem supplies the boundary square-root coefficient, and determinant/explicit exponential arguments settle embeddability. All essential equations and the rational negative-diagonal example were reconstructed independently, with 3,360 exact rational matrix-power checks and separate root/limit checks by `verify.py`. The finite checks are supplemental, not an infinite proof.

**Originality — PASS, with qualified historical priority.** Active extrapolation's scalar stability function is explicitly known in Zlatev et al. (2017), and general equal-input Markov algebra and its positivity criterion are known in Baake–Sumner (2020/2022 and 2024/2025). Neither inspected analysis supplies the exact two-state extrapolation rate threshold, its single reentrant negative-step interval, iteration-parity phase diagram, square-root cusp or numerical embeddability classification as one specialized theorem. Closest published mathematical records include the scalar Richardson barrier and five-state pure-birth Richardson positivity recovery; the former omits rate asymmetry and the latter has five states and a different graph. These are not being asserted as novel. A more obscure prior treatment of this same special case could exist; the search is evidence rather than a proof of priority. Earlier published general algebra sharply limits how much conceptual novelty can be claimed for the projection step.

**Value — PASS.** Numerical CTMC integrators must preserve probabilities, which scalar L-stability does not guarantee. This complete two-state classification identifies a sharp, unexpected *finite* window of negativity caused by extreme rate asymmetry even though the step is stable, and proves exact positivity recovery under even repetition. The determinant criterion additionally distinguishes stochastic numerical steps that cannot represent any finite-rate Markov flow. This provides a compact diagnostic for numerical kinetics and clear boundary data for positivity-preserving method design. An even-power block may be stochastic although its intermediate one-step state is not, so the result does not justify displaying such intermediate states as probabilities. It does not establish a broad new theorem for arbitrary generators.

**Literature and limitations.** Key lawful accessible material was read in the 2017 monograph, the 2021 implicit-Euler extrapolation flux-limiting paper, the 2023 numerical-method paper, and the 2025 equal-input article. The 2021 work requires a nonlinear limiter to obtain guaranteed bounds, and does not classify uncorrected Richardson steps. The 2020 equal-input paper's abstract and available bibliographic record were read, but its complete original PDF was not available in this review; the closely related 2025 full article was inspected instead. Results cited only as research-record summaries were compared at the stated claim level, not misrepresented as complete-text inspections. The claim is limited to an irreducible, homogeneous, two-state generator and repeated fixed-size active Richardson steps. Publication priority and mathematical validation by an independent reader remain outstanding.
