# Scientific review

## Correctness

PASS. The kernel is algebraically half the difference between squared pair distance and an odd signed midpoint--separation term. The sum-of-squared-pair-differences identity yields the exact statistic, and reflection makes the population contrast vanish for every centered symmetric law. Standard nondegenerate/degenerate order-two U-statistic projection theory, finite fourth moments, and consistency of the sample variance give the stated limiting normal family. Direct uniform integration yields variance \(1/7\). A folded-normal conditional calculation gives the null variance as an explicit one-dimensional integral. The finite checker passed 18,443 assertions; quadrature is supplementary rather than an infinite proof.

## Originality

PASS, with bounded historical-priority risk. The inspected original HTML preprint, Kattumannil, arXiv:2001.07932v4, Sec. 2, uses the signed integrated fixed-point contrast and asserts its general discrimination, but does not establish a reflection-odd identity, all-symmetric nonnormal blind class, or the precise uniform limiting rejection law. The earlier Betsch--Ebner tests, arXiv:1803.07069, are based on nonnegative squared \(L^2\) discrepancies; their global consistency does not imply coverage of this signed-integral failure. Direct searches for the original statistic, reflection symmetry, equivalent order-statistic form and uniform power law, including mathematical research-index records, did not identify a covering claim. Search incompleteness and earlier signed L-statistic literature remain residual risks; the full Betsch--Ebner paper was not inspected, although its published abstract identifies a different functional.

## Value

PASS. The scalarization of a pointwise normal characterization changes a potentially omnibus discrepancy into a signed reflection-odd contrast with an infinite-dimensional blind class. This is consequential for test selection, not a change of notation: a nonnormal uniform distribution with matching variance has asymptotic power approximately \(0.0563\%\), far below both consistency and the nominal 5% benchmark. The exact sign identity and limiting variance make the failure reproducible and structurally explain why additional observations alone cannot repair it.

## Limits

Only the uncentered-input statistic for the zero-mean Gaussian null is used for the numerical power claim. Finite checker enumerations and quadrature do not certify general theorems by themselves. No claim is made about separate fitted-location, empirical-likelihood, or squared-distance tests. Historical priority is not certified.
