# Reflection parity and quotient homology of nested-reversal tournament flag complexes

## Finding

For any integers \(m\ge1\) and \(r\ge1\), put \(n=m+2r\). Begin with the transitive tournament on labels \(0,\ldots,n-1\), with arcs from smaller to larger labels, and reverse exactly the \(r\) nested arcs \((j,n-1-j)\) for \(0\le j<r\). Write \(X_{m,r}\) for its directed flag complex: a set of vertices spans a simplex precisely when its induced tournament is transitive. Reflection \(\rho(i)=n-1-i\) sends the entire directed tournament to its converse and therefore preserves the simplicial complex \(X_{m,r}\).

**Theorem.** For every specified \(m,r\), the complete integral homotopy type, reflection action, and reduced rational homology of the *ordinary geometric* reflection quotient are

\[
X_{m,r}\simeq\bigvee_{d=1}^{r}S^d,\qquad
\rho_*|_{\widetilde H_d(X_{m,r};\mathbb Z)}=(-1)^d\operatorname{id},\quad(1\le d\le r),
\]

\[
\widetilde H_d(X_{m,r}/\langle\rho\rangle;\mathbb Q)\cong
\begin{cases}\mathbb Q,&2\le d\le r\text{ and }d\text{ even},\\0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}
\]

Each displayed nonzero integral reduced homology group before quotient is a free rank-one group. No claim is made about the integral homology of the quotient.

## Assumptions and scope

The initial middle block comprises \(m\) vertices; it is a transitive subtournament and hence its directed flag complex is a simplex. The nested reversed arcs are pairwise vertex-disjoint. The exact set of forbidden triples in the final tournament consists of every \(\{i,k,j\}\) for which \((i,j)\) is a reversed outer edge and \(i<k<j\). Only the ordinary non-equivariant homotopy type and the actual integral representation on homology are classified; no equivariant wedge decomposition is asserted. For a tournament every acyclic induced subset has a unique linear extension, so its directed flag complex is an ordinary abstract simplicial complex.

## Proof

**1. The one-step decomposition.** Let \(T\) be any finite nonempty tournament and \(K=\operatorname{dFl}(T)\). Add vertices \(s,t\), direct all arcs \(s\to v\to t\) for \(v\in T\), and direct \(t\to s\). Each triangle \(s,v,t\) is cyclic; a set containing exactly one new vertex is acyclic precisely when its old subset is. Therefore the *exact* simplicial equality is

\[
\operatorname{dFl}(T')=(s*K)\cup_K(t*K)\cup\{\{s,t\}\}.
\]

The first two cones form the unreduced suspension \(\Sigma K\). Since \(K\neq\varnothing\), this suspension is connected. Attaching an additional edge between its suspension tips adds one circle up to homotopy, yielding \(\operatorname{dFl}(T')\simeq\Sigma K\vee S^1\). This decomposition is a standard CW pushout, not an assertion that the new edge itself is a circle.

**2. Iteration and degree range.** Apply the operation successively to the central transitive \(m\)-vertex tournament, adding the outer pair each time. Its simplicial complex initially is a contractible simplex. After \(r\) steps the reversed arcs are exactly the stated nested pairs. The suspension-circle recurrence and the suspension of a wedge prove by induction that its homotopy type is one sphere in each degree \(1,\ldots,r\); in particular, there is no integral torsion.

**3. Reflection on homology.** The middle reflection maps a transitive tournament to its converse and acts simplicially on its (contractible) directed flag complex. At each new step the label reflection exchanges \(s\) and \(t\) and restricts to the previous middle reflection. Thus, on the suspension piece, reflection induces **minus** the old reflection on suspended reduced integral homology: exchanging the two cone directions reverses the suspension coordinate. For every \(d\ge2\), the newly attached edge cannot affect \(H_d\), so the induced sign in degree \(d\) equals minus the sign previously in degree \(d-1\). Meanwhile the new first homology is generated by the attached edge followed by a path in the suspension. The suspension of the preceding connected complex is simply connected, so this new group is free of rank one and reflection, which reverses that edge, acts by \(-1\). Starting from the contractible middle simplex, induction yields exactly \((-1)^d\) in each degree \(1\le d\le r\). No choices of homology generator can change these scalar signs.

**4. Ordinary reflection quotient.** A finite simplicial involution becomes admissible after barycentric subdivision. With rational coefficients, cellular transfer/averaging identifies the homology of the orbit complex with the invariant rational homology of the original complex, even when the action has fixed points. The one-dimensional rational module in degree \(d\) is invariant exactly for even \(d\). This proves the quotient formula.

## Verification

The accompanying standalone \(`verify.py`\) enumerates every face independently using the forbidden-triangle test, computes all reduced mod-two homology ranks by exact bitset Gaussian elimination, and checks the *signed simplicial Lefschetz number* of the reflection. For \(1\le r\le4\) and \(1\le m\le3\), all 12 cases have one nonzero reduced mod-two class in each degree \(1,\ldots,r\), and the unreduced Lefschetz number is \(r+1\), consistent with the specified orientation signs. Run \(`python3 verify.py`\). These finite checks are not used to infer the infinite statement; the preceding simplicial decomposition and suspension-sign argument prove that statement over the integers.

## Relationship to prior work

Govc, Levi, and Smith introduced tournament-based flag constructions and directionality filtrations (arXiv:2003.00324, first publicly posted 29 February 2020). Govc investigated directed flag homotopy types computationally and proved structural results about tournaments (arXiv:2006.05333). Research on reversed pairings in transitive tournaments (arXiv:2312.04039) considers tournament indecomposability rather than the reflection homology representation. A previously recorded classification of exactly **two** disjoint reversed arcs already proves the nested two-arc case \(S^1\vee S^2\) and uses the suspension-plus-edge construction in that setting. That existing nonequivariant case is not claimed new here. The result here is the simultaneous all-degree integral reflection character and all-order ordinary quotient ranks for \(r\) nested faults, with unboundedly many independently surviving quotient degrees. Searches of the indexed record and inspected primary texts found no theorem asserting these new equivariant and quotient conclusions.

## Limitations

Reflection is a simplicial symmetry, not an automorphism preserving the original tournament's arrows: it sends its orientation to the converse. No integral torsion, fundamental group, cup product, fixed-point-space homotopy type, or equivariant homotopy splitting is claimed for the quotient. Source indexing is incomplete and an unindexed equivalent theorem may exist. Only the stated concentric reverse-pair family is treated, not arbitrary arc reversals.
