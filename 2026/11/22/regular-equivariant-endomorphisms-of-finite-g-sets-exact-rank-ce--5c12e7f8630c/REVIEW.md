# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

Every equivariant map sends a transitive orbit surjectively onto a target orbit. If \(fgf=f\), restriction of \(g\) to the image of \(f\) is an equivariant section, forcing an isomorphic source orbit for every image orbit. Conversely independently choose inverse maps on corresponding source orbits to construct an equivariant section. For fixed target support \(\mathbf s\), regularity is precisely the condition that each chosen type-\(i\) target orbit is hit from a type-\(i\) source. Inclusion–exclusion gives \(F_i(\mathbf s)\), including the boundaries \(s_i=0\), \(m_i=1\), and repeated image-rank exponents. The global-regularity criterion is proved in both directions by a unique-type-source contradiction and explicit nonregular counterexamples. A standalone checker directly enumerated equivariant inner inverses and corroborated all ten finite cases; finite checks are not substituted for the proof.

The older 1995 projective-act work has a narrower action category; the available excerpt does not establish this general mixed-stabilizer result. The published nilpotent-map census for pointed finite group actions concerns eventual collapse, not semigroup regularity. No inspected stronger result directly implies both of these claims. Earlier implicit or unindexed treatment remains a publication-priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The regular subset of an equivariant transformation monoid is a natural and generally proper semigroup-theoretic stratum. Its exact rank distribution across every finite group action is meaningful beyond individual examples, while the simple structural criterion characterizes when the whole monoid is regular. The order-two action with one free orbit and two fixed points shows the distinction is non-vacuous (\(14\) regular maps versus \(16\) total). The theorem links a classical group invariant, the table of marks, to a natural transformation-semigroup statistic not captured by total-map enumeration.
