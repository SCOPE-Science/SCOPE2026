# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

For \(N=2n\ge6\), the square of the cycle has exactly the nearest- and next-nearest-neighbour bonds counted by the stated Potts Hamiltonian. Pair-state cyclic transfer enumerates each labelled spin configuration with its exact defect and distinguished-colour counts. The three relevant determinant coefficients have been explicitly recomputed with integer polynomial arithmetic. The trace–logarithmic-derivative identity proves the full constant/linear/quadratic ground-state formula for arbitrarily large size. Independent exact enumeration and direct traces confirm the result at finite sizes but are not used as an infinite-size inference. There is no unproved step in the asserted range.

## Originality — PASS on examined evidence

The nearest prior antiprism result is the chromatic-polynomial and chromatic-root calculation of Lopez-Bonilla, Allagan and Morgan, which addresses only proper colourings. Its results establish the defect-zero branch (and general transfer methods), but they do not calculate the first nonzero defect orders, their one-colour occupancy distribution or the quadratic finite-size ground-state sector. Salas and Sokal work on chromatic polynomials for different lattice strips and provides a general transfer method rather than this evaluated statement. MathWorld provides the antiprism graph identification and chromatic polynomial. Searches of closely related published research revealed zero-field three-state Potts frustration counts on different graph families, none giving an antiprism all-size equivalent. Residual risk: a prior unindexed transfer-matrix computation for the exact periodic nearest/next-nearest q=3 Potts chain could contain the same result. That risk cannot be eliminated by a database partition or search absence.

## Mathematical value — PASS

This classifies a natural, planar, four-regular, geometrically frustrated Potts family at all sizes, showing an exact residue-three minimum-frustration law and a constant-to-linear-to-quadratic jump in finite-size degeneracy. The full fixed-colour ground-state polynomial resolves the sector selection under an infinitesimal colour preference without claiming the finite-field diagram. In particular, the nonzero defect branches cannot be inferred from a proper-colouring count alone, and the quadratic sector has a specific, motivated combinatorial and statistical-mechanical meaning. Its narrow scope is acknowledged.
