# Right-polynomial maps on every finite radical-square-zero quiver algebra

## Finding

Let \(Q\) be an arbitrary nonempty finite directed multiquiver, allowing loops and parallel arrows, with vertex set \(Q_0\), arrow set \(Q_1\), and \(k=\mathbb F_q\), where \(q\ge2\) is any prime power. Let \(A=kQ/J^2\), with \(J\) the ideal generated by arrows. Put \(v=|Q_0|\), \(m=|Q_1|\), and \(r=|\{i\in Q_0:\text{at least one arrow starts at }i\}|\). A right-polynomial map is \(x\mapsto\sum_{d\ge0}c_dx^d\), with \(c_d\in A\) on the left.

**Complete classification and exact census.** Writing \(c_d=\sum_i c_{id}e_i+\sum_a c_{ad}a\), identify each polynomial with scalar coefficient polynomials \(f_i(X)=\sum_d c_{id}X^d\) and \(h_a(X)=\sum_d c_{ad}X^d\) in \(k[X]\). Two polynomials induce the same function on \(A\) if and only if their vertex polynomials agree modulo \(H^2\) at every vertex with an outgoing arrow, agree modulo \(H\) at other vertices, and their arrow polynomials agree modulo \(H\), where \(H(X)=X^q-X\). Thus the full null ideal is the following explicit two-sided ideal of \(A[X]\):

\[
\mathcal N(A)=\bigoplus_{i:\operatorname{outdeg}(i)>0} e_iH^2k[X]
\oplus\bigoplus_{i:\operatorname{outdeg}(i)=0} e_iHk[X]
\oplus\bigoplus_{a\in Q_1} aHk[X].
\]

The number of distinct right-polynomial functions is
\[
|\mathcal F_{\rm right}(A)|=q^{q(v+r+m)}.
\]
The exact number of such functions that are bijections of the underlying set of \(A\) is
\[
|\mathcal P_{\rm right}(A)|=(q!)^v(q-1)^{qr}q^{qm}.
\]
No closure of \(\mathcal P_{\rm right}(A)\) under composition is asserted. Indeed, the full set of right-polynomial functions is closed under functional composition **if and only if** all arrows in \(Q\) are loops, equivalently \(A\) is commutative.

This is one classification theorem with its null-ideal, census, and composition consequences; the formulas are simultaneously valid for arbitrary oriented cycles, loops, parallel arrows, and disconnected quivers.

## Assumptions and scope

Use the convention \(e_{s(a)}a=a=ae_{t(a)}\) for an arrow \(a\), and all products of two arrows vanish in \(A\). The symbol \(X\) is central in the formal ring \(A[X]\), but evaluation is *right evaluation*, with coefficients on the left; evaluation is not generally multiplicative. Count distinct induced maps, not distinct polynomials. The primes dividing \(q\) may be arbitrary. An outgoing loop contributes to the outgoing-vertex count \(r\).

For \(b=\sum_i x_i e_i+\sum_a y_a a\), with \(x_i,y_a\in k\), write the divided difference
\[
\Delta_f(s,t)=\begin{cases}(f(s)-f(t))/(s-t),&s\ne t,\\f'(s),&s=t.\end{cases}
\]
The complete evaluation normal form is
\[
\left(\sum_i e_if_i+\sum_a ah_a\right)(b)
=\sum_i f_i(x_i)e_i+
\sum_{a\in Q_1}\left[h_a(x_{t(a)})+y_a\Delta_{f_{s(a)}}(x_{s(a)},x_{t(a)})\right]a.
\]
For loops the displayed divided difference is always the derivative case.

## Proof

**1. Path-power formula.** Because \(J^2=0\), the coefficient of an arrow \(a:i\to j\) in \(b^d\) is \(y_a\sum_{h=0}^{d-1}x_i^{d-1-h}x_j^h\) for \(d\ge1\), and the coefficient of \(e_i\) is \(x_i^d\). This follows by choosing the unique radical factor in any surviving product of \(d\) factors. Multiplying on the left by \(e_i\) or \(a\) yields the displayed evaluation normal form. The geometric-sum identity identifies the coefficient with \(\Delta_{f_i}\), including the derivative at equal arguments.

**2. Exact kernel.** Suppose the evaluation is zero. Setting every arrow coordinate to zero forces \(f_i(s)=0\) at every \(s\in k\), so \(H\mid f_i\). Taking the coefficient of a fixed arrow with its coordinate zero forces \(h_a(s)=0\) for all \(s\in k\), so \(H\mid h_a\). Varying the arrow coordinate then forces \(\Delta_{f_i}(s,t)=0\) for every arrow starting at \(i\), for all \(s,t\in k\) (only \(s=t\) is available for loops). Since \(f_i\) vanishes on \(k\), all unequal-argument divided differences already vanish. Therefore the additional condition is \(f_i'(s)=0\) for every \(s\in k\) at each emitting vertex. As \(H'= -1\), writing \(f_i=Hg_i\) proves \(H\mid g_i\), equivalently \(H^2\mid f_i\). Conversely, these divisibilities visibly make the evaluation zero. The displayed decomposition of \(\mathcal N(A)\) follows. It is a two-sided ideal: a vertex coefficient \(e_iH\) can multiply an arrow on its right only if \(i\) emits it, in which case the vertex coefficient is divisible by \(H^2\); every other Peirce multiplication respects the indicated divisibilities, and two arrow coefficients multiply to zero.

**3. Independent interpolation parameters.** For each vertex, \(f_i\) gives arbitrary field values \(F_i:k\to k\). At each emitting vertex, it independently gives arbitrary derivative data \(D_i:k\to k\). At each arrow, \(h_a\) gives arbitrary values \(B_a:k\to k\). Indeed, the pair of value and derivative functions is parametrized by \(k[X]/H^2\) by the Chinese remainder theorem on the coprime squares \((X-s)^2\), while a value function alone is parametrized by \(k[X]/H\). These data are individually recoverable from the map: use diagonal inputs for \(F_i\), zero-arrow inputs for \(B_a\), and vary one arrow coordinate with its endpoints equal to recover \(D_i\). Thus there are exactly \(q^{qv}\), \(q^{qr}\), and \(q^{qm}\) independent choices, respectively. This gives the total count.

**4. Permutation criterion and census.** The projection of \(A\) to its diagonal-coordinate set \(k^v\) intertwines a polynomial map with \((x_i)_i\mapsto(F_i(x_i))_i\). Every diagonal fiber has cardinality \(q^m\). A bijection of \(A\) must therefore induce a bijection of \(k^v\), hence every \(F_i\) must permute \(k\). On each fiber, the arrow-coordinate map is diagonal affine, with multiplier \(\Delta_{f_{s(a)}}(x_{s(a)},x_{t(a)})\) on arrow \(a\). For unequal endpoint values this multiplier is nonzero automatically because \(F_{s(a)}\) is injective; for equal values it is \(D_{s(a)}(s)\), which must be nonzero at every \(s\in k\). The converse follows from the same diagonal-affine expression. There are \((q!)^v\) choices for the vertex permutations, \((q-1)^{qr}\) choices for their requisite nonzero derivatives, and \(q^{qm}\) free arrow offsets. Their product gives the asserted exact count.

**5. Sharp composition boundary.** If every arrow is a loop, all basis generators commute, hence \(A\) is commutative; ordinary polynomial substitution proves composition closure. Conversely, let \(a:i\to j\) have distinct endpoints. Consider the two right-polynomial maps \(G(b)=e_ib\) and \(F(b)=b^2\). If \(b=se_i+te_j+ya\), with all other coordinates zero, then \(F(G(b))=s^2e_i+sya\). Were this composite a right-polynomial map, its source-vertex value function would necessarily be \(s\mapsto s^2\), so its \(a\)-coefficient multiplier at unequal endpoint values \(s,t\) would have to be \(s+t\), by the normal form. Yet the actual multiplier is \(s\). Taking \(s=0\), \(t=1\), and comparing inputs with \(y=0\) and \(y=1\) gives a contradiction over every finite field. Thus there is no composition closure as soon as a nonloop arrow exists.

## Verification

The accompanying standalone `CHECKER.py` constructs the multiplication tables of small truncated path algebras directly, evaluates all coefficient monomials through degree \(2q-1\), computes the finite-field rank of the evaluation map, enumerates every distinct function in the resulting image, and counts the bijections. It runs without third-party packages. Use `python CHECKER.py` after extraction. Its terminating marker is `RIGHT_POLYNOMIAL_QUIVER_CHECKS_OK`.

Exhaustive cases include \(q=2\) with one loop, two parallel loops, one directed edge, a two-cycle, a two-edge fork, and a directed three-vertex chain, as well as \(q=3\) with one loop and a single directed edge. In particular the source fork over \(\mathbb F_2\) has \(4096\) distinct polynomial functions while the three-vertex directed chain has \(16384\); in each case there are \(128\) bijective ones. These are finite consistency checks, not substitutes for the proof valid for all finite fields and quivers.

## Relationship to prior work

Werner, *Some results on null ideals of finite rings* (first public posting 2026-08-26, arXiv:2608.25853), proves that nilpotency index at most three of the Jacobson radical ensures the null ideal is two-sided. That theorem covers the **two-sidedness conclusion**, but does not give the displayed quiver-dependent kernel, arbitrary-quiver cardinalities, bijection classification, or composition threshold. Frisch, *Polynomial functions on upper triangular matrix algebras* (2016 preprint, published 2017), gives general null-polynomial criteria for full upper triangular matrix rings. These methods cover special triangular cases, but do not state the all-quiver, orientation-sensitive census and composition boundary. Al-Maktry and El-Deken, *Polynomial functions on a class of finite non-commutative rings* (2024, arXiv:2409.10208), analyze centrally adjoined square-zero variables and polynomial permutations; this covers related commutative-loop and central-extension behavior, but arbitrary nonloop quiver arrows are not central and arbitrary cyclic quivers are not of the full-upper-triangular type.

The key additional structural step is the explicit simultaneous source-dependent divided-difference/target-dependent offset normal form for all directed multiquivers, which yields both counts and the universal nonclosure witness. The general theory of finite-field Hermite interpolation, including \(H^2\)-residues, is standard background rather than claimed as new.

## Limitations

Novelty is assessed against the specified sources and record searches, not certified priority against all literature. Specializations to commutative square-zero extensions and upper triangular matrix rings can be derived from earlier general theorems; the claimed contribution is the complete all-quiver classification and exact census, including noncommutative quivers with oriented cycles and the composition criterion. The checker is exhaustive only in its finite listed cases and handles prime fields, while the proof treats all prime powers. No result for general finite rings with higher radical powers is claimed. The bijective right-polynomial functions need not form a group under composition.
