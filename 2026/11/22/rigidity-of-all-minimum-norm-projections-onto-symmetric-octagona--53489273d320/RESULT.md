# Rigidity of all minimum-norm projections onto symmetric octagonal planes

## Finding

For the real normed plane \(X_\beta=(\mathbb R^2,N_\beta)\) with
\[
N_\beta(x,y)=\max\{|x|,|y|,\beta(|x|+|y|)\},\qquad \frac12\le\beta\le1,
\]
its absolute projection constant, defined by
\[
\lambda(X)=\sup_{i:X\hookrightarrow Y\ \mathrm{isometric}}\ \inf_{P:Y\to i(X),\ P|_{i(X)}=\mathrm{id}}\|P\|,
\]
is exactly
\[
\boxed{\lambda(X_\beta)=\frac{\beta}{2\beta^2-2\beta+1}.}
\]
**The new rigidity conclusion:** the canonical four-coordinate embedding has exactly one minimal linear retraction, even among completely nonsymmetric maps. Its full eight real coefficients are forced. The exact projection constant itself is recoverable from classical planar self-length theory and is used here as background for the uniqueness theorem. The function is strictly greater than one for \(1/2<\beta<1\), has a unique peak \((1+\sqrt2)/2\) at \(\beta=1/\sqrt2\), and returns to one at \(\beta=1\). Thus the regular octagon is the uniquely least-injective member of this natural one-parameter family (up to the stated parametrization), although both endpoints describe squares by linear isometry.

## Assumptions and scope

The scalar field is **real**. At \(\beta=1/2\), the third norm constraint is redundant and the unit ball is the coordinate square. At \(\beta=1\), the coordinate constraints are redundant and the unit ball is the rotated square (the real \(\ell_1^2\) ball). For every strict interior parameter, the unit ball is an eight-sided polygon invariant under independent coordinate sign changes and coordinate swaps. The formula is for the absolute projection constant, not the relative projection norm of an arbitrary fixed ambient inclusion.

Embed isometrically into \(\ell_\infty^4\) using
\[
J_\beta(x,y)=(x,y,\beta(x+y),\beta(x-y)).
\]
Let \(q_\beta=2\beta^2-2\beta+1\) and set
\[
a_\beta=\frac{1-\beta}{q_\beta},\qquad b_\beta=\frac{2\beta-1}{2q_\beta}.
\]
The unique unrestricted optimal retraction is
\[
R_\beta(u_1,u_2,u_3,u_4)
=(a_\beta u_1+b_\beta(u_3+u_4),\ a_\beta u_2+b_\beta(u_3-u_4)).
\]
It satisfies \(R_\beta J_\beta=\mathrm{id}\) and \(\|R_\beta\|=\lambda(X_\beta)\). The associated actual projection in \(\ell_\infty^4\) is \(J_\beta R_\beta\).

## Proof

**1. The canonical embedding attains the absolute constant.** For any normed superspace \(Y\) containing an isometric copy \(i(X_\beta)\), extend the four coordinate functionals of \(J_\beta i^{-1}\) from \(i(X_\beta)\) to \(Y\) using the real Hahn–Banach theorem, without increasing their norm (each has norm at most one). Their tuple gives a contraction \(\Phi:Y\to\ell_\infty^4\) with \(\Phi i=J_\beta\). For any retraction \(R:\ell_\infty^4\to X_\beta\) of \(J_\beta\), the map \(iR\Phi\) is a projection of norm at most \(\|R\|\). Conversely, the choice \(Y=\ell_\infty^4\) yields the reverse inequality. Hence \(\lambda(X_\beta)\) equals the minimum norm of such retractions.

**2. Symmetry reduction.** Let \(G\) be the order-eight group of signed-coordinate symmetries of \(\mathbb R^2\), generated by swapping coordinates and changing one coordinate's sign. Each symmetry of the eight-sided norm induces a signed permutation of the four linear coordinate functionals defining \(J_\beta\), so it extends to an isometry of \(\ell_\infty^4\) compatible with \(J_\beta\). Averaging any retraction over \(G\) produces an equivariant retraction of no larger norm. A direct calculation of the intertwining relations (first use the individual sign changes, then the coordinate swap) shows that every equivariant retraction has exactly the form
\[
R_{a,b}(u)=(a u_1+b(u_3+u_4),\ a u_2+b(u_3-u_4)),\qquad a+2\beta b=1,
\]
for real \(a,b\). Therefore it suffices to minimize over this one-parameter family, and the equivariant minimizer can be identified by two-branch equalization. We then prove separately that no nonsymmetric minimizer exists.

**3. Its operator norm.** The extreme points of the real \(\ell_\infty^4\) unit ball are the sixteen sign vectors. For any such sign vector, the expression for \(R_{a,b}\) has at most one output coordinate receiving both occurrences of \(b\), while the other receives none. Choosing signs to align with \(a\) and \(b\) attains the bounds. Thus
\[
\|R_{a,b}\|=\max\{|a|+2|b|,\ 2\beta(|a|+|b|)\}.
\]
Write \(u=|a|\), \(v=|b|\). The retraction identity implies \(u+2\beta v\ge1\). Put \(q=2\beta^2-2\beta+1\) and choose \(\theta=\beta(2\beta-1)/q\in[0,1]\). The exact identity
\[
\theta(u+2v)+(1-\theta)2\beta(u+v)=\frac{\beta}{q}(u+2\beta v)
\]
shows that \(\|R_{a,b}\|\ge\beta/q\). The proposed nonnegative coefficients \(a_\beta,b_\beta\) obey \(a_\beta+2\beta b_\beta=1\) and both branches equal \(\beta/q\), proving the sharp bound.

**4. Uniqueness among equivariant retractions.** For \(1/2<\beta<1\), equality forces both branches of the maximum to equal their convex combination and the triangle inequality \(|a+2\beta b|\le|a|+2\beta|b|\) to be sharp. The two linear branch equations and \(a+2\beta b=1\) yield uniquely \(a=a_\beta\), \(b=b_\beta\). At \(\beta=1/2\), \(\|R_{a,b}\|\ge|a|+2|b|\ge|a+b|+|b|=1+|b|\), so \(b=0\), \(a=1\). At \(\beta=1\), \(\|R_{a,b}\|\ge2(|a|+|b|)\ge|a+2b|+|a|=1+|a|\), so \(a=0\), \(b=1/2\). In all cases the equivariant minimizer is unique.

**5. Every, even nonsymmetric, minimizer is the symmetric one.** Let \(R\) be any retraction with \(\|R\|=L_\beta=\beta/q_\beta\). Averaging its conjugates over the eight signed-coordinate symmetries gives \(R_\beta\), by the uniqueness just proved among equivariant minimizers. Let \(e\) be a source sign vector and \(\ell\) a target norm-one functional such that \(\ell(R_\beta e)=L_\beta\). Since
\[
L_\beta=\ell(R_\beta e)=\frac18\sum_{g\in G}\ell(g^{-1}R\widetilde g\,e),
\]
and every summand is at most \(L_\beta\), *all* summands equal \(L_\beta\). Taking the identity summand gives \(\ell(Re)=L_\beta\). Thus every norming pair of the averaged projection also norms every minimizing retraction.

Here are eight specific pairs, written as \((e,\ell)\), whose saturation can be checked by direct substitution for **every** \(1/2\le\beta\le1\):
\[
\begin{aligned}
&((-1,-1,-1,-1),-x),&&((-1,-1,-1,-1),-\beta(x+y)),\\
&((-1,-1,-1,1),-y),&&((-1,-1,-1,1),-\beta(x+y)),\\
&((-1,1,-1,-1),-x),&&((-1,1,-1,-1),-\beta(x-y)),\\
&((-1,1,1,-1),y),&&((-1,1,1,-1),-\beta(x-y)).
\end{aligned}
\]
Each \(\ell\) is a member of the eight supporting functionals defining \(N_\beta\). For a general retraction represented by a real \(2\times4\) matrix \(M\), the eight constraints \(\ell(Me)=L_\beta\) are eight **linear equations in its eight entries**. Order entries row by row and write each coefficient row as the tensor product \(\ell\otimes e\). Elementary row elimination yields
\[
\boxed{\left|\det\left[\ell_i\otimes e_i\right]_{i=1}^{8}\right|=64\beta^4>0.}
\]
Therefore the eight saturation equations have exactly one solution. The displayed \(R_\beta\) is a solution, so \(M=R_\beta\) for every minimizing retraction. This establishes uniqueness **without a symmetry assumption**, including both degenerate square endpoints. It is not asserted that the analogous projection is unique in every other ambient isometric superspace.

**6. Supporting parameter behavior (not a separate novelty claim).** Differentiation gives
\[
\frac{d}{d\beta}\lambda(X_\beta)=\frac{1-2\beta^2}{(2\beta^2-2\beta+1)^2}.
\]
Its unique zero in \([1/2,1]\) is \(\beta=1/\sqrt2\), which is a strict maximum. The endpoint values are one and the maximal value is \((1+\sqrt2)/2\). First-order Taylor expansion at the endpoints gives
\[
\lambda(X_\beta)-1=2(\beta-1/2)+O((\beta-1/2)^2),\qquad\beta\downarrow1/2,
\]
and
\[
\lambda(X_\beta)-1=(1-\beta)+O((1-\beta)^2),\qquad\beta\uparrow1.
\]

## Verification

The included stand-alone script `verify_octagonal.py` checks the eight independent saturated-pair equations and their nonsingular determinant, alongside the exact rational formulas, nonnegative optimal coefficients, retraction identity, weighted dual lower bound, and all sixteen extreme sign vectors for a grid of six rational parameters including both endpoints. It independently enumerates the norm of \(R_\beta\) without assuming its closed form, checks every defining support functional, and reports the number of exact assertions. A separate exploratory linear-programming calculation over all eight coefficients of unrestricted retractions agreed with the analytic formula at nine parameters, including the regular octagon, to numerical precision. Only the analytic proof above establishes the all-parameter result; finite checks are not infinite proofs. Run `python3 verify_octagonal.py` using only the Python standard library.

## Relationship to prior work

Kobos (arXiv:2611.19902v1, first public 15 November 2026) establishes the characterization of real and complex finite-dimensional norm-one absolute projection constants and a global stability estimate. It does not state an exact octagonal interpolation profile. Chalmers and Shekhtman (1985) calculate projection constants for **regular** polyhedral spaces, including the regular polygon special member; that special value is prior knowledge here, not a newly claimed independent invariant. König and Tomczak-Jaegermann (1992) establish dimension-wide sharp bounds, including the maximal real planar value \(4/3\) attained by a hexagonal space, whereas the present octagonal family has a smaller exact peak. The classical exact planar self-length identity \(p(X)=8/\lambda(X)\) for planes with enough symmetries (Chalmers, Franchetti and Giaquinta, 1996, following earlier results) already implies the **numerical constant** here by an elementary polygon-perimeter calculation. Consequently the numerical value, its peak and endpoint rates are presented as background, not independent discoveries. The new assertion is the rigidity of **every** norm-minimizing projection on the four-coordinate overspace, with an eight-equation invertibility certificate. A related published five-coordinate plane has a separate strong uniqueness theorem; its ambient embedding and plane are different. The method uses classical averaging and Hahn–Banach extension; these general principles are not claimed as new.

## Limitations

The statement is real-scalar and two-dimensional. It proves uniqueness of the minimal retraction only in the specified four-coordinate ambient embedding; no assertion is made for every possible superspace. It makes no assertion about sharp Banach–Mazur stability exponents in arbitrary dimensions. The projection-constant *value* is a corollary of the known symmetric-plane perimeter identity, not novel. The older projection-uniqueness literature, especially the theorem of Franchetti–Votruba (1976), was not available in full and may contain broader optimizer-rigidity information; the claim of new uniqueness remains bibliographically qualified. Independent audit has not been performed.
