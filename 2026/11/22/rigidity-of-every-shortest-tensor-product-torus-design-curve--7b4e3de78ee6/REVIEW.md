# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The proof reconstructs the arclength derivative-energy identity and the sharp spectral bound for the precise frequency cube, including all three parity cases. Equality requires the complete span of the lowest Fourier modes because the spectral gap is strict. Each constant-modulus finite Fourier series is a single character; taking ratios of adjacent grid characters makes every coordinate of the path affine. Exhaustion of the consecutive spectral integers then yields a collision-free base-\(q\) digit-sum tiling, whose induction forces exactly the powers of \(q\). The converse verifies all Fourier moments. The finite exact-rational test suite checks representative traces, digit tilings, and frequency-annihilator conditions, not the infinite theorem.

**Originality — PASS, qualified.** Ehler’s *Optimal bounds for design curves on the torus* (arXiv:2611.03362, Section 2) proves the sharp bound and exhibits a Korobov extremizer. The inspected full text does not classify every equality case. The new result is precisely that classification, incorporating the no-nonlinear-minimizer assertion and exact enumeration of all winding directions. Kunis’ *Positive quadrature and mobile sampling of multivariate trigonometric polynomials* (arXiv:2608.11915) studies positive generalized quadrature and Korobov quasioptimality; its accessible abstract does not settle all equality cases. Its full text could not be inspected, so older implicit coverage remains a stated priority risk. Related available published claims about spherical design curves and finite point quadrature concern different domains or objectives. No searched result is shown to imply the classification; search absence alone is not proof of priority.

**Value — PASS.** Exact length optimization is a natural curve-based quadrature design problem: constructions attaining a bound do not identify whether curved or arithmetically different optimizers exist. The theorem establishes a rigid, fully classified optimizer family in every dimension and at every degree, including a concrete orbit count. It eliminates hidden alternative minimizers and identifies the only optimal winding architecture, rather than presenting a parameter-specific numerical improvement. This is structural rather than a routine restatement of the known length formula.

**Closest-source and proof limitations.** The spectral inequality, isotropic grid, and Korobov existence are Ehler’s prior results; only their equality-case consequences and the integer-weight classification are asserted here. The generalized weighted setting of Kunis is outside the theorem; that precursor’s full text remains an access risk. The result has not been checked by an independent mathematician, and the supplementary exhaustive experiments do not replace proof.
