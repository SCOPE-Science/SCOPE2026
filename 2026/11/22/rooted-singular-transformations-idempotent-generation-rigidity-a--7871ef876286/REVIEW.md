# Scientific review

## Correctness (PASS)
The theorem is proved for every finite size \(n\ge2\), not extrapolated from computations. A rank-deficit induction reduces arbitrary pointed singular transformations to corank-one transformations. Those with an unrooted kernel pair are ordinary singular transformations on the unmarked points; those with a rooted kernel pair admit the explicit factorization \(f=e_{a\to0}g\), where \(g\) is an unpointed singular transformation. The inverse necessity argument uses invariant preimages of the fixed point. For minimum rank, every doubleton kernel needs its own leading rank-\(n-1\) generator, and the remaining directed graph obeys the classical strong-tournament criterion. The exceptional two-movable-point case is handled separately. The strong-tournament count follows from the unique initial strongly connected component. The extracted checker exhaustively verifies all nonroot tournaments for \(n=4,5\), the four-generator case \(n=3\), and a strong cyclic construction for \(n=6\).

## Originality (PASS, provisional historical priority)
Assiri--Al-bar (2026, arXiv:2606.28477) identify the pointed monoid through dominions and count its elements/idempotents; their inspected arguments do not give generating-set classification or the idempotent rank of its singular ideal. Howie (1966, 1978), and East--Gadouleau--Mitchell (2017), concern the *unpointed* singular semigroup and establish the key strong-tournament input, explicitly credited in the proof. Dolinka--East--Mitchell (2015) concern partition-preserving transformations rather than a common fixed point. The 2022 paper on 1-singular transformations has a different abstract definition and is not used to infer priority beyond the abstract; full-text access remained limited. Searches for pointed fixed-point singular ideals and generating tournaments did not locate directly covering statements. A historical unindexed statement may still exist.

## Value (PASS)
The singular ideal of a pointed full transformation monoid is a natural subsemigroup of the dominion appearing in the 2026 study. Its exact idempotent rank, classification of *all* idempotent generating sets, sharp small-size exception, and enumeration of every smallest generating set were not automatic from the numerical cardinality of the dominion. The theorem explains precisely how imposing one fixed point changes permissible idempotent generators while, from four points onwards, retaining the unpointed binomial rank. The compulsory rooted arrows and the nonroot strongly connected tournament form a structural rigidity statement useful in comparisons of transformation ideals. No claim about the entire pointed monoid is made.
