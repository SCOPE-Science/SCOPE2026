# Review of exact Santaló geometry for regular-simplex Cayley bipolar cubes

## Correctness

**PASS.** The precise object, normalization, and axial polar are explicit in the statement. The primal volume (5) is obtained by an elementary sign-orthant decomposition of a simplex difference body, followed by a beta integral. The polar volume (7) is independently derived from all \(2(n+1)\) supporting inequalities, an ordered-gap chamber decomposition, and a checked Euclidean Jacobian. Logarithmic differentiation shows strict convexity and a unique minimizing axial center. Simplex permutation symmetry proves that this axial minimum is the global Santaló center. The uniform asymptotic logarithmic expansion and a sign bracket of the exact derivative prove both the scaled-center limit and the volume-product limit. The standalone checker reconstructs the original convex hulls and halfspace intersections in dimensions two through six, making 35 direct numerical volume comparisons, and checks the planar radical formula. Finite evidence is distinguished from the analytic proof.

## Originality

**PASS, subject to historical-priority risk.** The principal motivating work of Pavlík, arXiv:2611.13896v1, Section 5.1 and Proposition 5.6, provides the Cayley-to-bipolar-cube construction but does not evaluate the volume or Santaló center. The published simplex reflected-Minkowski-sum profile gives volumes of horizontal slices; it does not supply the off-center polar of their Cayley union or the asymptotic constant \(e\). The ratio-bounded simplex central polytope result studies a different one-level polytope in lower dimension. General Santaló geometry supplies existence and uniqueness but not the exact profile. The searches and detailed implication comparisons are recorded in AUDIT.json. Broader older constructions of prismatoid and Cayley-product polar volumes remain an independently checkable priority risk.
