# Saturation of independent-set homotopy under parity-class edge faults in the four-cube

## Finding

Let \(Q_4\) be the graph on four-bit words, with edges joining words that differ in exactly one bit. Fix a coordinate \(i\in\{0,1,2,3\}\) and a parity \(p\in\{0,1\}\). Let \(F_{i,p}\) consist of the four edges in coordinate direction \(i\) whose three fixed coordinates have parity \(p\). For every nonempty \(A\subseteq F_{i,p}\), put \(K_A=\operatorname{Ind}(Q_4-A)\), where \(\operatorname{Ind}\) denotes the ordinary simplicial independence complex. Then

\[
|K_A|\simeq\bigvee^{6}S^3.
\]

More precisely, for any nonempty \(A\subseteq B\subseteq F_{i,p}\), the natural inclusion \(K_A\hookrightarrow K_B\) is a **simple-homotopy equivalence**: after relabeling by an automorphism of \(Q_4\), \(K_B\) simplicially collapses onto \(K_A\). In contrast, for every \(e\in F_{i,p}\), the map

\[
\widetilde H_3(\operatorname{Ind}(Q_4);\mathbb Z)
\longrightarrow\widetilde H_3(K_{\{e\}};\mathbb Z)
\]

is a split surjection \(\mathbb Z^7\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb Z^6\) with primitive rank-one kernel generated by an induced octahedral three-sphere. All other reduced integral homology vanishes for every complex in the statement.

## Assumptions and scope

All graphs are finite, simple and unweighted. Word coordinates are indexed \(0,1,2,3\), with integer encodings \(0,\ldots,15\). An independence face is a vertex subset containing no graph edge. The eight matchings \(F_{i,p}\) are not arbitrary four-edge matchings: each is a parity class of parallel edges, and only subsets of a **single** such matching are covered. The graph vertex set is retained after edge deletion.

By coordinate permutations and bit translations, all eight matchings are equivalent. Within one matching, translations of even parity in the other three coordinates, together with permutations of those coordinates, induce the full symmetric group on the four edges. Thus a nonempty subset is determined up to an automorphism by its size. It suffices to use the representatives

\[
F=\{\{0,1\},\{6,7\},\{10,11\},\{12,13\}\},
\qquad F_t=\text{the first }t\text{ edges of }F,
\quad K_t=\operatorname{Ind}(Q_4-F_t),\quad 0\le t\le4.
\]

The simple-homotopy assertion concerns inclusions between fault sets nested inside the same parity class. Nothing is asserted about arbitrary four-edge faults, other dimensions, or an equivariant homotopy decomposition.

## Proof

**1. Intact complex.** Enumerate all \(2^{16}\) vertex subsets of \(Q_4\) and retain exactly those without an edge. The dimensions-zero-through-seven face vector of \(K_0\) is \((16,88,208,228,128,56,16,2)\). On the nonempty face poset, repeatedly pair each still-unmatched face omitting pivot \(v\) with the face obtained by adding \(v\), for \(v=0,\ldots,15\). The specified finite certificate has 367 pairs and critical faces with membership masks

\[
1,\ 1680,\ 2400,\ 10370,\ 17028,\ 24600,\ 33064,\ 33096.
\]

Every critical face except mask \(1\) has four vertices. Reversing each matched arrow in the complete Hasse graph yields an acyclic directed graph, verified on every one of its 2,816 incidences. Discrete Morse theory therefore gives a CW complex with one zero-cell and seven three-cells; the three-cell attaching maps land in a point. Hence \(|K_0|\simeq\bigvee^7 S^3\). These base-complex facts were independently established previously; the embedded certificate permits their fresh reconstruction without using external certificate files.

**2. Link of the first new edge.** Deleting graph edge \(e=\{0,1\}\) creates exactly the independence faces containing both endpoints. Let \(N[e]\) denote the union of the two closed vertex neighborhoods in the *intact* graph, and set

\[
L=\operatorname{Ind}(Q_4-N[e]).
\]

The eight residual graph vertices are \(6,7,10,11,12,13,14,15\). The induced subgraph on \(6,7,10,11,12,13\) is the disjoint union of the three edges \(\{6,7\},\{10,11\},\{12,13\}\); its independence complex is the octahedral two-sphere \(T=(S^0)^{*3}\). An explicit certificate of eight elementary collapses takes \(L\) onto \(T\); it checks all free faces, not just homology ranks. In particular \(|L|\simeq S^2\), and the inclusion \(T\hookrightarrow L\) induces an isomorphism on integral second homology.

The edge-deletion complex is the union

\[
K_1=K_0\cup\bigl(\{0,1\}*L\bigr),
\qquad K_0\cap\bigl(\{0,1\}*L\bigr)=S^0*L,
\]

where \(\{0,1\}\) in the second term denotes the **one-simplex** on the two endpoints, and \(S^0\) denotes their two isolated vertices. Thus \(K_1\) is the homotopy cofiber of \(S^0*L\longrightarrow K_0\), with source homotopy equivalent to \(S^3\).

**3. The attachment kills a primitive generator.** The restriction of the attaching map to \(S^0*T\) is the octahedral three-sphere \((S^0)^{*4}\) arising from the four graph edges in \(F\). Its signed fundamental three-cycle consists of the sixteen tetrahedra obtained by picking one endpoint from each edge. The tetrahedron \(\sigma=\{0,6,11,13\}\) occurs with coefficient \(\pm1\) and is a maximal face of \(K_0\): no fifth vertex can be adjoined independently. Hence the integral degree-three cochain taking value one on \(\sigma\) and zero on other tetrahedra is a cocycle. Its evaluation on the octahedral three-cycle is \(\pm1\), so that cycle generates a **primitive nonzero** subgroup of \(\widetilde H_3(K_0;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^7\).

Since \(K_0\simeq\bigvee^7S^3\) is two-connected, the Hurewicz isomorphism identifies homotopy classes of the three-sphere attaching map with integral third homology. A primitive vector in \(\mathbb Z^7\) can be taken to the first standard basis vector by a homotopy self-equivalence of the sphere wedge. Attaching the contractible cone therefore kills precisely that one sphere and gives

\[
|K_1|\simeq\bigvee^6S^3.
\]

The long exact homology sequence also identifies the natural map from \(K_0\) as a split surjection with the asserted primitive kernel.

**4. All later faults are collapsible.** The supplied face-by-face elementary collapse certificates establish

\[
K_4\searrow K_3\searrow K_2\searrow K_1,
\]

using respectively 40, 32 and 26 face/coface pairs. In each pair, the lower-dimensional face lies in exactly one remaining immediate coface; the remaining complex at the end is exactly the stated lower stage. These are genuine collapses of inclusions, stronger than equal Betti numbers. Applying the verified full symmetric-group action on the four edges of \(F\) transports the result to arbitrary nonempty nested subsets of each \(F_{i,p}\), completing the proof.

## Verification

Execute `python3 verify.py certificate.json` with Python 3.10 or later; only the standard library is needed. The script regenerates all graph edges and all independent subsets, validates every pair of the 367-pair discrete Morse matching, reconstructs and checks the complete acyclic Hasse diagram, checks the eight link collapses and all 98 later-fault collapses individually, verifies the maximal tetrahedron detecting primitivity, and validates the 24-element subgroup acting as all four-edge permutations. As an independent numerical check it reduces each complete simplicial boundary complex over \(\mathbb F_2\), obtaining third Betti numbers \((7,6,6,6,6)\) and no others at stages \(0,1,2,3,4\).

The nonempty face counts across these five stages are \((742,785,837,901,981)\). These finite certificates are exhaustive for the stated sixteen-vertex graph and do not claim anything about arbitrary larger cubes.

## Relationship to prior work

Matsushita–Wakatsuki's 2022 work [1], read through its main statements and proof framework, classifies independence-complex homotopy types of **open** rectangular grid graphs; its result does not cover toroidal \(4\times4\) grids with edge faults. Adamaszek [2] develops the general edge-deletion cofiber sequence and collapsible-residue criterion, but studies free, cylindrical and certain intact toroidal grid recurrences; that general tool does not specify which third-homology class is killed by the first parity-class fault, nor the four-stage simple-homotopy saturation here. Jonsson [3] studies cross-polytope cycles on periodic grids; its available abstract and a related primary comparison support the background, but its complete text was not accessible during this inspection.

Two earlier published exact calculations [4,5] identify the intact four-cube's seven-sphere wedge and its eight primitive octahedral homology carriers. Their full mathematical result texts were compared. Those claims concern the **unfaulted** independence complex: they neither compute a faulted complex nor establish that later edge deletions in a parallel parity class induce simple-homotopy equivalences. The present claim includes a separate link collapse, a primitive attaching-map calculation and 98 new relative collapse pairs, with all steps reverified in the attached checker.

This also differs from the published first higher-independence complex of the four-cube, from cut and matching complexes of the cube, and from flag complexes of a **halved** cube: those are different simplicial complexes or different graph operations. No inspected covering result or exact precomputed fault table was identified.

## Limitations

Only the ordinary independence complex of \(Q_4\) is treated; the strong saturation conclusion applies only within one of eight parity classes of four parallel graph edges. The proof gives ordinary integral homotopy types and simple-homotopy equivalences of specified inclusions, not an equivariant homotopy model. The claim relies on exhaustive finite combinatorial certificates; it does not prove an infinite-dimensional generalization. The primary novelty comparison is grounded in explicitly identified literature and published mathematical results; unindexed literature remains a residual risk.
