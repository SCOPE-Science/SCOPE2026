# Scientific assessment

## Correctness: PASS

The exact conditional means and covariance formulas of two event-count models were reconstructed from their underlying point-process laws. Differentiation leads to a strict one-crossing equation in the unconditioned case and a strictly monotone power-sum equation in the fixed-count case. Both yield unique global optima; the Riemann-sum limit and Poisson scaling yield the common root and information-loss coefficient. The packaged checker passed 7,219 independent finite tests, including stochastic path simulation, but the proof does not rely on those tests.

## Originality: PASS, with documented historical risks

The 1976–1978 independent-increment integration results already establish the exact-count comparator, which is not claimed as new. Here the observed sigma-field is instead generated by the saturated bit, changing posterior prediction and optimal timing. Full-text comparisons with the 1978 stochastic-integral paper and a 2017 missed-event study, public record searches, and a 2026 binary occupancy design show no direct implication of the finite one-bit node formulas and the two-ensemble information-loss law. The positive root itself is already a known maximizer in a different binary design and is not presented as independently new. Older general Poisson sampling comparisons and a 2017 partial-interval-count paper were not fully accessible and remain priority risks.

## Value: PASS

One-bit saturation transforms a natural integral-estimation resource problem. The result gives precise time placement and a sharp quantitative limit on what one yes/no snapshot can recover when event counts are large, contrasted with full-count readings. It also identifies a common normalized high-density penalty for a fixed-population point process and its Poisson-mixture counterpart. No downstream application-specific empirical performance is asserted.

## Limits and closest literature

The theorem covers a known constant rate or a known total event count, one fixed-time exact binary report, and square loss. It excludes rate estimation, false reports, missed arrivals, multiple or adaptive samples, and nonuniform intensities. Closest previous work: Cressie (1978), Samaniego (1976), Dokter et al. (2017), Yoder et al. (2017), and an earlier binary two-visit occupancy optimization of a related scalar objective. Scientific and publication-priority review has not been independently performed.
