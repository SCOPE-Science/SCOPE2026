# Scale-two Rips topology of finite honeycomb subgraphs with arbitrary missing bonds

## Finding

Let \(\mathcal H\) be the infinite hexagonal (honeycomb) graph with black vertices \(B_{i,j}\), white vertices \(W_{i,j}\) for \((i,j)\in\mathbb Z^2\), and precisely the edges from \(B_{i,j}\) to \(W_{i,j}\), \(W_{i-1,j}\), and \(W_{i,j-1}\). Let \(G\) be **any finite connected nonempty subgraph** of \(\mathcal H\), permitting arbitrary deletion of vertices or bonds, without requiring an induced subgraph. Write \(v=|V(G)|\), \(e=|E(G)|\), \(b=e-v+1\), and let \(h\) be the number of distinct unoriented simple six-cycles in \(G\). Form the inclusive graph-distance Vietoris–Rips complex \(K=\operatorname{VR}_{\le2}(V(G),d_G)\).

Then \(h\le b\) and the following is an **integral homotopy classification**:
\[
\boxed{K\simeq\left(\bigvee^{b-h} S^1\right)\vee\left(\bigvee^h S^2\right).}
\]
Moreover \(H_1(G;\mathbb Z)\to H_1(K;\mathbb Z)\) is surjective, with **primitive free kernel of rank \(h\)** generated by the individual oriented hexagon boundaries; \(H_2(K;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^h\) and higher reduced homology vanishes. Thus the transition from Rips scale one to two kills exactly \(h\) independent integral degree-one classes and creates exactly \(h\) degree-two classes. A graph with a simply connected filled polyhex region and \(h\) complete hexagons yields \(\bigvee^h S^2\); holes and missing bonds are accounted for by the circle rank \(b-h\).

**Sharp bond-defect consequence.** If an edge \(f\) is not a bridge, and belongs to exactly \(t\in\{0,1,2\}\) graph hexagons, deleting \(f\) changes the two Rips Betti numbers by
\[
\Delta\beta_1=t-1,\qquad \Delta\beta_2=-t.
\]
In particular, deleting an internal bond shared by two hexagons creates one degree-one class and removes two degree-two classes. Distances for the new graph are recomputed after the deletion.

## Assumptions and scope

An unoriented six-cycle is counted only once, up to starting point and reversal. Every simple six-cycle in the infinite honeycomb lattice is the boundary of a single elementary planar hexagonal face. All metric distances are shortest-path distances **inside the finite graph \(G\)** (including after defects), never ambient Euclidean distances or inherited distances from \(\mathcal H\). No regularity, face-convexity, connected boundary, absence of cut vertices, or restriction on overlapping hexagon edges is assumed. The empty wedge means a point. The theorem is about nonempty connected graphs; disconnected graphs can be treated componentwise with separate path components. No assertion about scales three or higher is made.

## Proof

**1. Complete classification of short cliques.** The infinite honeycomb is bipartite, has girth six and maximum degree three. Its distance-two graph within either color class is the triangular lattice: edges are the six elementary triangular-lattice directions. This graph has cliques of size at most three. Its triangles are of precisely two types: three neighbors of a common vertex of the other color, and three alternating vertices on an elementary hexagonal face. Two same-color vertices at distance two have a unique common neighbor, since two such neighbors would form a four-cycle. A mixed-color pair has distance at most two precisely when it is an original graph edge. Consequently a four-vertex Rips clique of the **full infinite** honeycomb must contain a vertex of one color and three of the other, all neighbors of that vertex; a larger clique cannot occur. The same restrictions hold for the subgraph \(G\), since \(d_G\ge d_{\mathcal H}\). Moreover any same-color triple whose three length-two connections are present in \(G\) has its uniquely determined intermediate vertices present: it is either contained in a closed-neighborhood simplex \(\Delta[N_G[v]]\), or is one of the two alternating triples of a six-cycle in \(G\). Every six-cycle is an elementary face, and each complete elementary face contributes precisely two such noncentered triangles. Hence every simplex of \(K\) is contained in a centered simplex or is one of these \(2h\) exceptional triangles; none of the exceptional triangles lies in a larger simplex.

**2. The centered core collapses relative to \(G\).** Let \(Y=\bigcup_{v\in V(G)}\Delta[N_G[v]]\subseteq K\). Each face of \(Y\setminus G\) contains some distance-two edge \(ab\) and lies in exactly one centered simplex: the unique midpoint of the length-two path from \(a\) to \(b\) determines its center. For every center \(v\), match each face \(\sigma\subseteq N_G(v)\) containing at least two neighbors with \(\sigma\cup\{v\}\); process these in descending size. Unique centers prevent interference between the pairs, and within one centered simplex all competing larger cofaces have been removed earlier. Each pair is therefore a legitimate elementary free-face collapse, and \(Y\searrow G\), fixing \(G\) pointwise. In particular, a new edge \(ab\) becomes homotopic relative endpoints to the two original edges \(a-v-b\).

**3. A two-disk model for every complete hexagon.** By Step 1, \(K\) is obtained from \(Y\) by attaching exactly two exceptional triangular faces for each hexagon, each along its boundary inside \(Y\). By Step 2, the three distance-two edges of an exceptional triangle retract through their unique midpoint paths. Their concatenation is the six-edge boundary loop of that hexagon (up to reversal). Homotopy invariance of CW attaching maps and the relative collapse \(Y\searrow G\) yield
\[
K\simeq G\cup_{C_1}(D_1\sqcup D'_1)\cup\cdots\cup_{C_h}(D_h\sqcup D'_h),
\]
where \(D_i\) and \(D'_i\) are two two-cells independently attached along the same simple six-cycle \(C_i\). No direct collapse of the full Rips complex along a distance-two edge is asserted: exceptional triangles may use that edge.

**4. Planarity removes the first disks.** Attach only the first disk along each \(C_i\), producing \(X\). By the chosen embedding of \(\mathcal H\) in \(\mathbb R^2\), these disks fill actual disjoint interiors of elementary planar hexagons, so \(X\) is a finite planar two-dimensional cell subcomplex of the honeycomb tiling. A nonempty finite collection of such hexagons has an external boundary edge incident to exactly one included hexagon. That edge and its incident two-cell are a free CW pair. Removing them repeatedly collapses \(X\) to a connected graph \(T\), removing exactly one edge per hexagon and never changing connectedness. Thus \(\operatorname{rank}H_1(T;\mathbb Z)=e-v+1-h=b-h\ge0\), and \(X\simeq\bigvee^{b-h}S^1\). This also proves the oriented hexagon boundary classes are independent and generate a saturated (primitive) sublattice in \(H_1(G;\mathbb Z)\): its quotient is \(H_1(X;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{b-h}\).

**5. Add the second disks.** The attaching loop of each second disk \(D'_i\) already bounds its corresponding first disk in \(X\), hence is nullhomotopic. Attaching a two-cell along a nullhomotopic based loop adds one wedge \(S^2\); this may be done independently for all \(h\) cells. Therefore \(K\simeq X\vee\bigvee^h S^2\), which is the claimed integral homotopy type. Only two-cells were added to the graph model, so the scale-one first-homology map is onto, has the primitive hexagon-boundary lattice as kernel, and the other homology assertions follow.

**6. Bond-defect rule.** For a nonbridge \(f\), the graph \(G-f\) remains connected and its cycle rank falls by one. Every six-cycle containing \(f\) disappears, no new six-cycle can be created by deleting an edge, and an edge of the planar honeycomb belongs to at most two elementary hexagons. Thus \(b'=b-1\) and \(h'=h-t\), so the displayed two Betti changes follow immediately from the proven classification. \(\square\)

## Verification

The standalone, standard-library-only `verify_honeycomb.py` independently constructs lattice windows and their randomly bond- and vertex-deleted connected subgraphs; computes their **internal** shortest-path metrics; enumerates simple six-cycles and all cliques of the radius-two graph; checks that all exceptional triangles are exactly the alternating hexagon triples and all tetrahedra are precisely degree-three centered neighborhoods; then computes every mod-two Betti number by binary boundary-rank elimination. It checks 16 rectangular coordinate windows, 135 random deleted subgraphs, and a targeted missing internal bond producing \(h=7\), \(b-h=1\), confirming the nontrivial circle/sphere transition. Run `python3 verify_honeycomb.py` alongside `verification_output.txt` to reproduce the complete deterministic output. These finite checks support Step 1 and the numerical consequences but are **not** substituted for the all-subgraph proof, the integral primitive-kernel argument, or the homotopy equivalence.

## Relationship to prior work

Adamaszek (2013, Proposition 2.2) proves that scale-two Rips complexes of graphs with girth at least seven collapse back to the graph. The hexagonal lattice has girth six, so the theorem cannot apply; the single \(C_6\) sphere is a known base example. A published 2026 hexagon-packing theorem covers arbitrary girth-six graphs with **pairwise edge-disjoint** six-cycles. Here adjacent hexagonal faces share edges (indeed a finite full polyhex can have many such shared edges), so that theorem does not imply the present result. The 2026 periodic honeycomb classification obtains \(T^2\vee\bigvee^{L^2}S^2\) for a regular quotient **without boundaries**. It gives no classification for arbitrary nonregular finite graph subgraphs, boundary deletions or the resulting primitive circle-defect lattice. The 2026 all-overlaps hexagon theorem for **line graphs** studies \(\operatorname{VR}_{\le2}(L(G))\), not the present vertex Rips complex of \(G\); the two constructions cannot be exchanged in this case since honeycomb graphs are triangle-free of maximum degree three and are not line graphs of graphs containing degree-three vertices. The new result exploits the planar separation of overlapping actual hexagonal faces, which replaces the edge-disjointness hypothesis. A broader but unindexed overlapping-hexagon theorem may exist and is a residual priority risk.

## Limitations

Only subgraphs of the standard infinite honeycomb graph and the inclusive graph-distance radius-two threshold are classified. Arbitrary planar cubic graphs of girth six can have additional short cliques and require separate analysis. The homotopy equivalence is not asserted equivariant or canonical, and it does not determine the topology for Rips scales above two, Euclidean embeddings, or metric interiors of edges. Finite checks are not an independent proof, and independent mathematical audit has not been performed.
