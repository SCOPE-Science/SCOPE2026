# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The finite face predicate is exhaustive on all \(2^{14}\) arc subsets. The 1849-face augmented Hasse diagram supports a checked 910-pair acyclic matching, leaving exactly 29 five-cells and no other critical faces; unaugmenting gives one zero-cell and the required five-sphere bouquet. The nontrivial translation-orbit condition is proved for every face and establishes a free action and an actual sevenfold covering. The low-degree quotient groups follow from the vanishing low cover homology and the standard Cartan–Leray sequence; the top free rank comes from quotient Euler characteristic and top-cell freeness. The rational rotation character follows from Lefschetz traces. Independent signed cellular chain construction checks \(\partial^2=0\) and mod-two, mod-three and mod-seven Betti numbers. Limits: exact finite digraph, no additional family or integral equivariant splitting.

## Originality — PASS against inspected evidence

The close published work by Courtney (2019 seminar and 2020 thesis abstract) introduces the same complex and studies Cayley graphs in general, but the full 2020 thesis could not be read; this is an explicitly retained risk. Jojić's full paper treats directed-tree shellability via other hypotheses. Singh's full text contains a result for underlying forest and directed-acyclic structures, not this two-step strongly connected digraph. Deshpande–Sawant's available full preprint treats 2-acyclic or single-cycle underlying graphs under relevant criteria, neither of which applies to this graph. The closest indexed seven-vertex tournament directed-forest result involves 21 arcs, not 14; the cyclic-tournament multipath quotient uses a different face rule (outdegree restriction) and different digraph. A two-shortcut cycle result includes only two extra arcs, not the full seven-element orbit. None of the inspected statements implies the combined 29-sphere and quotient-torsion classification. No search proves literature uniqueness; the unread thesis and unindexed equivalences remain risks.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The smallest odd-prime 2-regular circulant beyond the 5-vertex tournament provides a natural sparse, transitive test of directed-forest topology. The **same** finite CW space supplies an exact nonequivariant integral homotopy classification, a free symmetry action, and an explicit nontrivial odd-primary torsion phenomenon in two different homological degrees of the quotient. The primitive graph symmetry, fundamental group, and complete quotient homology distinguish this from face-count recalculation and provide a reproducible benchmark for how free actions of a cyclic group transform a torsion-free topological graph invariant. No parameter-family generality is asserted.
