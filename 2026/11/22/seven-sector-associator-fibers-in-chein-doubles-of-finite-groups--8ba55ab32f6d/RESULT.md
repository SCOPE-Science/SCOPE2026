# Seven-sector associator fibers in Chein doubles of finite groups

## Finding

**Theorem.** Let \(G\) be any finite group, let \(n=|G|\), and let \(L=M(G,2)\) denote Chein's Moufang double. For \(X,Y,Z\in L\), define the **left-valued associator** \(D(X,Y,Z)\) uniquely by

\[
 (X*Y)*Z=D(X,Y,Z)*(X*(Y*Z)).
\]

For each \(g\in G\), set \(C_G(g)=\#\{(a,b)\in G^2:a^{-1}b^{-1}ab=g\}\). Then every value of \(D\) lies in the embedded group \(G\), and for every \(g\in G\),

\[
 N_L(g):=\#\{(X,Y,Z)\in L^3:D(X,Y,Z)=g\}
 =n^3\mathbf1_{g=1}+7nC_G(g).
\]

Indeed, each of the seven parity sectors other than \((0,0,0)\) contributes exactly \(nC_G(g)\) triples to the fiber. Equivalently, for independent uniform elements of \(L\),

\[
 \Pr(D=g)=\frac18\mathbf1_{g=1}+\frac78\frac{C_G(g)}{n^2}
 =\frac18\mathbf1_{g=1}+\frac{7}{8n}
 \sum_{\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(G)}\frac{\chi(g)}{\chi(1)}.
\]

The final equality uses the **classical** Frobenius character formula for group commutator fibers; that formula is not claimed as new. The support of the associator-value map is precisely the set of *single* group commutators, and its generated subloop is exactly the group-derived subgroup \([G,G]\) embedded in \(L\). The support need not exhaust the whole derived subgroup as a set of individual values.

## Assumptions and scope

Let \(\bar a\) denote the barred copy of \(a\in G\). Multiplication is fixed by these four rules, for arbitrary \(a,b\in G\):

\[
 a*b=ab,\qquad a*\bar b=\overline{ba},\qquad
 \bar a*b=\overline{ab^{-1}},\qquad
 \bar a*\bar b=b^{-1}a.
\]

Here products and inverses on the right are those in the group \(G\). There is **no** hypothesis that \(G\) is abelian, has exponent two, or is generated by two elements. The associator convention places \(D\) to the **left** of \(X*(Y*Z)\); some literature chooses a right-valued associator, so equality of values under different conventions is not asserted. The indicator \(\mathbf1_{g=1}\) equals one only at the group identity. The finite-group character sum is over all ordinary irreducible complex characters.

## Proof

Write \(X=a\) or \(\bar a\), \(Y=b\) or \(\bar b\), and \(Z=c\) or \(\bar c\), with parity bits \((i,j,k)\in\{0,1\}^3\). Both bracketings have the same parity, so the equation defining \(D\) has a unique solution in \(G\). If both bracketings are unbarred, then \(D=((X*Y)*Z)(X*(Y*Z))^{-1}\); if both are barred, their group-coordinate words are \(\overline L\) and \(\overline R\), and \(D=R^{-1}L\), since \(d*\overline R=\overline{Rd}\).

Define the group commutator \([s,t]=s^{-1}t^{-1}st\). Directly applying the four multiplication rules gives the complete eight-case table below. In rows involving \(u\), its definition is specific to that row.

| Parity \((i,j,k)\) | Exact value of \(D\) |
| --- | --- |
| \((0,0,0)\) | \(1\) |
| \((0,0,1)\) | \([a,b]\) |
| \((0,1,0)\) | \([a,c^{-1}]\) |
| \((1,0,0)\) | \([b^{-1},c^{-1}]\) |
| \((0,1,1)\) | \([u^{-1},a^{-1}]\), where \(u=c^{-1}b\) |
| \((1,0,1)\) | \([u^{-1},b]\), where \(u=c^{-1}a\) |
| \((1,1,0)\) | \([u^{-1},c^{-1}]\), where \(u=b^{-1}a\) |
| \((1,1,1)\) | \(b^{-1}[s,r]b\), where \(r=ab^{-1}\), \(s=cb^{-1}\) |

For example, in sector \((0,1,1)\), the left-bracketed product is \(c^{-1}ba\) and the right-bracketed product is \(ac^{-1}b\), whence \(D=(c^{-1}b)a(c^{-1}b)^{-1}a^{-1}=[(c^{-1}b)^{-1},a^{-1}]\). For the all-barred sector, the left and right barred words have group coordinates \(cb^{-1}a\) and \(ab^{-1}c\); their quotient is \(c^{-1}ba^{-1}cb^{-1}a=b^{-1}[cb^{-1},ab^{-1}]b\).

In each of the first six nontrivial sectors, the change of variables from \((a,b,c)\) to its displayed pair of commutator arguments and one remaining free group variable is a bijection on \(G^3\). Thus each such sector contributes \(nC_G(g)\) to the fiber over \(g\). For sector \((1,1,1)\), the map \((a,b,c)\mapsto(r,s,b)\) is a bijection; for each fixed \(b\), conjugation by \(b\) permutes the commutator pairs and preserves \(C_G(g)\), because \(C_G\) is constant on conjugacy classes. The sector therefore also contributes \(nC_G(g)\). The all-unbarred sector contributes \(n^3\) at the identity and zero elsewhere. Summation proves the fiber law.

Division by \(|L|^3=8n^3\) gives the probability law. Frobenius' established identity \(C_G(g)=n\sum_{\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(G)}\chi(g)/\chi(1)\) gives the character expression. Since every individual value is a single commutator, and every group commutator occurs in sector \((0,0,1)\), the support and generated-subloop assertions follow.

## Verification

The accompanying `verify_associators.py` independently implements the four-case loop multiplication, computes the group commutator census, and directly enumerates all \(8n^3\) ordered loop triples. It checks both the exact table by direct equality for every triple and the individual fiber identity in **every** parity sector. Reproduction: run `python3 verify_associators.py` with standard Python. The accompanying `checker_output.txt` is its captured output.

For \(G=S_4\), with \(n=24\), the identity target has \(33984\) witnesses; each of the three nonidentity double transpositions has \(9408\), while each of the eight three-cycles has \(6048\). This illustrates information lost when only the identity/associativity-degree fiber is retained. Exhaustive tests are confirmation, not a substitute for the symbolic eight-case proof.

## Relationship to prior work

Chein's multiplication rules and standard Moufang-double framework are classical; see Vojtěchovský (arXiv:math/0701706), and Drápal–Vojtěchovský, section 9 of *Moufang Loops That Share Associator and Three Quarters of Their Multiplication Tables*. The latter investigates constructions preserving associators and associator subloops; its inspected section does not state this all-target fiber law. Ahmadidelir's 2015 presentation **already gives the identity-fiber consequence**, the associativity degree \(\Pr(D=1)=(1+7k(G)/n)/8\), where \(k(G)\) is the number of group conjugacy classes. That statement is **not** claimed as new, and neither is Frobenius' group-commutator character formula.

Levin's 2025 note *How Associative Can a Non-Associative Moufang Loop Be?* proves general upper bounds for the probability of association under nuclear-commutator hypotheses. Its inspected arXiv HTML does not present the Chein-double pointwise law. The present theorem concerns the exact **nonidentity** fibers, their uniform seven-sector decomposition, and the complete character-valued distribution; these are not determined by a scalar identity probability.

## Limitations

The result addresses **only Chein doubles of finite groups**, with the left-valued associator convention stated above. It does not classify all Moufang loops or general loop-associator word maps. The word-image support can be a proper subset of the derived subgroup; no equality of those sets is claimed. The known identity specialization and standard character formula are not novel. A formally equivalent all-target result could exist in historical loop literature not indexed by the searches; the present literature check cannot prove global uniqueness. No independent external mathematical audit has been performed.
