# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

For any edge-dominating flag set, the uncovered points and lines are incidence-free. Every three points in the order-three affine plane meet at least nine lines, forcing a minimum of seven flags. Equality forces exactly two uncovered points and all seven selected flags to form a matching; the residual incidence graph is a twelve-cycle and supplies exactly two matchings for each of the 36 point pairs. The exchange counts are finite and exhaustively certified in two independent self-contained implementations: one constructs minima geometrically; the other inspects every edge subset of size at most seven. The two outputs agree on all 72 minima, the intersection distribution and the radius/diameter data. This does not claim an infinite-order statement.

## Originality — PASS, with historical-priority risk

Spiro–Adriaensen–Mattheus already imply the numerical lower bound seven via their general incidence-free-pair inequality; that bound is explicitly credited, not claimed as original. Direct examination of their arXiv full text and open-access journal text found no minimum-set enumeration or exchange result for the affine plane of order three. Caro–Davila–Henning–Pepper give general matching/edge-domination inequalities but no corresponding count. Published comparisons for the same affine incidence network address vertex domination/control, while published minimum-matching exchanges address other graph families. No retrieved theorem implied the present complete 72-minimum structure plus four-edge mobility. Residual risk: unpublished, unindexed or old computational enumerations.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The first nonbinary affine plane is a canonical incidence structure and the 21-vertex Levi graph is a natural test case for incidence-free-pair methods. This result goes beyond a readily available scalar lower bound by classifying every optimum through a geometric twelve-cycle, giving exact multiplicity, and proving a sharp nontrivial gap between isolated optimum configurations and connected batch-exchange dynamics. The 72-vertex exchange graph's exact distance profile provides reproducible structure rather than an arbitrary graph statistic.
