# Scientific review: quadratic Ehrlich–Aberth dynamics

## Correctness — PASS
The proof reduces the exact denominator-cleared one-coordinate Ehrlich–Aberth update to a monomial map using a rational root-coordinate substitution. Both transformed maps are independently recovered by symbolic algebra; their invariants, exact iterates, convergence basins and sharp error constants are then algebraic consequences. The claim explicitly requires a forward-regular orbit; genuine poles, collisions, root starts and finite arithmetic are not silently included. The periodic multipliers are obtained by conjugating the exponent matrix, and all prime periods are constructed using primitive roots of unity. The supplied checker reproduces the critical identities and exact rational native iterations. Its finite trials are supplementary, not a proof of the all-iteration results.

## Originality — PASS, with residual literature risk
Grabbel–Srkalovic–Schleicher (arXiv:2611.02143v1, sections 1–3) establishes attracting cycles at degree at least seven and records Möbius symmetry and the Jacobi/cyclic distinction; the material inspected does not state a complete quadratic cross-ratio basin classification or the exact third- versus fourth-power rates. Reinke (arXiv:2011.01660, sections 1–3) proves divergence for Jacobi Ehrlich–Aberth at degree at least four and treats the two update schemes generally, without giving these degree-two formulas. Shemyakov et al. (arXiv:2004.03217, sections 2, 3 and 5–6) compares root-finding methods and uses cyclic implementations but gives experimental complexity comparisons rather than this explicit quadratic classification. An earlier local cubic convergence bound is too weak to imply the global complex separator, cyclic exponent, or complete regular periodic spectrum. Searches for quadratic, degree-two, conjugacy, cross-ratio and cyclic versions identified no statement implying the full claim. The source expressly anticipates separate work on special-degree cycles, and older unindexed numerical-analysis treatments might contain a similar calculation; no exhaustive literature-completeness claim is made.

## Value — PASS
The two-root problem is the first nontrivial simultaneous-root case. Its common generic basin boundary and variant-dependent pole exceptions, nonattracting periodic families and normalization-invariant error law clarify what local cubic convergence does and does not imply about global reliability. The explicit cyclic fourth-power law quantifies a genuine, implementationally relevant difference between simultaneous and immediate-update schemes without changing the basin partition. This is a complete natural-domain classification, not an arbitrarily selected numerical example or a prescribed small-step gain.

## Limits and interpretation
The theorem applies to distinct roots, exact complex arithmetic and forward-regular starts. It makes no assertion about high-degree convergence, rounding robustness, or singular projective continuation. Source comparison covers the identified primary full text and accessible historical material, not every unpublished or unindexed work. The symbolic and exact-rational verifier does not replace the global algebraic proof.
