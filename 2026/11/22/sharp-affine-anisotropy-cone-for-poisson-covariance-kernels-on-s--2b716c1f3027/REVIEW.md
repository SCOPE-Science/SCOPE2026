# Scientific review: sharp affine anisotropy cone for spherical Poisson kernels

## Correctness — PASS
The precise domain is integer \(d\ge2\), \(0<r<1\), real \(a\) and \(v\in\mathbb R^d\), and all finite complex Gram forms. The selection rule for coordinate multiplication follows from an explicit homogeneous-harmonic polynomial decomposition. Poisson eigenvalues yield the exact intertwining identity, hence factorization with weight \(a+(1+r)\langle v,z\rangle/\sqrt r\). Dense polynomial tests prove necessity, not merely numerical sampling. Nonzero finite atomic measures have nonzero Poisson smoothing, and the boundary weight vanishes at no more than one point, proving strict finite-site positivity. Independent rational and high-precision quadrature checks support the key identities; finite computations are not used to infer the universal theorem.

## Originality — PASS, with residual priority risk
The accessible full text of Buhmann–Jäger (arXiv:2105.02586, Theorem 2 and Proposition 1) provides abstract harmonic coefficient criteria but not the explicit affine Poisson cone, its dimension-independent threshold or bridge identity. Guella–Jäger (arXiv:2205.07396, Theorem 1 and Theorem 4) treats diagonal eigenfunction coefficient kernels and support-based sufficient conditions, rather than adjacent-degree coupling. The indexed circle Poisson modulation theorem, inspected at complete result/proof level, has frequency couplings separated by an even shift and finite Gram singularities at a boundary polygon; it does not logically cover this affine-coordinate family. Search alone is not treated as novelty proof. A 2024 axially symmetric space-time paper could only be compared through its accessible abstract, and an unindexed equivalent remains possible.

## Value — PASS
The family \(P_r(x,y)[a+\langle v,x+y\rangle]\) is a natural first-order, axially anisotropic deformation of the spherical Poisson covariance kernel. The complete dimension-free admissibility cone is sharper than pointwise nonnegative variance (which only requires \(a\ge2\|v\|\)) and supplies an exact threshold for valid nonstationary spatial covariance models. The surprising persistence of strict finite-site positivity on an operator boundary and the exact bridge midpoint law are structural, not just computed parameter instances. It does not assert results for arbitrary anisotropies or spectral sequences.
