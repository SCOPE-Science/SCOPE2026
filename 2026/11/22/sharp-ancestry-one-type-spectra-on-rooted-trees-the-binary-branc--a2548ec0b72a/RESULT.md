# Sharp ancestry one-type spectra on rooted trees: the binary-branching threshold

## Finding
Fix \(m\geq1\) distinct named vertices \(A=\{a_1,\ldots,a_m\}\) of any finite rooted tree \(T\), in the relational signature consisting only of strict ancestry \(\prec\) and equality. Write \(t(T,A)\) for the number of complete realized quantifier-free one-variable types over \(A\), and \(t_{\mathrm{ext}}(T,A)\) for the number realized outside \(A\). Then
\[
t_{\mathrm{ext}}(T,A)\leq 3m,\qquad t(T,A)\leq4m.
\]
These inequalities are simultaneously sharp for every \(m\), even among trees of maximum outdegree two. A binary extremizer can have exactly \(4m\) vertices, which is the least possible order for \(4m\) distinct realized types. Restricting to rooted paths changes the exact all-vertex maximum to \(2m+1\).

More precisely, for unmarked \(v\) define the strict descendant-mark support
\[
D(v)=\{a\in A:v\prec a\},\qquad P(v)=\{a\in A:a\prec v\},
\]
and let \(\mathcal F=\{D(v):v\in V(T)\setminus A,\ D(v)\ne\varnothing\}\), removing repetitions. Equality \(t(T,A)=4m\) holds **if and only if** (i) \(\mathcal F\) contains \(2m-1\) sets, hence is the complete laminar hierarchy of a full binary tree on its \(m\) singleton leaves; (ii) each \(a\in A\) has an unmarked strict descendant; and (iii) some unmarked vertex is incomparable with every member of \(A\). These conditions also imply \(t_{\mathrm{ext}}(T,A)=3m\).

The number of subsets of \(V(T)\) definable by quantifier-free formulas with one free variable and constants from \(A\) is \(2^{t(T,A)}\); therefore its sharp upper bound is \(2^{4m}\), or \(2^{2m+1}\) on a rooted path. These are bounds on **realized quantifier-free types**, not on complete first-order types of arbitrary quantifier rank.

## Assumptions and scope
A rooted tree is a finite connected acyclic undirected graph with a designated root, oriented away from the root. The relation \(u\prec v\) means that \(u\) is a strict ancestor of \(v\). It is the **only** nonlogical relation symbol. In particular, the binary relation for immediate parentage is not available, and neither the root nor any tree vertex outside \(A\) is named by a constant. The named parameters \(a_1,\ldots,a_m\) are pairwise distinct, and complete quantifier-free types can use Boolean combinations of \(v=a_i\), \(v\prec a_i\), and \(a_i\prec v\). For a fixed \(T,A\), relations involving constants only have fixed truth values and cannot distinguish vertices.

## Proof
**1. Laminar support bound.** For two vertices \(u,v\), either one is an ancestor of the other, or their rooted subtrees are disjoint. Hence the family \(\mathcal F\) of nonempty descendant-mark supports is laminar: for any two members \(X,Y\), either \(X\subseteq Y\), \(Y\subseteq X\), or \(X\cap Y=\varnothing\). Every laminar family of distinct nonempty subsets of an \(m\)-element set has at most \(2m-1\) members. Indeed, add all singletons and the whole set if missing, form the inclusion forest, and note that in the resulting rooted hierarchy every internal member has at least two children, so its number of internal nodes is at most its number of leaves minus one. In particular \(|\mathcal F|\leq2m-1\).

**2. A nonempty support determines the entire external type.** Suppose \(u,v\notin A\) and \(D(u)=D(v)\ne\varnothing\). Select \(a\in D(u)\). Both vertices strictly precede \(a\), so they are comparable. Suppose \(u\prec v\). If a mark \(b\) lay on the path strictly below \(u\) and no lower than \(v\), then \(b\in D(u)\setminus D(v)\), which is impossible. Consequently \(P(u)=P(v)\), and the complete quantifier-free types agree. Thus at most \(2m-1\) external types have nonempty support.

**3. Empty support types.** When \(D(v)=\varnothing\), all marked strict ancestors of \(v\) form a chain. If \(P(v)\) is nonempty, it is exactly the set of marked ancestors (including itself) of its deepest member. There are at most \(m\) such possibilities, plus the empty set. Therefore at most \(m+1\) external types have empty support, for at most \(3m\) external types overall. The \(m\) marked vertices have distinct equality types, giving \(t(T,A)\leq4m\).

**4. Rigidity of equality.** Equality forces exactly \(2m-1\) distinct nonempty supports, and all \(m+1\) possible empty-support profiles. Any maximal-size laminar family of nonempty subsets of \(A\) contains \(A\) and all singletons (each may otherwise be adjoined without crossing existing sets). Its inclusion tree therefore has exactly \(m\) singleton leaves and \(m-1\) internal nodes, each of precisely two children. That is a full rooted binary laminar hierarchy. It also forces marked vertices to be pairwise incomparable: if \(a\prec b\) for two marks, no unmarked vertex can have descendant-mark support exactly \(\{a\}\). With pairwise incomparable marks, an empty-support vertex having nonempty \(P\) can have only one marked ancestor. Thus all \(m\) such profiles exist precisely when every mark has an unmarked strict descendant; the remaining empty profile exists precisely when an unmarked vertex is incomparable with all marks. Conversely these three conditions realize \(2m-1+m+1=3m\) external types, and the marked vertices supply the other \(m\).

**5. Sharp binary construction and least order.** Take any full rooted binary tree with \(m\) leaves, having \(2m-1\) vertices; when \(m=1\), this is a single vertex. Regard its \(m\) leaves as unmarked gates. Beneath each gate attach its own named mark, and beneath that mark attach one unmarked terminal vertex. At the first gate also attach one additional unmarked offshoot as a second child. The resulting rooted tree has exactly \((2m-1)+2m+1=4m\) vertices and maximum outdegree two. The original internal vertices and gates realize the \(2m-1\) nonempty clade supports; the \(m\) terminal vertices give the \(m\) distinct nonempty ancestor profiles with empty support; the extra offshoot realizes the entirely incomparable profile; and the \(m\) marks have distinct equality profiles. Thus all \(4m\) vertices have different types. Any tree realizing \(4m\) types necessarily has at least \(4m\) vertices.

**6. Paths and definability.** On a rooted path, the parameters occur in root-to-leaf order. The unmarked vertices lie in at most \(m+1\) gaps before, between, and after marks, and each gap gives one type, so \(t(T,A)\leq2m+1\). Alternating unmarked and marked vertices along a path of length \(2m\), beginning and ending unmarked, attains equality. Each realized type is definable by a conjunction of positive/negative atomic comparisons with the named parameters. Every union of type classes is then defined by a disjunction, proving that the unary quantifier-free definable Boolean algebra has exactly \(2^{t(T,A)}\) members.

## Relationship to prior work
Dreier, Mählmann and Toruńczyk (arXiv:2611.08143, first posted 5 November 2026), Section 1.1 and Section 6, prove polynomial general type bounds over parameter sets in monadically dependent classes of finite structures. Their result covers much more expressive logical signatures and arbitrary fixed quantifier rank but does **not state** the exact \(4m\)/\(3m\) strict-tree-ancestry constant, the path-to-binary transition, or the saturation characterization. The new theorem is a specialization in subject area but is **not implied as an exact numerical or structural result** by the cited polynomial bound. Campbell and Köhler (arXiv:2512.02617; ICALP 2026), Section 3, show an MSO transduction between laminar set systems and their trees. The classical laminar bound \(2m-1\) is used here, not claimed as new; that transduction does not determine the full equality/ancestry comparison type spectrum. Comparisons with nearby published results on homogeneous-tree finite-variable theories and universal-poset one-types concern different objects and quantifiers. Searches cannot establish historical uniqueness, and an older equivalent ancestor-comparison result remains a residual priority risk.

## Limitations
The bound is only for quantifier-free formulas in the strict-ancestor-and-equality signature over individually named distinct parameters. Introducing a direct-parent predicate, unary vertex labels, successor functions, or unbounded quantifiers can produce different type counts. The statement does not classify complete first-order types or arbitrary graph classes. The accompanying finite checks are corroborative, not proofs of the universal quantification. Historical priority has not been independently audited; unindexed older tree-comparison literature may contain equivalent bounds.
