# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
For the normalized weighted Bergman and Hardy norms, images of distinct normalized monomials are orthogonal because addition by the fixed exponent vector is injective. The exact gamma-factor ratio for each singular value follows from an elementary radial integral. Uniform degree bounds establish the compact/noncompact/unbounded trichotomy, and rescaled lattice counting on a bounded star-shaped region with null boundary gives the Weyl coefficient via the Dirichlet beta integral. Inverting the counting function yields ordered singular numbers; the weak endpoint, logarithmic trace, and spectral zeta pole follow from harmonic comparison. At the critical Bergman weight, the simplex supremum yields the exact essential norm. The accompanying standalone Python checks independently encode and test finite factorial versions of the diagonal coefficients, but not the infinite-dimensional limit.

## Originality — PASS, with priority risk
Miihkinen–Pau–Perälä–Wang (arXiv:1904.00359v1) establish Bergman-to-Hardy boundedness. Chen–Pau–Wang (doi:10.1007/s00025-021-01403-8, Theorem 1.3) already establish the compact Schatten *membership threshold*, so that is not new here. Their general Schatten-Herz criteria and regularity-limited spectral rate statements do not supply the exact gamma-product leading ordered singular-value asymptotic, logarithmic trace coefficient, or boundary essential norm. Liu–Yuan–Zeng (2022) use Bergman as target, not Hardy. Closest previously available spectral results for generalized Volterra companions put derivatives on the input or use composition damping and do not imply this theorem. The possibility that a broad older Toeplitz eigenvalue law entails the gamma coefficient has not been conclusively excluded; see the references and structured audit.

## Value — PASS
The previously established class boundary is sharpened to the natural, exact spectral scale for the canonical monomial tests of the Bergman-to-Hardy integral operator. Its explicit angular coefficient quantifies the effect of the multiindex shape and supplies a nontrivial logarithmic singular-value residue, not just a qualitative ideal inclusion. The classification covers every complex dimension, every nonconstant monomial, and all Hilbert Bergman exponents within the compact range, together with a sharp critical essential norm. These are useful benchmarks for more general symbols; no general-symbol result is claimed.

## Limitations
The theorem assumes a homogeneous monomial symbol and standard radial Hilbert norms, has no second Weyl term, and is not independent mathematical validation. A general unlocated spectral theorem could affect priority.
