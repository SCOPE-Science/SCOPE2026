# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The mathematical object is a centered Gaussian founder density in \(\mathbb R^d\) with \(d\ge2\), fixed covariance trace, and a finite covariance condition-number ceiling. The area/volume criterion is stated separately from actual persistence. The proof derives the exact superlevel ellipsoid volume including its zero-volume case and converts the problem into minimizing the founder mass over one feasible determinant interval. The determinant lower extreme is computed from the vertices of the bounded-spectrum trace polytope; all intermediate determinants are attained along a feasible covariance segment. The derivative of the required-founder function has exactly one zero. The integer multiplicity rule follows from a strictly convex finite-index objective. The checker independently samples feasibility and numerically reoptimizes. Boundary values, orientation nonuniqueness, possible multiplicity ties, and integer rounding are addressed. The proof does not confuse finite experiments with universal verification.

None of the inspected mathematical statements supplies the exact **static** trace- and anisotropy-constrained Gaussian level-volume minimum, the sharp three-regime transition, or the dimension-dependent finite eigenvalue-multiplicity selection. The comparison does not treat title differences as evidence. Established general ingredients include Gaussian ellipsoid volume, determinant AM–GM, convex-polytope vertex minimization, and scalar calculus; an undiscovered formulation in broader Gaussian shape/design literature remains a specific priority risk.

## Value — PASS
Observed effects of both density and occupied area on persistence motivate asking how a fixed dispersal second moment and bounded anisotropy jointly limit the local high-density footprint. An area-only criterion does not guarantee persistence, but it is a precise, interpretable **screen**. The theorem gives the globally minimum founder count needed to pass that screen for arbitrary dimension and all positive parameters. Its dimension-dependent integer-multiplicity problem and two transition volumes identify exactly when a maximally elongated, intermediate, or isotropic Gaussian dispersal design minimizes required founding mass. The finite anisotropy cap prevents a mathematically degenerate infinite-eccentricity release and yields a positive small-volume founder floor. These are not merely a numerical recomputation of a known table.

Goodsman and Lewis, DOI 10.1111/2041-210X.12573, derive a dynamic founder threshold for isotropic Gaussian dispersal. Soboleva et al., DOI 10.1016/S0304-3800(02)00357-5, examine nonradial invasion nuclei. The present optimization is a static density-and-volume screen rather than a dynamical persistence theorem; therefore any applied inference must independently account for shape-sensitive demographic dynamics. Further literature on ellipsoid/spectrum extremal inequalities may contain a mathematically equivalent restatement. No independent validation is claimed.
