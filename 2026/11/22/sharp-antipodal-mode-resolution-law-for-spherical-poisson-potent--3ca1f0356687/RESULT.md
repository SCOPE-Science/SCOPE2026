# Sharp antipodal mode-resolution law for spherical Poisson potentials

## Finding

Fix an integer \(d\ge2\), a concentration radius \(0<r<1\), a unit vector \(e\in\mathbb R^d\), and a mixing mass \(1/2\le p<1\). On \(\mathbb S^{d-1}\) let \(U\) be the surface-mean-one harmonic-measure density resulting from two interior poles at \(re\) and \(-re\):
\[
U(\xi)=(1-r^2)\left[\frac{p}{|\xi-re|^d}+\frac{1-p}{|\xi+re|^d}\right].
\]
This quantity is a convex combination of the two *boundary harmonic-measure densities* generated by the interior poles; the standard density with respect to unnormalized surface area is \(U/|\mathbb S^{d-1}|\).

Set \(q=2p-1\), \(s=d+2\), and
\[
q_c(d,r)=\tanh\!\big(s\operatorname{artanh}r\big)
=\frac{(1+r)^s-(1-r)^s}{(1+r)^s+(1-r)^s}.
\]
**Complete mode classification.** If \(0\le q<q_c(d,r)\), both poles \(e,-e\) are strict local maxima (the former is globally maximal), and the entire set of minima is one latitude \(\{\xi:\xi\cdot e=x_0\}\) with \(-1<x_0\le0\). There are no other critical directions. If \(q=q_c(d,r)\), \(e\) is the sole maximum and \(-e\) is the sole, quartically degenerate minimum; if \(q>q_c(d,r)\), \(e\) is the sole maximum and \(-e\) is a nondegenerate minimum. In either latter case there are no other critical directions. At \(q=0\) the two maxima are equal; for \(q>0\), \(e\) is strictly higher.

In the two-maximum phase set
\[
a=1+r^2,\quad b=2r,\quad \gamma=d/2,\quad k=\gamma+1,
\quad t=\left(\frac{p}{1-p}\right)^{1/k}.
\]
Then the unique minimizing axial coordinate and minimum value are
\[
x_0=\frac{a}{b}\frac{1-t}{1+t},\qquad
\min_{\mathbb S^{d-1}}U=\frac{(1-r^2)(1+t)^k}{(2a)^\gamma(1+t^k)}.
\]
For \(d=2\), this latitude consists of two points; for \(d\ge3\), it is a full \((d-2)\)-sphere. In all phases the peak values at the two poles are
\[
U(e)=\frac{p(1+r)}{(1-r)^{d-1}}+\frac{(1-p)(1-r)}{(1+r)^{d-1}},
\quad
U(-e)=\frac{(1-p)(1+r)}{(1-r)^{d-1}}+\frac{p(1-r)}{(1+r)^{d-1}}.
\]

**Sharp source-separation threshold.** For every \(0<q<1\), the secondary peak exists precisely when
\[
r>r_c(d,q)=\tanh\!\left(\frac{\operatorname{artanh}q}{d+2}\right).
\]
This threshold is strictly decreasing in the ambient dimension, and
\(r_c(d,q)\sim\operatorname{artanh}(q)/(d+2)\) as \(d\to\infty\) with \(q\) fixed.

**Critical birth law.** Fix \(d,r\) and let \(\delta=q_c(d,r)-q\downarrow0\) through positive values. Put
\[
A=(1+r)^2,\quad B=(1-r)^2,\quad c=1-r^2,
\]
and define the positive constants
\[
H=\frac{(A^k+B^k)^2 AB}{2kb(A+B)(AB)^k},\qquad
C=\frac{c\gamma(A^k+B^k)^3 AB}{8k(A+B)(AB)^{2k}}.
\]
Then the valley approaches the weaker pole and the weaker peak's height above the valley obeys
\[
x_0+1=H\delta+O_{d,r}(\delta^2),\qquad
U(-e)-\min U=C\delta^2+O_{d,r}(\delta^3).
\]
Equivalently the angular separation from \(-e\) is \(\sqrt{2H}\,\delta^{1/2}+O_{d,r}(\delta^{3/2})\). At the critical mass, if \(\vartheta\) is the geodesic angle from \(-e\),
\[
U(\xi)-U(-e)=\frac{c\gamma k b^2(A+B)}{8AB(A^k+B^k)}\,\vartheta^4+O_{d,r}(\vartheta^6).
\]
Every displayed constant is strictly positive. The strict and equality cases are included above.

## Assumptions and scope

Here \(d\) is an integer at least two, \(0<r<1\), \(e\) is arbitrary on the unit sphere, and the larger of the two masses is by convention at \(+e\). The case \(p=1\) is the single classical Poisson kernel and has one maximum, but is excluded from the displayed valley formula because its odds ratio diverges. All modal claims concern *angular* extrema on the sphere, not the radial dependence of the harmonic function inside the ball. The source distribution is constrained to two exactly antipodal, equal-radius poles.

## Proof

Write \(x=\xi\cdot e\in[-1,1]\); \(|\xi\mp re|^2=a\mp bx\), with \(a=1+r^2>b=2r>0\). Thus
\[
G(x)=U(\xi)=c\big[p(a-bx)^{-\gamma}+(1-p)(a+bx)^{-\gamma}\big],\qquad c=1-r^2.
\]
Its derivative is
\[
G'(x)=c\gamma b\big[p(a-bx)^{-k}-(1-p)(a+bx)^{-k}\big].
\]
For \(0<p<1\) the stationary equation reduces exactly to
\[
\frac{p}{1-p}=\left(\frac{a-bx}{a+bx}\right)^k.
\]
The right-hand side is strictly decreasing on \([-1,1]\), so there is at most one stationary latitude. Solving the equation gives \(x_0=(a/b)(1-t)/(1+t)\). With \(p\ge1/2\), \(t\ge1\), hence \(x_0\le0\). The equality \(x_0=-1\) occurs when \(t=(a+b)/(a-b)=A/B\), equivalently
\[
\frac{p}{1-p}=\frac{A^k}{B^k}.
\]
Since \(A=(1+r)^2\) and \(B=(1-r)^2\), this is precisely \(q=q_c(d,r)\). When \(q<q_c\), \(G'(-1)<0\), \(G'(1)>0\), and \(G'\) changes once from negative to positive: both poles are maxima and the intermediate latitude is the global minimum. When \(q\ge q_c\), \(G'(x)>0\) for all \(x>-1\); therefore \(+e\) is the only maximum and \(-e\) the minimum.

For \(d\ge2\), the angular gradient of \(U=G(\xi\cdot e)\) vanishes only at the poles and wherever \(G'=0\). The local angular Taylor law near the weak pole is \(x=-\cos\vartheta=-1+\vartheta^2/2-\vartheta^4/24+O(\vartheta^6)\). Thus a strict sign of \(G'(-1)\) gives a nondegenerate maximum or minimum, whereas at equality \(G'(-1)=0\) and
\[
G''(-1)=\frac{c\gamma k b^2(A+B)}{AB(A^k+B^k)}>0,
\]
which proves the exact quartic degeneracy and its coefficient. The strong pole has \(G'(1)>0\) in every case, hence a nondegenerate maximum. For \(q=0\) antipodal symmetry makes the two maxima equal, whereas \(G(1)-G(-1)=cq\big[B^{-\gamma}-A^{-\gamma}\big]>0\) for \(q>0\).

Substituting \(t^k=p/(1-p)\) and the formula for \(x_0\) into \(G\) yields the stated closed formula for the minimum. Substitution at \(x=\pm1\) yields the two peak expressions. Inverting the monotone function \(q_c(d,r)=\tanh((d+2)\operatorname{artanh}r)\) gives the sharp radius law; its dimension monotonicity and large-dimensional expansion follow directly from strict monotonicity of \(\tanh\) and \(\tanh z=z+O(z^3)\) near zero.

For the critical expansions, fix \(d,r\) and write \(\delta=q_c-q\). At the critical mass \(p_c=A^k/(A^k+B^k)\), the first derivative has the exact perturbation
\[
G'(-1)=-\frac{c\gamma b}{2}(A^{-k}+B^{-k})\delta.
\]
Since \(G''(-1)>0\) at the boundary, analytic implicit differentiation gives
\[
x_0+1=-\frac{G'(-1)}{G''(-1)}+O_{d,r}(\delta^2)=H\delta+O_{d,r}(\delta^2).
\]
Taylor's theorem applied at \(-1\), using \(G'(x_0)=0\), gives
\[
G(-1)-G(x_0)=\frac{[G'(-1)]^2}{2G''(-1)}+O_{d,r}(\delta^3)=C\delta^2+O_{d,r}(\delta^3).
\]
The square-root angular separation follows from \(1-\cos\vartheta=\vartheta^2/2+O(\vartheta^4)\). This closes the proof for every indicated dimension and parameter.

## Relationship to prior work

The 2018 public preprint of Golzy and Markatou defines precisely the spherical Poisson-kernel density and general mixtures, proving identifiability and algorithmic convergence rather than the sharp two-antipodal angular mode threshold, latitude, or near-critical contrast. The 2009 full text of Došlá proves modality criteria for *line* mixtures of unimodal densities, including ordinary Cauchy distributions; those are not formulas for spherical harmonic measures and do not supply the displayed dimension-uniform critical radius and quartic-collapse constants. The present problem concerns boundary maxima of a harmonic function on the sphere; it does not assert any new general mixture-identifiability result. A historical exact treatment of the symmetric Poisson antipodal case not located in the checked literature remains possible.

## Limitations

The classification requires two sources at precisely opposite directions and the same radius. It makes no claim for unequal radii, non-antipodal source directions, more source atoms, arbitrary domains, or inference performance of any clustering procedure. The quantitative constants are singular as \(r\to0\) or \(r\to1\) and the asymptotic error estimates are only for fixed \(d,r\). Published priority has been checked in the named sources and selected indexed results, not exhaustively established; no independent audit has been performed.
