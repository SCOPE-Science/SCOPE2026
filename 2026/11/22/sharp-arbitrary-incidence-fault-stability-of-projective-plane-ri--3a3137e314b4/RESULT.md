# Sharp arbitrary-incidence-fault stability of projective-plane Rips complexes

## Finding
Let \(\Pi\) be any finite projective plane of order \(q\ge2\), let \(G\) be its unweighted point–line incidence graph, and choose **any** set \(F\subseteq E(G)\) of \(t\le q\) incidence edges. Put \(H_F=G-F\); its graph metric is recomputed after all deletions. For the inclusive graph-metric Vietoris–Rips complex \(X_F(r)=\operatorname{VR}(H_F;r)\),
\[
X_F(1)\simeq\bigvee^{q^3-t}S^1,\qquad
X_F(2)\simeq\bigvee^{q^3-t}S^2,\qquad
X_F(r)\simeq\{*\}\quad(r\ge3).
\]
At radius zero, the complex consists of \(2(q^2+q+1)\) isolated vertices. Consequently, its **positive-degree integral persistence** consists of precisely \(q^3-t\) classes \([1,2)\) in degree one and \(q^3-t\) classes \([2,3)\) in degree two, and no other positive-degree intervals. Every such graph remains connected.

Moreover, whenever \(F\subseteq F'\) and \(|F'|\le q\), the natural inclusion \(X_{F'}(2)\hookrightarrow X_F(2)\) induces a primitive injection on integral second homology with cokernel \(\mathbb Z^{|F'|-|F|}\), and its homotopy cofiber is \(\bigvee^{|F'|-|F|}S^2\) (a point when the sets coincide). The corresponding first-homology map at radius one has the same primitive cokernel. The uniform deletion budget \(q\) is optimal for an assertion covering **every** fault set: deleting the \(q+1\) incidences of one point isolates that point.

## Assumptions and scope
A finite projective plane of order \(q\) has \(N=q^2+q+1\) points and \(N\) lines; each line contains \(q+1\) points, each point lies on \(q+1\) lines, and every two distinct points (respectively lines) have a unique joining line (respectively intersection point). Only existing projective planes are considered, not hypothetical orders. Every retained graph edge has unit length. Radius \(r\) includes a simplex when all pairwise distances are \(\le r\), with equality allowed. The comparison uses recomputed graph distances, not inherited distances from the intact graph. No geometry, transitivity, or coordinatization assumption is required.

## Proof
**1. Universal witnesses and graph connectivity.** Each of the at most \(q\) faulty lines contains \(q+1\) points, so their union contains at most \(q(q+1)<N\) points. Choose a point \(u\) on none of these lines. Every incidence on any line through \(u\) survives, and every other point \(x\) lies with \(u\) on a unique intact line. Thus \(d_{H_F}(u,x)=2\). Likewise the at most \(q\) faulty point pencils cover fewer than \(N\) lines: choose a line \(m\) containing none of the faulty point endpoints. Every other line meets \(m\) in a point where both incidences survive, so \(d_{H_F}(m,\ell)=2\). Each vertex retains at least one incident edge, since its original degree is \(q+1>t\). The preceding point paths and retained incidences connect all vertices.

**2. A radius-two suspension lemma.** Let \(H\) be a finite bipartite graph with no four-cycle and no isolated vertices. Let \(K_P\) and \(K_L\) be the induced radius-two Rips subcomplexes on its two shores. If both are contractible, then \(\operatorname{VR}(H;2)\simeq\Sigma H\). Indeed, a radius-two edge between opposite shores is exactly a graph incidence, since cross-shore paths have odd length. A Rips clique cannot contain two vertices from each shore, since the four cross edges would be a four-cycle. Thus the full complex is \(A\cup B\), where \(A\) comprises faces containing at most one line and \(B\) faces containing at most one point. Their intersection is exactly the one-dimensional graph \(H\). In \(A\), the faces containing a fixed line \(\ell\) constitute the simplex on \(\{\ell\}\cup N_H(\ell)\); its base is already in \(K_P\). Since line vertices occur in no other faces of \(A\), these simplices collapse onto their bases, so \(A\simeq K_P\). Similarly \(B\simeq K_L\). Both are contractible, and finite simplicial inclusions are cofibrations, so the homotopy pushout \(A\cup_H B\) is the suspension \(\Sigma H\). The Mayer–Vietoris connecting isomorphism \(\widetilde H_2(\operatorname{VR}(H;2);\mathbb Z)\cong H_1(H;\mathbb Z)\) is natural for nested graphs satisfying the hypotheses.

For \(H=H_F\), bipartiteness and four-cycle-freeness are inherited from the projective plane. The point \(u\) is distance at most two from every point, so \(K_P\) is a cone on \(u\); analogously \(K_L\) is a cone on \(m\). The lemma applies. Since \(H_F\) is connected with \(2N\) vertices and \((q+1)N-t\) edges, its graph cycle rank is
\[
(q+1)N-t-2N+1=q^3-t.
\]
Any connected graph is homotopy equivalent to the wedge of as many circles as its cycle rank, and suspension turns that wedge into an equally sized wedge of two-spheres. The radius-one Rips complex is the triangle-free graph \(H_F\) itself.

**3. The later filtration.** The point \(u\) is distance at most three from every line \(\ell\). If \(u\) is not incident with \(\ell\), choose a surviving neighbor point \(x\) of \(\ell\); the joining line through \(u\) and \(x\) is intact (because it contains \(u\)), giving a length-three path. Therefore \(u\) is adjacent to every vertex in the radius-three Rips graph. The complex at radius three, and at all larger radii, is a cone. Since distances are integers, these are all filtration changes. This proves the integral barcodes.

**4. Natural maps, primitivity, and cofibers.** For \(F\subseteq F'\) with \(|F'|\le q\), the inclusions of all three pieces in the suspension lemma commute. Mayer–Vietoris naturality identifies the radius-two homology map with the inclusion of integral graph cycle lattices \(H_1(H_{F'};\mathbb Z)\subseteq H_1(H_F;\mathbb Z)\). Relative to oriented graph edges, the quotient embeds into the free abelian group on the \(|F'|-|F|\) newly deleted edges, so it has no torsion. Its rank is the graph cycle-rank difference \(|F'|-|F|\), giving the claimed free cokernel. Each Rips complex at radius two is simply connected; the quotient of the larger by the connected smaller complex is simply connected and has reduced integral homology only in degree two, of rank \(|F'|-|F|\), by the relative homology sequence. The Hurewicz and homology Whitehead theorems give the asserted sphere-wedge homotopy cofiber. The radius-one map is just graph-cycle inclusion and has the same lattice description.

## Verification
The standalone `verify.py` builds the Desarguesian projective planes of orders two and three using normalized homogeneous coordinates. For **all** \(232\) fault sets of at most two incidences in order two and **all** \(23,479\) fault sets of at most three incidences in order three, it independently recomputes retained adjacency and checks the universal point and line witnesses, distance bounds, positive degrees, and radius-three conical condition. It separately enumerates full maximal cliques and faces for representative fault patterns at each order, computes exact binary boundary ranks, and observes reduced second Betti number \(q^3-t\) with other reduced homology zero. The cases of zero faults and multiple three-edge patterns are included. The finite computations verify the construction and low-order instances; the unrestricted integral and homotopy assertions are proved in the preceding argument, not inferred from these tests. Run `python3 verify.py` with Python 3 and no third-party packages.

## Relationship to prior work
The established intact-plane classification gives \(\operatorname{VR}(G;2)\simeq\bigvee^{q^3}S^2\) and its two- and three-scale barcode. A published single-point/line deletion classification loses \(q\) integral two-sphere generators. Neither determines the arbitrary-pattern incidence-edge fault theorem here. Previously examined special patterns of faults do not imply the unrestricted \(t\le q\) range: the proof must show that the two shore complexes remain cones *simultaneously* for any placement of up to \(q\) faults. The general decomposition and graph-power literature provides techniques and bounds but not the fault-pattern independence, primitive maps, and sharp unconditional threshold. The proof does not claim the homotopy-pushout construction itself is new.

## Limitations
The result applies only to genuine finite projective planes and inclusive graph-metric Rips complexes. Arbitrary fault sets of \(q+1\) edges are not covered; an isolated point gives a counterexample to unconditional connectivity. The formula does not extend automatically to affine or partial incidence geometries, where two same-shore vertices need not share a neighbor. The finite verifier uses prime orders two and three; all existing orders rest on the structural argument. Bibliographic priority against unindexed or inaccessible work is not guaranteed, and there has been no independent mathematical audit.
