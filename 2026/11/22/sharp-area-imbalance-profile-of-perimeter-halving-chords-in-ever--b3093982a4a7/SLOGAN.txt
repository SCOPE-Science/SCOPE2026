For every Euclidean triangle, the entire range of area fractions attainable by straight perimeter-halving chords is exactly \([1-M,M]\), where \(M\) has a sharp two-branch formula in the two shortest side lengths. The equilateral triangle uniquely minimizes the worst area imbalance, with \(M=9/16\), and the extremizing chord changes from a balanced two-edge cap to a vertex splitter at the shortest-side threshold.
