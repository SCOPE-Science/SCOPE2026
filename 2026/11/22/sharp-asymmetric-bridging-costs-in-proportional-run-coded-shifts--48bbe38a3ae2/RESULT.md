# Sharp asymmetric bridging costs in proportional-run coded shifts

## Finding
For every integer \(k\ge1\), let \(X_k\) be the bilateral binary coded shift generated by all words \(0^r1^{kr}\), \(r\ge1\). For integers \(m,n\ge1\), define the *minimum-bridge profile*
\[
B_k(m,n)=\max_{u\in\mathcal L_m(X_k),\ v\in\mathcal L_n(X_k)}\ \min\{|w|:uwv\in\mathcal L(X_k)\}.
\]
The empty connecting word is permitted. For **all** \(m\ge2\) and \(n\ge k+2\), the following explicit two-sided bound holds:
\[
\boxed{\quad k(m-1)+\left\lfloor\frac{n-2}{k}\right\rfloor-2
\ \le\ B_k(m,n)\ \le\ km+\left\lceil\frac nk\right\rceil+k-1.\quad}
\]
In particular, uniformly in the two independent lengths,
\[
\boxed{B_k(m,n)=km+\frac nk+O_k(1)}
\]
as \(m,n\to\infty\), with **different and sharp** costs \(k\) and \(1/k\) for the outgoing and incoming word lengths. On the diagonal, \(B_k(n,n)/n\to k+1/k\). The explicit lower bound is witnessed by a single family of pairs; the upper bound is constructive for **every** admissible pair.

## Assumptions and scope
The alphabet is \(\{0,1\}\), \(k\) is a fixed positive integer, and \(\mathcal L(X_k)\) is the set of finite contiguous factors of finite concatenations of \(0^r1^{kr}\), \(r\ge1\). Each such block is called a *generator*. Its boundary with the next generator is the transition `10`; a generator itself consists of a positive zero run followed by a one run exactly \(k\) times as long. All words in the definition of \(B_k(m,n)\) have the indicated exact positive lengths. A bridge is any finite binary word, including the empty word, and the minimum is taken over bridges preserving *language admissibility*, not over alignments in any fixed configuration.

For \(k\ge2\), \(X_k\) is a proper coded subsystem of the one-sided Becker–Baraviera frequency shift at parameter \(k/(k+1)\). The result applies **only to \(X_k\)** (equivalently its natural one-sided language), never to the larger frequency shift. The qualifying primary dynamical classification is MSC2020 37B10.

## Proof
**1. Universal completion to a generator junction on the right.** Fix an arbitrary \(u\in\mathcal L_m(X_k)\). We show that there is a word \(R(u)\), with \(|R(u)|\le km\), such that \(uR(u)\) ends precisely at the end of some generator in a generator concatenation. This is a completion *of the observed suffix*, and unobserved symbols may be prepended when embedding it.

If \(u\) ends in a run \(0^a\), take \(R(u)=1^{ka}\). If the run begins after a visible 1, its complete zero length is \(a\); if it starts at the left edge of \(u\), it can be embedded as the entire zero run of length \(a\). In both cases the proposed completion works and \(ka\le km\).

If \(u\) ends in \(1^b\) preceded by a visible zero run \(0^a\), distinguish whether this zero run starts immediately after a visible 1. In that case the zero-run length is forced to be \(a\), and admissibility implies \(b\le ka\); finish with \(R(u)=1^{ka-b}\). Otherwise the zero run begins at the left edge of \(u\), and we may choose its complete length \(r=\max\{a,\lceil b/k\rceil\}\le m\), extending it only in the unobserved past. Finish with \(R(u)=1^{kr-b}\). If \(u\) consists solely of ones, use \(r=\lceil m/k\rceil\) and finish with \(1^{kr-m}\). In each case the right completion has length at most \(km\). Previously completed visible generators, if any, remain unchanged.

**2. Universal backward completion from a generator junction.** Fix arbitrary \(v\in\mathcal L_n(X_k)\). We construct \(L(v)\) of length at most \(\lceil n/k\rceil+k-1\) such that \(L(v)v\) begins at the start of a generator in some concatenation.

If \(v\) begins with \(0^a\) and contains no 1, take \(L(v)\) empty. Otherwise let \(1^b\) be its first one run. If that run is followed by a visible zero, then it is complete, so \(b=kr\) for some \(r\ge a\); take \(L(v)=0^{r-a}\). Since \(b\le n\), its length is at most \(n/k\). If the run extends to the end of \(v\), put \(r=\max\{a,\lceil b/k\rceil\}\) and take \(L(v)=0^{r-a}\), whose length is at most \(\lceil n/k\rceil\). Both completions place the first complete generator at the start; later generators are already compatible because \(v\) was admissible.

If \(v\) begins in \(1^b\), select \(r=\lceil b/k\rceil\) and take \(L(v)=0^r1^{kr-b}\). Its length obeys \(|L(v)|=r+(kr-b)\le\lceil n/k\rceil+k-1\). Its initial ones are now the suffix of a full generator with zero run \(r\); the remainder of \(v\) retains its admissible generator structure.

**3. Universal bridge upper bound.** The completion in Step 1 places \(uR(u)\) at a generator boundary; the completion in Step 2 places \(L(v)v\) at the beginning of a generator. Therefore \(uR(u)L(v)v\) is a factor of a generator concatenation, obtained by concatenating the corresponding left and right complete blocks. Choosing \(w=R(u)L(v)\) proves
\[
\min\{|w|:uwv\in\mathcal L(X_k)\}\le km+\lceil n/k\rceil+k-1
\]
for every admissible pair, and hence proves the stated upper bound for \(B_k\).

**4. Uniform sharp-slope witnesses and lower bound.** Let \(m\ge2\), \(n\ge k+2\), and set \(t=\lfloor(n-2)/k\rfloor\ge1\). Choose the admissible words
\[
u=0^{m-1}1,\qquad v=01^{kt}0^{n-kt-1}.
\]
Both occur in generator concatenations: the first starts a generator with zero run \(m-1\), and the second is a suffix-plus-prefix of two consecutive generators, with its first zero forming the last zero of a zero run of length \(t\). In every admissible \(uwv\), the last 1 of \(u\) starts a ones run whose preceding complete zero run has length \(r\ge m-1\). That ones run must therefore have exactly \(kr\) symbols and must end before the first 0 of \(v\). Since one of its symbols already lies in \(u\), the bridge \(w\) contains at least \(k(m-1)-1\) ones from this run.

The \(kt\) consecutive ones immediately following the first 0 of \(v\) form a *complete* run, since \(v\) also contains a later zero. Their immediately preceding full zero run consequently has length exactly \(t\). The first 0 of \(v\) is the final symbol of that full zero run, so \(w\) contains its other \(t-1\) zeros. These are disjoint from the first required ones. Thus
\[
|w|\ge k(m-1)-1+(t-1)=k(m-1)+\lfloor(n-2)/k\rfloor-2.
\]
This estimate is *attained for the displayed pair* by the bridge
\[
w_*=1^{k(m-1)-1}0^{t-1},
\]
which concatenates a generator of zero-run length \(m-1\) with a generator of zero-run length \(t\). Taking the maximum over pairs yields the lower bound.

**5. Asymptotic conclusion.** For fixed \(k\), the explicit lower and upper bounds differ from \(km+n/k\) by constants depending only on \(k\), not on either length. Consequently \(B_k(m,n)=km+n/k+O_k(1)\) uniformly for \(m\ge2\), \(n\ge k+2\). Sending both lengths to infinity proves the asserted directional slopes, and setting \(m=n\) gives the sharp diagonal coefficient \(k+1/k\). \(\square\)

## Verification
The included standard-library Python program `check_bridge.py` builds a finite, explicitly labeled generator-cycle graph from the defining blocks rather than relying on any forbidden-word classification. It independently verifies admissibility for every listed finite word and the constructive completions. Run:

```sh
python3 check_bridge.py
```

The observed output was `VERIFY_OK pairwise_completed 6234 sharp_witnesses 132 ruled_out_short_bridges 13901`. For each \(k\in\{1,2,3\}\) and \(1\le m,n\le5\), all language-admissible pairs were checked using the explicit joining construction; 132 lower witnesses were verified for \(1\le k\le4\), \(2\le m\le7\), \(k+2\le n\le9\); for the small cases with lower-witness gap at most 10, all candidate shorter bridges were ruled out by direct graph tests (13,901 candidates). These finite checks **do not establish the infinite theorem**; Steps 1–5 are the proof for all quantified parameters.

## Relationship to prior work
Carlos Reyes, *Frequency shifts: non-soficity, absence of specification and intrinsic ergodicity* (first publicly posted 7 November 2026), defines these generators in Sections 5–6, proves that the resulting coded system is transitive and synchronized, establishes the qualitative absence of specification in the surrounding frequency-shift family, and supplies a linear lower bound for one specific connecting-word pattern in Theorem 4.1. Lemma 6.5 gives a general nonsharp length bound for completing fragments in a particular language decomposition. Those published structural and qualitative conclusions are not claimed here. The present theorem concerns the distinct **optimization over all length-\(m\) and length-\(n\) words in the coded subsystem**, supplies separate sharp leading coefficients in its two input lengths and a single explicit witness saturating the lower profile to bounded error.

The independently published *Complete three-family antidictionary of proportional-run coded shifts* determines the minimal forbidden language of precisely this coded subsystem. Its forbidden-word classification provides an alternative way to decide finite membership but does not state or imply the optimal asymmetric growth profile without the completion and extremal bridge arguments above. Standard general work on specification and nonuniform specification supplies definitions, but not the numerical profile for this family. The original 2023 Becker–Baraviera paper introduces the ambient frequency constraints; its accessible abstract does not supply the present bridge optimization.

## Limitations
The theorem identifies the complete **leading-order two-variable profile**, with explicit bounded-width inequalities, but does not give an exact formula for \(B_k(m,n)\) at every finite pair of lengths. The new sharp asymptotic applies to the coded subsystem \(X_k\), not the larger ambient shift for \(k\ge2\), and does not imply a fixed-length universal specification gap. The finite checker covers only the stated finite ranges. Published and earlier calculations of the generator structure and partial completion bounds are credited; possible unindexed historical equivalents remain an originality risk. Independent mathematical audit has not been performed.
