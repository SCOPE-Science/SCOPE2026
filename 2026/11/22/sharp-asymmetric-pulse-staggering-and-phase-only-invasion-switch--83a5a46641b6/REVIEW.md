# Review of the asymmetric bilinear pulse-phase result

## Correctness: PASS

The theorem specifies two periodic exponential residue profiles, a scalar linearized invasion equation, and all parameter domains. Direct integration on the two time subintervals gives the stated positive sum of exponentials for joint exposure. Its strict second derivative, endpoint derivative signs, and equal endpoint values establish a single interior minimum and the sharp two-crossing invasion classification. The Taylor/sawtooth expansion isolates the universal \(PQ/8\) high-frequency gap, while the explicit minimizer and minimum give the long-period exponential law. The exact rational Taylor certificate proves a strict, phase-only pest invasion switch in one admissible example. Standalone diagnostic checks are supplementary, not a replacement for the analytic inequalities.

## Originality: PASS on inspected evidence

The 2023 impulsive pest-management model of Al Basir, Chowdhury and Torres supplies independently decaying control agents but uses additive effects in its susceptible-pest equation. Tang et al. (2011) treat decaying residual control and its threshold effects, while Ackleh et al. (2023) study combined chemical and biological control in a discrete, stage-structured model. A 2024 residual-pesticide analysis addresses different spray frequencies; a published two-patch culling theorem concerns noncommuting matrix controls. None of the relevant inspected formulations implies this two-residue product-killing model's exact unequal-decay least-overlap phase, two neutral invasion phases, and universal short-cycle advantage. Circular cross-correlation and scalar Floquet integration are established mathematical tools, not asserted novel. The full text of Liang, Tang and Cheke (2012) was not accessible; it is a residual priority risk, as is unexamined classical scheduling literature. Novelty is not certified beyond the described comparison.

## Value: PASS

The problem is motivated by the measurable decay of intervention residues and the interaction of control modalities, and asks a natural design question with identical doses and identical marginal cumulative exposures. The sharp phase boundary can reverse a positive-versus-negative invasion exponent, the generally non-half-period worst staggering is counterintuitive for unequal decay rates, and its high-frequency coefficient is independent of either lifetime. These statements give a directly reusable benchmark for testing richer nonlinear, stage-structured, or toxicity-aware models. The bilinear synergy parameter is assumed, not measured; the claim is theoretical, not a pesticide-use recommendation.

## Limitations and further scrutiny

The conclusion is confined to first-order, independently cleared residues and a scalar infinitesimal pest model with positive bilinear control synergy. No field efficacy, environmental safety, nonlinear eradication, or uniqueness across the entire published literature is asserted. The 2012 paper's full text remained inaccessible through available authorized routes; this is reported rather than silently treated as ruled out.
