# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The full proof reduces the torus operator norm by Plancherel to an exact Euclidean \(\ell_2\)-versus-\(\ell_1\) minimax. Equality cases determine the complete axial/diagonal maximizer geometry. A strictly concave logarithmic stationarity equation gives unique positive- and negative-error extrema; opposite monotonicities give a single transverse minimax crossing. Rounded Fourier lattice witnesses, whose radial first derivatives vanish, give a uniform \(O_d(t)\) difference. Tail and monotonic witness bounds prevent optimizers from escaping a compact calibration interval. These steps prove the global small-time law and the stronger all-optimizer \(O_d(t^{3/2})\) estimate. The standalone checker corroborates but does not substitute for proof.

## Originality — PASS (best-knowledge)

The nearest inspected primary papers are Xu, arXiv:2105.12175, whose Poisson generator is the isotropic \(\sqrt{-\Delta}\) and whose results are vector-valued square-function bounds, and Duong–Hu–Li, DOI 10.2969/jmsj/78287828, about product Hardy-space function-space characterizations. Neither establishes the claimed sharp difference of heat \(e^{-t\|k\|_2^2}\) and product Poisson \(e^{-s\|k\|_1}\), its unique minimax calibration, its axis–diagonal transition, or its dimension asymptotic. Related published statements were also compared at the level of their mathematical implications. Exact historical uniqueness remains unproved; a paper about semigroup comparison with another title could conceivably cover the result.

## Value — PASS

The result identifies a nonzero obstruction to *operator-norm* approximation between two natural smoothing mechanisms that both converge strongly to the identity, gives an exact dimension-dependent optimal calibration rather than just a qualitative failure, and classifies all continuum worst-frequency directions. Its increasing error approaching one with dimension quantifies a sharp and naturally motivated curse of anisotropic smoothing. It distinguishes the product Poisson construction from isotropic subordination; no peripheral or arbitrary finite slice is required.

## Scope and risks

The theorem is for fixed \(d\ge2\), \(L^2\), the \(2\pi\)-periodic torus, and product Poisson smoothing. No uniform-in-dimension \(O(t)\) estimate, finite-time exact optimizer, or broad semigroup theorem is claimed. Literature priority remains provisional despite statement-level comparison of pertinent accessible sources.
