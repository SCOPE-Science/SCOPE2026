# Sharp binary Fourier flatness on the nineteen-point hexagonal aperture

## Finding

Let the radius-two hexagonal support in axial integer coordinates be
\[
\Lambda_2=\{(j,k)\in\mathbb Z^2:\max(|j|,|k|,|j+k|)\le 2\}.
\]
It has exactly nineteen sites. For every choice of signs \(\varepsilon_{j,k}\in\{-1,1\}\), put
\[
P_\varepsilon(z,w)=\sum_{(j,k)\in\Lambda_2}\varepsilon_{j,k}z^jw^k,
\qquad M(\varepsilon)=\int_{\mathbb T^2}|P_\varepsilon(z,w)|^4\,dm(z,w),
\]
where \(m\) is normalized Haar probability measure on the two-torus. Then the *exact* binary minimum, not merely a numerical upper bound, is
\[
\boxed{\min_{\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}^{\Lambda_2}}M(\varepsilon)=467.}
\]
There are exactly 144 minimizing sign arrays. After fixing \(\varepsilon_{0,0}=1\), the 72 minimizers split into exactly three orbits, of 24 arrays each, under hexagonal rotations, reflections, and the four binary character modulations \(\varepsilon_{j,k}\mapsto(-1)^{uj+vk}\varepsilon_{j,k}\), \(u,v\in\{0,1\}\). Every nonminimizing array has \(M(\varepsilon)\ge475\), a sharp gap of eight.

With the sites ordered lexicographically by \((j,k)\), excluding the origin, represent a sign array with central sign \(+1\) by the integer bitmask whose \(b\)-th binary digit equals one when the \(b\)-th noncentral site has sign \(-1\). The three canonical orbit representatives are the integer masks `3441`, `8151`, and `10446`. Explicitly their negative sites, respectively, are

- \(\{(-2,0),(-1,0),(-1,1),(-1,2),(0,-1),(0,2),(1,-2)\}\);
- \(\{(-2,0),(-2,1),(-2,2),(-1,0),(-1,2),(0,-2),(0,-1),(0,1),(0,2),(1,-2),(1,-1)\}\);
- \(\{(-2,1),(-2,2),(-1,-1),(-1,2),(0,-2),(1,-2),(1,0)\}\).

Since \(\int|P_\varepsilon|^2\,dm=19\), the result is equivalently the sharp integrated spectral-flatness defect
\[
\boxed{\min\int_{\mathbb T^2} (|P_\varepsilon|^2-19)^2\,dm=106.}
\]

## Assumptions and scope

The coefficient support is exactly all nineteen points of the *full second closed hexagonal shell*, including its center. Every coefficient must be one of the two real signs; there are no zero or arbitrary complex coefficients. The fourth power is averaged with normalized probability measure. The symmetry classification uses the concrete group generated by
\[
R(j,k)=(-k,j+k),\qquad F(j,k)=(k,j),\qquad
\varepsilon_{j,k}\longmapsto(-1)^{uj+vk}\varepsilon_{j,k},
\]
with \(u,v\in\{0,1\}\), after quotienting out the global sign. The transformations preserve the support. There is no claim of optimality among all unimodular complex phases.

## Proof

**Exact Fourier reduction.** For \(d\in\Lambda_2-\Lambda_2\) define the aperiodic autocorrelation
\[
C_\varepsilon(d)=\sum_{p\in\Lambda_2\,:\,p+d\in\Lambda_2}
\varepsilon_p\varepsilon_{p+d}.
\]
Orthogonality of characters on \(\mathbb T^2\) gives the exact integer identity
\[
M(\varepsilon)=\sum_{d\in\Lambda_2-\Lambda_2}C_\varepsilon(d)^2.
\]
The origin contributes \(C_\varepsilon(0)^2=19^2=361\). As \(C_\varepsilon(-d)=C_\varepsilon(d)\), and there are exactly thirty positive representatives of the nonzero difference pairs, the problem becomes minimization of
\[
M(\varepsilon)=361+2\sum_{d\in D_+}C_\varepsilon(d)^2,
\quad
D_+=\{d\in(\Lambda_2-\Lambda_2)\setminus\{0\}:d_1>0\text{ or }(d_1=0,d_2>0)\}.
\]
Every term is an exact integer; no floating-point operations occur in the optimization.

**Exhaustive, reproducible finite proof.** Multiplying all nineteen signs by \(-1\) changes no autocorrelations, so set the central sign to \(+1\). The remaining eighteen independent binary variables have exactly \(2^{18}=262144\) assignments. The complete verification consists of the following finite algorithm, implemented in `verify_hex19.cpp`:

1. Construct the nineteen lattice sites by the displayed inequality, enumerate every unordered site pair, and assign each pair to its difference class up to sign; exactly thirty classes occur.
2. Initialize all signs to \(+1\), each autocorrelation as the number of pairs of its class, and the squared moment from the displayed identity.
3. Visit **every** eighteen-bit word exactly once in binary reflected Gray-code order. At each step exactly one noncentral sign flips; update each affected pair-product by its exact change and update the corresponding squared-autocorrelation sum as an integer. Record the complete histogram, all smallest-value masks, and the next distinct value.
4. Confirm that the exhaustive 262144 values have minimum 467, attained at 72 masks, that their next distinct value is 475, and that there are no unvisited assignments. The global sign symmetry doubles the 72 masks to 144 sign arrays.

This is an **exhaustive proof of a finite optimization statement**, not an extrapolation from partial testing. The second implementation `verify_hex19.py` computes every one of the same 262144 moments directly, without Gray-code updates, from vectorized pair-products for each of the thirty shift classes. It independently obtains the same minimum, multiplicities, next value, and maximum. The separate derivations and distinct enumeration paths catch errors that a single reported search log would not exclude.

**Complete orbit certification.** Using the exact integer set of 72 minimizing masks, the Python implementation generates all \(6\cdot2\cdot2\cdot2=48\) rotations/reflections/character actions on each selected representative, with the center fixed. It finds exactly three disjoint orbits of cardinality 24, containing all 72 masks, with least representatives `3441`, `8151`, and `10446`. All images are explicitly verified to be among the global minimizers. Thus there can be no additional minimum orbit or exceptional sign configuration. For every displayed representative, direct substitution gives \(\sum_{d\in D_+}C_\varepsilon(d)^2=53\), and hence \(M=361+2\cdot53=467\).

**Discrete Fourier transfer.** The differences lie within \([-4,4]^2\), so for integers \(m,n\ge9\) no two distinct difference vectors alias modulo \((m,n)\). Therefore the exact same integer identity and all minimum and orbit conclusions hold when the fourth moment is averaged on the finite Fourier grid \(\mathbb Z_m\times\mathbb Z_n\). This is a direct consequence of difference-character orthogonality, not a further optimization claim.

## Verification

The package includes `verify_hex19.cpp` (standard C++17, no external libraries), `verify_hex19.py` (Python 3 plus NumPy), and `CHECKS.txt` (recorded results from running these exact files). To repeat the exhaustive finite proof in a directory containing the files, run

```sh
g++ -std=c++17 -O2 -Wall -Wextra -pedantic verify_hex19.cpp -o verify_hex19
./verify_hex19
python3 verify_hex19.py
```

The C++ program exits with failure unless the site count, thirty shift classes, 262144 assignments, minimum, next value, maximum, and minimizing count exactly match the theorem. The Python program independently recomputes the complete moment histogram, proves the orbit partition by explicit enumeration of group actions, and checks every expected exact count. The recorded results are `INCREMENTAL_EXHAUSTIVE_PASS` and `DIRECT_EXHAUSTIVE_PASS`. The finite proof requires successful execution of the source code and the elementary Fourier identity; a stored log alone is not a proof.

## Relationship to prior work

Balister, Bollobás, Morris, Sahasrabudhe and Tiba (arXiv:1907.09464v1, publicly available 2019-07-22) prove asymptotic existence of one-variable flat Littlewood polynomials. Their theorem explains why discrete-sign spectral flatness is natural, but it is neither an exact two-variable nineteen-site minimization nor a finite optimizer classification.

Ding, Noshad and Tarokh (arXiv:1506.02160v1) explicitly draw this **same nineteen-site hexagonal support** in their Figure 10, then delete the center and construct several jointly complementary eighteen-site phase arrays in Section II-F. Their Section II-D also studies seven-site complementary arrays. Joint autocorrelation cancellation across several arrays does not imply the minimum of squared sidelobes for **one** binary nineteen-site array, and their exact same-support discussion does not give the number 467 or the three sign-orbit classes. This paper is essential same-object prior work and the support geometry is not claimed as new.

The public seven-site radius-one hexagonal fourth-moment classification (2026-11-08) proves a continuous-unimodular result on seven sites, not nineteen. The published six-site triangular, nine-site square, ten-site triangular, and sixteen-site square results likewise have genuinely different supports; changing a support changes every cross-shell autocorrelation and none of the listed extremal values implies this theorem.

Ramakrishna–Mow (2005) report an exhaustive search of binary **peak sidelobe level** on small arrays, a maximum-autocorrelation objective, not the integrated sum of squares in this result. Busboom–Lüke (2001) study hexagonal binary arrays with **periodic/odd-periodic** perfect correlation. These are related design goals but do not supply the present aperiodic single-array fourth-moment minimum in the material available for inspection.

## Limitations

The numerical values and equality cases concern only the fixed nineteen-site support, real binary coefficients, and the stated fourth-moment metric. The proof is a complete finite enumeration, not a closed-form lower-bound inequality uniform over larger hexagonal shells, and it does not assert ideal peak sidelobes, optimal beam patterns in physical instruments, or superiority over complex-phase designs. The two exhaustive implementations, despite differing in algorithms, remain computational verification; their source and outputs are provided so their finite loops and arithmetic can be audited. Full historical priority against unindexed tabulations, especially older peak-sidelobe array tables, is unresolved. Independent mathematical and bibliographic validation have not been performed.
