# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The statement quantifies over every degree, every modulus and every real polynomial with irrational top coefficient. The Vandermonde moment argument correctly supplies a nonconstant irrational coefficient to each nonzero integer linear combination of the first \(d\) translated polynomial values. Weyl's theorem gives vector equidistribution. The finite-difference identity then reduces every last-letter choice to the floor of a sum of independent uniform variables with binomial weights. Its support is an interval of exactly \(2^d\) consecutive integers, all positive, and the displayed weighted Irwin–Hall inclusion-exclusion formula gives the individual frequencies. An independent exact-integer test exercises degrees one through five with perturbed coefficients. No finite experiment is treated as proof.

General frequency-existence results and the classical degree-one case are explicitly credited; unindexed equivalents remain a residual risk.

The motivating modular-equidistribution result leaves open what can be said about simultaneous consecutive residues. The universal threshold \(m\le2^d\) is a sharp transition from complete to forbidden \((d+1)\)-letter patterns, while the exact formula resolves the nonuniform probabilities in the first constrained block length. This is a natural complete block invariant for a broad class of integer-part sequences, not an arbitrary parameter slice or an isolated numerical example.
