# Sharp Boolean two-shell Fourier ratio and its unique Gaussian limit

## Finding
Let \(d\ge2\), \(G_d=(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^d\), and consider the two-dimensional space of complex signals
\[
 \mathcal V_d=\left\{f_{a,b}=a\mathbf1_{\{0\}}+b\mathbf1_{\{|x|=2\}}:a,b\in\mathbb C\right\}.
\]
For the unitary Walsh transform \(\widehat f(\xi)=2^{-d/2}\sum_{x\in G_d}f(x)(-1)^{x\cdot\xi}\), define the Fourier ratio \(\mathrm{FR}_d(f)=\|\widehat f\|_{\ell^1}/\|\widehat f\|_{\ell^2}\) for nonzero \(f\). Write \(B_d\sim\mathrm{Binomial}(d,1/2)\), \(Z_d=(d-2B_d)/\sqrt d\), and
\[
 u_{d,j}=\frac{(d-2j)^2}{d},\qquad 0\le j\le\lfloor d/2\rfloor.
\]
Then the sharp ratio over **all complex coefficients** is the explicit finite minimum
\[
 \boxed{\inf_{f\in\mathcal V_d\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\mathrm{FR}_d(f)}{2^{d/2}}
 =c_d:=\min_{0\le j\le\lfloor d/2\rfloor}
 \frac{\mathbb E|Z_d^2-u_{d,j}|}
 {\sqrt{(1-u_{d,j})^2+2(1-d^{-1})}}.}
\]
For each minimizing index \(j\), all its equality cases are exactly the nonzero complex multiples of
\[
 \boxed{\quad (1-u_{d,j})\mathbf1_{\{0\}}+\frac2d\mathbf1_{\{|x|=2\}}.\quad}
\]
There are no additional complex-phase minimizers. Multiple minimizing indices, when present, give their respective lines.

There is a **universal sharp Gaussian limit**:
\[
 \boxed{c_d\longrightarrow c_*=2t_*\phi(t_*)\sqrt{(1-t_*^2)^2+2}
 =0.549426226034278\ldots,}
\]
where \(\phi(t)=(2\pi)^{-1/2}e^{-t^2/2}\), \(\Phi(t)=\int_{-\infty}^t\phi(s)\,ds\), and \(t_*=0.479855965645868\ldots\) is the **unique** solution in \((0,1)\) of
\[
 4\Phi(t)-3=2t(t^2-1)\phi(t).
\]
Every minimizing index sequence satisfies \(u_{d,j_d}\to t_*^2=0.230261747765929\ldots\). Consequently
\[
 \lim_{d\to\infty}2^{-d}\left(\inf_{f\in\mathcal V_d\setminus\{0\}}\mathrm{FR}_d(f)\right)^2=c_*^2
 =0.301869177854\ldots.
\]
These are exact statements about the prescribed two-shell signal class; the last number is a normalized effective Fourier dimension, not a recovery lower bound for arbitrary sampling procedures.

## Assumptions and scope
The Hamming weight \(|x|\) counts nonzero binary coordinates, and the two shells are the singleton origin and the full Hamming weight-two sphere. The Fourier transform has the displayed unitary counting normalization. Both \(\ell^1\) and \(\ell^2\) are **counting** norms on all \(2^d\) Fourier frequencies; changing to probability norms changes the ratio and its normalization. Complex coefficients are unrestricted; there are no unimodularity or positivity assumptions. The index range includes the middle Hamming layer and one representative of each distinct squared imbalance. The asymptotic limit is along all integer dimensions \(d\to\infty\).

## Proof
**1. Exact Walsh reduction.** On any frequency of Hamming weight \(k\), the Walsh sum of the weight-two sphere is the degree-two Krawtchouk value
\[
 K_2(k)=\binom{d-k}{2}-k(d-k)+\binom{k}{2}
 =\frac{(d-2k)^2-d}{2}.
\]
Therefore \(\widehat f_{a,b}(\xi)=2^{-d/2}[a+bK_2(|\xi|)]\). By Walsh orthogonality,
\[
 \|\widehat f_{a,b}\|_2^2=|a|^2+\binom d2|b|^2,
\qquad
 \frac{\mathrm{FR}_d(f_{a,b})}{2^{d/2}}
 =\frac{\mathbb E|a+bK_2(B_d)|}{\sqrt{|a|^2+\binom d2|b|^2}}.
\]
In particular \(\mathbb E K_2(B_d)=0\) and \(\mathbb E K_2(B_d)^2=\binom d2\). The random variable \(K_2(B_d)\) takes the distinct values \(d(u_{d,j}-1)/2\), each with positive probability.

**2. The real optimization has a zero-value extremizer.** First require \(a,b\in\mathbb R\). The positive-definite quadratic denominator is constant on the ellipse \(a^2+\binom d2 b^2=1\). Its finitely many sign regions are arcs cut out by the lines \(a+bK_2(j)=0\). On any such open arc, the numerator is a strictly positive linear functional of \((a,b)\) and hence a strictly positive linear functional of unit-circle coordinates after rescaling \(b\). Its second derivative with respect to angle equals the negative of the functional, so it is strictly concave there and cannot take an interior minimum. Every minimizer therefore lies on a zero line \(a+bK_2(j)=0\). The lines cover all boundary points and none requires \(b=0\); they are generated by \(a=1-u_{d,j}\) and \(b=2/d\). Substitution, using \(K_2(B_d)=d(Z_d^2-1)/2\), yields precisely the displayed finite minimum. Strict concavity excludes all other real equality lines.

**3. No additional complex equality cases.** Write complex coefficients \((a,b)=(a_1,b_1)+i(a_2,b_2)\), all four components real. For \(X=|a_1+b_1K_2(B_d)|\), \(Y=|a_2+b_2K_2(B_d)|\), the triangle inequality for the Euclidean norm and the real sharp inequality imply
\[
 \mathbb E\sqrt{X^2+Y^2}
 \ge\sqrt{(\mathbb E X)^2+(\mathbb E Y)^2}
 \ge c_d\sqrt{a_1^2+a_2^2+\binom d2(b_1^2+b_2^2)}.
\]
If either real component is nonzero, equality in the second inequality forces that component onto one of the real minimizing lines. If both are nonzero, equality in the first forces \((X,Y)\) to have constant direction at all support values. Different zero lines cannot meet that condition: at a zero of one affine function, the other would be nonzero. Thus both real components occupy the same real minimizing line, and the complex pair is a complex scalar multiple of the indicated real generator. Conversely each such line plainly attains equality.

**4. Gaussian limit and its uniqueness.** Let \(Z\) be standard normal. The central limit theorem gives \(Z_d\Rightarrow Z\); also \(\mathbb E Z_d^4=3-2/d\le3\), so \(Z_d^2\) is uniformly integrable. On every bounded interval of \(u\ge0\), the numerator \(\mathbb E|Z_d^2-u|\) converges uniformly to \(\mathbb E|Z^2-u|\): pointwise convergence follows from uniform integrability, and each function is one-Lipschitz in \(u\). The denominator converges uniformly on compact intervals. Moreover for \(u>1\),
\[
 \frac{\mathbb E|Z_d^2-u|}{\sqrt{(1-u)^2+2(1-d^{-1})}}
 \ge\frac{u-1}{\sqrt{(u-1)^2+2}},
\]
which tends to one uniformly in \(d\) as \(u\to\infty\). Since the eventual minimum is strictly below one, minimizing grid parameters stay bounded. The discrete grid \(\{(d-2j)^2/d\}\) becomes dense in every compact subset of \([0,\infty)\). It follows that all limit points of its minimizers minimize
\[
 R(u)=\frac{L(u)}{\sqrt{(1-u)^2+2}},\qquad L(u)=\mathbb E|Z^2-u|,\quad u\ge0,
\]
and that the minima converge to \(\min_{u\ge0}R(u)\).

For \(u=t^2>0\), differentiation under the integral gives
\[
 L'(u)=4\Phi(t)-3,\qquad L(u)=(u-1)L'(u)+4t\phi(t).
\]
Thus \(R'(u)\) has the sign of
\[
 H(t)=4\Phi(t)-3-2t(t^2-1)\phi(t),\qquad
 H'(t)=2\phi(t)(t^2-1)(t^2-3).
\]
We have \(H(0)=-1\), \(H(1)>0\); \(H\) is strictly increasing on \((0,1)\), strictly decreasing on \((1,\sqrt3)\), and increasing after \(\sqrt3\). The Gaussian-tail inequality \(1-\Phi(t)\le\phi(t)/t\) gives
\[
 H(\sqrt3)\ge1-\frac{16}{\sqrt3}\phi(\sqrt3)>0.
\]
Consequently \(H\) has exactly one positive zero, \(t_*\in(0,1)\), and \(R\) decreases before \(u=t_*^2\) and increases after it. The defining equation and the identity for \(L\) give \(L(t_*^2)=2t_*\phi(t_*)[(1-t_*^2)^2+2]\), proving the stated sharp limit and the convergence of **every** discrete optimizer parameter. Squaring gives the effective-dimension limit.

Its run passed 503 supplementary checks, yielding \(t_*=0.479855965645869\ldots\) and \(c_*=0.549426226034278\ldots\). Tests are not a substitute for the infinite-dimensional proof or a uniqueness certificate.

## Relationship to prior work
Burstein, Iosevich and Krause (arXiv:2609.22568, initially public 2026-09-18) motivate the Fourier ratio as a parameter for restricted isometry and entropic-descent Fourier sampling; their theorem gives sampling guarantees for bounded ratio and does not optimize the ratio on this two-shell Boolean family. Aldaleh et al. (arXiv:2511.19560) study Fourier ratios and signal complexity at a general level. Kirshner and Samorodnitsky (arXiv:1909.11929) study moment-ratio inequalities with \(p\ge2\) and Krawtchouk polynomials, not this sharp \(\ell^1/\ell^2\) lower problem.

A particularly close published two-shell calculation, *Apex-weighted Hamming two-shells: an exact Gabor rank discriminant and a universal two-atom spectral law* (published-finding corpus, November 2026), uses precisely the origin-plus-weight-two window with an arbitrary complex apex parameter, but computes the rank and singular-value distribution of a **Gabor intensity-lifting operator**, not the minimum Fourier ratio of that window; its exact Krawtchouk identity is shared classical input. The published work *Exact degree-two Hamming projection norms and the Gaussian–Poisson crossover* (published-finding corpus, October 2026) evaluates a projection operator's endpoint norm, a distinct optimization from the Fourier ratio of a vector in the two-shell time-domain subspace. *Minimum radial sampling layers for a tight quadratic Walsh frame* (published-finding corpus, November 2026) solves radial cubature feasibility, not Fourier-ratio minimization. *Sharp endpoint projection constants and Gaussian growth for bounded-order interactions* (published-finding corpus, October 2026) likewise concerns a projection operator norm. Krawtchouk identities, Gaussian convergence and L1/L2 norm reduction are classical proof ingredients, not themselves novel claims. The specific full complex equality classification, exact all-dimensional two-shell constant and its uniquely optimized Gaussian value are the present assertion.

## Limitations
The theorem concerns only origin-plus-entire-weight-two **radial** signals, not all functions supported on the Hamming radius-two ball or arbitrary sparse signals. The finite exact answer is a minimum of at most \(\lfloor d/2\rfloor+1\) explicit binomial expressions, not an asserted universal closed-form minimizing index. No finite-dimensional rate of convergence is proved. The Gaussian limit is not asserted to be a universal lower Fourier ratio over arbitrary supports. Prior nonindexed variants of sharp Gaussian \(L^1/L^2\) optimization are a residual bibliographic risk; novelty is assessed against the inspected literature, not independently certified.
