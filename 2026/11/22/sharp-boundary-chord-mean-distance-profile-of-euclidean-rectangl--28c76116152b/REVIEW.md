# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

The claim is an exact one-parameter classification of arclength-uniform Euclidean boundary-chord mean distance on rectangles. The ordered side-pair integral, its arctangent-free inverse-hyperbolic closed form, the derivative relation \(4(1+t)^4\mathcal R'(t)=F(t)\), and the strictly positive auxiliary \(H\) yield a global proof. The crucial exact identity \(H'(z)=(z-1)^2(z+1)/(z^2+1)^{3/2}\) was recomputed symbolically and verified against the written differentiations; \(H(1)>0\) and the two zero endpoint values of strictly concave \(F\) prove strict monotonicity. Both Taylor coefficients were recomputed separately. Finite numerical checks in the embedded checker independently reconstruct the boundary integral; they are not substituted for proof.

Search comparisons also distinguished previous work on rectangles' chord-interception laws, boundary–interior differences, and one-point radial distances. ## Value — PASS

Rectangles are the canonical extremizing degeneration for the sharp universal planar lower bound \(1/6\). Establishing the complete finite-aspect interpolation, identifying its unique square maximum, and quantifying two different sharp endpoint rates supplies nontrivial geometric stability information that the universal inequality cannot give. The result is an exact self-contained family classification, rather than an arbitrarily selected numerical instance. It does not claim a general convex-body stability theorem.
