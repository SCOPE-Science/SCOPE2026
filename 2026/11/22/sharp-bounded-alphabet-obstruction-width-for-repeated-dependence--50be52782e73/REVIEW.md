# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** Hall's theorem gives an exact row-capacity \(r\), column-capacity one assignment criterion for every finite team. In a minimal failure, a violating rectangle must support every edge and exceed capacity by one. No row can have at most \(r\) edges, since it could be assigned to the row-specific slots after the remaining team is split; therefore the row support has size at most the column support plus one. The resulting \((r+1)(q+1)\) cutoff is sharp by the explicitly verified cyclic-window construction. Equality forces \(q+1\) rows all of degree \(r+1\), and precisely the stated proper-subset Hall sparsity criterion. Independent finite matching and rectangle implementations agreed in all tested cases, but the proof—not finite testing—establishes arbitrary parameter values.

**Originality — PASS within inspected evidence.** Fröhlich–Kolaitis–Meier's full paper characterizes two-atom cases; the known \(r=1\) consequence is acknowledged and not claimed as new. The published four-by-four Boolean three-clause census yields a maximum thirteen-edge obstruction for four labels on each shore, which cannot cover the fifteen-edge example on five rows and four columns here. Barlag et al. address coherence and first-order rewritability at a much broader qualitative level. The new claim is the all-multiplicity sharp alphabet cutoff plus exact hypergraph extremizer condition. Classical capacitated matching is a proof ingredient, not independent novelty. A historical full-generalization in transversal matroid circuit theory is possible and not excluded by the available search.

**Value — PASS.** The cutoff identifies the exact number of assignments needed to witness failure of a non-idempotent repeated team split with bounded outcome alphabet, even when the determinant alphabet is arbitrarily large. This turns a qualitative bounded-palette coherence phenomenon into a sharp and executable local test and identifies all equality configurations, rather than an arbitrary one-size table or routine fixed instance.
