# Scientific review: quadratic Jacobi projection in bounded variation

## Correctness
**PASS.** For every real \(p>0\), the three normalized Jacobi moments yield the displayed reproducing kernel. Two beta integration identities give an exact affine derivative for projection of each one-jump input. Direct absolute integration gives \(F_p(a)=A_p(a)J((2p+3)|a|)\). Its derivative has the sign of the stated quadratic, whose two roots are separated by the precise intervals \(((2p+3)^{-2},(2p+3)^{-1})\) and \(((2p+3)^{-1},1)\). The logarithmic lower bound strictly excludes the central competitor. The Stieltjes representation and strict triangle inequality establish the global norm, attainment and complete equality classification. Beta asymptotics establish the two limiting laws. The packaged checker independently recomputes moments and step integrals, and runs random signed-step diagnostics; these do not substitute for the unrestricted proof.

## Originality
**PASS, with residual historical-priority risk.** The published two-branch quadratic symmetric-Jacobi range-envelope result uses the same orthogonal projector and derives an exact one-jump response but maximizes **pointwise output range** for monotone inputs, not \(\int |(P_pf)'|\), and neither its result nor its proof gives the claimed global BV maximization or off-centre rigidity. The published quadratic ultraspherical large-parameter analysis concerns the \(L^1\)/\(L^\infty\) operator norm, not the BV seminorm; its \(4\phi(1)p\) coefficient differs from the present \(\sqrt{2/(\pi e)}p\). Kaber (2006) concerns Gibbs phenomena for high-degree Jacobi expansions. Kopotun et al. (2011) concern shape-constrained polynomial approximation and Calderón–Urbina (2012) Abel-summability kernel estimates. Searches for BV/Jacobi/Legendre projection-norm equivalences and parameterized step-response variation produced no dominating prior result. Indexed-search incompleteness and older unindexed work are genuine residual risks; novelty is not independently certified.

## Mathematical value
**PASS.** Unweighted total variation of the fitted quadratic, unlike pointwise range or operator \(L^\infty\) gain, quantifies oscillatory instability under discontinuous perturbations. The theorem determines an exact attained norm over all BV signals at each Jacobi shape, the complete equality geometry, and a nontrivial shift of the worst jump toward the center as weight concentrates. The linear growth constant and low-shape boundary give useful structural distinctions between variation stability and previously studied range behavior. This is not a mere fixed-parameter numerical evaluation or renaming of the range theorem.

## Scope and uncertainty
Real inputs, fixed polynomial degree two, symmetric integrable Jacobi weights, and unweighted distributional total variation only. No higher-degree, asymmetric, norm-comparison or independent historical-priority claim. The exact polynomial and asymptotic statements are analytical, while finite checker tests are corroborative.
