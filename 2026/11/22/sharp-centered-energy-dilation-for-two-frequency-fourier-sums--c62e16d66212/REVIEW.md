# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

For arbitrary real frequencies and complex coefficients, carrier/translation/dilation invariance reduce the claim to the orthogonal cosine/sine sectors. Exact symmetric interval integration reduces the sharp dilation bound to global decrease of \((z+\sin z)/z^3\) and \((z-\sin z)/z^3\) for all positive arguments. Explicit derivatives and an elementary monotonicity argument prove both strict inequalities, including all high-frequency lobes. An anti-symmetric near-collision witness gives the exact sharp supremum and its nonattainment. The numerical checker only verifies supplementary finite instances.

## Originality — PASS relative to accessible evidence

The relevant passage of Zhang (arXiv:2607.10501v2, Section 5.3/Proposition 5.6) explicitly formulates actual integrated exterior transfer and gives general \(k\)-scale upper and lower bounds, not this exact \(k=2\) value. Published two-frequency point-evaluation results give sharp pointwise leverage, and the elementary fixed-gap decomposition by itself does not supply the all-gap integrated sharp supremum. A known pointwise bound integrates to a strictly weaker exterior constant. The genuine overlap risk is that an inaccessible appendix or an older sine-integral inequality may establish the same sharp nested-interval norm. Such a possibility is acknowledged, not dismissed as proved absent.

## Value — PASS

The actual integrated transfer constant, rather than a fixed-gap eigenvalue or an integrated pointwise envelope, is a natural explicitly defined invariant for two-frequency Fourier prediction outside a training interval. The exact global constant and its nonattainment identify the worst possible frequency geometry with no frequency-separation assumption, for every exterior aperture. They replace generic exponential-in-bandwidth or upper-envelope estimates with a cubic exact formula in the first nontrivial sparse class. Neither noisy recovery nor multi-frequency extensions are claimed.
