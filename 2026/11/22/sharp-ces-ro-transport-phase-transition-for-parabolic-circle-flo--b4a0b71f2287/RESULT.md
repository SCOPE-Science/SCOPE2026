# Sharp Cesàro transport phase transition for parabolic circle flows

## Finding

Let \(\mathbb T=\mathbb R/(2\pi\mathbb Z)\) have geodesic distance \(d\), and let \(p=0\). For every \(\gamma\ge 1/2\), the flow \(\phi_t^{(\gamma)}\) generated by
\[
\dot\theta=(1-\cos\theta)^\gamma
\]
fixes \(p\). On \(A_m=C(\mathbb T,M_m(\mathbb C))\), for every integer \(m\ge1\), set \(K_t f=f\circ\phi_t^{(\gamma)}\), \(P f\equiv f(p)\), and \(M_T=T^{-1}\int_0^T K_t\,dt\). Write \(\operatorname{Lip}(f)=\sup_{x\ne y}\|f(x)-f(y)\|_{\mathrm{op}}/d(x,y)\). Define the sharp Lipschitz-to-uniform Cesàro deviation
\[
D_\gamma(T)=\sup_{\operatorname{Lip}(f)\le1}\|M_Tf-Pf\|_\infty,\qquad T>0.
\]
There is a unique \(a_\gamma(T)\in(0,\pi)\) satisfying
\[
\int_{a_\gamma(T)}^\pi\frac{ds}{(1-\cos s)^\gamma}=\frac T2,
\]
and the **exact finite-time norm** is
\[
\boxed{D_\gamma(T)=\frac2T\int_{a_\gamma(T)}^\pi\frac{s\,ds}{(1-\cos s)^\gamma}.}
\]
The unique worst initial point is the point whose time-\(T/2\) image is antipodal to \(p\). Every matrix size \(m\) has the same sharp norm, attained by the scalar-matrix observable \(f(x)=d(x,p)I_m\).

Its large-time asymptotics form a sharp three-regime transition:
\[
\boxed{D_\gamma(T)\sim\begin{cases}
\displaystyle \frac{2I_\gamma}{T},&\frac12\le\gamma<1,\\[5pt]
\displaystyle\frac{4\log T}{T},&\gamma=1,\\[5pt]
\displaystyle C_\gamma T^{-1/(2\gamma-1)},&\gamma>1,
\end{cases}}
\]
where
\[
I_\gamma=\int_0^\pi\frac{s\,ds}{(1-\cos s)^\gamma},\qquad
C_\gamma=\frac{2\gamma-1}{2\gamma-2}
\left(\frac{2^{\gamma+1}}{2\gamma-1}\right)^{\!1/(2\gamma-1)}.
\]
At the source-model parameter \(\gamma=1\), the finite-time formula reduces to
\[
\boxed{D_1(T)=2\arctan(2/T)+\frac2T\log(1+T^2/4).}
\]
At \(\gamma=1/2\), \(I_{1/2}=4\sqrt2\,G\), where \(G=\sum_{k\ge0}(-1)^k/(2k+1)^2\) is Catalan's constant.

In contrast to the Lipschitz-to-uniform decay, for **every finite** \(T>0\),
\[
\|M_T-P\|_{A_m\to A_m}=2,
\]
although \(M_Tf\to Pf\) in \(\|\cdot\|_\infty\) for every fixed continuous \(f\in A_m\).

## Assumptions and scope

The circle has circumference \(2\pi\); the distance is the shorter arc length and is therefore at most \(\pi\). The exponent \(\gamma\ge1/2\) ensures that the vector field is globally Lipschitz, vanishes at \(p\), and cannot carry an orbit through \(p\) in finite time. All operator norms on matrices are spectral (not entrywise) norms. The seminorm \(\operatorname{Lip}(f)\) ignores constants, but \(M_T-P\) annihilates constants, so the variational definition is finite; equivalently one can use the pointed Banach Lipschitz norm \(\|f(p)\|+\operatorname{Lip}(f)\). The assertion concerns Cesàro averages over the forward interval \([0,T]\), not a pointwise-in-time convergence rate and not the unbounded Lipschitz norm of the composed observable.

## Proof

**1. Global time coordinate.** For \(0<\theta<2\pi\), put \(v_\gamma(\theta)=(1-\cos\theta)^\gamma\) and
\[
U_\gamma(\theta)=\int_\pi^\theta\frac{ds}{v_\gamma(s)}.
\]
Near either endpoint \(v_\gamma(\theta)\asymp d(\theta,p)^{2\gamma}\); hence \(U_\gamma\) maps \((0,2\pi)\) continuously and strictly increasingly onto all of \(\mathbb R\) when \(\gamma\ge1/2\). It satisfies \(U_\gamma(\phi_t^{(\gamma)}(\theta))=U_\gamma(\theta)+t\) for all real \(t\). Reflection \(\theta\mapsto2\pi-\theta\) gives \(U_\gamma(2\pi-\theta)=-U_\gamma(\theta)\).

**2. Exact norm and worst start.** Define \(g_\gamma(u)=d(U_\gamma^{-1}(u),p)\). This is even, positive, continuous, strictly decreasing on \([0,\infty)\), has \(g_\gamma(0)=\pi\), and tends to zero at infinity. For any \(f\) with \(\operatorname{Lip}(f)\le1\),
\[
\|M_Tf(x)-Pf\|\le\frac1T\int_0^T d(\phi_t^{(\gamma)}(x),p)\,dt.
\]
Equality for every \(x\) holds when \(f(x)=d(x,p)I_m\). At \(x=p\) the deviation is zero. For the remaining points, put \(u=U_\gamma(x)\); the right side is \(T^{-1}\int_u^{u+T}g_\gamma(w)\,dw\). Its derivative in \(u\) is \([g_\gamma(u+T)-g_\gamma(u)]/T\), positive for \(u<-T/2\) and negative for \(u>-T/2\). Hence the unique maximizing start has \(u=-T/2\); its orbit meets the antipode at time \(T/2\). Therefore
\[
D_\gamma(T)=\frac2T\int_0^{T/2}g_\gamma(w)\,dw.
\]
For \(w\ge0\), writing \(s=g_\gamma(w)\) yields \(w=\int_s^\pi v_\gamma(q)^{-1}dq\) and \(dw=-ds/v_\gamma(s)\). This produces the stated exact formula and the unique \(a_\gamma(T)\).

**3. Subcritical, critical and supercritical asymptotics.** Since \(v_\gamma(s)\sim2^{-\gamma}s^{2\gamma}\) as \(s\downarrow0\), the integral \(I_\gamma\) is finite exactly for \(\gamma<1\). In that range monotone convergence in the exact formula gives \(TD_\gamma(T)\to2I_\gamma\). For \(\gamma=1\),
\[
\int_s^\pi\frac{dq}{1-\cos q}=\cot(s/2),
\]
so \(g_1(w)=2\arctan(1/w)\) for \(w>0\); integrating explicitly gives the claimed closed form and \(D_1(T)\sim4\log T/T\).

For \(\gamma>1\), write \(\beta=1/(2\gamma-1)\in(0,1)\). The time coordinate satisfies
\[
w=\int_{g_\gamma(w)}^\pi\frac{ds}{v_\gamma(s)}\sim\frac{2^\gamma}{2\gamma-1}g_\gamma(w)^{-(2\gamma-1)},
\]
whence
\[
g_\gamma(w)\sim\left(\frac{2^\gamma}{(2\gamma-1)w}\right)^\beta.
\]
Integrating this regularly varying tail and substituting into the exact norm yields \(D_\gamma(T)\sim C_\gamma T^{-\beta}\) with the coefficient as displayed. At \(\gamma=1/2\), use \(U_{1/2}(\theta)=\sqrt2\log\tan(\theta/4)\), so for \(w\ge0\), \(g_{1/2}(w)=4\arctan(e^{-w/\sqrt2})\). The change of variables \(z=e^{-w/\sqrt2}\) gives \(I_{1/2}=4\sqrt2\int_0^1\arctan(z)/z\,dz=4\sqrt2G\).

**4. Norm/strong convergence separation.** The uniform norm upper bound \(\|M_T-P\|_{A_m\to A_m}\le2\) follows since both maps are contractions. For any finite \(T\), choose the maximizing nonfixed initial point from Step 2. Its compact orbit segment avoids \(p\); by normality of the circle, there exists a continuous scalar function equal to \(+1\) on that segment and equal to \(-1\) at \(p\). Multiplying by \(I_m\) attains norm two. Nevertheless, for every neighborhood \(V\) of \(p\), every nonfixed orbit traverses \(\mathbb T\setminus V\) in total time at most \(\int_{\mathbb T\setminus V}v_\gamma(\theta)^{-1}\,d\theta<\infty\), uniformly over initial points. Given \(f\in A_m\), continuity makes \(\|f(\theta)-f(p)\|\) small on \(V\), while the complementary contribution to its averaged norm is bounded by this uniform residence time divided by \(T\). Thus \(\|M_Tf-Pf\|_\infty\to0\).

## Verification

The proof uses only explicit coordinate changes, monotonicity of an even unimodal function, scalar integral identities and elementary estimates; no finite test substitutes for an infinite-domain argument. The displayed \(\gamma=1\) formula can be differentiated and evaluated at \(T\downarrow0\): its limit is \(\pi\), the maximal circle distance. Its large-\(T\) coefficient is \(4\), agreeing with the general critical integral. For \(\gamma=1/2\), \(I_{1/2}=4\sqrt2G\) checks the small-speed integrability endpoint. The \(\gamma>1\) coefficient follows algebraically from the tail exponent and is positive for every \(\gamma>1\).

## Relationship to prior work

Kania and Niwiński, *The e-Property for Quantum Markov Semigroups* (arXiv:2611.00703, publicly posted 31 October 2026), Example 5.9, use the case \(\gamma=1\) with matrix-valued observables and establish qualitative norm convergence of averages and failure of ordinary equicontinuity. The present result credits those facts and adds the exact Lipschitz-to-uniform norm, unique worst phase, complete power-speed transition with leading constants, and the explicit distinction between strong and full-operator-norm Cesàro convergence. Ben-Artzi and Morisse (2023) prove uniform ergodic rates for different periodic fiber flows on an annulus in Hilbert-space norms, not the present point-attractor circle flow. Kantorovich–Rubinstein duality supplies the known connection between Lipschitz observable norms and transport to a Dirac mass; that general duality is not claimed as new. The new calculation is the sharp time-window optimization and all-exponent critical profile for the stated parabolic family.

## Limitations

No independent mathematical audit is claimed. The exponent range starts at \(\gamma=1/2\) because below it the vector field loses local Lipschitz regularity at \(p\) and the standard global flow is not uniquely defined there. No assertion is made about non-geodesic metrics, multiple attracting points, perturbations of the exact speed, stronger Lipschitz-to-Lipschitz convergence, or convergence in total variation. The originality comparison is based on the accessible cited sources and semantic research database searches; a more general unlocated result on exact Wasserstein occupation rates could cover the same formula.
