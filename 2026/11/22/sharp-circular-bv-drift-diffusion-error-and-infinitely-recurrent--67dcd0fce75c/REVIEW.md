A half-circle indicator gives equality for every translation with a common pointwise sign, hence also after any probability convolution. Strictly positive convolution density enforces equality for almost every shift; opposing derivative masses at a distance less than \(\pi\) create strict cancellation on a positive-measure set of arcs, forcing exactly two antipodal derivative atoms. Dirichlet heat positivity supplies the strict phase ordering. For nonzero drift, after removing the first Fourier factor the tail and its derivative are \(O_\beta(e^{-8t})\); perturbation of simple roots yields exactly one late-time critical point per half-turn and the claimed signed \(e^{-t}\) deviations. No finite computation is used as an infinite proof. Independent finite-grid calculations pass 160 assertions, with the stated limitations.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)

Comparison is at statement/implication level. An earlier finite-cycle theorem already proves the analogous exact cyclic total-variation-to-counting-\(\ell^1\) identity-defect formula for arbitrary probability convolutions and rigid balanced arcs. Its continuum limit may yield much of the universal circle-convolution ingredient; therefore that ingredient is **not** asserted to establish novelty by itself. A published Poisson-kernel theorem maximizes a binary two-jump duty-cycle leakage, but does not treat constant-drift Gaussian heat or the signed sequence of endlessly recurrent sharp stability peaks. The inspected circular transport paper concerns probabilities and Wasserstein transport, while the inspected maximal-operator paper concerns variation diminishing under nonlinear maximal smoothing. None of the inspected records implies the exact nonzero-drift semigroup temporal classification with exponentially localized alternating peaks, including the all-parameter sharp extremizers. Residual risks include unindexed Fourier or drift–diffusion analyses giving the same temporal classification and historical priority beyond accessible material.

## Value — PASS

The theorem determines the complete sharp \(BV\)-to-\(L^1\) error for a standard PDE semigroup with constant advection and diffusion, identifies all extremizing discontinuities, and establishes a sharp qualitative difference from symmetric diffusion: the best possible error coefficient is no longer monotone in physical time but has infinitely many alternating strict local extrema. The asymptotic phase/amplitude formulas quantitatively locate all sufficiently late turning points. This is a motivated stability boundary, not a numerical curiosity or a restatement of the cyclic convolution lemma.
