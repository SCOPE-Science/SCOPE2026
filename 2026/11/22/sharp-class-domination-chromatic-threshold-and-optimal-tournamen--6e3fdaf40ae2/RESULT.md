# Sharp class-domination chromatic threshold and optimal tournament colourings of two-subset Kneser graphs

## Finding
For each integer \(n\ge5\), the class-domination chromatic number (also called dominated or CD chromatic number) of the two-element Kneser graph is
\[
\chi_{\mathrm{cd}}(\operatorname{KG}(n,2))=\begin{cases}n-1,&5\le n\le7,\\n-2,&n\ge8.\end{cases}
\]
For every \(n\ge8\), all *unlabelled* minimum colour partitions are in bijection with a three-element subset \(Q\subset[n]\) and a labelled tournament on \([n]\setminus Q\) with maximum outdegree at most \(n-6\). Thus their exact number is \(\binom n3 T(n-3,n-6)\), where \(T(m,d)\) counts tournaments on a fixed \(m\)-vertex set whose outdegrees are all at most \(d\). In particular, there are exactly \(1344\) and \(435456\) unlabelled optimal partitions at \(n=8\) and \(n=9\), respectively. With distinct names assigned to the optimum palette, multiply these figures by \((n-2)!\).

## Assumptions and scope
Vertices of \(\operatorname{KG}(n,2)\) are the two-element subsets of \([n]\); two vertices are adjacent exactly if the subsets are disjoint. A class-domination (CD) colouring is a **proper** partition of the vertex set into nonempty classes such that, for each class, some (possibly separately chosen) vertex of the Kneser graph is adjacent to **all** members of that class. This is the **class-wise dominated-colouring** convention, not dominator colouring or total dominator colouring requiring each vertex to dominate at least one colour class. The witness vertex need not be differently coloured from all graph vertices; a class-wise witness is necessarily outside its class because the graph has no loops. All claims are for \(n\ge5\); optimum partitions ignore colour-name permutations.

## Proof
**1. Class shape and witness capacity.** Regard each Kneser vertex as an edge of the complete graph on \([n]\). Properness means that edges belonging to one colour class pairwise intersect. Such a family either is contained in a star, or is the three-edge triangle on a three-element set: for three edges with no common endpoint, they form a triangle and no other edge intersects all three. A star consisting of \(r\) edges has ground-element support of size \(r+1\), hence admits a common disjoint two-set (a class witness) exactly when \(r+1\le n-2\), equivalently \(r\le n-3\). A three-edge triangle admits a common disjoint pair whenever \(n\ge5\). Every CD class therefore contains at most \(\max\{3,n-3\}\) graph vertices.

**2. Sharp small-order lower bounds.** The ordinary chromatic number of \(\operatorname{KG}(n,2)\) is \(n-2\) (standard Kneser theorem). At \(n=5,6,7\), a putative CD colouring with only \(n-2\) colour classes could cover at most \((n-2)\max\{3,n-3\}\) Kneser vertices, which evaluates to \(9,12,20\), respectively. The true vertex counts are \(\binom52=10\), \(\binom62=15\), \(\binom72=21\). Hence at least \(n-1\) CD colours are required.

**3. Sharp small-order constructions.** List classes as sets of two-symbol edges on ground elements \(1,\ldots,n\). For \(n=5\), four classes are
\[\{12,13,23\},\quad\{14,15,45\},\quad\{24,34\},\quad\{25,35\}.\]
For \(n=6\), five classes are
\[\{12,13,14\},\quad\{15,16,56\},\quad\{25,35,45\},\quad\{23,24,34\},\quad\{26,36,46\}.\]
Here a two-digit symbol denotes its corresponding two-set; the classes visibly partition all edges, are pairwise intersecting, and each uses at most three ground labels (a triangle) or is a star of at most \(n-3\) edges. For \(n=7\), divide ground elements into five centres and two remaining symbols, orient a regular tournament on the five centres, and form one class per centre from its two edges to the remaining symbols plus its two outgoing centre-centre edges. The final class is the singleton edge joining the two remaining symbols. These six classes partition all \(21\) edges; each centre star has four edges, meets the capacity \(n-3=4\), and the singleton is valid. This proves the stated \(n-1\) values.

**4. Construction at every larger order.** For \(n\ge8\), choose any triple \(Q\subset[n]\) and let \(S=[n]\setminus Q\), so \(|S|=n-3\). Assign all three edges within \(Q\) to one triangle class. For each centre \(s\in S\), assign the three edges from \(s\) into \(Q\) to the class centred at \(s\). Orient all centre-centre edges as a tournament on \(S\), placing each such edge in the class of its tail. Every centre class has exactly \(3+d^+(s)\) edges, and is CD precisely when \(d^+(s)\le n-6\). A near-regular tournament exists at every order \(m\), with maximum outdegree \(\lceil(m-1)/2\rceil\); at \(m=n-3\ge5\), this is at most \(n-6\). Thus a CD colouring with \(1+(n-3)=n-2\) colours always exists. The ordinary chromatic bound proves optimality.

**5. Uniqueness and tournament enumeration at the optimum.** The established classification of *all* ordinary optimal \((n-2)\)-colourings of \(\operatorname{KG}(n,2)\) (Behtoei--Omoomi, also explicitly restated in Zhao--Zhang--Jia--Ye, Proposition 1.4) says that each has a unique three-edge triangle class on a ground triple \(Q\), while the other \(n-3\) classes are stars at distinct centres \(s\notin Q\), must contain all their edges to \(Q\), and distribute each centre-centre edge to exactly one endpoint class. Therefore every ordinary optimum has exactly the representation in part 4. The class-wise CD condition on a centre star is exactly the bound \(3+d^+(s)\le n-3\); the triangle is always CD. The triple and tournament are uniquely recoverable from the unlabelled partition, giving the claimed bijection. Direct enumeration of the \(2^{\binom52}\) and \(2^{\binom62}\) tournaments gives \(T(5,2)=24\) and \(T(6,3)=5184\), so the first counts are \(\binom83\cdot24=1344\) and \(\binom93\cdot5184=435456\). Independent explicit-graph checking is described below.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify_constructions.py` in a directory containing the accompanying standalone source. It checks full edge coverage, properness and the existence of a common-neighbor witness for every colour class, for every integer \(5\le n\le100\), with no dependency beyond Python 3; it prints `CONSTRUCTIONS_OK`. To check both numerical tournament counts independently, compile `count_optimum.cpp` with `c++ -std=c++17 -O2 -Wall -Wextra count_optimum.cpp -o count_optimum` and run `./count_optimum`. It explicitly constructs every canonical colouring for all \(2^{10}\) tournament orientations at \(n=8\) and all \(2^{15}\) orientations at \(n=9\), checks graph edges and direct witness vertices, and prints `TOURNAMENT_CENSUS_OK`. These finite checks corroborate the proof; the universal claim rests on the structural classification and balanced-tournament existence, not on finite enumeration.

## Relationship to prior work
Zhao, Zhang, Jia and Ye (published online on 2024-02-05) explicitly pose the dominated-colouring value of \(\operatorname{KG}(n,2)\) as Open Problem 1 at the end of their *Dominator coloring of Kneser graph* article. Their main result concerns a different vertex-wise dominator parameter. Proposition 1.4 in that article reproduces the earlier optimal proper-colouring classification, which is used as a known premise of part 5 rather than claimed here. The paper *Parameterized and Exact Algorithms for Class Domination Coloring* precisely defines the class-wise invariant and develops broader algorithms but does not specify this Kneser threshold. The pre-existing published result on Kempe connectivity of optimal two-subset Kneser colourings treats their recolouring dynamics, not the CD witness-capacity restriction or this optimum census. Queries involving class domination, dominated chromatic number, Kneser aliases, star/triangle normal forms, tournaments and stronger coverage found no statement implying this formula; this is a best-knowledge originality assessment, not a proof of publication priority.

## Limitations
The result applies only to two-subset Kneser graphs and the open-neighbourhood, class-wise dominated-colouring convention. The theorem relies on the previously published canonical structural description of *all* optimum proper colourings; the original locating-colouring article was not accessed in full here, although its theorem is reproduced in the inspected 2024 primary article and independently restated in subsequent mathematical literature. Counts at \(n=8,9\) are finite enumerations with direct checking; there is no asserted simple closed form for the number of bounded-score tournaments at all orders. Earlier unindexed results or other terminology may exist. Independent mathematical review has not been performed.
