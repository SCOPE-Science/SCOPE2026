# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The theorem is stated for the nonnegative unit ball of \(\ell^1(\mathbb Z)\), with positive biased nearest-neighbor mutation and finitely supported equal mortality relief. The equilibrium summation identity forces the effective birth factor below one; this rules out spurious nonsummable spectral branches. A finite-rank Green-kernel reduction produces an if-and-only-if existence threshold, and Perron uniqueness fixes the profile. Strict entrywise matrix comparison proves the clustering optimum; a proved tridiagonal boundary equation gives the \(k^{-2}\) coefficient. The critical escape conclusion follows from the equilibrium summation identity, positivity and bounded pointwise Green kernels. A clean standalone standard-library checker independently verifies finite spectral and equilibrium calculations. No finite experiment is used as a proof for unbounded trait sets.

## Originality — PASS, with historical-priority limitations

The closest initiating publication is P. Pollett, *Mutation–selection balance on an infinite trait space* (arXiv:2609.02086v1). Its accessible abstract and introductory statement concern global deterministic approximation, bounded versus confining mortality, and a compact-operator persistence threshold; they do not record this finite-island layout optimization or its asymptotic coefficient. Complete primary full-text inspection remained inaccessible and therefore is an acknowledged residual risk, not a claim of documented absence. The classical Birman–Schwinger principle supplies the finite-rank mechanism, and the Kac–Murdock–Szegö matrix and its inverse are established linear algebra (Higham, 2021). Neither fact alone supplies the derived biological if-and-only-if threshold together with strict optimal arrangement, growing-block asymptotic and critical non-tightness. Mailler–Schapira (2026) considers stochastic catalytic branching random walks, not this density-regulated deterministic finite-island family. A source-level originality check is best-knowledge only; unindexed treatments of the identical specialization could dominate.

## Value — PASS

The question is motivated before computation: can finitely many low-mortality phenotypes overcome persistent mutational drift, and how should those phenotypes be located? The theorem provides a complete answer for every finite equal-quality favorable set, a sharp global impossibility boundary despite unlimited births, a strict optimal-clustering theorem, a quantitative finite-size penalty and a simple two-site ecological rescue mechanism absent from isolated sites. The mass-escape result separates demographic occupancy from local adaptation and shows why an apparently robust population can lack a stationary trait profile at criticality. This is a structural population-theoretic question, not an arbitrary numerical slice.
