In a density-regulated infinite-trait population with directional nearest-neighbor mutation, a finite set of favorable traits supports a localized positive equilibrium only below an explicit pattern-dependent mortality threshold. Among \(k\) favorable traits, consecutive placement uniquely maximizes this threshold, which approaches \(2\sqrt{pq}\) with the sharp correction \(\pi^2\sqrt{pq}/k^2\); at the critical boundary, equilibrium mass escapes to higher traits while total mass stays positive.
