**Correctness (C): PASS.** The assumed conditional Bernoulli independence yields an exact integrable union probability. Equation (1) is obtained by elementary integration on the three ranges cut out by the two sensors. Scientific limitation: these finite assertions do not establish any continuum or asymptotic claim and the detector model itself is idealized.

**Originality (O): PASS, provisional.** The most relevant exact published discrete-path trap theorem assumes certain on-site detection, distinct detector sites and integer positions; its fixed-range thirds and long-range quarters are credited, but its statements cannot yield the present \(p\)-dependent co-location boundary, free continuous centering proof or noncommuting perfect-response limit. A related unpublished planar Gaussian/half-normal model already has a different co-location phase; it does not imply this compact-uniform, exponential-absolute-distance result, nor optimize all pairs. The 2011 correlated-signal sensor study optimizes fusion schemes; the 2011 fixed-footprint study uses a piecewise constant distance response; the 2014 field study simulates parameter-estimation efficiency for a half-normal kernel, and the 2014 unreliable-sensor paper minimizes worst-case distance rather than encounter probability. Their actual mathematical objectives do not dominate the present claim. Searches for aliases, equivalent probability-of-union formulations and stronger sensor-placement results found no exact covering theorem in the inspected sources. Risk: broader facility-location or probabilistic-coverage papers not located may contain an equivalent exact result, and no independent exhaustive historical search exists.

**Value (V): PASS.** The motivation is concrete: under a fixed two-device wildlife-observation budget, maximizing the chance to record at least one uniformly located animal may favor redundant co-located detectors when their on-site response is imperfect. The exact boundary \(p=e^{-\lambda L/2}\) separates two qualitatively different designs, gives a unique free-placement solution at every parameter value, and reveals a singular perfect-detection limit and a linear (rather than square-root) onset. These are operationally interpretable, nontrivial structural benchmarks; neither a renamed formula nor an arbitrary finite parameter slice. No claim of species-specific suitability or field calibration is made.

## Closest literature and residual limits

Michaelides and Panayiotou (2011, DOI:10.1186/1687-6180-2011-11) study correlated-signal sensor fusion; Sun, Fuller and Royle (2014, DOI:10.1371/journal.pone.0088025) study wildlife trap-array estimation precision; Frasca and collaborators (2014, arXiv:1404.7711) study expected worst-case proximity under complete sensor failures. Litvak and collaborators (Section 5.2.1 of their surveillance study) derive a uniform-target no-overlap law for fixed-radius sensor footprints, which is not applicable to this smooth, infinite-support response. The published 2026 discrete-trap theorem shares the union-detection objective and some asymptotic geometry but excludes co-location and \(p<1\) at the trap center. A related prior unpublished Gaussian-half-normal calculation shares the high-level phase phenomenon but not the model, sharp boundary or global free-centering proof. The model ignores correlated failures, false positives, installation constraints, animal movement and parameter-estimation objectives. Historical novelty is provisional; the review is not independent.
