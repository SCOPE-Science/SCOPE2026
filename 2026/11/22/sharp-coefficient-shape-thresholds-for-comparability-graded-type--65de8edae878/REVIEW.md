# Scientific review: comparability-graded singleton types

## Correctness (PASS)
Every nonalgebraic singleton type in the generic homogeneous poset is uniquely represented by a compatible ideal/filter pair. For disjoint unions of chains, pure pairs give twice the product of geometric sums minus one duplicate, while mixed pairs reside in one chain and contribute \(j-1\) at grade \(j\). This establishes the generating function for all sizes. The two-chain coefficient formula is derived by a three-region convolution and gives a complete symbolic shape classification, including all boundary cases. A complete integer-partition enumeration at sizes up to eight separately verifies sharp global first thresholds, and a direct ternary-relation enumerator agrees on each finite case. Infinite persistence is established symbolically. The verification script is included and produces `VERIFY_OK`.

## Originality (PASS, with residual priority risk)
The finite-base ideal/filter description and ungraded lower bounds are credited. Full-text comparison with the 2026 published incomparability-sensitive result shows that its statement concerns ungraded scalar counts, with no claim about the grade distribution or coefficient shape. Cameron–Nešetřil and Pach et al. provide the generic-poset setting, and Kwiatkowska–Sullivan–Winkel (2025) develop finite valid triples for extension types while proving an embedding theorem; none gives the graded coefficient-shape classification. Knauer and coauthors' Theorem 4.9, which cites Simion's 1984 real-rooted descent theorem for disjoint chains, concerns linear-extension descents and Ehrhart \(h^*\) coefficients, not the extension-type comparability statistic (indeed the two shapes behave differently). Searches of indexed published findings and primary papers located no result implying the new shape thresholds. Unindexed equivalent results and the original 1984 full text remain residual risks.

## Value (PASS)
The result isolates the exact first regularity failures of a natural model-theoretic invariant, gives a complete infinite classification for two-chain parameters, proves minimal unique counterexamples in all disjoint-chain profiles, and supplies persistent witnesses at every larger size. It shows that simple ideal/filter counting does not preserve familiar regularity properties of classical descent enumerators. Neither merely restating ungraded type counts nor finite testing alone would give the structural dichotomy.

## Limitations
The complete two-chain iff statements are not asserted for three or more chains. Exhaustive calculations prove first thresholds only within the indicated finite domain, while separate symbolic formulas prove all-size two-chain classification and persistence. No independent mathematical or historical-priority certification is asserted.
