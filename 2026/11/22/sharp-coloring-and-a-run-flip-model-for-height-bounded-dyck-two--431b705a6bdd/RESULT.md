# Sharp coloring and a run-flip model for height-bounded Dyck two-read deletion graphs

## Finding

Let \(G_n^{(H)}\) have as vertices the binary Dyck words of semilength \(n\ge1\) and height at most \(H\ge1\). An edge means at least two **distinct** common single-deletion descendants. Write \(m=n-1\), let \(D_m\) be the same two-read deletion graph on **all** binary words of length \(m\), and let \(Q_m\) be the \(m\)-dimensional cube on the same words. Then
\[
G_n^{(2)}\cong D_m\cup Q_m
\]
as graphs on \(2^m\) vertices, with disjoint edge sets on the right. The explicit isomorphism encodes the Dyck word as \(1B_{b_1}\cdots B_{b_m}0\) with \(B_0=10\), \(B_1=01\), then sends \(b\) to \(x_i=b_i\oplus((i-1)\bmod 2)\). Before the mask, the graph move is a flip of one nonempty constant interval in \(b\). In particular, the local neighborhoods of the constrained Dyck language can be obtained from an unrestricted binary ambiguity graph by adding exactly the single-bit changes, not by replacing the deletion criterion with Hamming distance.

## Assumptions and scope

The symbols \(1,0\) are up- and down-steps of a path starting at height zero, never negative, and ending at height zero. The ceiling is inclusive. Deletions are considered as sets of words, not multisets of deletion locations. At \(m=0\) both graphs on the right have one vertex and no edges. The chromatic results below concern partitions into codebooks, not the largest individual codebook.

## Proof and consequences

By the published Type-A criterion of Cai–Kiah–Nguyen–Yaakobi, two distinct words have two common distinct single-deletion descendants exactly when the first-to-last difference interval (of length at least two) consists of opposite phases of a binary alternating word in the two sources. For height at most two, the first and last symbols are \(1,0\); every odd prefix length has height one by parity. Thus the \(m\) pairs between the endpoints start and end at height one, with intermediate height either zero or two. They are precisely the pairs \(10\) and \(01\), in arbitrary order. This gives the asserted bijection before applying the mask.

Every changed bit of \(b\) changes both positions of the corresponding pair. Hence the first-to-last difference interval in the Dyck words consists of complete pairs. They are opposite alternating phases throughout that interval if and only if all indices in the bit interval change and the first source has identical pair orientation throughout. This proves the constant-interval rule. The alternating mask converts a constant interval into an alternating interval, and commutes with flipping. For intervals of length at least two the resulting moves on \(x\) are exactly the edges of \(D_m\) by the Type-A criterion. A singleton interval instead flips one bit and lies in \(Q_m\), never in \(D_m\). This proves the graph identification.

**Run statistics.** If \(b\) has maximal constant runs of lengths \(r_1,\ldots,r_s\), its degree is \(\sum_i\binom{r_i+1}{2}\), since each nonempty subinterval of a run gives one distinct neighbor. Equivalently, the one-runs of the adjacent-bit XOR of the masked word have positive lengths \(r_i-1\), yielding the known unrestricted degree \(\sum_i\binom{r_i}{2}\); adding the cube degree \(m\) gives the same answer. The maximum is \(\binom n2\), at the two constant vectors for \(n\ge2\) (splitting a run into positive pieces strictly reduces the triangular sum). For an interval of length \(\ell\), the location and the unchanged exterior determine one unordered edge, so
\[
|E(G_n^{(2)})|=\sum_{\ell=1}^{n-1}(n-\ell)2^{n-1-\ell}=(n-2)2^{n-1}+1.
\]
For \(n=1\) this is zero. For \(n\ge2\) it is also the sum of the established length-\(m\) binary deletion-graph edge count and the cube edge count.

**Codebook partitions and an induced odd hole.** Trimming the common first and last symbols preserves adjacency by the Type-A criterion, and embeds any of the bounded-height Dyck graphs as an induced subgraph of the length-\(2m\), weight-\(m\) binary layer. Its known checksum coloring uses \(m+1=n\) colors (position sum modulo \(n\)); trimming changes the position sum by the same constant for all words. Its standard clique \((10)^j(01)^{m-j}\), \(0\le j\le m\), lies inside the trimmed height-two source. Hence \(\chi=\omega=n\) for \(H\ge2\), with the singleton case \(n=1\) immediate; height one has just \((10)^n\). At height two the first step of each \(01\)-pair visits zero, and the final step accounts for one further return. Thus the component count is \(1+|b|_1\), a proper coloring because a constant-interval flip changes the weight. Its classes have sizes \(\binom{n-1}{j-1}\). For \(n\ge5\), the vectors \(1111,1101,0101,0100,0000\), padded with the same \(n-5\) final zeros, induce a five-cycle: consecutive pairs differ by one constant-interval flip; for other pairs the differing positions are nonconsecutive or their interval is not constant. The height-two source is an induced subgraph of every higher-ceiling graph, so these graphs are not perfect for \(n\ge5\).

## Relationship to prior work

The alternating-interval characterization is due to [Cai et al., Definition 8 and Proposition 9](https://arxiv.org/html/2001.01376v2). The [fixed-composition coloring result](https://github.com/Resultary/2026/blob/main/2026/11/22/SCOPE-sharp-fixed-composition-coloring-of-the-two-read-deletion-graph--1e0b06ffdce5/RESULT.md) supplies the checksum, the clique inherited after trimming, and a five-cycle whose length-eight words already consist of \(10\)/\(01\) pairs. The shared catalogue date does not resolve priority. The [unrestricted binary degree law](https://github.com/Resultary/2026/blob/main/2026/11/2/SCOPE-complete-degree-spectrum-of-the-binary-two-read-deletion-reconst--f45a481280b1/RESULT.md) and the [unrestricted edge formula](https://github.com/Resultary/2026/blob/main/2026/10/4/SCOPE-exact-edge-count-of-the-finite-alphabet-two-read-single-deletion--737fed325e91/RESULT.md) yield the height-two run statistics via the graph identification. The [unrestricted-height Dyck graph's edge bijection and sharp degree](https://github.com/Resultary/2026/blob/main/2026/11/8/SCOPE-dyck-restricted-two-read-deletion-graph-a-vertex-marked-bijectio--3635f93f657f/RESULT.md) concern the full Dyck language rather than the cube-augmented binary model.

The distinct contribution is the height-two graph identification. The coloring, degree and edge counts, and odd hole are presented as applications or direct checks, not as separate originality claims. The model does not classify larger-height Dyck graphs or the size of a largest codebook. The frozen `verify_decks.py` and `verify_structure.py` are finite corroboration of the language and its constant-interval graph; the all-parameter isomorphism follows from the proof.
