# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The normalized kernel is an explicit rational function, and complete Pick is equivalent to coefficientwise positivity of its reciprocal defect. The proof reconstructs the initial coefficients, the full recurrence, both discriminant regimes and the first negative sign. It separately establishes the analytic radius and nonvanishing in the admissible region, handles the double root and the \(a=1\) endpoint, and derives the critical asymptotic by the discriminant expansion. Exact-rational computations are supplementary, not substitutes for the all-parameter proof.

**Originality — PASS (qualified).** Luo and Rydhe prove concave-shift sufficiency, not a necessary-and-sufficient two-defect classification; Quiggin provides a sufficient monotone-weight criterion. The prior general reciprocal-kernel criterion and related de Branges–Rovnyak and monomial-defect constructions do not explicitly determine the not expansive interval \(4a/(a+1)^2\le b<1\), the sharp boundary, or the first-negative-index asymptotic. A published monomial-defect result was read in full and concerns a different perturbation form. Existing examples of expansive rank-two nonconcave complete-Pick shifts do not imply a criterion for the not expansive branch. Residual risk: a preexisting general recurrence-positivity result could subsume this exact quadratic specialization even without matching terminology.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** This is a natural smallest independently variable two-bond perturbation of the Hardy shift. The complete parameter classification distinguishes kernel interpolation from operator concavity and expansivity, and the exact first-negative-index law explains why any fixed-order coefficient test becomes arbitrarily misleading close to the transition. This is more than a finite coefficient calculation or routine sufficient example.

The result does not classify all finite-rank Hardy perturbations, nor assert independent checking or worldwide priority.
