For Newton's method applied to \(\sin z\), the numerically proposed radius \(R\) satisfying \(\tan R=2R\) is rigorously the largest centered complex convergence-disk radius; only the two real points \(\pm R\) fail to converge on its boundary. Every finite-time maximal modulus on a smaller disk is exactly obtained by iterating the real scalar map \(t\mapsto\tan t-t\), yielding a sharp worst-case convergence law.
