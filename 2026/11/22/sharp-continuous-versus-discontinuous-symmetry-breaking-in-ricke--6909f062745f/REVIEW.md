# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

The theorem applies to all integers \(m\geq2\) and all positive dimensionless stocks. Compactness supplies a maximum. Shifting stock into an empty habitat excludes boundary maxima. The derivative equality and Hessian distinguish equal configurations from exactly one overfull habitat and \(m-1\) equal lower habitats. Every admissible one-overfull candidate is parametrized by its large-coordinate fraction; strict convexity of the ratio \(G_{m,p}\) gives one positive stock crossing. Reparameterization yields the exact infimum \(s_m\). The small-parameter expansion forces attainment and a first-order transition for \(m\geq3\); hyperbolic monotonicity proves the two-habitat continuous case. Derivative matching gives the large-stock limit. The numerical checker is supplementary only; finite diagnostics cannot certify infinitely many cases.

## Originality — PASS on inspected evidence

The 2022 spawner study uses Ricker recruitment and predetermined patch-selection strategies to quantify assessment bias; it does not state the exact global optimization threshold or classify all identical habitat counts. The 2018 salmon nesting study treats aggregation and recruitment empirically, with non-Ricker main aggregation analysis. More general published ecological selection and Ricker dynamical studies may contain implicit special cases; this literature comparison is not a comprehensive priority certification. The new statement distinguishes a continuous two-site transition from a discontinuous all-higher-site transition and gives an exact scalar threshold, not just a Ricker substitution or a familiar concavity test. One older related Ricker habitat-selection paper could not be fully inspected; this is a residual historical-priority risk, not evidence of novelty.

## Value — PASS

Overcompensating density-dependent recruitment makes equal individual survival incentives potentially incompatible with maximum aggregate offspring. A complete classification of when unequal allocation is globally more productive, with the sharp minimum habitat count for a discontinuous switch and the divergent high-stock performance ratio, supplies a natural baseline for mathematical population ecology. The exact result is not a claim about observed behavior, optimal conservation policy or empirical fit.

## Closest literature and limits

Skoglund et al. (2022), DOI:10.1002/ece3.9679, is the closest model and motivating source; Bouchard et al. (2018), DOI:10.1002/ecs2.2178, examines empirical spatial aggregation; Brännström and Sumpter (2005), DOI:10.1098/rspb.2005.3185, treats population competition and clustering. The 1998 sink-habitat Ricker study has only partial material available. The theorem assumes identical deterministic isolated habitats and does not settle nonlinear multi-year population trajectories or heterogeneity.
