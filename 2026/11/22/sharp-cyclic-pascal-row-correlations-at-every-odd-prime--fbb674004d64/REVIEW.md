# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
Lucas and Fine give the exact digit-product representation. The proof partitions every cyclic shifted pair into digit cases, obtains the two-coefficient recurrence without truncation, and deduces convergence by strong induction on fixed positive shifts. The two polynomial identities establish the strict extremal bound and all equality cases. The exact checker reproduces 10,456 assertions including finite full cyclic sums, all fixed-prime coefficient identities, and one thousand rational limiting lags at each of five odd primes. The infinite result rests on the explicit proof, not on those tests.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
The complete 2010 Rowland paper and the 2016 Spiegelhofer–Wallner paper concern individual Pascal-row divisibility statistics, not two-row cyclic correlations. A directly relevant published binary-only result determines all-shift correlations for base two and the bound \(2/3\); the present theorem is restricted to odd primes and proves a nonconstant sharp prime-dependent bound with equality characterization. published-finding corpus searches for shifted Pascal correlations at odd primes and for equivalent digit-product extremal bounds recovered the binary result and related distinct objects, but no stronger odd-prime implication. General weighted digit-correlation literature remains a concrete historical-priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
For any odd prime, the number of binomial entries surviving modulo that prime is a natural row statistic, and dependence between separated rows is not determined by its single-row Fine formula. The exact all-shift maximum, its complete equality classification, the evaluated prime-dependent constant and the full rational shift recursion answer a motivated joint-distribution question, extending beyond a routine single-lag count.

## Closest literature and limits
Fine (1947), Rowland (arXiv:1001.1783), Spiegelhofer–Wallner (arXiv:1604.07089), and the published binary-only all-shift correlation result are the main comparisons. The theorem is for fixed cyclic lags and odd primes only. No claim of independent mathematical certification, historical uniqueness, or full coverage of arbitrary weighted digit autocorrelations is made.
