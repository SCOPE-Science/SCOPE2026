# Sharp dependence-robust equilibrium and optimal shares in a two-destination congestion game

## Finding
For fixed \(a>0\), suppose two marginal valuation distributions are known to be uniform on \([0,1]\), while their dependence is unknown. In a nonatomic two-destination congestion game with payoffs \(U-a x\) and \(V-a(1-x)\), the **entire** identified sets of first-destination equilibrium shares and socially optimal shares, over all strictly positive smooth copula densities, are respectively
\[
  \mathcal E_a=\left(\frac{a}{1+2a},\frac{1+a}{1+2a}\right),
  \qquad
  \mathcal P_a=\left(\frac{2a}{1+4a},\frac{1+2a}{1+4a}\right).
\]
Neither endpoint is attained. Every interior point of each set is attained by a strictly positive smooth copula density with the stipulated uniform marginals. For any **single** such density, if its equilibrium share is \(x\) and its planner share is \(y\), then either \(x=y=1/2\), or \(y\) is strictly between \(x\) and \(1/2\). In particular, the planner always chooses a more balanced aggregate destination allocation than individually optimal congested choice, without knowing the valuation dependence structure.

## Assumptions and scope
There is a nonatomic population of mass one. Each individual has valuations \((U,V)\in[0,1]^2\); the joint law has a \(C^\infty\) density \(f\) that is strictly positive on the square and both marginal laws are uniform. Such a density is a smooth strictly positive copula. A share \(z\in[0,1]\) assigned to destination one incurs congestion cost \(a z\) per individual there, and the other destination incurs cost \(a(1-z)\) per individual, for fixed \(a>0\). Equilibrium agents compare private payoffs; the planner maximizes expected intrinsic value of the assignment minus total congestion costs. Ties occur with probability zero. Social welfare is utilitarian; no transfers, capacity constraints, or individual utility weights are included. This is a two-destination specialization of the heterogeneous-valuation game studied by Su, Mehr and Sastry [1].

Define the valuation difference \(D=U-V\) and the survival function \(Q(t)=\Pr(D>t)\). For a positive smooth density \(Q\) is continuous and strictly decreasing on \((-1,1)\), and equals one for \(t\le -1\) and zero for \(t\ge1\). For any \(b>0\), write \(z_b\) for the unique zero in \((0,1)\) of \(z-Q(b(2z-1))\). The result concerns \(x=z_a\) and \(y=z_{2a}\).

## Proof
**1. Unique equilibrium and optimum.** Given population share \(z\), an agent selects the first destination precisely when \(D>a(2z-1)\), so consistency gives \(z=Q(a(2z-1))\). Its right-hand side is continuous and nonincreasing in \(z\), while the left-hand side is strictly increasing; their difference has opposite signs at \(0\) and \(1\). Consequently there is exactly one equilibrium, \(x=z_a\). In a social assignment with mass \(z\) choosing one, sorting by decreasing \(D\) maximizes intrinsic valuation. The social congestion bill is \(a[z^2+(1-z)^2]\), whose derivative with respect to \(z\) is \(2a(2z-1)\). Since \(D\) is atomless, the marginal sorted valuation difference is strictly decreasing, so the welfare objective is strictly concave. Its unique maximizer \(y\) satisfies \(y=Q(2a(2y-1))\), i.e. \(y=z_{2a}\). This argument remains valid when the maximizing share is found by threshold sorting; no unspecified stochastic assignment can improve on sorted intrinsic valuation at a fixed share.

**2. Distribution-free sharp outer bounds.** For \(0\le t\le1\), the event \(U-V>t\) is contained in \(U>t\), so \(Q(t)\le1-t\). For \(0\le s\le1\), its complement at \(-s\) is contained in \(V\ge s\), so \(Q(-s)\ge s\). At strictly interior thresholds the inequalities are strict for a positive density, because each excluded complementary open triangle in the unit square has positive mass. Let \(z=z_b\). If \(z\ge1/2\), set \(t=b(2z-1)\). The fixed-point relation forces \(t<1\); hence \(z=Q(t)<1-t\) if \(t>0\), and rearranging gives \(z<(1+b)/(1+2b)\). If \(z<1/2\), take \(s=b(1-2z)\), obtain \(z=Q(-s)>s\), and deduce \(z>b/(1+2b)\). For \(z=1/2\) both bounds are automatic. Therefore for every \(b>0\),
\[
\frac{b}{1+2b}<z_b<\frac{1+b}{1+2b}.
\]
Using \(b=a\) and \(b=2a\) proves the stated outer intervals.

**3. Every interior share is achieved by a positive smooth copula.** Fix \(b>0\) and a desired share \(z\) strictly between the bounds in step 2. It suffices to consider \(z>1/2\) by interchanging valuations. Select two distinct shares \(z_-<z<z_+\), both inside the upper-half interval, and put \(\delta_\pm=1-z_\pm\). Let \(W\) be uniform on \([0,1)\) and define the periodic rotation coupling \(U=W\), \(V=(W-\delta_\pm)\bmod1\). Its two marginal distributions are uniform, and \(D\) takes values \(\delta_\pm\) with probability \(1-\delta_\pm=z_\pm\), and \(\delta_\pm-1\) otherwise. At the chosen common threshold \(t=b(2z-1)\), the strict relations \(\delta_\pm-1<t<\delta_\pm\) hold when \(z_-\) and \(z_+\) are chosen sufficiently close to \(z\), because \(z\) lies strictly inside the upper-half interval. Hence the two rotation couplings satisfy \(Q_-(t)=z_-<z<z_+=Q_+(t)\).

Replace each rotation by the smooth positive circular-noise coupling \(U=W\), \(V=(W-\delta_\pm+Z_\varepsilon)\bmod1\), where \(Z_\varepsilon\) is independent and has a strictly positive smooth wrapped circular density concentrating at zero. The joint density is strictly positive and smooth on the square; both marginals remain uniform. Since neither limiting rotation puts mass at \(t\), the strict inequalities \(Q_-^\varepsilon(t)<z<Q_+^\varepsilon(t)\) persist for sufficiently small positive \(\varepsilon\). An appropriate convex mixture of these two joint densities has exactly \(Q(t)=z\), still with uniform marginals, smoothness and strict positivity. Its unique equilibrium for feedback coefficient \(b\) is exactly \(z\). Interchanging \(U,V\) handles the lower-half interval, and independent uniform valuations attain \(z=1/2\). Thus the complete attainable set for \(z_b\) is the open interval in step 2, and both endpoints are limits of admissible densities as desired shares tend toward the endpoints.

**4. Planner allocation is strictly more balanced.** If \(x=z_a>1/2\), then \(Q(0)>1/2\). Thus \(y=z_{2a}>1/2\). At \(z=x\) the planner equation's right-hand side is \(Q(2a(2x-1))<Q(a(2x-1))=x\): the inequality is strict because \(a(2x-1)\in(0,1)\), and either the larger threshold is below one, where \(Q\) is strictly decreasing, or it is at least one, where \(Q\) vanishes. Uniqueness and monotonicity of the fixed-point equation then imply \(y<x\). If \(x<1/2\), the argument with reversed signs gives \(x<y<1/2\). If \(x=1/2\), then \(Q(0)=1/2\), so \(y=1/2\) as well. This proves the within-copula welfare comparison.

## Verification
The proof depends only on uniform-marginal tail inclusions, the strictly decreasing scalar equilibrium condition, the planner's marginal congestion derivative and the explicit smoothed rotation construction. The supplied standalone \(\texttt{verify.py}\) numerically checks hundreds of smooth positive discrete approximations, the claimed bounds for feedback coefficients across several orders of magnitude, the planner-versus-equilibrium ordering, and the exact inequalities underlying the rotation construction. These finite checks support implementation but do not establish statements for all positive smooth copulas; steps 1–4 above are the mathematical proof.

For example, when \(a=1\), the equilibrium share can be any value in \((1/3,2/3)\) and the socially optimal share independently ranges across \((2/5,3/5)\). These are separate marginal identified sets: they do not assert that every pair from their Cartesian product can be attained with one common joint density.

## Relationship to prior work
Su, Mehr and Sastry [1] introduce the heterogeneous-valuation nonatomic congestion game and characterize equilibria and social optima by aggregate-flow thresholds and optimal transport. The present result conditions on **known marginal valuations but unknown cross-destination dependence** and exactly determines the set of possible endogenous equilibrium and planner aggregate shares. Bhattacharya and Makiranta [2] prove equilibrium uniqueness for a broad class of negative-externality discrete-choice models with a **given joint preference law** and examine empirical identification; the present identification experiment observes only two uniform marginals rather than the joint distribution or choice microdata. Makarov [3] and the analysis of Zhang and Richardson [4] give dependence-free sharp distributional bounds for a sum or difference with fixed marginals. The one-sided uniform tail estimates in step 2 are special simple instances of such copula inequalities; their use is not claimed as new. The distinct contribution is the simultaneous sharp **endogenous fixed-point identified set**, **exact attainment by positive smooth copulas**, and **planner-versus-equilibrium balancing** for this natural congestion specialization.

## Limitations
The results assume identical standard-uniform valuation marginals, positive smooth joint density, two destinations, symmetric linear congestion, and a nonatomic unit-mass population. Different marginals, nonlinear congestion, multiple routes, capacity constraints, or discrete players require separate treatment. Interval endpoints are limits and are **not** attainable under the strictly positive density hypothesis. Optimal and equilibrium intervals were characterized separately; the exact jointly attainable region of the pair \((x,y)\) was not classified. No finite-population, statistical confidence, or empirical causal claim is proved. The novelty assessment is bounded by the inspected literature; unindexed equivalent prior work cannot be ruled out.
