# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

For strictly descending positive real parameters, the known vertex-orbit description is an explicit input. A nonnegative coordinatewise distance inequality bounds every vertex-pair squared distance by the sum of the largest \(n\) entries of a doubled square-weight multiset. Complementary balanced-support vertices attain equality and strictness classifies all cases. Strong convexity and permutation symmetry force a unique scalar ball center. Exact vertex norms give a finite rank maximum, and monotone rank increments prove that the contact ranks are one or two consecutive integers. All three discount regimes follow from geometric sums, exact rank sign conditions, and Stirling/Riemann-sum asymptotics. The included checker passed 12 exhaustive small cases (165,555 vertex pairs), 41,644 rank pairs, 495 generic rational profiles and eight larger asymptotic tests. Those finite tests supplement, rather than replace, the proof.

## Originality — PASS (priority provisional)

The closest primary work, Joswig–Klimm–Spitz (2021), gives the SIM-body vertex presentation, facets and generalized-permutahedron structure but not the complete ordinary Euclidean diameter/enclosing-ball/contact-entropy classification in the inspected body of the paper. Doker's earlier Q-polytope presentation and the original SIM auction literature focus on volume, faces or auction criteria. The related partial-permutohedron classification, indexed as *Exact circumball and contact-rank transitions of partial permutohedra*, applies to a special parameter family, and its available abstract does not imply the arbitrary-weight formula or the exponentially discounted regimes. An indexed summary is not full-text evidence of noncoverage; its inaccessible body and unindexed broader treatments remain priority risks. The mathematical claim is conditional on these known antecedents, and does not reuse their known vertex formulas as an originality claim.

## Value — PASS

This is a natural geometric invariant of an established \(n\)-parameter convex family, not an arbitrary finite slice. The exact sphere and full contact locus distinguish how Euclidean geometry responds to weight deformations even though combinatorial type is fixed. The sharp balanced-support diameter classification and the three different asymptotic rates of logarithmic contact count make this a complete, reproducible and potentially useful metric baseline for SIM-bodies and other polymatroid polytopes. The diameter argument in isolation is elementary; value rests primarily on the complete combined contact classification and the nontrivial scale transition.
