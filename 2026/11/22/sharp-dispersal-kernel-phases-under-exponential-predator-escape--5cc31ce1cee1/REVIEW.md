# Scientific assessment: mean-constrained seed dispersal

## Correctness — PASS

The exact domain is all Borel laws on the nonnegative half-line with one finite prescribed mean; the objective is expected value of \(f(r)=\exp(-cr-\beta e^{-r})\) for \(c,\beta>0\). Differentiating the objective yields the unique pointwise peak (or the endpoint peak), and the explicit critical curvature root \(B(c)=c+1/2+\sqrt{c+1/4}\). In the high-exposure regime the tangency function \(N(r)=rf'(r)-f(r)+f(0)\) rises and then falls, crossing zero exactly once between its inflection and peak distances. A concave majorant built from the origin chord, the strictly concave piece, and a plateau is valid globally. Jensen's inequality bounds every probability law, the stated atoms or point masses achieve it where attainable, and unique contact/equality conditions force the claimed supports. For excessive prescribed mean the explicitly constructed rare-long-distance sequence approaches the peak but no admissible law attains it. These are exact analytic statements for all allowed parameters, not numerical extrapolations. The standalone checker exercises curvature, tangent, envelope, two-point comparisons, limiting sequences and a parameter example.

Residual scientific limits: independence and exogeneity of survival mechanisms, unlimited admissible travel distances, and an exact first-moment budget. A bounded travel range or alternative predation interaction changes the optimization.

## Originality — PASS, with historical-priority risk

The evaluated claim is the entire explicit threshold/attainment/support classification for **this specified survival function under an exact mean-distance constraint**. Dimorphic dispersal is not a new biological observation, and the concave-envelope/Jensen method is classical. The 2002 open-access full text by Rousset and Gandon uses freely evolving distance kernels on a kin-competition lattice, rather than prescribing their first moment and maximizing a fixed exogenous establishment curve. The 2004 full text by Nathan and Casagrandi explicitly recognizes seed-dispersal dimorphism and uses a feedback-sensitive predation/recruitment model, not the claimed fixed-mean envelope. The 2003 and 2014/2015 full-text studies treat spatially structured evolutionary dispersal and maternal investment. Ezoe's 1998 and Harada's 1999 abstracts identify a fixed Gaussian/seed-size tradeoff and a lattice two-mode evolutionarily stable allocation, respectively; their inaccessible full texts remain priority risks, not evidence of noncoverage. Indexed mathematical claim searches and the accessible historical claim ledger found no exact or dominating statement. No search alone establishes priority or independence.

## Value — PASS

The natural object is a seed-distance distribution in a simple Janzen–Connell-type escape-versus-transport-mortality benchmark. Precisely fixing mean travel distance makes the mathematical question substantive: the response may be concave, convex–concave, or strictly decreasing, and optimizing over every possible dispersal kernel changes both the optimal **support** and **attainment**. The explicit sharp exposure threshold separates mandatory deterministic dispersal from uniquely optimal non-disperser/positive-distance dimorphism; beyond a second mean threshold, no optimum exists without tail constraints. This identifies exactly when mean distance alone is insufficient to define a realizable ecological optimum. The result is a complete natural classification, not a small finite table or a claim about actual species.
