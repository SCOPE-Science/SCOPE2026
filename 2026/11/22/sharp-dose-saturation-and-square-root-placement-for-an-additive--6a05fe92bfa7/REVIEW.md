Their unique crossing proves global optimality; postpeak formula follows by differentiation. The dose boundary follows from the fully capped branch and the weak-dose displacement from the curvature identity \(g''(t_c)=-abM\). Physical and zero-dose boundaries are explicit. `CHECK.py` replay passed 48,022 diagnostic assertions, but finite numerics cannot prove all-parameter claims.

**Originality — PASS under inspected evidence, priority provisional.** published-finding corpus search for additive river aeration, dose caps, equal-deficit minimax placement, and equivalences returned the fractional-aerator finite-reach finding as closest. Its full text assumes fixed **fraction** and explicitly excludes site-dependent effectiveness, while the present fixed **absolute dose** has position-dependent fraction and a different dose threshold. Rinaldi and Soncini-Sessa (1977), in accessible material, treat variable in-stream aerator cost subject to oxygen standards and develop general placement principles, not the explicit sharp fixed-capped-dose unbounded minimax theorem. The Streeter–Phelps report establishes the sag curve, and a 1971 EPA methodology considers oxygen-deficit standards and aeration sites, without the new threshold. Koivo–Phillips (1975) and general nonlinear impulse optimization remain real priority risks, not asserted absent.

**Value — PASS.** Absolute aerator capacities rather than proportional fractional removal make the physical saturation cap operative. The result gives a complete two-regime placement and cost-of-overprovision diagnostic, plus the non-obvious square-root sensitivity that warns against linear placement adjustment near zero dose. It separates dose efficiency from location efficiency in the classical river oxygen-sag baseline. It is not an empirical engineering prescription.

**Residual limits.** No independently certified priority determination; one title-close primary older work unavailable in full. The fixed-dose model does not include variable flow, multiple aerators, or finite reach, and the small-dose expansion cannot be applied at strictly zero dose.
