# Sharp dual-window norm and support transition for the box Gabor frame

## Finding
Let \(g=\mathbf 1_{[-1,1]}\), with time-translation step \(1\) and frequency-modulation step \(\beta\) strictly between \(2/3\) and \(3/4\). Among all Bessel Gabor windows \(h\) whose Gabor systems are dual to the system generated by \(g\), and with \(h\in L^1(\mathbb R)\), the exact minimum is
\[
\inf_h\|h\|_{L^1(\mathbb R)}=4\beta-2.
\]
Independently, among all dual Gabor windows, the minimum Lebesgue measure of the essential nonzero set is
\[
\inf_h\big|\{y\in\mathbb R:h(y)\ne0\}\big|=4-\frac2\beta.
\]
Both infima are attained by the same explicitly described bounded compactly supported windows. Every \(L^1\)-minimizing dual is classified below, including its exact support measure; the minimum-support duals are exactly the binary-valued members of that class.

Put \(\delta=\beta^{-1}-1\), so \(1/3<\delta<1/2\). For almost every \(x\in[-1,0)\), write \(h_k(x)=h(x+k)\). Every \(L^1\)-minimizer is, and conversely every such choice defines, a window with these fibers:
\[
\begin{array}{c|l}
\text{fiber interval}&\text{only potentially nonzero values}\\\hline
[-1,\delta-1)&h_1=\beta\\
[\delta-1,-\delta)&h_{-1}=-u,\quad h_0=u,\quad h_1=\beta-u,\quad h_2=u-\beta\\
[-\delta,0)&h_0=\beta
\end{array}
\]
Here \(u:[\delta-1,-\delta)\to[0,\beta]\) is any measurable function. In particular the choice \(u=\beta\) gives the explicit two-block window
\[
h_*(y)=\beta\mathbf1_{[\delta-1,\delta)}(y)
-\beta\mathbf1_{[\delta-2,-1-\delta)}(y).
\]
For a general minimizer from the displayed family its support measure equals
\[
4-\frac2\beta+2\big|\{x\in[\delta-1,-\delta):0<u(x)<\beta\}\big|.
\]
Consequently all, and only, simultaneous \(L^1\)- and support-minimizers have \(u(x)\in\{0,\beta\}\) almost everywhere. The same binary condition classifies all global support-minimizers, whether or not they are initially assumed to minimize \(L^1\).

## Assumptions and scope
The ambient Hilbert space is \(L^2(\mathbb R)\). For integers \(m,n\), set
\[
(T_nf)(y)=f(y-n),\qquad (M_{m\beta}f)(y)=e^{2\pi i m\beta y}f(y).
\]
The box-window system is \(\mathcal G(g,1,\beta)=\{M_{m\beta}T_ng:m,n\in\mathbb Z\}\). A *dual Gabor window* is a window \(h\in L^2\) such that both generated Gabor families are Bessel and their associated cross-synthesis/reconstruction operator is the identity. The two infima are taken over such genuine duals; \(L^1\)-integrability is stipulated for the norm problem. Essential support and all equalities are understood modulo Lebesgue null sets. Complex-valued windows are allowed.

The assertions cover every real \(\beta\in(2/3,3/4)\), including irrational values, but do not claim an endpoint classification at \(\beta=3/4\), a formula for other windows, or results for different translation steps.

## Proof
**1. Exact duality fibers.** For two Bessel Gabor systems at translation step \(1\), the Janssen–Ron–Shen characterization states that they are dual precisely when, for every integer \(n\),
\[
\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}g(x+k+n/\beta)h(x+k)
=\beta\,\mathbf1_{\{n=0\}}
\quad\text{for almost every }x\in[-1,0).
\tag{1}
\]
The box function is real, so conjugation makes no difference. Write \(t=\beta^{-1}=1+\delta\). At \(n=0\), only \(k=0,1\) contribute, and therefore
\[
h_0+h_1=\beta.\tag{2}
\]
On the middle interval \(I=[\delta-1,-\delta)\) of length \(1-2\delta>0\), the \(n=1\) equation has precisely \(k=-1,0\) and the \(n=-1\) equation precisely \(k=1,2\); thus
\[
h_{-1}=-h_0,\qquad h_2=-h_1 \qquad (x\in I).\tag{3}
\]
Outside \(I\), these two pairs do not both occur. On the left interval \(L=[-1,\delta-1)\), the \(n=1\) equation still contains \(k=-1,0\); on the right interval \(R=[-\delta,0)\), the \(n=-1\) equation contains \(k=1,2\). Here and below all such statements are almost everywhere, so boundary conventions do not matter.

**2. Lower bounds without a support hypothesis.** For any integrable dual \(h\), Tonelli's theorem gives
\[
\|h\|_1=\int_{-1}^0\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}|h_k(x)|\,dx.
\]
Equation (2) implies \(\sum_k|h_k|\ge\beta\) on the entire fiber interval. On \(I\), equations (2)–(3) imply the stronger bound
\[
\sum_k|h_k|\ge2(|h_0|+|h_1|)\ge2\beta.
\]
Since \(|L|+|R|=2\delta\) and \(|I|=1-2\delta\), integration yields
\[
\|h\|_1\ge \beta\big(2\delta+2(1-2\delta)\big)=4\beta-2.
\tag{4}
\]
The same equations imply that at least one fiber coordinate is nonzero on \(L\cup R\), and at least two are nonzero on \(I\), even when \(h\notin L^1\). By Tonelli for indicator functions, every dual has essential support of measure at least
\[
2\delta+2(1-2\delta)=4-\frac2\beta.
\tag{5}
\]

**3. Equality classification.** If (4) is equality then all its pointwise inequalities are equalities almost everywhere. On \(L\cup R\) this means no indices other than \(0,1\) can be present, and \(h_0,h_1\) are nonnegative real values summing to \(\beta\). On \(L\), the \(n=1\) condition now forces \(h_0=0\), hence \(h_1=\beta\). On \(R\), the \(n=-1\) condition forces \(h_1=0\), hence \(h_0=\beta\). On \(I\) equality forces every entry outside \(k=-1,0,1,2\) to vanish, with (3) holding and \(h_0,h_1\) nonnegative reals summing to \(\beta\). Setting \(u=h_0\) gives precisely the fiber table. No complex phase freedom remains because the sum in (2) is the positive real \(\beta\).

**4. Every displayed fiber is genuinely dual.** The table defines a bounded, compactly supported \(h\), so its Gabor system is Bessel. Verify (1) by checking which integer translates of the box intersect a given fiber. Equation (2) holds by construction. For \(n=1\), the potentially contributing index pairs are \((-1,0)\) when \(x<-\delta\), and \((-2,-1)\) when \(x>-\delta\); the table makes their sums zero. For \(n=-1\), the pairs are \((2,3)\) when \(x<\delta-1\) and \((1,2)\) when \(x>\delta-1\), also with zero sum.

For \(n=2\), the only index from the table's support that could occur is \(k=-1\), and this would require \(x<-2\delta\). Yet \(h_{-1}\) can be nonzero only when \(x\in I\subset(-2\delta,0)\), using \(\delta>1/3\). For \(n=-2\), the only candidate is \(k=2\), requiring \(x>2\delta-1\), whereas \(h_2\) can be nonzero only for \(x\in I\subset(-1,2\delta-1)\). For \(|n|\ge3\), the box support never meets any nonzero fiber indices \(-1,0,1,2\). Hence (1) holds for all \(n\). This also proves that \(\mathcal G(g,1,\beta)\) is a frame, since it has an explicit Bessel dual.

The fiber table has absolute sum \(\beta\) on \(L\cup R\) and \(2\beta\) on \(I\), proving equality in (4). On \(I\), there are exactly two nonzero values when \(u=0\) or \(u=\beta\), and four otherwise. This proves the support formula. Finally, if any dual attains equality in (5), the fiber nonzero-coordinate count is pointwise minimal. On \(I\), relation (3) then forces one pair to vanish and the other to equal \(\beta,-\beta\); on the outer intervals the same reasoning as above fixes the unique one-site entries. Consequently every global support-minimizer has a binary-valued \(u\), and is automatically an \(L^1\)-minimizer. This completes all assertions.

## Relationship to prior work
Díaz Martín, Lammers, Miller, and Powell (2026) formulate the global \(L^1\)-minimal Gabor dual problem, prove explicit descriptions at \(\beta\le1/2\) or \(\beta=2/3\) for suitable supported windows, and in their Remark 3.4 reduce other parameters to pointwise finite-dimensional linear programs. Their Theorem 3.3 supplies a support-truncation principle. The theorem here supplies a sharp closed-form value, full complex equality family, and simultaneous sharp support bound for the entire previously unevaluated open interval \(2/3<\beta<3/4\), for the box generator. The use of duality identities and local linear optimization is inherited; the asserted additional result is the exact overlapping-two-alias solution, not a new general duality theory.

Christensen, Kim, and Kim's supported-window results establish existence and geometric restrictions on compact duals, not this exact optimal-norm/support continuum. Lemvig and Nielsen's supported-window parametrizations address general compact duals, not sharp global \(L^1\) and minimal-support values for this interval. Dong, Han, and Li study compact dual parametrization through \(\beta=2/3\), not the open interval here. Other formulations or earlier calculations of this explicit box special case may exist and are a residual bibliographic risk.

## Limitations
The window is the flat box \(\mathbf1_{[-1,1]}\), not an arbitrary supported function; the translation spacing is fixed at \(1\). The theorem asserts nothing for \(\beta\ge3/4\), where extra alias constraints arise, nor for pointwise regularity of the necessarily discontinuous sparse minimizers. It is not a claim about the canonical \(L^2\)-minimizing dual. Its review is same-model only; independent mathematical validation has not been performed. The originality comparison is confined to the identified available literature and indexed records, and does not establish absolute bibliographic uniqueness.
