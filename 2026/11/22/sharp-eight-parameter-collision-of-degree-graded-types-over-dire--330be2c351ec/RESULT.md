# Sharp eight-parameter collision of degree-graded types over directed paths without cyclic triangles

## Finding
Let \(\mathcal H\) be the Fraïssé limit of all finite oriented graphs with no directed three-cycle. An *oriented graph* here has at most one directed arc between distinct points, with nonadjacency allowed. Fix an induced finite base whose underlying undirected graph is a path on \(n\) individually named points \(v_1,\ldots,v_n\). Write a word \(e=e_1\cdots e_{n-1}\in\{0,1\}^{n-1}\) for its orientation, where \(e_i=0\) denotes \(v_i\to v_{i+1}\) and \(e_i=1\) denotes the reverse arc.

For each complete nonalgebraic one-point type \(p\) over that named base, let \(a(p)\) count the parameters adjacent to the new point in either direction. Define the *degree-graded one-type polynomial*
\[
Q_e(z)=\sum_p z^{a(p)}.
\]
It is a polynomial with nonnegative integer coefficients, intrinsic to the base up to graph isomorphism and also unchanged by globally reversing all arcs. It has the exact transfer expression below for every \(n\).

The polynomial distinguishes all unoriented-path isomorphism classes of orientations through \(n=7\), after also identifying a directed path with its global arc converse. At \(n=8\) there are exactly \(36\) such converse/isomorphism classes but only \(35\) polynomials, with exactly one unordered collision pair. With the bit convention above its representatives are
\[
e=0010010,\qquad f=0101110.
\]
These are not related by reversing the vertex order or reversing all arcs, yet they share the full polynomial
\[
\boxed{Q_e(z)=Q_f(z)=1+16z+105z^2+368z^3+753z^4+923z^5+664z^6+257z^7+41z^8.}
\]
In particular both have \(3128\) nonalgebraic one-point types. The finer bivariate profile \(R_e(u,v)\), which records the numbers of outgoing and incoming arcs separately, distinguishes the two: its coefficient of \(u^3v^3\) is \(163\) for \(e\) and \(164\) for \(f\).

Taking the disjoint union of each witness with \(k\) isolated named vertices preserves this indistinguishability for every \(k\ge0\), since it multiplies the common polynomial by \((1+2z)^k\). These are nonisomorphic base structures at every order \(n\ge8\), although the larger examples need not be connected.

## Assumptions and scope
The class of finite oriented graphs omitting the cyclically oriented triangle is hereditary and closed under free amalgamation: no directed triangle can contain edges from both sides of a free amalgam without a cross-edge. Its countable Fraïssé limit \(\mathcal H\) is ultrahomogeneous and every permitted finite one-point extension embeds over the named parameters. As the language is finite relational, one-point orbital types over finite bases are exactly the admissible arc/nonarc status patterns.

A path base has no triangles; any directed cycle of length three containing a new point must use an edge between consecutive old path vertices. Give the new point the status \(0\) at an old vertex when it points to that vertex, \(1\) when the old vertex points to it, and \(2\) when the pair is nonadjacent. For an old forward edge the unique forbidden consecutive status pair is \((0,1)\); for a reverse edge it is \((1,0)\). Therefore the admissible ternary words are exactly those avoiding the indicated local pairs. The exponent in \(Q_e\) is the number of non-\(2\) status letters.

## Proof
**Exact transfer identity.** Let \(J\) be the three-by-three all-ones matrix and \(E_{ij}\) the matrix unit with a one in row \(i\), column \(j\), using indices \(0,1,2\). Put \(A_0=J-E_{01}\), \(A_1=J-E_{10}\), and \(D(z)=\operatorname{diag}(z,z,1)\). Then for \(n\ge1\), with \({\bf1}\) the all-ones column vector,
\[
\boxed{Q_e(z)={\bf1}^{\mathsf T}D(z)A_{e_1}D(z)\cdots A_{e_{n-1}}D(z){\bf1}.}
\]
Every matrix factor prescribes exactly one status at a path vertex, assigns weight \(z\) when the new point is adjacent to it, and excludes exactly the directed triangle forbidden at the next path edge. This identity proves both the exact type interpretation and the recurrence used for enumeration. Replacing \(D(z)\) by \(\operatorname{diag}(u,v,1)\) gives \(R_e(u,v)\).

**Canonical finite classification.** To classify bases by their underlying oriented-path isomorphism type, reversal of the vertex order sends \(e\) to its reversed, complemented word. Global reversal of every arc sends \(e\) to its complemented word. Thus the group generated by word reversal and complementation yields exactly the natural equivalence classes for comparing this scalar polynomial. For each length \(n-1\le7\), enumerate all \(2^{n-1}\) binary words, reduce each to the lexicographically smallest of its four reversal/complement images, and evaluate the displayed integer transfer product. This enumerates every orientation and every class without sampling or heuristic graph-isomorphism shortcuts.

The numbers of canonical classes for \(n=1,\dots,8\) are
\[
1,1,2,3,6,10,20,36.
\]
The numbers of distinct polynomials are
\[
1,1,2,3,6,10,20,35.
\]
The unique size-two fibre at order eight consists exactly of the two displayed canonical words; all other fibres are singletons. The complete coefficient vector in increasing powers of \(z\) is
\[
(1,16,105,368,753,923,664,257,41).
\]
Direct expansion of the bivariate transfer product gives the stated two different \(u^3v^3\) coefficients. This proves sharpness and uniqueness *within the oriented-path family modulo global arc converse*.

**Persistence.** Adjoining an isolated named old point adds no old edges, so each old one-point status word has three independent new possibilities there, contributing weights \(1,z,z\). Hence the polynomial is multiplied by \(1+2z\). Iterating yields the assertion. A connected component that is the original eight-point path remains distinguished from the isolated components, so the two padded bases remain nonisomorphic.

## Verification
The reproducible standard-library checker `code/verify_orientation.py` performs the complete binary-word classification for every size through eight, computes integer coefficient vectors by a dynamic recurrence independent of symbolic matrix multiplication, and additionally verifies all coefficients against direct ternary-word enumeration for every orientation through six vertices and for both eight-vertex witnesses. It independently checks the two bivariate witness coefficients by direct ternary-word enumeration. The exact output is in `verification/output.txt`. Execute `python3 code/verify_orientation.py` from the directory containing this document; it ends with `VERIFY_OK`.

The finite exhaustive census proves only the sharp classification through eight vertices; the transfer expression and isolated-vertex extension hold at arbitrary sizes. Neither finite testing nor numerical evidence is used to infer an unproved all-order reconstruction theorem.

## Relationship to prior work
Kühn, Osthus, Townsend and Zhao (first arXiv public version 2014-04-24) develop global asymptotic structure and enumeration for finite oriented graphs forbidding directed cycles, motivated by Cherlin's classification of homogeneous oriented graphs. Their theorem concerns almost-all structure rather than complete one-point types over a fixed individually named path or a sharp graded-collision census. Nešetřil and Zhu's work on homomorphisms *between* oriented paths supplies related combinatorial context but not this three-status extension invariant. General fixed-target homomorphism partition-function techniques, including Cai and Chen's work, explain why transfer matrices are natural but do not by themselves imply the first-collision classification. Published finite-base results for transitive tournament parameters in the same homogeneous forbidden-cycle class concern *complete* old bases rather than induced directed paths, and do not imply the path-orientation reconstruction threshold.

The recurrence and basic Fraïssé extension argument are standard tools, not claimed as new. The contribution claimed here is the exact path-specific degree-graded invariant, its first and unique eight-point failure, and separation by a natural finer directional grading.

## Limitations
The first-collision classification is exhaustive only within induced oriented paths, through eight named points, modulo global arc converse. Equality of \(Q_e\) does not imply equality of all complete types, their defining formulas, or the finer directional polynomial. The examples of persistent larger collisions obtained by isolated-vertex padding are disconnected. The literature searches cannot exclude an unindexed equivalent computation, and independent validation has not been performed.
