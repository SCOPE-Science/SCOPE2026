# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

A signed-variation transport decomposition of zero-endpoint nodal vectors reduces every input to interval-indicator blocks at an exactly accounted variation cost. Their Bernstein images are nonnegative, unimodal and vanish at endpoints, so their variations are twice their peaks. The Markov representation bounds every such peak by interior survival, uniquely maximized at the central starting state; the full-interior plateau attains equality. Binomial moments, a positive transient matrix and its Perron eigenpair establish the finite formulas and sharp exponential rate. Exact-rational computations and random checks reproduce finite test cases but do not stand in for these proofs.

## Originality — PASS (with residual historical risk)

The closest sources address ordinary variation diminution, spectral convergence or distribution estimation by iterated Bernstein operators, rather than the exact anchored Jordan-variation norm at every power, its nodal extremizers and strict submultiplicativity. Specifically, Kelisky–Rivlin (1967) covers fixed-degree convergence, Cooper–Waldron (2000) gives eigenstructure, Manté (2014 preprint) studies statistical iteration, Gonska–Raşa (2014) addresses composite approximation and Czudek (2016 preprint) extends variation diminution. Their identified results do not entail the exact endpoint-constrained survival norm without the additional block-extremizer argument. This comparison does not exclude an unindexed or inaccessible equivalent historical theorem; independent literature validation has not occurred.

## Value — PASS

The baseline variation diminishing inequality has sharp coefficient one on unrestricted inputs and supplies no quantitative strict contraction for matched-endpoint disturbances. The exact all-degree/all-iteration contraction, rigidity of maximizing data, strict multi-step gain and sharp spectral decay supply a natural quantitative stability benchmark. It is directly motivated by the classic Bernstein approximation/iteration problem and the absorbing binomial-process interpretation.

## Scientific limits

The norm is restricted to real, pointwise Jordan-BV functions with matched endpoints and to the classical fixed-degree Bernstein scheme. The exponential asymptotic is for each fixed degree; the result is not a joint degree–power limit or a claim about generalized Bernstein kernels. The originality assessment is qualified by possible older specialized bounds.
