# Scientific review

**Correctness (PASS).** The weighted bipartite spanning-tree formula follows from the matrix-tree cofactor and supplies the sign-multiplier identity. The only vanishing characters are the two middle-shore layers. Translation averaging proves the canonical projection has least norm among *all* competing projections, and Krawtchouk duality plus the exact generating polynomial \((1-u^2)^r\) evaluates its kernel absolutely. Tensor-product factorization gives \(\Phi(m)\Phi(n)\) and Stirling gives the leading growth constant. A standalone program independently enumerates trees for six small bipartitions and checks 672 exact assertions. Finite cases do not prove the general theorem; the symbolic steps do.

**Originality (PASS under bounded comparison).** Degree-weighted tree enumeration is already provided by Aalipour et al. and Ge--Yu, and the general least-norm Walsh projection principle is already in Defant et al. Neither familiar ingredient is claimed new. The candidate contribution is the explicit evaluated closed product for the odd-degree *vertex-profile* observation of every \(K_{m,n}\), together with its sharp endpoint instability despite asymptotically full Fourier rank. Comparisons also covered published edge-incidence tree convolution on \(K_{2,n}\), a distinct projection of a different ambient Boolean cube, and an odd-dimensional Hamming-ball resonance result, neither of which supplies the claimed constant by direct implication. Historical priority is bounded; an unindexed equivalent binomial norm formula is possible.

**Value (PASS).** Vertex-degree parity is a natural graph invariant with substantial collisions across spanning trees; whether stable reconstruction of its convolution range is possible is a concrete harmonic-analysis question. The infinite product law is exact and exhibits a qualitative instability: both shores can carry almost all Fourier modes, while the optimal endpoint projection becomes exponentially large. This is a natural sharp stability parameter rather than arbitrary finite data or a numerical increment.

**Limits.** Completeness of the bipartite graph is essential; no arbitrary graph assertion is made. The theorem concerns a projection onto the *range*, not a bound for inverse convolution on the endpoint spaces. General-purpose Fourier projector optimality and the elementary tree multiplier are prior work. Independent mathematical audit is outstanding.
