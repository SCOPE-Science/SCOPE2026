# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The displayed exact constant follows from the sharp positive-contraction row-sum lemma, the resolvent Fréchet formula for the principal logarithm, and the observation that the same positive rank-one low-eigenspace projection saturates every resolvent row bound simultaneously. Interior two-input perturbations justify the global supremum even though the limiting extremizer is on the spectral boundary. The closed form and both asymptotics follow by explicit integration and dominated convergence. The exact domain is real symmetric positive-definite matrices on \(aI\preceq A\preceq bI\) for \(0<a<b\) and all \(n\ge1\). The accompanying checker supplies corroborating finite experiments rather than a replacement for proof.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)
A 2020 matrix-function derivative preprint and its full 2021 open-access article establish resolvent formulations and numerical Krylov approximation bounds for the matrix logarithm. They do not establish the exact global maximum-entry modulus over every real symmetric matrix in a fixed spectral band or the critical-width crossover. Crouzeix–Kressner's 2020 full text gives numerical-range/Frobenius-induced derivative inequalities, not an optimized entrywise coefficient. Higham–Relton's full 2014 article concerns first- and second-derivative norm estimation and examples. Existing broad half-line stability bounds are not sharp on an upper-bounded spectral band. Published-record searches and a claim-level claim-level overlap comparison did not yield a dominating theorem. An unindexed equivalent or indirect historical consequence remains a genuine residual-priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Maximum-entry noise is a natural robustness measure in covariance and network matrix transforms, while the logarithm is a fundamental functional-calculus object. The exact spectral-band dependence is stronger than a condition-number-only or half-line bound; the dimension-growth coefficient and \(n^{-1/2}\) critical-window law identify which conditioning/precision regimes change the scale of entrywise amplification. Neither the finite-band closed form nor the spectral-width crossover follows by direct substitution into the earlier half-line formula.

## Scope and limitations
The claimed result is a rigorous global supremum, not an attainment assertion. General nonnormal or complex input classes, induced row-operator norms, sparsity restrictions and full priority beyond inspected literature have not been established. The numerical checker is finite and is not an independent audit.
