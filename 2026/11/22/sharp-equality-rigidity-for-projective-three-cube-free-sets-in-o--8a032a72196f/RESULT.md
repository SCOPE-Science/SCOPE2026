# Sharp equality rigidity for projective three-cube-free sets in odd abelian groups

## Finding

For an additive abelian group \(G\), a **projective three-cube** is the set of nonempty subset sums of three group elements \(x,y,z\), with repetitions permitted:
\[
\{x,y,z,x+y,x+z,y+z,x+y+z\}.
\]
A set \(A\subseteq G\) is *three-cube-free* if it contains none of these sets (including degenerate cubes).

**Theorem.** Let \(G\) be any finite abelian group of **odd** order \(N\) with \(3\mid N\), and write \(r=\dim_{\mathbb F_3}(G/3G)\ge1\). Then:

1. Every three-cube-free subset of \(G\) has at most \(2N/3\) elements, and this bound is attained.
2. Every equality set \(A\) is the inverse image of an equality set in the elementary ternary quotient \(G/3G\). Conversely, every such inverse image is three-cube-free. Thus the two families of equality sets are in exact bijection.
3. If \(r=1\), the **unique** equality set is \(G\setminus3G\). If \(r=2\), the **exactly four** equality sets are the complements of the four index-three subgroups of \(G\).
4. For \(r\ge2\), there are at least \((3^r-1)/2\) distinct equality sets, one for each index-three subgroup. Consequently, uniqueness occurs if and only if the three-primary component of \(G\) is cyclic.

The theorem does not classify all equality sets for ternary rank \(r\ge3\).

## Assumptions and scope

The group operation is written additively. The subgroup \(3G=\{3g:g\in G\}\) is the image of the multiplication-by-three homomorphism. The quotient \(G/3G\) is a vector space over \(\mathbb F_3\); its dimension is the three-rank \(r\). The phrase 'equality set' refers to three-cube-free sets with exactly \(2N/3\) elements. All forbidden cubes include repeated generators; in particular \(\{0\}\) is forbidden. The odd-order hypothesis is used to make multiplication by two bijective and to bound the index of a proper subgroup by at least three.

## Proof

**Step 1: The universal density bound for odd-order groups.** Write \(B=G\setminus A\). Because the triple \((0,0,0)\) is a forbidden cube, \(0\notin A\). Since doubling is a permutation of \(G\), set
\[
S=\{x\in G:x\in A,\ 2x\in A\}=A\cap2^{-1}A,
\qquad |S|\ge2|A|-N.
\]
If \(S=\varnothing\), then \(|A|\le N/2\). Otherwise, choose \(x\in S\). For every \(y\in G\), at least one of \(y,y+x,y+2x\) belongs to \(B\), since otherwise the repeated-generator cube \((x,x,y)\) would lie in \(A\). Summing the inequality
\[
\mathbf1_B(y)+\mathbf1_B(y+x)+\mathbf1_B(y+2x)\ge1
\]
over all \(y\) gives \(3|B|\ge N\), hence \(|A|\le2N/3\).

**Step 2: Equality forces a period subgroup.** Assume \(|A|=2N/3\), so \(|B|=N/3\), and \(|S|\ge N/3\). For **every** \(x\in S\), the inequality in Step 1 has equal totals on the two sides, so it is equality for **every** \(y\):
\[
\mathbf1_B(y)+\mathbf1_B(y+x)+\mathbf1_B(y+2x)=1.
\]
Subtract the same identity at \(y+x\). The result is
\[
\mathbf1_B(y)=\mathbf1_B(y+3x)
\quad (y\in G,\ x\in S).
\]
The subgroup \(H=\langle S\rangle\) must be all of \(G\). Indeed, if \(H\) were proper, its index in an odd-order group would be at least three, so \(|H|\le N/3\). But \(S\subseteq H\setminus\{0\}\), which would imply \(|S|\le |H|-1<N/3\), a contradiction. Consequently, the period relations for \(3S\) make \(B\), and therefore \(A\), invariant under addition by every element of \(3G\).

**Step 3: Exact quotient correspondence.** Let \(\pi:G\to Q=G/3G\) be the quotient. Step 2 implies \(A=\pi^{-1}(C)\) for a set \(C\subseteq Q\) of size \(2|Q|/3\). If \(C\) contained a projective three-cube generated by \(\bar x,\bar y,\bar z\), choosing any lifts in \(G\) would put their entire cube inside \(\pi^{-1}(C)=A\). Conversely, the image under \(\pi\) of a forbidden cube in \(A\) is one in \(C\). Thus \(C\) is three-cube-free if and only if its full inverse image is. Distinct \(C\)'s give distinct inverse images.

**Step 4: Ternary rank one and two.** In \(Q\cong\mathbb F_3\), a two-point three-cube-free set cannot contain zero; it must be \(\mathbb F_3\setminus\{0\}\). This gives the unique rank-one equality set.

In \(Q\cong\mathbb F_3^2\), let \(D=Q\setminus C\). Then \(|D|=3\) and \(0\in D\). If the two nonzero members of \(D\) were linearly independent, a group automorphism would send \(D\) to \(\{(0,0),(1,0),(0,1)\}\). But in this case the repeated-generator triple
\[
x=(0,2),\qquad y=z=(1,2)
\]
has all its nonempty subset sums in the complement: they are exactly \((0,2),(1,2),(2,1),(1,1),(2,0)\), contradicting cube-freeness. Hence \(D\) is a one-dimensional vector subspace. There are four such subspaces, and their complements are cube-free by Step 5. This gives the four rank-two equality cases.

**Step 5: Existence and nonuniqueness.** Let \(H\le G\) have index three and let \(\chi:G\to\mathbb F_3\) be a surjective homomorphism with kernel \(H\). Any triple of elements of \(G\setminus H\) maps to three nonzero elements of \(\mathbb F_3\). Any three nonzero elements of \(\mathbb F_3\) have a nonempty subset summing to zero: either two have opposite signs, or all three have the same sign. Thus the triple has a nonempty subset sum in \(H\), and \(G\setminus H\) is three-cube-free of size \(2N/3\). Such subgroups always exist because \(r\ge1\). There are exactly \((3^r-1)/2\) distinct index-three subgroups, since nonzero linear forms on \(Q\) have the same kernel precisely when they differ by a nonzero scalar. For \(r\ge2\), this yields more than one equality set, completing the uniqueness criterion. \(\square\)

## Verification

The standalone Python 3 script `verify_projective_cubes.py` enumerates all maximum-cardinality subsets in the additive groups \(\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z\), \(\mathbb Z/9\mathbb Z\), \(\mathbb Z/15\mathbb Z\), and \((\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z)^2\), independently building all forbidden cube masks with repeated generators. It finds respectively one, one, one, and four equality sets, directly checks the period relation \(B+3x=B\) for each \(x\in S\), and checks full inverse-image cube-freeness in \((\mathbb Z/9\mathbb Z)\times(\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z)\). The mathematical steps above prove the all-order theorem; the code is finite corroboration, not an infinite certificate.

## Relationship to prior work

Meng's earlier work proves the \(2/3\) upper bound for **cyclic** groups and exhibits the residue-class extremizer when three divides the group order. The theorem here establishes the equality-case period subgroup in **all odd abelian groups**, an exact quotient correspondence, and complete uniqueness and rank-two equality classifications; these conclusions are not stated in that upper-bound theorem. Long and Wagner focus on projective cubes in cyclic groups of two-power order, disjoint from the present odd-order class. A later 2026 preprint by Ranđelović establishes a strict density gap for cyclic orders not divisible by three, a disjoint range; its first public version postdates the frozen eligibility boundary and does not determine these equality cases. Karam–Keevash's recent structure theorem for *cube-avoiding sumsets* uses a different forbidden set \(Z_0^n\), rather than projective subset-sum cubes. Hassler–Treglown's results concern Schur-triple-free sets and maximum/maximal sum-free counts, not this three-cube equality rigidity. These related works motivate the structural question but are not claimed as its proof.

## Limitations

The full equality-set classification for ternary quotient ranks at least three remains open here; only the quotient reduction and index-three-subgroup lower count are proved. Odd-order groups not divisible by three and even-order groups are outside the equality classification. Although searches of available indexed mathematical literature and research records found no covering theorem, unindexed or inaccessible priority precedents remain possible. An independent priority review has not been claimed.
