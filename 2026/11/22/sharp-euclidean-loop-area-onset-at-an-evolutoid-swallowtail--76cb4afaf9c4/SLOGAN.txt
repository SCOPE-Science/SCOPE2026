At the first swallowtail of a smooth convex oval's evolutoid, the *ordinary Euclidean area* of each newborn two-cusped loop grows sharply as \(\delta^{7/2}\), with an explicit coefficient determined by the curvature radius and the nondegenerate minimum of its logarithmic derivative. For ellipses, the sum of the two loop areas has a closed radical coefficient whose fixed-area minimum occurs uniquely at semiaxis ratio \((1+\sqrt{22})/\sqrt{21}\); the previously known threshold angle and cusp-birth topology are not claimed as new.
