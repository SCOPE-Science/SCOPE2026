# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The proof characterizes exactly when a partial isomorphism between selected induced equivalence structures extends in its original finite host, then independently derives both full rank enumerators from selected-block multiplicities. A capacity-crossing block pair of original sizes differing by \(\delta\) forces deletion of at least \(\delta\) points from one side. At exact first deficiency, equality forces all deletions into one class in each selected set; the change in the selected profile uniquely identifies the ordered pair of original capacities. Counting the incompatible block permutations yields the stated positive coefficient. No finitary experiment is presented as an infinite proof. The standalone verifier checks 30 full polynomial pairs and 13 additional seven-point first-defect cases, as described in `VERIFICATION.md`.

## Originality — PASS, with priority risk

Actual semantic searches compared nonuniform equivalence classes, induced partial isomorphisms, near-total-rank extendibility, the minimum gap between class capacities, and equivalent complete multipartite graph/ultrametric formulations. The closest published exact rank polynomial is for equal-sized clique unions: those are the homogeneous zero-defect case, and their formulas do not imply the new gap threshold or the nonuniform first-defect coefficient. Chubb et al. address reconstruction of equivalence structures from partial automorphism inverse semigroups; Jajcay et al. develop inverse-monoid structural theory; Bartoš et al. treat partial automorphisms in different ultrametric categories. Their inspected materials do not provide this quantified nonuniform-host law. Search absence is not a proof of priority; specialized older enumerations could still subsume the result. This is a self-review, not an independent literature certification.

## Value — PASS

The smallest distance between distinct equivalence-class cardinalities measures a precise, intrinsic finite extension obstruction: it gives the exact number of points that must be omitted before a partial symmetry can fail to extend. The coefficient formula counts all first obstructions, while the complete rank polynomials distinguish genuine partial symmetries from restrictions of global ones. These are natural invariants of the finite partial-automorphism inverse semigroup and a sharp boundary of finite homogeneity, rather than an arbitrary sampled size or isolated small case.
