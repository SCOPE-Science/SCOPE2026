# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The theorem quantifies over every integer \(N\ge2\) and \(0<\alpha\le1\). The proof was reconstructed from the exact coefficient identities \(Q_{N,\alpha}=\alpha C_N+(1-\alpha)C_{N-1}\), the two derivative recurrences and the half-angle Chebyshev derivative identity; each step handles the endpoint and degree-drop cases. There is no unit-circle root for an interior parameter, the \(N-2\) finite roots initially leave outward, the extra root comes from infinity, and root-continuity completes the argument. The first-order velocity is obtained by differentiating at a simple endpoint root. The fixed-\(\gamma\), positive-\(t\) kernel corollary properly invokes Fu's compact-uniform limit and is not asserted at the endpoint. The packaged standard-library checker passed 196 exact identities, 91 Chebyshev comparisons, 112 exterior-root calculations and 36 endpoint-velocity tests. Finite checks supplement the analytic proof.

## Originality — PASS, best-knowledge comparison
The closest source is Q. Fu, arXiv:2611.15406v1 (first public 2026-11-11). Its complete limiting-polynomial construction, root counts, multiple-root restriction and angular concentration were compared with the claim; none yields the full \(|z|>1\) exclusion at each fixed parameter or the sharp endpoint velocity. The published *Parity-controlled real zeros of generalized Hartogs vertex polynomials* gives the exact real-root phase diagram but leaves complex-root moduli unresolved. Deng–Fu, arXiv:2611.07480v1, and Edholm–Mathew, arXiv:2505.20489, study a distinct rational, palindromic oblique-slice polynomial; its inside/outside pairing is not a stronger version of this root result. Fell's 1980 work on convex combinations assumes equal-degree monic endpoints. Searches were also made for Chebyshev-derivative mixtures, unit-disk stable truncations and the same Hartogs-vertex aliases. Residual risk: an older unindexed self-inversive/orthogonal-polynomial theorem could imply the same exterior statement. Novelty is not independently certified.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The radius-one barrier is canonical: it separates the classical oblique product scale \(|s|=|t|\) from the newly known singular-vertex zero region for every non-endpoint exponent. It answers the full complex-root confinement question, not just the previously classified negative-root count. Its boundary is sharp at the discrete endpoint exponents, and the explicit root-by-root escape velocities quantify how zeros leave that boundary. This is useful for local zero geometry and quantitative kernel nonvanishing, beyond finite testing or a rewording of Fu's existence theorem.

## Scope and limitations
The theorem uses Fu's published kernel series and local convergence and does not reprove them. It does not treat arbitrary complex product \(t\), all zeros over the entire admissible section, or the finite-\(t\) closed core at \(\alpha=1\). A finite check does not certify the infinite family. Strong published results on rational oblique sections are credited and not subsumed by this theorem. Independent audit has not been performed.
