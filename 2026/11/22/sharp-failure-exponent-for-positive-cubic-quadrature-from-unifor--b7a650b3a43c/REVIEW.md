# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The two-point Gauss identity holds exactly through cubic degree. Every possible negative-sign arrangement of a cubic is treated: terminal, two terminal, interior, or terminal plus interior. The delicate three-root case is controlled by a strictly negative quadratic upper bound whenever the negative support has less than the asserted length. The random-polytope proof uses independent remaining nodes and an almost-sure simplicial moment-curve hull, and the two-sided bounds imply the exact exponential root. The extracted checker verifies supporting symbolic identities and finite examples; it is not substituted for proof.

**Originality — PASS, with historical-priority limitations.** The 2026 motivating preprint gives general high-probability existence of positive random quadrature and nearly natural weights, not the sharp cubic fixed-space failure exponent. The closest published records compute degree-two random-node feasibility, whose rate \(2/3\) neither implies nor bounds sharply the cubic rate. Searches for cubic moment-curve halfspace depth, equivalent moment-feasibility events and sharp probability constants identified no record proving this cubic exponent. General convex-hull depth literature may encode related tools, and a relevant 2022 publisher article could not be read in full; independent literature verification remains necessary.

**Value — PASS.** Degree three is the first case requiring a three-dimensional moment polytope. The exact failure exponent directly quantifies how much oversampling is needed for a stable positive-exact quadrature rule, revealing a strict exponential-rate deterioration relative to quadratic exactness. The result is a natural precise benchmark independent of weight-construction algorithms, rather than an arbitrary parameter slice.

The proof does not give an exact finite-sample probability, sharp prefactor, near-equal weights, or a constructive algorithm. Its confidence concerns the specific fixed-degree, uniform-sampling model, not general positive quadrature.
