# Sharp finite-alphabet probe complexity of adjacent sorting monoids

## Finding

For integers \(n,m\ge 2\), put \(C_m=\{0,1,\ldots,m-1\}\) with its usual order. On \(C_m^n\), let \(c_i\), \(1\le i<n\), replace coordinates \(i,i+1\) by their minimum and maximum. Let \(H_{n,m}\) be the transformation monoid generated by the \(c_i\). A **separating probe set** is a subset \(S\subseteq C_m^n\) such that for any two distinct functions \(f,g\in H_{n,m}\), some \(x\in S\) satisfies \(f(x)\ne g(x)\). Then

\[
|H_{n,m}|=n!,\qquad \min\{|S|:S\text{ is separating}\}=\left\lceil\frac{n-1}{m-1}\right\rceil.
\]

The minimum is achieved by a fully explicit nonadaptive set of inputs. In particular, binary probes need exactly \(n-1\) words, and a single probe suffices exactly when \(m\ge n\). The classical \(0\)-Hecke identification and existence of a faithful binary action are **not** claimed as new; the claim concerns the exact optimal number of finite-alphabet input probes.

## Assumptions and scope

Every comparator \(c_i\) is **ascending**: it sends an adjacent pair \((a,b)\) to \((\min(a,b),\max(a,b))\). The monoid includes the identity and contains functions, not comparator word syntaxes; words that induce the same function are identified. Probes are arbitrary input words of length \(n\) over the fixed chain \(C_m\), chosen in advance and reused against all functions. The labels of output coordinates and values are observed exactly. No noise or partial observation is assumed. We require \(n,m\ge2\); for \(n=1\) or \(m=1\), there is one transformation and the minimum number of probes is zero.

## Proof

**Step 1: the normal forms and a faithful binary family.** The comparator transformations satisfy \(c_i^2=c_i\), \(c_ic_j=c_jc_i\) if \(|i-j|>1\), and \(c_ic_{i+1}c_i=c_{i+1}c_ic_{i+1}\). Thus there is a homomorphism from the classical type-A \(0\)-Hecke monoid \(\mathcal H_n\), which has \(n!\) elements, to \(H_{n,m}\) (see Mazorchuk and Steinberg, Section 2.1). To prove injectivity, first use an auxiliary chain of \(n\) strictly distinct symbols and its descending input \(\omega=(n,n-1,\ldots,1)\). Every \(0\)-Hecke normal form takes \(\omega\) to a different permutation: comparisons swap adjacent inversions and the resulting permutation is the usual \(0\)-Hecke (weak-order) normal form. For each \(k\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}\), set \(b_k=(1^k,0^{n-k})\in C_m^n\). Let \(h_k\) be the nondecreasing threshold from the auxiliary chain to \(\{0,1\}\) which sends the largest \(k\) symbols to \(1\). Since a nondecreasing map commutes with \(\min\) and \(\max\), it commutes coordinatewise with every comparator. Hence \(f(b_k)=h_k(f(\omega))\) for every \(f\in\mathcal H_n\). The \(n-1\) threshold images of a permutation of \(n\) distinct symbols determine that permutation uniquely. Therefore \(\{b_1,\ldots,b_{n-1}\}\) separates the entire \(0\)-Hecke monoid already inside the binary alphabet. It follows that \(H_{n,m}\cong\mathcal H_n\) and \(|H_{n,m}|=n!\). This step recovers known faithfulness; it is not the new optimality assertion.

**Step 2: upper bound by packing cuts into levels.** Partition the indices \(\{1,\ldots,n-1\}\) into \(t=\lceil(n-1)/(m-1)\rceil\) disjoint groups \(G_1,\ldots,G_t\), each of cardinality at most \(m-1\). For a group \(G\), define one probe \(x^G\in C_m^n\) by

\[
x^G_i=\#\{k\in G:k\ge i\},\qquad 1\le i\le n.
\]

These values lie in \(\{0,\ldots,|G|\}\subseteq C_m\). If \(G=\{k_1<\cdots<k_r\}\), then for each \(s=1,\ldots,r\), thresholding the coordinates of \(x^G\) at level \(r-s+1\) yields the binary word \(b_{k_s}\) (strict threshold at that level, with the convention \(h_s(a)=1\) iff \(a\ge s\)). More directly, every change in its successive coordinate values is exactly at an index \(k\in G\), and each such change is realized by one threshold. Applying the same threshold to the output of any comparator word equals applying that comparator word to the thresholded input. Thus, if two monoid elements agree on every \(x^{G_j}\), they agree on every \(b_k\), and Step 1 implies that they coincide. The \(t\) constructed probes are separating.

**Step 3: optimality by the distinct top coatoms.** Let \(F\) be the full sorting transformation, represented by the longest element of \(\mathcal H_n\); thus \(F(x)\) is the nondecreasing rearrangement of \(x\). For every \(j\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}\), let \(G_j\) represent the unique length-one-below-longest \(0\)-Hecke normal form whose action on \(\omega\) is the ascending permutation with positions \(j,j+1\) exchanged. It exists by weak-order accessibility and is distinct from \(F\). Applying \(c_j\) to its output finishes the sorting operation, so

\[
c_j\circ G_j=F.
\]

For any input \(x\), if \(G_j(x)\ne F(x)\), then the \(j\)-th and \((j+1)\)-st entries of \(F(x)\) must be **strictly different**: otherwise both must be equal before as well as after the final comparator \(c_j\), and the comparator cannot change the vector. Since \(F(x)\) is a nondecreasing word on \(m\) available symbols, its number of strict adjacent increases is at most \(m-1\). Therefore one input probe can distinguish \(F\) from at most \(m-1\) of the \(n-1\) distinct transformations \(G_j\). Every separating set must distinguish \(F\) from every \(G_j\), so it has cardinality at least \(\lceil(n-1)/(m-1)\rceil\). Equality with Step 2 proves the claim.

## Verification

The accompanying standalone `verify.py` enumerates full transformation functions, their \(0\)-Hecke normal forms, and the proposed probe fingerprints. It checks all \(2\le n\le6\) and \(2\le m\le3\), including the \(720\)-element monoid for \(n=6\), and checks the \(n-1\) long/coatom pairs used in the lower bound. The preserved `verification_output.txt` records the actual run. These finite checks corroborate, but are not a replacement for, the all-parameters proof.

## Relationship to prior work

Militaru, *Solutions of the set-theoretic quantum Yang–Baxter equation arising from posets*, arXiv:2611.15540v1 (publicly posted November 11, 2026), establishes the idempotent sorting braid solution for comparable pairs in a poset (Theorem 2.1), with primary MSC2020 16T25. Its treatment gives the natural algebraic setting for these generators. The work of Mazorchuk and Steinberg, *Double Catalan monoids* (arXiv:1105.5313, 2011; J. Algebraic Combin. 2012), Section 2 and Corollary 17, already establishes classical \(0\)-Hecke normal forms and an effective action on the vertices of the Coxeter complex. Their Section 7 determines **minimal faithful representation dimension and set-action degree**. None of those cited results states the present minimum number of *length-\(n\), \(m\)-ary input probes* in this specified coordinatewise sorting action. The problem is distinct from testing a fixed hardware sorting circuit for stuck-at faults, as in Piestrak's 1990 fault-testing paper.

## Limitations

The assertion is about nonadaptive exact-output probes for the fixed adjacent-sorting monoid and finite ordered alphabets; it does not classify faulty hardware, optimize comparator counts, address noisy outputs, or prove an optimal result for all possible \(0\)-Hecke actions. The \(0\)-Hecke structure and faithful binary action are classical. Although the specified quantitative formula was not found in the consulted sources, publication-priority searches are incomplete: older semigroup-testing and sorting-network literature may use different terminology, and Piestrak's 1990 full text was not available for an exact whole-document comparison. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
