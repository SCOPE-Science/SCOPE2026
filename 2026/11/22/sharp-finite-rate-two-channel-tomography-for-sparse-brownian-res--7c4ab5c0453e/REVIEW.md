# Scientific review — two-channel Brownian resetting tomography

## Correctness (C): PASS

The final theorem is about a Brownian particle on the cut circle \((0,L)\) with the two endpoints representing different continuous approach directions to one absorber. It assumes a known deterministic initial position, known diffusivity and circumference, a rate-independent Borel reset law, independent instantaneous Poisson resets, exact mean-time and exit-side observations, and prescribed rates \(r_j=D\kappa^2j^2\) for \(j=1,\ldots,K\). The two Dirichlet discounted exit transforms are explicit. First-cycle conditioning reconstructs their reset-law averages from the measured pair. Sum and difference hyperbolic identities recover \(2K+1\) consecutive bilateral moments of a positive compactly supported pushforward measure. A positive-square Gram identity proves Hankel singularity if and only if there are no more than \(K\) sites. Nonsingular Vandermonde and annihilating-polynomial calculations reconstruct and orient all sites. Invariance of domain establishes the rate-count lower bound from the \(2K-1\)-dimensional strictly positive \(K\)-site family. No stochastic simulation is offered as proof of the infinite or all-order statements. The included checker passed 2,764 exact-rational and physical-response assertions from the packaged code.

## Originality (O): PASS with priority qualification

The ring-resetting first-passage study of Julián-Salgado et al., arXiv:2605.08414 (first public 2026-05-08), derives forward MFPT and mirror-symmetric optimization diagrams; it does not supply paired arrival-direction reconstruction or the finite-rate support-rank certificate. Sezik et al., arXiv:2508.07386, §4.3, derives resetting splitting probabilities and conditional hidden-state inference, but not inversion of a freely resampled reset-position distribution with both mean and exit-side observations over a sharp finite rate set. Abundo, arXiv:2410.16889, treats inverse prescribed first-passage data, including one randomly chosen reset position, without this sharp finite-data theorem. Relevant public prior technical results on boundary-current reflection blindness, two-target MFPT curves, and finite-reset-site arrival-direction reversals were compared by mathematical implication; their observables and identifiability statements do not subsume the Hankel certificate or its sharp rate count. The moment-reconstruction algebra itself is classical and not claimed as novel. A general inverse-source or finite-exponential-sum theorem could imply the result after a nontrivial reduction; independent priority verification remains open.

## Value (V): PASS

The result answers a motivated finite-observation experimental design question: what is the smallest number of reset-rate settings at which two tractable first-passage observables reveal the number, weights, and oriented positions of a finite set of relocation sites? It provides not merely recovery under an assumed sparse source but a positive Hankel determinant test that excludes *every* nonsparse Borel alternative. The direction channel resolves the geometric reflection blindness of mean time alone, and \(K\) rate pairs are information-theoretically sharp for \(K\) unknown sites. This is an exact mathematical benchmark; noise robustness and uncalibrated parameters are not claimed.

## Limitations and residual scientific risks

The rank/determinant test is exact and becomes ill-conditioned under measurement error or nearly colliding sites. Observations of final arrival direction require side-resolved continuous hits. Correlated, delayed, non-Poisson, or rate-dependent relocation is excluded. Classical Prony and positive truncated moment theory should receive full credit; broader inverse moment literature may contain an equivalent statement in different observables. No independent scientific audit or empirical validation has been performed.
