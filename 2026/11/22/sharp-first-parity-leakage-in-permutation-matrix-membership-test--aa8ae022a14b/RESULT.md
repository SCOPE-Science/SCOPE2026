# Sharp first parity leakage in permutation-matrix membership tests

## Finding
For each integer \(n\ge2\), let the instance domain be the full assignment board \(X_n=[n]\times[n]\). A permutation \(\pi\in\mathfrak S_n\) defines the Boolean concept \(G_\pi=\{(i,\pi(i)):i\in[n]\}\). Let \(\mathcal E_n\) consist of the concepts belonging to even permutations and let \(\mathcal O_n\) consist of the odd-permutation concepts.

**Parity-blind trace theorem.** For **every** support \(S\subseteq X_n\) containing at most \(n-2\) cells and **every** response assignment \(\epsilon:S\to\{0,1\}\), exactly the same number of even and odd permutations realize \(\epsilon\). Moreover, such an assignment is realizable (in either parity class) if and only if its positive cells form a bipartite matching. Thus, through \(n-2\) arbitrary cell-membership queries, parity filtering preserves the entire trace collection **and every individual trace multiplicity up to a factor of two**. This conclusion remains true for adaptive query trees of depth at most \(n-2\), leaf by leaf.

**Sharp first parity leakage and optimal adaptive test.** If a support \(S\) has exactly \(n-1\) distinct cells, its parity-specific trace counts remain identical for every assignment unless \(S\) is a bipartite matching. For a matching \(S\), let \(\pi_S\) be its unique permutation completion, and let \(F\subseteq S\) be the cells answered zero. Then the number of even completions minus the number of odd completions for that exact trace equals
\[
(-1)^{|F|}\operatorname{sgn}(\pi_S).
\]
Thus every one of the \(2^{n-1}\) assignments is informative by exactly one signed permutation count. If an unknown permutation is sampled uniformly from either parity class with equal prior probabilities, any (possibly randomized) adaptive strategy using at most \(n-2\) Boolean cell-membership queries has zero distinguishing advantage. With at most \(n-1\) queries, the **maximum total-variation distance between its two transcript distributions** is exactly
\[
\boxed{\mathrm{TV}_{\max}(n,n-1)=\frac{2^{n-1}}{n!}.}
\]
This is attained by nonadaptively asking the \(n-1\) cells of any near-perfect matching; a maximum-likelihood parity prediction from its answers depends only on whether the number of negative responses is even or odd (and on the parity of \(\pi_S\)). With equal priors its exact optimal success probability is \(\tfrac12+2^{n-2}/n!\).

**Exact shattering classification.** A support \(S\subseteq X_n\), of any cardinality, is VC-shattered by \(\mathcal E_n\) if and only if \(S\) is a bipartite matching with \(|S|\le n-2\). The same statement holds for \(\mathcal O_n\). Consequently the complete shattered-support polynomial of either parity class is
\[
\Phi_n(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}\binom nk^2k!\,z^k.
\]
The shattering complex is precisely the \((n-3)\)-skeleton of the standard \(n\)-by-\(n\) chessboard (matching) complex; it is pure of dimension \(n-3\) for \(n\ge3\), and consists of the empty face for \(n=2\).

**Static and adaptive dimensions.** For all \(n\ge2\),
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{VCdim}(\mathcal E_n)=\operatorname{Ldim}(\mathcal E_n)=n-2.}
\]
The identical formula holds for \(\mathcal O_n\). Thus parity restriction decreases each of these dimensions by exactly one from the unrestricted perfect-matching incidence class, while a restriction forbidding the entire diagonal has static dimension \(n-2\) but online dimension \(n-1\) for \(n\ge5\) (a previously established comparison).

For a matching support \(S\) of size \(k\le n-2\) and any requested positive trace \(T\subseteq S\), write \(t=|T|\) and \(r=k-t\). The **exact number of even permutations**, and separately the exact number of odd permutations, realizing \(G_\pi\cap S=T\) is
\[
\frac12\sum_{j=0}^{r}(-1)^j\binom rj(n-t-j)!.
\]
Examples are \(\Phi_3(z)=1+9z\), \(\Phi_4(z)=1+16z+72z^2\), \(\Phi_5(z)=1+25z+200z^2+600z^3\), and \(\Phi_6(z)=1+36z+450z^2+2400z^3+5400z^4\).

## Assumptions and scope
The two copies of \([n]\) are distinguished as rows and columns. Concepts record **row-to-image equality**, not relative order comparisons between symbols. The parity of a permutation is its sign relative to fixed row and column label orders. Query supports are sets of distinct cells; a trace has value one precisely on the selected cells occupied by a permutation matrix. Both parity families are nonempty for \(n\ge2\). For \(n=2\), each parity family contains exactly one concept and shatters only the empty support. The formula and the skeleton convention include this case. The theorem does not concern arbitrary forbidden subboards or arbitrary permutation subgroups.

## Proof
**Lemma 1 (vanishing signed trace counts).** Fix \(S\subseteq X_n\) with \(|S|\le n-2\), and any binary assignment. Denote its positive cells by \(T\) and negative cells by \(F=S\setminus T\). If \(T\) repeats a row or a column, no permutation realizes it, so both parity counts vanish. Otherwise inclusion-exclusion on the forbidden cells gives the signed difference of the two counts as
\[
\sum_{U\subseteq F}(-1)^{|U|}
\sum_{\substack{\pi\in\mathfrak S_n\\T\cup U\subseteq G_\pi}}
\operatorname{sgn}(\pi).
\]
For each \(U\), if \(T\cup U\) is not a matching, the inner sum is zero. If it is a matching of size \(s\), then \(s\le|S|\le n-2\); there are at least two unassigned rows and columns. Fix any completion of the prescribed edges, then interchange the images in two remaining rows. This gives a fixed-point-free sign-reversing involution on all completions, so again the inner sum is zero. Every term vanishes, proving equality of the even and odd trace multiplicities. The argument works even when \(S\) is not itself a matching.

**Lemma 2 (every compatible short trace occurs).** With \(S,T,F\) as above and \(T\) a matching, prescribe the edges of \(T\). The residual assignment board has \(m=n-|T|\) rows and columns, and at most \(r=|F|\le m-2\) cells are forbidden. A complete balanced bipartite graph of order \(m\) decomposes into \(m\) edge-disjoint perfect matchings (e.g. the cyclic shifts after labeling the two shores). Each forbidden cell lies in only one factor, so at most \(r\) of these factors are excluded. At least \(m-r\ge2\) factors survive and give permutations realizing exactly the prescribed trace. Lemma 1 then ensures that each parity has exactly half the total number, hence at least one. Conversely, if \(T\) is not a matching, no permutation realizes it. This proves trace universality and parity balance simultaneously.

**Counting the trace multiplicity.** For \(S\) a matching and \(T\subseteq S\), after fixing its \(t\) positive edges, the remaining \(r\) forbidden queried edges are pairwise disjoint on an \((n-t)\)-square board. Ordinary inclusion-exclusion gives \(\sum_{j=0}^r(-1)^j\binom rj(n-t-j)!\) completions. By Lemma 1, each parity contributes exactly half, giving the stated integer expression.

**The first nonzero parity signature.** For arbitrary \(S\) of size \(n-1\), reuse Lemma 1’s inclusion-exclusion. Every term for a proper forbidden subset \(U\subsetneq F\) fixes at most \(n-2\) cells, so its signed sum is zero. Only \(U=F\) remains, and its fixed-edge set is the entire support \(S\). This contributes zero unless \(S\) is a matching; when it is, its unique completion \(\pi_S\) contributes \((-1)^{|F|}\operatorname{sgn}(\pi_S)\). This proves the exact formula even for traces not realized by either parity class (where the formula correctly gives zero). A near-perfect matching has \(2^{n-1}\) possible Boolean response patterns, each with signed difference of absolute value one, so its transcript distributions under uniformly even versus uniformly odd permutations have total variation \(2^{n-1}/n!\).

For any adaptive binary query tree of depth at most \(n-1\), each leaf is determined by its (possibly repeated) queried cells and their responses. A leaf using fewer than \(n-1\) distinct cells has signed count difference zero; a leaf using exactly \(n-1\) distinct cells has difference of absolute value at most one. A binary tree of depth at most \(n-1\) has at most \(2^{n-1}\) leaves. The total-variation distance of the transcript laws is therefore at most \((1/n!)2^{n-1}\), attained by the fixed near-perfect matching. Randomizing the query strategy cannot improve the bound: condition on its private randomness and then average the resulting transcript distributions, using convexity of total variation (or reveal the random seed). Bayes’ rule for equal parity priors yields success probability \(\frac12(1+\mathrm{TV})\), as asserted. No adaptive strategy can leak parity at depth below \(n-1\) by Lemma 1.

**The shattering boundary.** If a support is shattered, its all-one trace is realized, so the support must be a matching. Lemma 2 shatters every matching of cardinality at most \(n-2\). Now let \(S\) be a matching of size \(n-1\). It has a unique completion \(\pi\) to a full permutation. For the parity class of \(\pi\), the full-positive trace exists, but if one edge of \(S\) is required negative while the other \(n-2\) edges remain positive, there are only two completions of the residual two rows and columns. The completion \(\pi\) violates the negative requirement; the other completion switches two images, has the opposite sign, and is not in that parity class. The opposite parity class does not realize the full-positive trace. Therefore neither parity family shatters \(S\). A larger matching cannot be shattered because shattering is hereditary, and nonmatchings were already excluded. The number of \(k\)-edge matchings on the complete \(n\)-by-\(n\) board is \(\binom nk^2k!\). This proves the classification, counting polynomial, purity statement, and \(\operatorname{VCdim}=n-2\).

**Adaptive dimension.** The standard inequality \(\operatorname{VCdim}\le\operatorname{Ldim}\) gives the lower bound \(n-2\). To prove the reverse bound, induct on \(n\). At \(n=2\) each parity family is a singleton and has online dimension zero. For \(n\ge3\), consider a complete mistake tree rooted at any queried cell \((i,j)\). Its positive child fixes \(\pi(i)=j\). Deleting that row and column identifies the remaining hypotheses with **one sign coset of** \(\mathfrak S_{n-1}\). Relabeling one remaining shore by an odd permutation interchanges sign cosets, so their edge-query Littlestone dimensions agree. By induction, this positive branch has Littlestone dimension \(n-3\). Accordingly no rooted complete tree can have depth exceeding \(n-2\), since its positive child would have to shatter depth at least \(n-2\). The same argument applies to the odd family, finishing the proof.

**Adaptive parity blindness below the cutoff.** In a deterministic query tree with at most \(n-2\) distinct cell queries on every root-to-leaf path, each leaf specifies a (possibly shorter) partial assignment on a support of size at most \(n-2\). Lemma 1 balances its two parity counts. Repeated tests contribute either a redundant constraint or an impossible leaf, so the statement holds for all deterministic trees of depth at most \(n-2\), including those with repeated queries. The cutoff is sharp for trace-multiplicity equality: prescribe the positive membership of any \(n-1\) disjoint cells. Its unique completion has exactly one parity, so the two parity counts differ.

## Verification
The included Python 3 program `verify.py` independently enumerates every assignment on every support of cardinality at most \(n-2\), for all \(2\le n\le5\), and checks equality of parity-specific trace counts. It additionally checks the exact factorial inclusion-exclusion multiplicity on every shattered matching support, verifies the signed parity-leakage formula for every response on every matching support of size \(n-1\), and confirms nonshattering, and tests the polynomial coefficients. The separate C++17 checker `verify.cpp` enumerates all \(66{,}712\) supports of size at most four on the \(6\)-square board and verifies every trace is equally frequent in both parity classes. It also verifies all \(5{,}040\) matching supports of sizes five and six fail to shatter and checks the first signed parity-leakage coefficient across all \(4{,}320\) size-five matching supports. A separate Python verifier `verify_adaptive.py` computes the exact optimal adaptive transcript imbalance for all query trees on \(2\)-, \(3\)-, and \(4\)-square boards and matches the theorem. All replay commands and actual outputs are in `VERIFICATION.md`. These finite tests corroborate but are not substitutes for the universal proof.

## Relationship to prior work
The 2019 manuscript by Bucić, Devlin, Hendon, Horne and Lund studies perfect-matching intersection and graph derangements, not signed Boolean edge-incidence traces. The 2021 first version of Johnson and Wickes studies shattering subsets of *symbols* by relative orders, not binary row-image cell queries. General Littlestone theory in Alon–Livni–Malliaris–Moran (2018) and Ghazi–Golowich–Kumar–Manurangsi (2020) does not yield the present exact assignment-family dimensions.

Related exact studies of permutation assignment concepts establish the derangement-support complex and the online ranks after deletion of a matching; both impose an **edge exclusion** rather than a permutation-sign restriction. Another result treats even permutations with *pair-comparison* queries. A previous finite census of fixed-point-free even permutations on six symbols concerns a smaller concept class on a different domain and does not imply the unrestricted all-order parity-balanced trace theorem. The classical facts that \(\mathfrak A_n\) is \((n-2)\)-transitive, that rook placements are counted by \(\binom nk^2k!\), and that complete bipartite graphs admit one-factorizations are standard inputs; none is claimed separately as new. The classical \((n-2)\)-transitivity of the alternating group already predicts low-order parity blindness, so that fact alone is not treated as novel. The additional assessed claim is the **exact first nonzero signed trace law**, the resulting sharp worst-case adaptive statistical distinguishing advantage, and its connection to the full shattering/online-dimension classification. Neither the signed boundary coefficient nor the adaptive optimality formula is supplied by the inspected exact sources.

## Limitations
No claim is made for restrictions other than the two full sign cosets, or for the ordering-query meaning of permutation shattering. The last two ranks of the chessboard complex are deliberately excluded, and no general homotopy classification beyond the skeleton identification is asserted. The signed count is an equality of multiplicities, not an assertion that all Boolean patterns are realizable: incompatible positive cells yield zero in both classes. Historical-priority searches may miss results under alternating-group designs, partial injections, or permutation-code terminology. The proof establishes the exact mathematical statements, while independent mathematical audit and exhaustive historical priority certification remain outstanding.
