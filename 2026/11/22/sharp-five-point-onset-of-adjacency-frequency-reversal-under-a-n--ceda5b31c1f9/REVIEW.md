The extension property gives exactly \(I(G)^2\) nonadjacent and \(B(G)\) adjacent ordered types. The exhaustive five-vertex edge-addition enumeration covers every labelled triangle-free graph and permissible new edge; every candidate is checked with exact integers, and every negative edge-marked pair is canonicalized by all relabellings. A separate four-state enumeration reproduces both channel counts. The star family has an exact partition-function factorization, and the negative sign for all \(m\ge4\) follows from a strict geometric comparison. Finite tests are not used as proof for all parameters.

Establishing the sharp minimal obstruction, the two structurally distinct first witnesses, and an infinite star family identifies a genuine limitation of monotonic reasoning for finite-base logical types. The result is not a claim about a random vertex distribution.
