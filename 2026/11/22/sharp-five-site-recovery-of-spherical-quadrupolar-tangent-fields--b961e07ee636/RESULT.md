# Sharp five-site recovery of spherical quadrupolar tangent fields and the spin-one latitude obstruction

## Finding

Let \(\mathcal V_\ell\) be the complex tangent-vector spherical-harmonic eigenspace on \(S^2\) generated by \(\nabla_S Y_{\ell m}\) and \(J\nabla_S Y_{\ell m}\), for \(-\ell\le m\le\ell\) and \(\ell\ge1\). Here \(J\) is the intrinsic rotation of a tangent vector through \(\pi/2\), and the source inner product is the full-sphere \(L^2\) inner product. For a nonpolar latitude \(h=\cos\theta\in(-1,1)\), measure both orthonormal tangent components at the \(M\) equispaced sites \(\phi_j=2\pi j/M\), dividing the summed squared measurement norm by \(M\).

**All-degree sampling criterion.** For \(M\ge 2\ell+1\), this vector-valued sampling map is injective if and only if \(h\) belongs to none of the following explicit nodal sets:

\[
\{h:P_\ell'(h)=0\},\qquad
\bigcup_{m=1}^{\ell}\{h:P_{\ell-m}^{(m-1,m+1)}(h)P_{\ell-m}^{(m+1,m-1)}(h)=0\}.
\]

Here \(P_\ell\) is the ordinary Legendre polynomial and \(P_n^{(\alpha,\beta)}\) a Jacobi polynomial. Counting zeros with the indicated polynomial multiplicity, there are exactly \(\ell^2-1\) interior forbidden-latitude occurrences. The equator is injective precisely when \(\ell\) is odd. For \(M<2\ell+1\), injectivity is impossible at every latitude by dimension. All nonexceptional latitudes admit an explicit reconstruction by one discrete Fourier transform and inversion of \(2\times2\) helicity blocks.

**Sharp quadrupole design.** For \(\ell=2\), the complete forbidden set is \(\{-1/2,0,1/2\}\), each causing rank eight rather than ten for \(M\ge5\). In the orthonormal source basis \(\nabla_S Y_{2m}/\sqrt6,J\nabla_S Y_{2m}/\sqrt6\), with the scalar harmonics normalized in spherical probability measure, the squared singular values at a latitude \(h\) are exactly \(5q_i(|h|)/4\), each with multiplicity two, where

\[
\begin{aligned}
q_0(t)&=6t^2(1-t^2),\\
q_{1-}(t)&=(1+t)^2(1-2t)^2,\\
q_{1+}(t)&=(1-t)^2(1+2t)^2,\\
q_{2+}(t)&=(1-t^2)(1+t)^2,\\
q_{2-}(t)&=(1-t^2)(1-t)^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

For every \(M\ge5\) the unique optimal **absolute height** among injective uniformly weighted regular latitude polygons is the root \(t_*\in(1/4,3/10)\) of

\[
10t_*^3-6t_*^2-3t_*+1=0.
\]

Its sharp **squared** spectral condition number is

\[
\boxed{\kappa_{\min}=\frac{(1+t_*)^2}{6t_*^2}=3.97235410006775\ldots,\qquad t_*=0.25759781238788\ldots.}
\]

The two optimal latitude circles are at heights \(h=\pm t_*\); an angular common rotation changes nothing. Taking additional equally spaced samples on the same circle does not improve this normalized conditioning. The theorem is not an optimization over arbitrary unequal node sets, measurements on multiple circles or mixed harmonic degrees.

## Assumptions and scope

The standard unit sphere is parameterized by \(x=(\sin\theta\cos\phi,\sin\theta\sin\phi,\cos\theta)\), \(0<\theta<\pi\). The tangent frame is \((e_\theta,e_\phi)\), with \(J e_\theta=e_\phi\) and \(J e_\phi=-e_\theta\). Complex scalars are permitted, and both tangent components are observed at each node. There is no radial component. The source modes are vector harmonics of one known exact scalar degree, not the band up to that degree. Squared singular values use \(M^{-1}\sum_j\|V(x_j)\|^2\) and the full spherical probability-norm source \(\|V\|_{L^2(S^2,d\omega/4\pi)}^2\); rescaling the source or samples uniformly leaves the squared condition number unchanged. The regular-polygon result needs \(M\ge2\ell+1\) and counts nodes, not measured scalar components.

## Proof

Fix \(\ell\). Choose normalized scalar spherical harmonics \(Y_{\ell m}(\theta,\phi)=c_{\ell m}P_\ell^m(h)e^{im\phi}\) with \(h=\cos\theta\). One can use Condon–Shortley associated Legendre functions and \(c_{\ell m}=\sqrt{(2\ell+1)(\ell-m)!/(\ell+m)!}\) for nonnegative \(m\), and the standard conjugation convention for negative \(m\). Since \(\int|\nabla_S Y_{\ell m}|^2=\ell(\ell+1)\), the gradient and rotated-gradient modes divided by \(\sqrt{\ell(\ell+1)}\) form an orthonormal source basis; their mutual integral vanishes by Stokes' theorem, or by the Hodge decomposition.

Write \(a_{\ell m}(h)=\partial_\theta P_\ell^m(\cos\theta)\) and \(b_{\ell m}(h)=P_\ell^m(h)/\sin\theta\). At fixed \(h\), in the local tangent frame the two vector modes of azimuthal index \(m\) have the same phase \(e^{im\phi}\) and, after removing their common nonzero normalization, form the matrix

\[
H_{\ell m}(h)=\begin{pmatrix}a_{\ell m}&-im b_{\ell m}\\im b_{\ell m}&a_{\ell m}\end{pmatrix}.
\]

This Hermitian matrix has eigenvalues \(a_{\ell m}\pm m b_{\ell m}\), and determinant \(a_{\ell m}^2-m^2b_{\ell m}^2\). For \(M\ge2\ell+1\), the discrete Fourier phases for distinct indices \(-\ell\le m\le\ell\) are orthogonal: \(M^{-1}\sum_j e^{i(m-n)\phi_j}=\delta_{mn}\). Thus sampling decomposes into independent helicity blocks, with exactly their local singular spectra. For fewer than \(2\ell+1\) nodes, \(2M<\dim_\mathbb C\mathcal V_\ell=4\ell+2\), proving the universal impossibility assertion.

For \(m=0\), \(a_{\ell0}=-\sin\theta P_\ell'(h)\), so singularity is equivalent to \(P_\ell'(h)=0\). For \(m\ge1\), write \(P_\ell^m(h)=(1-h^2)^{m/2}Q_{\ell m}(h)\), with \(Q_{\ell m}=(-1)^mP_\ell^{(m)}\). Removing the factor \((1-h^2)^{(m-1)/2}\) from the helicity eigenvalues gives the two polynomials

\[
U_{\ell m}^{\pm}(h)=m(h\pm1)Q_{\ell m}(h)-(1-h^2)Q_{\ell m}'(h).
\]

The Jacobi derivative identities, equivalently the Wigner small-\(d\) formula for spin-one harmonics, show that \(U_{\ell m}^{+}\) is a nonzero constant multiple of \((1+h)P_{\ell-m}^{(m-1,m+1)}(h)\), and \(U_{\ell m}^{-}\) is a nonzero constant multiple of \((h-1)P_{\ell-m}^{(m+1,m-1)}(h)\). Endpoint factors do not vanish inside the open latitude interval. Every Jacobi polynomial with parameters greater than \(-1\) has exactly its degree many simple interior roots, while \(P_\ell'\) has \(\ell-1\) interior roots. The listed nodal multiset therefore has cardinality \((\ell-1)+2\sum_{m=1}^{\ell}(\ell-m)=\ell^2-1\). Parity of associated Legendre functions shows that at \(h=0\) each \(m\ne0\) helicity block is invertible, whereas \(P_\ell'(0)=0\) exactly for even \(\ell\). The reconstructing inverse applies the discrete Fourier transform to each observed tangent component and solves the two eigenpolarizations of each nonsingular block.

For \(\ell=2\), substitute \(P_2(h)=(3h^2-1)/2\), \(P_2^1(h)=-3h\sqrt{1-h^2}\), and \(P_2^2(h)=3(1-h^2)\). Their normalized gradients give precisely the five functions \(q_i\) in the theorem, each twice: \(m=0\) contributes one level twice, and \(m=\pm1,\pm2\) each gives the two helicity levels with one copy for each sign. At \(h=0\) the zonal pair is lost, and at \(h=\pm1/2\) one helicity for each of the two signed first orders is lost, giving rank eight at each forbidden height.

To optimize, set \(t=|h|\). On \(0<t<1/2\), direct polynomial comparisons show \(q_{2+}\) is the largest among all five levels and \(\min_i q_i=\min(q_0,q_{1-})\). Indeed \(q_{1+}-q_{1-}=4t(1-2t^2)>0\); \(q_{2-}-q_{1-}=t^2(1+t)(3-5t)>0\); and the remaining inequalities follow by the positive factorizations \(q_{2+}-q_{1-}=t(1+t)^2(4-5t)\), \(q_{2+}-q_{1+}=t^2(1-t)(5t+3)\), \(q_{2+}-q_0=(1-t^2)(1+2t-5t^2)\), and \(q_{2+}-q_{2-}=4t(1-t^2)\). The unique switching point of the minimum is \(t=t_*\), because

\[
q_0-q_{1-}=-(t+1)(10t^3-6t^2-3t+1),
\]

and the cubic decreases strictly on \([0,1/2]\), with opposite endpoint signs. Before \(t_*\), the squared condition number is \(q_{2+}/q_0=(1+t)^2/(6t^2)\), strictly decreasing; after \(t_*\) and before \(1/2\), it is \(q_{2+}/q_{1-}=(1-t^2)/(1-2t)^2\), strictly increasing. For \(1/2\le t<1\), using only the positive levels \(q_{2+}\) and \(q_{2-}\), the squared condition number is at least \(q_{2+}/q_{2-}=((1+t)/(1-t))^2\ge9\), unless a level vanishes (when the condition number is infinite). At \(t_*\), the condition number is less than \(4\). Hence there is no second optimum, proving the global and sharp design claim.

## Verification

The standalone `CHECK.py` builds an independent vector point-evaluation matrix from associated Legendre values, their three-term differentiation recurrence, tangent frames and actual complex exponentials. For \(\ell=2\), it checks the five double singular-value branches against full singular-value decompositions at eleven latitudes, including each singular value and the exact ranks at \(h=0,\pm1/2\). It checks symbolic polynomial factorizations in the conditioning proof and the Jacobi helicity factorization for all \(1\le\ell\le10\) and \(1\le m\le\ell\). It independently checks equatorial parity ranks through degree eight. The analytic polynomial/Jacobi argument, not these bounded tests, proves the result at all degrees.

Reproduction: with Python, NumPy, SciPy and SymPy installed, execute `python CHECK.py`. The checker prints the optimal latitude and squared condition number and exits unsuccessfully on any assertion failure.

## Relationship to prior work

Elahi–Khalid–Kennedy–McEwen (2018) develop near-minimal full-band spin-weighted spherical-harmonic transforms using multiple latitude rings and numerical placement for conditioning. The present claim instead concerns one exact-degree poloidal/toroidal vector eigenspace, with a complete forbidden-latitude criterion and an exact *global* five-site quadrupole conditioning optimum. The Wigner/associated-Jacobi representation underlying the proof is classical, not claimed as new. Le Gia–Li–Wang (2019) develop fast global vector spherical-harmonic transforms from spherical quadrature, not an exact single-ring five-node condition-number theorem. Anh–Olenko–Wang (2021) motivate tangent-vector eigenmodes through fractional stochastic models, not sparse sampling design. Scalar-quadrupole latitude and multi-ring reconstruction theorems in nearby literature concern different unknown spaces and measurements, so they do not imply the vector helicity spectrum stated here.

## Limitations

The theorem requires a priori knowledge of one exact harmonic degree, both tangent components at every node, and equal-weight regular-polygon samples. It does not imply stable reconstruction of arbitrary bandlimited tangent fields, optimality over irregular or multiple-ring samplers, or dimension-uniform conditioning. The optimality proof is for the degree-two problem; general-degree forbidden sets are classified but their optimal conditioning heights are not. Prior literature could contain an equivalent specialized spin-one degree-two calculation not exposed by accessible indexing; historical priority is not independently certified.
