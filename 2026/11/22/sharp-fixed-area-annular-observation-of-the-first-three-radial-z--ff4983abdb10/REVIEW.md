# Scientific review: sharp fixed-area annular observation

## Correctness — PASS

Normalized planar polar area becomes uniform Lebesgue measure in the squared-radius coordinate. The degree-at-most-two restriction Gram at the centered interval has one odd eigenvalue \(q^3\) and an even block of trace \(q(9-10q^2+9q^4)/4\) and determinant \(q^6\). The trace inequality makes its least eigenvalue simple and even. Its eigenpolynomial \(y^2-\beta\), translated to follow any displaced interval, keeps the observation numerator fixed while raising the total energy by \((1/2-2\beta)\delta^2+\delta^4>0\). This proves global uniqueness for all \(0<q<1\), including intervals touching a disk boundary. The derived asymptotic follows from the even-block trace and determinant. Exact symbolic integration, independent planar polar-quadrature checks and 288 numerical offset cases corroborate, but do not substitute for, the analytic proof.

An indexed 2026 study entitled *Sharp thin-annulus Zernike concentration and a Laguerre crossover* concerns an outer-annulus thin-width regime and growing angular orders; its full text could not be inspected, so it remains a material but not demonstrably dominating priority risk. Related polyanalytic disk-localization results optimize other spaces and observations. No examined statement implies the fixed-area all-width maximin theorem or its shifted-eigenfield strict loss. This is a best-knowledge originality assessment, not a universal novelty guarantee.

## Value — PASS

The first three radial Zernike modes form a canonical finite optical-wavefront recovery model. Fixing detector area and varying only the annular placement poses a motivated E-optimal energy acquisition question. Its exact all-area placement, complete optimum spectrum and strict quantitative robustness provide a usable design rule and sharp small-area instability, beyond routine computation of one chosen annular Gram matrix. The result is explicitly limited to this natural three-mode sector, not extrapolated to all Zernike modes.

## Limitations

No exact off-optimum minimum eigenvalue or globally best condition-number placement is claimed. The main theorem is for one annulus of fixed area, not multiple rings, arbitrary masks or point samples. Finite checker cases are not infinite certifications; independent review and complete historical priority assessment remain outstanding.
