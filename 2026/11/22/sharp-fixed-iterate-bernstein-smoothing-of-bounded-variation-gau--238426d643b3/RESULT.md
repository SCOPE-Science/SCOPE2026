# Sharp fixed-iterate Bernstein smoothing of bounded variation: Gaussian constants and median rigidity

## Finding

Write \(b_{n,k}(x)=\binom nk x^k(1-x)^{n-k}\) for \(0\leq k\leq n\), \(0\leq x\leq1\). Consider the classical Bernstein–Kantorovich and Bernstein–Durrmeyer operators on real functions:
\[
(K_nf)(x)=(n+1)\sum_{k=0}^n b_{n,k}(x)
       \int_{k/(n+1)}^{(k+1)/(n+1)}f(y)\,dy,
\]
\[
(D_nf)(x)=(n+1)\sum_{k=0}^n b_{n,k}(x)
       \int_0^1b_{n,k}(y)f(y)\,dy.
\]
Let \(r\geq1\) be a fixed integer and \(1\leq p<\infty\). For \(T\in\{K,D\}\) define
\[
C_{n,r,p}^{T}=\sup_{f\in BV(0,1),\;|Df|(0,1)>0}
\frac{\|(I-T_n^{r})f\|_{L^p(0,1)}}{|Df|(0,1)}.
\]
Here \(|Df|(0,1)\) is distributional total variation and \(T_n^r\) means \(r\) compositions with the same \(n\). Let \(\Phi\) denote the standard normal distribution function, and put
\[
I_p=2\int_0^\infty[1-\Phi(u)]^p\,du,\qquad v_K=1,\quad v_D=2.
\]
**Theorem.** For each fixed \(r\in\mathbb N\), \(1\leq p<\infty\), and either operator,
\[
\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty}n^{1/(2p)} C_{n,r,p}^{T}
=\left(\frac{\sqrt{r v_T}}2 I_p\right)^{1/p}.}
\]
For each finite \(n\), a unit jump is an extremizer: if \(H_t=\mathbf1_{(t,1)}\), then \(C_{n,r,p}^{T}=\max_{0\leq t\leq1}\|(I-T_n^r)H_t\|_p\). Every sequence of maximizing jump positions tends to \(1/2\). More generally, if \(f_n\in BV(0,1)\) is asymptotically extremal in the sense that
\[
\frac{\|(I-T_n^r)f_n\|_p}{C_{n,r,p}^{T}|Df_n|(0,1)}\longrightarrow1,
\]
then its normalized variation measures concentrate at the midpoint: for every fixed \(\delta>0\),
\[
\frac{|Df_n|\big((0,1)\setminus(1/2-\delta,1/2+\delta)\big)}{|Df_n|(0,1)}\longrightarrow0.
\]
At the endpoint \(p=\infty\) there is a different exact result:
\[
\boxed{C_{n,r,\infty}^{K}=C_{n,r,\infty}^{D}=1}\quad(n,r\geq1),
\]
where the supremum is interpreted with essential supremum; it need not be attained by a fixed positive-width jump.

The coefficients distinguish the two integral mechanisms. In particular \(I_1=\sqrt{2/\pi}\) and \(I_2=(\sqrt2-1)/\sqrt\pi\), so for the same fixed \(r,p\) the leading Durrmeyer error exceeds the Kantorovich error by the exact factor \(2^{1/(2p)}\), and changing the iteration count from one to \(r\) changes the leading norm by \(r^{1/(2p)}\). The cases \(p=1,r=1\) belong to the established sharp-error literature; the contribution is the common fixed-iteration, all-finite-\(p\) asymptotic, its posterior-variance factor, and variation-measure rigidity.

## Assumptions and scope

The input functions are real and taken modulo almost-everywhere equivalence, with distributional variation restricted to the open interval. A unit step at \(t\in(0,1)\) has variation one. The end steps \(t=0,1\) are constant almost everywhere and have residual zero. The operator degree \(n\) tends to infinity while \(p\) and \(r\) are fixed; the statements do **not** assert uniformity for growing \(r\) or \(p\). The normalization of the interval and of Lebesgue \(L^p\) is exactly as displayed above.

## Proof

**1. Reduction to single jumps.** Both operators are positive and reproduce constants, as do their iterates. Every real \(f\in BV(0,1)\) has an almost-everywhere representation \(f(x)=c+\int_{(0,1)}H_t(x)\,d\mu(t)\), where \(\mu=Df\) and \(|\mu|((0,1))=|Df|(0,1)\). Minkowski's integral inequality therefore gives
\[
\|(I-T_n^r)f\|_p\leq
\int\|(I-T_n^r)H_t\|_p\,d|\mu|(t)
\leq |Df|(0,1)\sup_{0<t<1}\|(I-T_n^r)H_t\|_p.
\]
Taking \(f=H_t\) yields equality for the supremum. For fixed \(n,r,p<\infty\), the right-hand norm depends continuously on \(t\in[0,1]\) (the integral kernels are bounded polynomials, and the unsmoothed steps are continuous in \(L^p\)); it vanishes at the endpoints, and is positive somewhere. Hence the maximum is achieved by an interior jump.

**2. The underlying Markov transitions and their moments.** The kernel of \(K_n\) is the one-step law
\[
Y=\frac{B+U}{n+1},\quad B\sim\operatorname{Bin}(n,x),\quad U\sim\operatorname{Unif}(0,1),
\]
with independent \(B,U\). The kernel of \(D_n\) is generated by \(B\sim\operatorname{Bin}(n,x)\) followed, conditionally on \(B=k\), by \(Y\sim\operatorname{Beta}(k+1,n-k+1)\). Their means and variances are respectively
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_KY&=\frac{nx+1/2}{n+1},&
\operatorname{Var}_K(Y)&=\frac{n x(1-x)+1/12}{(n+1)^2},\\
\mathbb E_DY&=\frac{nx+1}{n+2},&
\operatorname{Var}_D(Y)&=\frac{(n+1)(2n x(1-x)+1)}{(n+2)^2(n+3)}.
\end{aligned}
\]
In particular, writing \(q(x)=x(1-x)\), both chains have one-step drift bounded by \(C/n\) and one-step centered-at-start second moment bounded by \(C(q(x)/n+n^{-2})\), with constants uniform in \(x\) and \(n\geq1\). For the chain \(X_0=x\), \(X_{j+1}\) given \(X_j\) by the chosen kernel, the concavity of \(q\), Jensen's inequality, and the bounded drift imply
\[
\mathbb E q(X_j)\leq q(\mathbb E X_j)\leq q(x)+Cj/n.
\]
Cauchy–Schwarz applied to the sum of \(r\) increments gives the uniform fixed-iterate estimate
\[
\mathbb E_x[(X_r-x)^2]\leq C_r\left(\frac{q(x)}n+\frac1{n^2}\right).\tag{1}
\]

**3. Interior Gaussian limit, also for moving centers.** If \(x_n\to a\in(0,1)\), the binomial central limit theorem implies
\[
\sqrt n(Y-x_n)\Longrightarrow N(0,q(a))\quad\text{for }K_n.
\]
For \(D_n\), use the independent gamma-sum representation of a beta variable: conditional on \(B\), take independent gamma variables of integer shapes \(B+1\) and \(n-B+1\) and divide the first by their sum. Their centered fluctuations, together with the binomial fluctuation of \(B\), yield two asymptotically independent Gaussian terms, each of variance \(q(a)\). Thus the one-step limit is \(N(0,2q(a))\). These conditional limits hold uniformly for initial points in any compact subinterval of \((0,1)\), by uniform binomial/gamma normal approximation on such sets. For fixed \(r\), (1) ensures the intermediate positions converge in probability to \(a\); successive characteristic-function conditioning (equivalently a bounded-length martingale-array central limit argument) now yields
\[
\sqrt n(X_r-x_n)\Longrightarrow N(0,r v_T q(a)).\tag{2}
\]
No nonvanishing drift survives multiplication by \(\sqrt n\).

**4. A complete scaled jump profile.** For a moving jump \(t_n\to a\in(0,1)\), put \(x_n=t_n+z/\sqrt n\). Since \(T_n^rH_{t_n}(x_n)=\mathbb P_{x_n}\{X_r>t_n\}\), (2) gives
\[
T_n^rH_{t_n}(t_n+z/\sqrt n)
\longrightarrow \Phi\left(\frac{z}{\sqrt{r v_Tq(a)}}\right).
\]
Consequently the pointwise \(p\)-power error profile tends to
\[
\begin{cases}
\Phi(z/\sigma)^p,&z<0,\\
[1-\Phi(z/\sigma)]^p,&z>0,
\end{cases}\qquad\sigma=\sqrt{r v_T q(a)}.
\]
Estimate (1) and Chebyshev give, at every \(z\ne0\), the integrable domination \(\min\{1,C_r/z^2\}\) for the scaled \(p\)-power error, uniformly when \(t_n\) stays in a compact subinterval. Indeed error probabilities lie in \([0,1]\), so raising them to \(p\geq1\) only decreases them. After the substitution \(z=\sqrt n(x-t_n)\), dominated convergence proves
\[
\lim_{n\to\infty}\sqrt n\,\|(I-T_n^r)H_{t_n}\|_p^p
=\sigma I_p=I_p\sqrt{r v_Ta(1-a)}.\tag{3}
\]
The identity holds for any convergent interior sequence of jump positions, not just a fixed jump.

**5. Excluding both endpoints uniformly.** For arbitrary \(t\in(0,1)\) and \(y=|x-t|>0\), the event that a jump at \(t\) is evaluated incorrectly implies \(|X_r-x|\geq y\). By (1), \(q(x)\leq q(t)+y\), and Chebyshev,
\[
|H_t(x)-T_n^rH_t(x)|^p\leq
\min\left\{1,C_r\left(\frac{q(t)/n+n^{-2}}{y^2}+\frac1{ny}\right)\right\}.
\]
Integrating on both sides of \(t\) and splitting the integral at \(y=\sqrt{q(t)/n+n^{-2}}\), and for the logarithmic term at \(y=1/n\), gives
\[
\|(I-T_n^r)H_t\|_p^p
\leq C_{r,p}\left(\sqrt{\frac{q(t)}n+\frac1{n^2}}+\frac{1+\log(n+1)}n\right).\tag{4}
\]
Therefore if \(t_n\to0\) or \(t_n\to1\), the scaled expression on the left of (3) tends to zero. Since the limit in (3) is strictly positive at \(t=1/2\), maximizing positions eventually stay away from the endpoints. Any subsequential limit \(a\) of such maximizing positions must maximize \(\sqrt{a(1-a)}\); its unique maximizer is \(a=1/2\). Inserting this into (3) proves the sharp constant and localization of all maximizing jumps.

**6. Rigidity for all near-extremizing inputs.** Fix \(\delta>0\). The compactness and uniform moving-center conclusions of (3)–(4) imply that, for some \(\eta_\delta>0\) and all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
\sup_{|t-1/2|\geq\delta}\|(I-T_n^r)H_t\|_p
\leq(1-\eta_\delta)C_{n,r,p}^T.
\]
Split the derivative measure in Step 1 into its inside and outside parts. The same Minkowski inequality then bounds the quotient by \(1-\eta_\delta\alpha_n\), where \(\alpha_n\) is the fraction of variation outside the indicated midpoint neighborhood. A quotient tending to one forces \(\alpha_n\to0\), as claimed.

**7. Endpoint norm.** Positivity and constant reproduction give \(\|(I-T_n^r)H_t\|_\infty\leq1\), hence \(C_{n,r,\infty}^T\leq1\) by the same derivative-measure argument. For fixed \(n,r\), the transition started at zero has a continuous distribution supported on \((0,1)\), so \(\mathbb P_0\{X_r>t\}\to1\) as \(t\downarrow0\). Continuity of the polynomial kernel in the initial point shows that when \(0<x<t\) and \(t\downarrow0\), the smoothed step approaches one uniformly in a small interval. The unsmoothed step there is zero. Thus the supremum of the residual essential norm is one.

The formulas \(I_1=\sqrt{2/\pi}\) and \(I_2=(\sqrt2-1)/\sqrt\pi\) follow by integrating one and two Gaussian survival functions, using Fubini and symmetry.

## Verification

The self-contained checker `checks/check.py` constructs both kernels as rational matrices on their Bernstein coefficient spaces. It evaluates single-jump residual polynomials exactly for \(1\leq n\leq7\), \(r=1,2,3\) and \(p=1,2\), and checks their stochasticity and reflection symmetries. This produced **266 exact-rational assertions**. Its optional NumPy/SciPy extension performed **16 numerical assertions** comparing quadrature at \(n=24,48\), \(r=1,2,3\) to the predicted central Gaussian scale (maximum relative deviation about \(1.15\%\)). These checks are finite corroboration; the theorem for all \(n\) and the asymptotic limits depend on Steps 1–7, not an extrapolation from the numerics. Run with `python checks/check.py` from the package root (Python 3.10+; NumPy and SciPy optional).

## Relationship to prior work

Classical work of Hoeffding established sharp \(L^1\) approximation theory for Bernstein-type polynomials; a sharp one-step \(L^1\) result is therefore not claimed anew. Jones's 1976 \(L^2\) Bernstein-paper concerns the point-sampling operator (and the accessible 2013 dissertation exposition presents an absolutely continuous-input result), not the two integral operators under every fixed iteration. Öksüzer–Karsli–Yeşildal (first public preprint dated 2016-04-30) proved variation-diminishing bounds and variation convergence for \(D_n\), rather than this sharp residual norm. Berdysheva–Dyn–Farkhi–Mokhov later proved general set-valued, pointwise, and nonsharp \(L^1\) error estimates for the same two integral kernels, not the all-\(p\), fixed-iterate limiting coefficient or variation-measure rigidity. Adell–Garrancho–Martínez-Sánchez studied pointwise discontinuity errors and concentration arguments, also without a covering integrated sharp norm. These statement-level comparisons do not imply proof of historical uniqueness.

## Limitations

The result treats real scalar-valued bounded-variation input and fixed iteration counts; it does not classify every finite-degree maximizing jump or quantify the speed of midpoint localization. It does not establish a uniform double limit when \(r\) or \(p\) grow with degree, nor any multidimensional counterpart. The exact \(p=1,r=1\) special case was already covered by earlier work. The 1976 original Jones paper could not be inspected in full, leaving a limited historical-priority risk for any unsuspected broader method there. Independent mathematical verification has not been performed.
