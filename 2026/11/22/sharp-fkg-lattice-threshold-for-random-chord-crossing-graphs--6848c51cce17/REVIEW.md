# Scientific review: sharp FKG lattice threshold for random chord-crossing graphs

## Correctness — PASS

The probability space is the labelled circle graph generated by independent uniform endpoint pairs, not a graph sampled uniformly from graph isomorphism classes. For three chords, all eight exact state masses are positive, and two elementary-square inequalities establish MTP2. For four chords, the exact probabilities of the empty graph, the two specified single-edge graphs, and their disjoint union yield a strict lattice ratio \(6/7\). The finite Boolean marginal-closure lemma is proved algebraically in RESULT.md, so a four-chord obstruction propagates to all larger graphs. Positive edge activities cancel in the lattice ratios. Independent endpoint reasoning gives covariance \(1/45\) for crossing events sharing a chord and zero for disjoint chord pairs. The attached exact-rational verifier reproduces the finite probabilities from two independent exhaustive models for one through five chords. No finite experiment is presented as proof of an infinite assertion.

## Originality — PASS, with a qualified priority assessment

Acan and Pittel (2014) investigate crossing-conditioned component behavior; Acan (2015) investigates degree and component properties; Acan's 2013 dissertation enumerates forest and unicyclic chord diagrams. No directly covering MTP2 classification or explicit four-to-all-chord lattice obstruction was located in the relevant inspected material. Karlin and Rinott (1980) provide general MTP2 marginalization theory, and Paguyo (2021) treats crossing-count and short-chord limit theorems; none by itself proves this specific distributional classification. The small-diagram counts may be obtainable from classical enumeration, and older unindexed equivalent formulations remain possible; neither the counts alone nor the generic marginal-closure theorem is represented as original.

## Value — PASS

FKG log-supermodularity is a standard robust sufficient condition for positive association in random graph models. This is a complete sharp classification of its applicability to a natural dependent-edge random graph ensemble. The result is not merely a negative numerical example: it proves a universal all-size cutoff, preserves it under arbitrary positive edgewise tilts, and contrasts it with nonnegative pairwise covariances. It identifies an exact boundary for transferring FKG-based arguments to random circle graphs.

## Limits and evidence

The theorem neither disproves positive association nor classifies all increasing-event covariances. It concerns independent uniform endpoint pairs and external-field tilts only. The main sources are arXiv:1406.2867, arXiv:1501.01489, Acan's 2013 dissertation, Karlin–Rinott (1980), and arXiv:2104.01134. The marginalization lemma and the independent crossing-indicator covariance computations are self-contained; finite counts are independently checked by `verify_chord_mtp2.py`. Acan's thesis includes broader forest enumeration, so an implicit earlier derivation of the finite witness cannot be ruled out. The analysis is a self-review, not an independent audit.
