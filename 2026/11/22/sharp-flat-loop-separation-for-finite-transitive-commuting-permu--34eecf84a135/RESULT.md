# Sharp flat-loop separation for finite transitive commuting-permutation systems

## Finding
Fix an integer \(N\ge2\). Consider all nonempty pointed finite transition structures \((W,f,g,w)\) of size at most \(N\), with two *named* total bijective atomic actions \(f,g\) satisfying \(fg=gf\), whose generated group is transitive. There are no propositional letters. Converse actions are available. A *flat word-loop test* is the formula \(\operatorname{loop}(f^{u}g^{v})\) for integers \(u,v\), where negative powers mean explicitly repeated converse actions, zero powers are omitted, and its action-word length is \(|u|+|v|\).

**One complete finite-model theorem.** Every such pointed structure determines its stabilizer lattice
\[
L=\{(u,v)\in\mathbb Z^2:f^{u}g^{v}(w)=w\}.
\]
Two structures satisfy precisely the same formulas of proposition-free converse propositional dynamic logic with unrestricted loop operators (including regular programs, Boolean formulas, tests and nesting) if and only if their lattices agree. For each \(d\ge1\), there are exactly \(\sigma_1(d)=\sum_{a\mid d}a\) such logical equivalence classes with exactly \(d\) states; thus among models with at most \(N\) states there are
\[
C(N)=\sum_{d=1}^{N}\sigma_1(d)=\frac{\pi^2}{12}N^2+O(N\log N)
\]
classes. Crucially, any two distinct classes at size at most \(N\) are distinguished by one flat word-loop test of length **at most \(N-1\)**, and \(N-1\) is the exact worst-case minimum possible length. Without the loop operator, proposition-free converse PDL identifies all these pointed structures: it has one equivalence class.

## Assumptions and scope
Actions are individually named and interpreted as commuting permutations, not arbitrary relations; transitivity means every world is \(f^ug^v(w)\) for some integers \(u,v\). The signature has no propositional predicates or named states. A length bound is claimed only for single *flat* loop tests of uncompressed action words, not for arbitrary regular-expression programs, formula symbol length, automata complexity, or unrestricted models. The index of \(L\) in \(\mathbb Z^2\) is \(|W|\). Formulas use the standard binary relation interpretation of converse, composition, union, Kleene star, tests and loop.

## Proof
**1. Stabilizer normal form and logical completeness.** The commuting permutations define a transitive \(\mathbb Z^2\)-action. As \(\mathbb Z^2\) is abelian, its point stabilizer is independent of the chosen world. The map \((u,v)+L\mapsto f^ug^v(w)\) identifies the action with \(\mathbb Z^2/L\). Conversely every finite-index subgroup \(L\) yields precisely this transitive action. If \(L=L'\), the coordinate identification is an isomorphism of pointed two-action models and preserves all formulas, even those containing regular programs and nested loops. If \(L\ne L'\), choose \((u,v)\) in the symmetric difference. Exactly one quotient satisfies \(\operatorname{loop}(f^ug^v)\). Thus full logical equivalence is exactly lattice equality.

**2. Exact census.** Each index-\(d\) subgroup has a unique column Hermite normal form with generators \((a,0),(b,c)\), where \(a,c\ge1\), \(ac=d\), and \(0\le b<a\). For each divisor \(a\mid d\), there are \(a\) possible \(b\), giving \(\sigma_1(d)\). Since \(\mathbb Z^2/L\) has exactly \(d\) points, the count is simultaneously a count of classes and of minimal realization sizes. Swapping the order of summation gives
\[
\sum_{d\le N}\sigma_1(d)=\sum_{k\le N}\frac{\lfloor N/k\rfloor(\lfloor N/k\rfloor+1)}2
=\frac{N^2}{2}\sum_{k\ge1}\frac1{k^2}+O(N\log N)
=\frac{\pi^2}{12}N^2+O(N\log N).
\]

**3. Universal short word test.** A subgroup with the displayed normal form is generated by vectors of \(\ell_1\)-norm at most its index \(d\): \(a\le d\) and \(b+c\le a-1+c\le ac=d\). Let \(L\ne L'\) have indices \(d\le d'\le N\). If \(d\le N-1\), at least one of the two normal-form generators of \(L\) is absent from \(L'\), since \(L\subseteq L'\) would imply \(d\ge d'\), with equality forcing \(L=L'\). Therefore a separating vector has norm at most \(d\le N-1\).

It remains to consider \(d=d'=N\). Take normal-form generators \((a,0),(b,c)\) of \(L\) with \(ac=N\). If \(a,c\ge2\), both generators have norm at most \(N-1\), since \(a\le N-1\) and \(b+c\le a+c-1\le ac-1\). A distinct index-\(N\) lattice cannot contain both. If \(a=1\), \(L\) contains the primitive vector \((1,0)\). If \(c=1\) and \(b\le N-2\), it contains the primitive vector \((b,1)\) of norm at most \(N-1\). If \(c=1\), \(b=N-1\), and \(N\ge3\), it contains the primitive vector \((1,-1)=(N,0)-(N-1,1)\), of norm \(2\le N-1\). Any primitive \(z\in\mathbb Z^2\) belongs to **exactly one** subgroup of any prescribed index \(N\): the subgroup is the inverse image of \(N\mathbb Z\) in \(\mathbb Z^2/\mathbb Z z\cong\mathbb Z\). Hence each of these short primitive vectors is absent from \(L'\). The remaining exceptional case is \(N=2\), \(L=\{(u,v):u+v\text{ even}\}\). Every other index-two subgroup contains either \((1,0)\) or \((0,1)\), and so a length-one test distinguishes it. This proves the bound.

**4. Sharpness.** Take \(L_N=N\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z\) and \(L_{N-1}=(N-1)\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z\), realized by cycles of lengths \(N\) and \(N-1\) for \(f\) with \(g\) the identity. Every vector of positive norm less than \(N-1\) belongs to both lattices precisely when its first coordinate is zero; but \((N-1,0)\) belongs only to the second. Hence their first distinguishing flat word-loop length is exactly \(N-1\).

**5. Loop-free collapse.** The full relation between the worlds of any two such models is a bisimulation for the atomic actions and their converses: each relation is total and serial in both directions. In the absence of propositional labels all worlds match. Induction on programs, tests and formulas (or PDL bisimulation invariance) gives identical truth of all proposition-free converse-PDL formulas. This is a single equivalence class without loop.

## Verification
Execute `python verify.py` using Python 3. The standalone script enumerates all index-at-most-20 Hermite lattices; checks their number against divisor sums; tests that the flat-loop signatures through length \(N-1\) are injective for every \(2\le N\le20\); independently executes the two commuting permutations on all points for hundreds of thousands of small loop tests; and checks exact worst-case witnesses for each bound. The script is a finite consistency check, not the proof of any all-\(N\) assertion. The all-size argument is Steps 1–5 above.

## Relationship to prior work
The recent LoopPDL work of Bednarczyk and Swoboda (first publicly posted 2026-10-11) treats the general finite satisfiability problem and its regular-cactus constructions. The present statement instead concerns the exact model-theoretic separation behavior of the restricted, transitive commuting-permutation class. Loop and converse semantics are standard, as reviewed in the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy. Hermite normal forms, subgroup index counts \(\sigma_1(d)\), and the divisor-sum asymptotic are classical facts, not new arithmetic results. The additional assertion that a **single uncompressed loop word of sharp length \(N-1\)** separates any two size-\(N\)-bounded logical types is the quantitative claim. Published single-generator loop-language classifications do not directly imply this two-generator, coupled-stabilizer bound.

## Limitations
No complexity claim is made for formula satisfiability or full LoopPDL outside the stated class. Nor is the short length bound a shortest arbitrary-formula bound: regular expressions with repetition or tests are not costed by flat word length. Prior literature may contain a lattice-separation theorem in different language; priority has not been established by independent specialist review. The enumerative identities apart from their logical interpretation are classical. The provided finite checker is not a proof for unbounded sizes.
