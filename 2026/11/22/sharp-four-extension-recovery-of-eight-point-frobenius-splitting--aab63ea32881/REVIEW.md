# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The standard genus-three hyperelliptic theta-subset description identifies odd characteristics with pairs of branch points. The Aronhold triple-parity criterion gives exactly the \(8\) stars and \(280\) four-stars-with-disjoint-triangle graphs. Counting invariant three-subsets produces the uniform formula for the number of rational heptads. Every one of the \(22\) partitions of eight has a checked complete Frobenius spectrum; all \(22\) four-count signatures are distinct and the unique degree-three collision is realized by squarefree degree-eight polynomials over each odd finite field. The standalone checker supplies a separate enumeration of the same finite configurations, not a substitute for the structural proof.

## Originality
PASS, qualified. Bergvall (arXiv:2106.13072, §§2.4 and 3.3) describes the \(288\) heptads and arithmetic counts of marked nonhyperelliptic moduli spaces, not the fixed-heptad scheme of individual hyperelliptic curves. Dolgachev's book already gives the \(8+280\) symmetric-group orbit decomposition, so it is explicitly credited. Gizatullin's §8 studies a block-design dissection of the \(288\) heptads, with no matching Frobenius inverse theorem in the examined section. A public computation of the integral heptad incidence lattice concerns another invariant. No reviewed source was shown to give the complete \(22\)-case fixed-heptad spectrum or the sharp four-extension recovery theorem; an unindexed equivalent cycle-index result remains a priority risk.

## Mathematical value
PASS. Rational heptads are natural finite étale level-two incidence structures. A single such configuration scheme recovers all eight-branch Frobenius splitting types from a bounded number of point counts, and the precise sharp obstruction is realized over every odd finite field. This contrasts with natural theta-tetrad spectra that can have permanent splitting ambiguities. The result concerns the arithmetic of configurations, not geometric reconstruction of the curve.

## Limits
The theorem uses the known theta-characteristic model and known \(288\)-heptad count, and is restricted to smooth genus-three hyperelliptic curves in odd characteristic. It does not recover branch coordinates or identify a curve uniquely. Historical completeness is not certified.
