# Scientific review: rectangular Thales-disk multiplicity transition

## Correctness
**PASS.** For each quadrant the four disk membership inequalities order by the scalar products with rectangle corners; direct Euclidean estimates ensure the closest-corner disk covers the quadrant. The second-largest projection makes the exact one-coverage condition radial. Comparing its cutoff to the rectangle boundary reduces nonemptiness to the quadratic \(2u^2-tu+1>0\), whose discriminant is \(t^2-8\). This proves all topology and endpoint assertions without sampling. Polar integration and an explicit derivative of \(G_t\) establish the exact area. Taylor expansion of the rational correction integrand at the repeated root yields the nonanalytic coefficient \(2\,2^{1/4}/9\). A standard-library independent Cartesian-disk membership checker and polar quadrature passed for ten aspect ratios, and rational algebra checked the correction-integrand identity. The analytic proof covers the continuum; the numerical checks do not.

## Originality
**PASS, subject to historical risk.** Pavlík's arXiv:2611.14277v1, Section 5, proves covering and a power-diagram partition but not multiplicity geometry or aspect-ratio transitions. The published regular-polygon multiplicity record supplies the square case but explicitly restricts to regular polygons, so it cannot imply the nonsquare rectangle formula or the transition at \(2\sqrt2\). The regular-polygon mean-depth record also lacks this parameter family. The two older side-diameter-disk papers concern a different diameter construction. Similarity search using the objects, aliases, topology and threshold found no equivalent stronger result, but equivalent unindexed configurations are a residual priority risk.

## Mathematical value
**PASS.** Exact one-coverage regions quantify the nonredundant part of a canonical disk covering: unlike the power diagram, they record when a point genuinely loses all alternative disks. The natural one-parameter family of all rectangles has a sharp four-to-eight topological bifurcation and a \(3/2\)-power singularity in its area, distinguishing genuine structure from a routine special-case coverage assertion. The theorem fully classifies this geometric invariant throughout the family without claiming a broader arbitrary-quadrilateral theorem.

## Closest literature and limits
The most relevant primary source is Jan Pavlík's *Thales's theorem in higher dimensions*, arXiv:2611.14277v1 (2026-11-10), especially Theorem 5.1 and Corollary 5.3. The strongest near-overlap is the public regular-polygon full multiplicity-spectrum record (2026), which already determines the square but not unequal-sided rectangles. The full rectangular result has no claim of global optimality among alternate center placements or arbitrary polygons. Historical indexing is incomplete; the result has no independent mathematical audit.
