# Review of sharp Fourier-bias balancing on incomplete finite-field line pencils

## Correctness (C): PASS

The claimed domain is every prime power \(q\ge2\), every \(2\le k\le q\), every selection of \(k\) different one-dimensional directions, every nontrivial field additive character, and every nonnegative probability measure supported on the pencil. The proof symmetrizes arbitrary measures without increasing the nonzero-frequency \(\ell^\infty\) bias. Exact annihilator character sums and the counting of remaining nonzero dual directions reduce the minimax to \(\max\{|t|,(1+(k-1)t)/k\}\) on \([-1/(q-1),1]\). Its unique minimum yields the two branches and sharp positive measures. Preservation of the center atom through both symmetrizations proves rigidity for every optimizer, not merely symmetric optimizers. The case \(k=q+1\) is deliberately excluded. Direct finite-field Fourier tests at eight field orders passed 4,376 supplementary coefficient comparisons. The universal proof is analytic.

## Originality (O): PASS relative to inspected sources

Fraser's *Fourier analytic properties of Kakeya sets in finite fields* (first arXiv appearance 2025-05-14, arXiv:2505.09464; 2026 journal version, Theorem 2.1 and §3.1) proves a general existence bound for measures supported on direction covers. For \(k\) concurrent planar directions, its incidence construction yields \(1/k\) as an off-zero Fourier upper bound. It does not determine the exact optimum or force an origin weight. The present bias is always strictly below \(1/k\) for \(k\ge2\), and all optimizer origin atoms are classified. Iosevich–Li–Yu (arXiv:2609.25404v2, §1 and §4) optimize a distinct full-Fourier \(\ell^\infty/\ell^r\) ratio, not the probability-measure offzero minimax. The closest indexed finite Kakeya results consider other supports and/or the uniform measure; prior sharp synthesis constants on line pencils concern another functional. Queries for aliases, full coverage, numerical tables, and implications were conducted in primary literature and related published mathematical results. No direct equivalent result was found. Nonindexed earlier min-bias work is a residual risk; no independence or exhaustive-literature certification is claimed.

## Mathematical value (V): PASS

Partial-direction pencils are the simplest exact model in the Fourier-flatness problem for finite-field Kakeya direction covers. Rather than just providing another upper bound, the finding gives an all-prime-power, all-orientation, all-admissible-direction-count **optimal** offzero Fourier bias, the precise transition between a flat nontrivial spectrum and a two-level spectrum at \(q=2k\), and a rigidity theorem forcing the central atom in every optimal probability measure. The optimal measure changes support exactly at the threshold; uniform mass is optimal only when \(q=2k-2\). These linked geometric and spectral features answer a naturally motivated minimax question, and are not implied by the general direction-cover existence theorem or the previously catalogued signed synthesis norms. The continuum of nonsymmetric optimizers is not classified.
