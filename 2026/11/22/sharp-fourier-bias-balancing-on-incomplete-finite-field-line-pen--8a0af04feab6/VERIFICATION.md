The proof uses exact frequency-line averages for arbitrary measures. They yield the rigid central mass and, by saturation of the complex disk bound, determine all the nonzero Fourier coefficients in the flat regime and the selected ones in the other regime. The example of distinct minimizers above the threshold is exact; its perturbation vanishes on the selected dual lines and is bounded by the spectral slack on the omitted ones.

A standalone standard-library Python checker is supplied as `check_bias.py` for the canonical construction. It samples field orders \(2,3,4,5,7,8,9,11\) and checks selected Fourier magnitudes numerically. It neither exhausts the continuum of probability measures nor tests all equality cases.

Normalizations: unnormalized finite-field Fourier transform; nonnegative probability masses; maximum only over nonzero frequencies. The full pencil with \(q+1\) directions is excluded.
