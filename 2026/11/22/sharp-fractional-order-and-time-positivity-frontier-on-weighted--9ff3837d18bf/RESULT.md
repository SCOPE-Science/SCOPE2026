# Sharp fractional-order and time positivity frontier on weighted triangles

## Finding

Let three vertices be joined by positive conductances \(a=w_{12}\), \(b=w_{13}\), and \(c=w_{23}\). Define the symmetric combinatorial Laplacian \(L\), its spectral power \(L^\alpha\), and the mass-preserving heat family
\[
K_\alpha(t)=\exp(-tL^\alpha),\qquad \alpha>0,\quad t\ge0.
\]
Put \(S=a+b+c\), \(Q=ab+bc+ca\), and
\[
\lambda_\pm=S\pm\sqrt{S^2-3Q},\qquad m=\min\{a,b,c\}.
\]
If the minimum conductance occurs twice, every \(K_\alpha(t)\) is entrywise nonnegative for every \(\alpha>0\) and \(t\ge0\), and strictly positive for \(t>0\). The same holds if all three conductances are equal.

If the minimum is unique, then \(\lambda_-<\lambda_+\), \(m<\lambda_-/3\), and the *finite sharp Markov-order frontier* is
\[
\alpha_*=
\frac{\log\bigl((\lambda_+/3-m)/(\lambda_-/3-m)\bigr)}
{\log(\lambda_+/\lambda_-) }>1.
\]
For \(0<\alpha\le\alpha_*\), all entries of \(K_\alpha(t)\) are strictly positive for \(t>0\). For \(\alpha>\alpha_*\), exactly the two symmetric entries corresponding to the uniquely weakest edge are negative for \(0<t<T_\alpha\), zero at \(t=T_\alpha\), and positive for \(t>T_\alpha\); all other entries are positive for every \(t>0\). Here \(T_\alpha\) is the unique positive solution of
\[
\frac{1-e^{-\lambda_-^\alpha T}}3
=\frac{\lambda_-/3-m}{\lambda_+-\lambda_-}
\bigl(e^{-\lambda_-^\alpha T}-e^{-\lambda_+^\alpha T}\bigr).
\]
In particular, the full matrix is entrywise nonnegative exactly at \(t=0\) and \(t\ge T_\alpha\), and its induced real \(\ell^\infty\) operator norm is exactly
\[
\|K_\alpha(t)\|_{\infty\to\infty}=
1+2\max\{0,-[K_\alpha(t)]_{ij}\},
\]
where \((i,j)\) is the weakest edge. The sole nontrivial transient peak (when \(\alpha>\alpha_*\)) occurs at
\[
t_{\rm peak}=\frac{1}{\lambda_+^\alpha-\lambda_-^\alpha}
\log\frac{(\lambda_-/3-m)\lambda_+^\alpha}
{(\lambda_+/3-m)\lambda_-^\alpha},
\qquad 0<t_{\rm peak}<T_\alpha.
\]

The transition is singular in two distinguishable conductance families. For weights \((1-\varepsilon,1,1)\), \(0<\varepsilon<1\),
\[
\alpha_*=
\frac{\log 3}{\log(3/(3-2\varepsilon))}
\sim\frac{3\log 3}{2\varepsilon}
\quad (\varepsilon\downarrow0).
\]
For weights \((1-\delta,1,C)\), fixed \(C>1\), as \(\delta\downarrow0\),
\[
\alpha_*=
\frac{\log(1/\delta)+\log\bigl(4(C-1)/3\bigr)}
{\log\bigl((2C+1)/3\bigr)}+o(1).
\]
Thus approaching the all-positive-order boundary has reciprocal-gap or logarithmic divergence depending on whether the limiting nontrivial spectral gap closes.

## Assumptions and scope

Every edge of the complete undirected three-vertex graph has strictly positive conductance. The Laplacian has entries \(L_{ii}=\sum_{j\ne i}w_{ij}\) and \(L_{ij}=-w_{ij}\) for \(i\ne j\). Spectral powers use \(0^\alpha=0\) and the ordinary positive real powers on positive eigenvalues. Inequalities between matrices are entrywise unless a norm is specified. The results concern all real orders \(\alpha>0\), all real times \(t\ge0\), and every such triple of weights, without scaling normalization. The two asymptotic families have fixed displayed reference weights.

## Proof

**Spectral projectors.** The nonzero eigenvalues are \(\lambda_-\le\lambda_+\) because \(\mathrm{tr}(L)=2S\) and the product of its nonzero eigenvalues is \(3Q\). The zero eigenspace is generated by \((1,1,1)\). With \(J=\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}\), if \(\lambda_-<\lambda_+\), the two other spectral projectors satisfy
\[
P_+=\frac{L-\lambda_-(I-J/3)}{\lambda_+-\lambda_-},\qquad
P_-=\frac{\lambda_+(I-J/3)-L}{\lambda_+-\lambda_-}.
\]
For an edge with conductance \(w\), their off-diagonal entries are
\[
(P_+)_{ij}=\frac{\lambda_-/3-w}{\lambda_+-\lambda_-},\qquad
(P_-)_{ij}=\frac{w-\lambda_+/3}{\lambda_+-\lambda_-}.
\]
Consequently, putting \(p=\lambda_+^\alpha\), \(q=\lambda_-^\alpha\),
\[
[K_\alpha(t)]_{ij}=
\frac{1-e^{-qt}}3
+\frac{\lambda_-/3-w}{\lambda_+-\lambda_-}
(e^{-pt}-e^{-qt}).\tag{1}
\]

**Only the weakest edge can fail.** Sort conductances \(m\le u\le v\) and put \(D=\sqrt{S^2-3Q}\). Direct expansion gives
\[
D^2-(S-3w)^2=-3(w-w')(w-w'')
\]
for each of the three choices of edge \(w\) and other edges \(w',w''\). Because \(S-3m\ge0\), we obtain \(m\le\lambda_-/3\), with equality exactly when the minimum occurs at least twice. Similarly \(u\ge\lambda_-/3\): if \(S-3u\le0\) it is immediate; otherwise the identity gives \(D\ge S-3u\) because \((u-m)(u-v)\le0\). For \(w\ge\lambda_-/3\), the second term of (1) is nonnegative, while the first is strictly positive for \(t>0\). If \(m=\lambda_-/3\), the same argument works for every edge. If the two positive eigenvalues coincide, \(a=b=c\), \(L^\alpha=(3a)^{\alpha-1}L\), and all off-diagonal heat entries equal \((1-e^{-t(3a)^\alpha})/3>0\).

**Sharp order and one recovery time.** For a unique minimum, write
\[
C_0=\frac{\lambda_-/3-m}{\lambda_+-\lambda_-}>0,
\qquad A_0=\frac{\lambda_+/3-m}{\lambda_+-\lambda_-}=C_0+\frac13.
\]
Equation (1) becomes \(k(t)=1/3+C_0 e^{-pt}-A_0 e^{-qt}\) for the weakest-edge entry. Its derivative at zero is \(k'(0)=A_0q-C_0p\). It is negative precisely when
\[
\left(\frac{\lambda_+}{\lambda_-}\right)^\alpha>
\frac{\lambda_+/3-m}{\lambda_-/3-m},
\]
i.e. precisely when \(\alpha>\alpha_*\). Since \(L\) itself has weak off-diagonal entry \(-m<0\), substituting \(\alpha=1\) proves \(\alpha_*>1\).

For \(t>0\), write \(y=e^{-qt}\) and \(r=p/q>1\). The sign of \(k(t)\) is the sign of
\[
\frac{1-y}{y-y^r}-3C_0.
\]
The ratio is strictly increasing with \(t\): its derivative with respect to \(y\) has numerator \(-1+y^r+r y^{r-1}(1-y)<0\), by the strict supporting-line inequality for the convex function \(y\mapsto y^r\). The ratio increases from \(q/(p-q)\) to \(+\infty\). Thus it starts below \(3C_0\) exactly when \(\alpha>\alpha_*\), and in that case crosses it exactly once. Otherwise the kernel entry is strictly positive for all positive times. At the crossing it is zero. All diagonal heat entries are positive since the spectral decomposition includes the strictly positive term \(J/3\).

**Sharp endpoint norm and peak.** Every row of \(K_\alpha(t)\) sums to one. The sole potentially negative pair occupies one entry in each of two rows. The absolute row sum is then \(1-2k(t)\) on those rows if \(k(t)<0\), and one otherwise. This proves the norm formula. When \(\alpha>\alpha_*\), the derivative \(k'(t)=A_0q e^{-qt}-C_0p e^{-pt}\) has exactly one positive zero, at \(t_{\rm peak}\) above, and changes from negative to positive there. Thus the amplification peak is unique and its time is strictly less than \(T_\alpha\).

**Two singular conductance limits.** For \((1-\varepsilon,1,1)\), direct substitution gives \(\lambda_-=3-2\varepsilon\), \(\lambda_+=3\), \(\lambda_-/3-m=\varepsilon/3\), and \(\lambda_+/3-m=\varepsilon\). The exact formula and its reciprocal-gap asymptotic follow. For \((1-\delta,1,C)\), \(C>1\), Taylor expansion of the two explicit eigenvalues gives
\[
\lambda_-=3-\frac32\delta+O(\delta^2),\qquad
\lambda_+=2C+1-\frac12\delta+O(\delta^2).
\]
Therefore \(\lambda_-/3-m=\delta/2+O(\delta^2)\), \(\lambda_+/3-m=2(C-1)/3+O(\delta)\), and \(\log(\lambda_+/\lambda_-)=\log((2C+1)/3)+O(\delta)\). Insertion into \(\alpha_*\) yields the asserted logarithmic asymptotic with an \(O(\delta\log(1/\delta))\) remainder.

## Verification

The standalone script `verify_triangle.py` independently evaluates the spectral powers through symmetric eigendecomposition, compares the analytic off-diagonal spectral formulas and heat entries to matrix exponentials, tests the weak-edge threshold and one-recovery-time norm profile, and compares both stated singular asymptotics. The deterministic run reported 20,272 successful assertions. These floating-point tests supplement, rather than replace, the algebraic proof. Its dependencies are Python 3, NumPy, and SciPy; run `python verify_triangle.py`.

## Relationship to prior work

Graph fractional diffusion and the preservation of the Markov property for orders in \((0,1]\) are established in the cited literature. The present claim is different: all positive orders, **arbitrary strictly positive triangle edge weights**, the exact order frontier, the sole negative-entry time interval, the precise \(\ell^\infty\)-operator norm and transient peak, and two distinct conductance-degeneracy laws. The published unweighted complete-graph example is covered as the equal-weight case only. No general claim about larger networks is made.

## Limitations

The theorem is confined to symmetric three-vertex complete networks with positive conductances. The time threshold is generally defined implicitly as the unique positive root of a specified scalar equation. This is a mathematical proof and reproducibility package, not an independent external validation or a guarantee that older literature cannot contain an equivalent statement. The class \(\alpha>1\) need not be physically interpretable as a Markov diffusion before the recovery time.
