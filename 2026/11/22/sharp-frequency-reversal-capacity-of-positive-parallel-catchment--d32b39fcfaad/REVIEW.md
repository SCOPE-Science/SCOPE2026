# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The exact signed-area expression follows from the unique periodic solutions of the linear reservoir equations. Its numerator is a degree-at-most-\(m-2\) polynomial. The crucial nonstandard surjectivity step is proved, not assumed: positive residues force the numerator of a Stieltjes-type sum to have \(m-1\) simple negative real zeros; an even quotient with possible poles only there is constant; therefore the signed-area polynomial map has one-dimensional kernel and full codomain rank. Positive weights realizing any prescribed roots follow by perturbing \(c=q\). Both explicit examples were confirmed by exact rational polynomial arithmetic and direct time-domain quadrature. Risks: the model restricts forcing, pathways, and observables; none of those restrictions is silently generalized.

**Originality — PASS relative to available evidence, with residual historical risk.** Searches of relevant mathematical findings, precise aliases, two-versus-three source mixing, transfer-function phase crossings, and published hydrology did not reveal this exact arbitrary-\(m\) sharp reversal-count plus positive-weight prescribed-root realization. Evans–Davies (1998) studies which *shapes of individual event hysteresis* can arise from two- or three-component source mixing; this does not in the accessible material establish the count of sign changes across harmonic frequency for fixed positive linear-reservoir parameters. Lloyd et al. (2016), inspected in its published eight-page PDF, develops a normalized index of storm-loop geometry; Outram et al. (2014), inspected in its 20-page article, reports empirical nutrient-hysteresis regimes. Both treat different inferential questions. Hornberger et al. (2001) is a particularly relevant modeling paper on hydrochemical timescales, but only accessible abstract-level content could be compared. The full Evans–Davies and Hornberger texts could not be checked, and the possibility of stronger prior frequency-domain results outside searched records cannot be excluded; priority is provisional.

**Value — PASS.** The theorem provides a sharp, scale-dependent structural diagnostic: observing \(r\) changes of signed loop orientation across forcing frequencies is impossible with fewer than \(r+2\) distinct positive first-order common-input pathways, while every allowed reversal configuration can occur even with strictly positive mixing weights. This improves on a descriptive statement that multiple runoff pathways can cause complex loops: it determines the *exact attainable reversal capacity* for arbitrary pathway count and proves the bound is sharp, including arbitrarily prescribed switching frequencies. This is relevant to interpreting variable-direction concentration–discharge loops and evaluating the expressivity of simplified hydrological models, while not purporting to diagnose an uncalibrated catchment or infer pathway counts without model checks.

**Closest literature and access risks.** Evans–Davies, DOI 10.1029/97WR01881, and Hornberger et al., DOI 10.1002/hyp.254, share the source-mixing and timescale setting; complete papers were unavailable. Full accessible primary material included Outram et al. (2014), DOI 10.5194/hess-18-3429-2014, and Lloyd et al. (2016), DOI 10.5194/hess-20-625-2016. The evidence supports the stated conditional claim but does not certify worldwide historical priority.
