# Mathematical review

**Correctness — PASS.** Explicit inversion gives the two Gaussian cardinal functions. A uniform Gaussian tail bound localizes every maximizer near the only two maxima of the scaled limiting profile. A second-order, locally derivative-uniform expansion in the strictly positive and negative coefficient branch proves uniqueness by strict concavity and gives both the p-dependent shift and the sharp powered constant. The independent standard-Python check validates the cardinal identities and representative asymptotics; finite tests do not establish the global claim.

**Originality — PASS (best-knowledge, with residual risk).** Wang–Song–Zhang's full relevant sections define the powered Lebesgue factor and prove its equality to the least relaxed representer factor; these points are not claimed as new. Their Gaussian example and separated-sample bounds do not give the coalescing two-site sharp value or location. The closest indexed Laplace-kernel theorem has a genuinely different kernel and bounded collision behavior. Searches for Gaussian equivalents, generic dominant results, and exact tables identified no covering claim. A general smooth-kernel theorem or the full 2016 RBF article could still contain equivalent information.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The sharp relaxed-factor divergence measures the precise loss of quasi-Banach representer stability under the smallest nontrivial Gaussian sample collision. Off-sample optimizer localization and its p-dependent second-order motion are needed for determining worst-case amplification, not merely the ill-conditioning of the sample Gram matrix. The contrasting bounded Laplace behavior identifies a structural smoothness sensitivity.

**Source comparison and limits.** Wang–Song–Zhang, arXiv:2611.08032v1, Sections 2–4; the publicly indexed Laplace-kernel two-sample finding; Gaussian RBF stabilization literature, DOI:10.1016/j.camwa.2016.04.048 (limited full-text access). The theorem is an h→0 asymptotic for fixed 0<p<1 and does not classify all separations, three-site sampling, or a general class of kernels.
