# Scientific review

**Correctness (PASS).** The three orthogonal star Laplacian projectors reproduce the entire heat kernel, including its one-dimensional stationary and hub-contrast modes and its \(m-1\) leaf-difference modes. Direct trace factorization yields the exact captured-trace difference and a strictly decreasing scalar crossing equation; expansions at the crossing give the Lambert-function scale. The fixed-time sampling calculations use the exact principal eigenvector and the first-step heat-kernel diagonal. The accompanying exact-rational checker verifies finite instances; the proof, rather than the finite checks, covers every \(m\ge2\) and all \(t>0\).

**Originality (PASS on inspected evidence; qualified).** Drineas–Mahoney (2005) and Gittens–Mahoney (2013) develop general Nyström sampling and error analysis. Chen–Epperly–Tropp–Webber (2024) formalize diagonal-greedy and randomized diagonal pivoting. These general constructions supply the objective but do not derive the specific time-dependent optimal-landmark classification on star heat kernels or its asymptotic sampling-regret separation. A screened-Laplacian resolvent counterpart may have a different transition law: the resolvent is not the heat kernel, and the nonlinear ratio of diagonal values at \(t\) and \(2t\) is not obtained by applying the resolvent result parameterwise. A same-family exact heat result not indexed by searched sources remains possible; this limits publication-priority confidence.

**Value (PASS).** Rank-one landmark selection for diffusion kernels is a canonical matrix-compression decision; the result quantifies a sharp change in what node matters most, demonstrates failure of an intuitive largest-diagonal first pivot, and shows that rank-one spectral-leverage and diagonal-proportional sampling may choose the optimal column with vanishing probability. The absolute limiting trace-error regret \(1-e^{-t}\) is not claimed to be a fixed fraction of the total approximation error, nor to invalidate oversampled general algorithms.
