# Review of the sharp hemisphere-breakdown theorem

## Correctness — PASS
The claim includes all nonempty surviving subsets of two centered regular polygons in orthogonal Euclidean two-planes; it makes no convex-hull interior assumption. The circular-largest-gap lemma gives the exact signed support minimum \(\cos(G/2)\) even past a semicircle. Independent angular minimization and radial balancing prove the exact two-factor sign trichotomy, with an explicit zero branch. When only one factor survives, its radial scale may be zero, which yields \(\min\{0,r\}\), correctly treating the degeneracy. For every regular \(n\)-gon, strictly negative support first occurs after \(\lfloor n/2\rfloor\) deleted vertices, with only consecutive blocks qualifying. Combining two factors proves the global threshold, exact \(pq\) count, and uniqueness of the deepest direction at the threshold. The square–pentagon census is exhaustive, not inferred from simulation. An ordinary Python verifier passed all \(n=3,\ldots,10\) gap-subset tests and all 256 relevant square–pentagon erasure subsets through count four; a separate convex-hull calculation agreed on 1,213 full-dimensional cases. These finite tests corroborate but do not replace the proof for arbitrary \(p,q\).

## Originality — PASS (provisional)
The closest inspected primary paper, Medgyes's arXiv:2611.10078v1, establishes the unique maximum-volume nine-site square–pentagon configuration, with direct-sum facet geometry, not deleted-site spherical covering. Medgyes's arXiv:2611.14674v1 gives the two-pentagon ten-site counterpart. The published result *Sharp cubic deep-hole tails for orthogonal joins of regular spherical polygons* evaluates the full-site nearest-direction distribution and the full-site covering radius; its full result and proof explicitly assume no erasures, and its signed support does not enter a negative regime. The published normal-cone angle result on the same joins treats maximizing-vertex probabilities rather than adversarial losses. The Löwner-ellipse erasure result for one regular polygon uses a distinct affine-ellipsoid objective and a one-third threshold, so neither its theorem nor its numerical cases determine this hemispherical-breakdown law. The searched current geometric records and accessible archived claims did not show an identical, equivalent, or stronger all-erasure theorem. Known support-function and spherical-join methods are relevant but were not found to contain the complete erasure count and signed transition as an explicit corollary; historical priority remains provisional.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The question is an intrinsic robustness invariant of spherical covering arrays that form distinguished maximum-volume inscribed four-polytopes, not an arbitrary parameter slice. The theorem classifies **all** failure patterns for every pair \(p,q\ge3\) from a pair of easily computed combinatorial gaps, even across a qualitative transition at covering angle \(\pi/2\). The sharp first-failure cardinality and exact count \(pq\), with unique deepest directions, yield both a constructive adversarial attack and a certification rule for a natural infinite family. The nine-site maximizer has a particularly informative stepped erasure response: one square deletion reaches a hemisphere, three deletions cannot exceed it, and exactly twenty four-site patterns break it; the complementary angles are exact. These statements are structural and not already implied by the existing full-site radius formula alone. The result does not assert robust optimality among all spherical codes or a perturbation theorem.

## Literature and limitations
Primary arXiv text inspected: Csaba Medgyes, *The inscribed 4-polytope of maximal volume with nine vertices*, arXiv:2611.10078v1 (Sections 2.4, 3, 5, 7), and *...ten vertices*, arXiv:2611.14674v1 (abstract, introduction, joined pentagons). Published research full texts inspected: *Sharp cubic deep-hole tails for orthogonal joins of regular spherical polygons*; *Universal two-level normal-cone angle inversion in orthogonal regular-polygon joins*; and *Sharp one-third erasure threshold for the Löwner ellipse of a regular polygon*. The historical equivalence search cannot exclude an unindexed theorem in older covering theory; the general support-function proof is elementary, so originality should not be interpreted as an assertion that these underlying lemmas are new. The result requires exact regularity and orthogonal planes, and no independent mathematical audit has occurred.
