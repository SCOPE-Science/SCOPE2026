# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** Fourier characters diagonalize the full defect Gram matrix with eigenvalues \(4\) and \(10\). The diagonal defect splits into one explicit nonsymmetric \(2\times2\) block and the doubling-permutation complement; trace, determinant, cycle Fourier modes and multiplicities reconstruct its entire singular spectrum. The least eigenvalue is strictly separated and sharp. A separately replayable small-group diagnostic confirms the algebra for representative noncyclic groups as well as cyclic ones. Finite tests are not offered as proof.

**Originality — PASS, qualified.** The direct Popa–Rus treatment of the same operator already proves qualitative injectivity and a doubling-orbit reconstruction for finite abelian groups (Example 8.9); these aspects are explicitly credited, not claimed new. Its inspected text does not contain a normalized Hilbert singular spectrum or full-versus-diagonal sharp stability gap. Earlier literature on quadratic functional equations treats solution/stability and general quadratic maps rather than these spectra, from the material inspected. Finite-affine-orbit recovery concerns a different projector measurement map. Semantic database queries for the exact defect, doubling orbits, simultaneous singular values, and alias stability problems returned related finite-group convolution and Fourier measurement results, not a dominating theorem. A possible equivalent older theorem under difference-operator or group-algebra language remains a genuine risk.

**Value — PASS.** The precise information loss from **full pairwise data to diagonal sampling** is a natural inverse-problem question. Unlike a qualitative nullspace computation, the exact results show the full measurement system is uniformly well-conditioned over every odd finite abelian group, whereas the diagonal inverse has unbounded \(\sqrt N\)-scale noise amplification carried by a single explicit direction. Supplying the identity-coordinate measurement restores a uniform gap; the complete orbit-dependent spectrum explains all remaining modes. The cutoff and sharp constants are structurally motivated, not chosen numerical parameters.

Independent bibliographic verification is not represented as complete. The result is not claimed to extend to even-order groups or non-Hilbert norms.
