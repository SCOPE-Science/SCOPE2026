# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

For an unweighted star, direct block inversion gives exactly two distinct off-diagonal Green entries (hub–leaf and leaf–leaf), with squared ratio \((1+\mu)^2\). The supported-Rademacher variance identity therefore reduces the entire global partition search to the exact integer pair-cost objective \(F(h)\). Balancing exchanges characterize all conditional minimizers, and strictly increasing discrete differences establish every minimizing occupancy, threshold, and tie. Factorial counting gives all unlabeled minimizer multiplicities. For fixed \(k,\mu\), the discrete optimality inequalities imply all class sizes equal \(m/k+O(1)\), so the one-split vs unrestricted energy ratio follows with a controlled \(O(m)\) remainder. The extracted exact-rational checker passes 367 assertions, including exhaustive partitions for stated finite parameters, direct resolvent inverses, thresholds, counts, and refinement examples. These checks support but do not replace the general proof.

## Originality — PASS on inspected evidence, subject to residual risk

The 2013 primary hierarchical-probing paper constructs efficient nested colorings for periodic lattices; its inspected sections do not provide a global arbitrary-partition optimum for stars or the sharp one-class-split penalty. The fully inspected relevant sections of the 2023 stochastic-probing analysis establish general variance formulas and bounds, not an exact all-budget hub-allocation theorem. The published two-color complete-bipartite finding includes the star when \(k=2\), but its fixed two-class scope does not imply the new \(k\ge3\) classification or the optimal cross-budget refinement ratio. The accessible related mathematical claims contain no same-claim implication. A later hierarchical-probing extension could only be inspected via its abstract; an unindexed equivalent theorem cannot be ruled out. These limitations temper publication-priority confidence but no decisive stronger coverage emerged.

## Value — PASS

A star is a canonical sparse matrix whose inverse is dense and whose highly connected hub is intrinsically asymmetric. The complete classification identifies when the usual proper coloring with a singleton hub is not variance-optimal, gives the exact screening threshold and every minimizer at fixed cost, and exposes an unavoidable \(9/8\) asymptotic loss from a one-class-only two-to-three-probe upgrade, even when each original parent was globally optimal. The result is a quantitative finite and asymptotic benchmark for the tradeoff between reusing a partition and redesigning a fixed-cost stochastic trace estimator. It does not purport to bound all correlated Hadamard hierarchies or solve general graphs.
