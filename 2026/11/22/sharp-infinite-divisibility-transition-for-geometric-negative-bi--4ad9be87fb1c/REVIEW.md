# Scientific review: sharp compound-Poisson transition in geometric/shape-five mixtures

**Correctness (C): PASS.** The original XRani density is a normalized positive mixture of Exp(rate \(\lambda\)) and Gamma(shape five, rate \(\lambda\)), with weights explicitly integrated. Poisson mixing yields the exact geometric/negative-binomial generating function. Factoring its quartic numerator gives for every integer \(n\ge1\) the real logarithmic intensity \(\nu_n=q^n(5-\sum_j\zeta_j^{-n})/n\). When \(\delta\ge\sqrt2\), all four \(|\zeta_j|\ge1\) ensure strictly positive summable intensities; when \(\delta<\sqrt2\), the near conjugate roots have modulus strictly below one, and recurrence of their argument forces infinitely many negative intensities. These two cases are exhaustive, and all equalities, convergence, degenerate-weight exclusions, and strict monotonicity of the Poisson–XRani rate boundary are accounted for. A standalone SymPy checker rederives the critical algebra and finite rational diagnostics; no finite sample is being used as an infinite proof.
