# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The proof represents distributional-BV functions as signed superpositions of one-sided jumps, uses preservation of the Lebesgue integral to derive the exact jump error, and differentiates it cell by cell. The decisive identity \(E_n'(s)=2p_{n,j}(s)[j+1-(n+2)s]\) is obtained from the beta-binomial tail relation and is checked by exact rational arithmetic. A binomial tail-moment identity yields all cell maxima. Strict log-concavity of \((k+1)^{k+1}/k!\) determines the parity-classified global maxima; sign opposition of two different jump-error profiles gives uniqueness of the single-jump equality cases. Stirling's formula proves the rate constant. Finite tests support but do not replace the analytical argument.

## Originality — PASS, with residual historical risk

The closest fully accessible 2011 Kantorovich source proves inequalities in the variation seminorm for smoother inputs rather than this sharp discontinuous-input integrated norm. The 2022/2024 accessible full article's global Kantorovich estimates have nonsharp implicit constants and do not fix global optimizing jumps. The accessible main-results text of Finta (2014) concerns modulus-of-smoothness \(L^p\) bounds. Published numerical approximation work and exact BV error results for Bernstein and Durrmeyer use different kernels and do not imply the present cell-wise parity law. Searches for the exact finite formula, BV-to-\(L^1\) norm, jump aliases and binomial equivalences returned no direct coverage. However, the full 2011 Gonska–Heilmann–Raşa paper was not available in the consulted access paths, and historical priority remains conditional on those and unindexed sources.

## Value — PASS

The degreewise constants supply tight and attained \(L^1\) error budgets for discontinuity-bearing data reconstructed by cell-integral sampling. The strict local extrema in every averaging cell and the odd-degree split of worst-case jumps reveal a nontrivial sampling-grid effect absent from the centered Durrmeyer extremizers and not described by standard variation diminution or rate-only estimates. Exact extremizers and the universal leading constant calibrate finite-resolution choices without requiring a downstream application.

## Scope and limitations

Real one-dimensional distributional-BV inputs, uniform averaging cells, classical unweighted Kantorovich polynomials and Lebesgue \(L^1\) error only. No historical uniqueness is certified. Exact checks up to degree 28 and numerical signed multijump tests are finite and noncertifying. The publisher text of one highly relevant 2011 article was inaccessible; the independent audit is not yet performed.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
