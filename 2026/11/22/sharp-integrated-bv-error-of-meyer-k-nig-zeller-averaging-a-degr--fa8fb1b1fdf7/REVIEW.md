# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The negative-binomial tail is the CDF of an explicit Beta law, giving an exact one-jump integrated absolute-error formula. BV signed-measure decomposition proves the sharp quotient is the supremum over jumps. Convexity reduces every cell to its endpoint candidates; a variance bound makes the search finite. Every small-degree comparison is exact rational, and the higher-degree transition is proved by strictly opposed monotonicities starting from a verified rational comparison at degree eight. Gamma-ratio CLT plus second-moment control and boundary estimates justify the all-degree sharp asymptotic and localization. The no-attainment assertion is limited to degrees one through seven.

**Originality — PASS with historical risk.** The closest fully inspected sources are Kivinukk–Metsmägi (2011), where the variation seminorm of the output and its error are studied, and Zeng–Cheng (2011), where the exact pointwise first absolute moment is investigated. The Gaussian profile shape is not claimed independently novel: the integrated step-response problem instead adds the all-degree finite exact norm computation and the seven-to-eight nonattainment/interior-dominance transition. Guo–Liu–Wang (2008) and Zeng–Zhao (2000) study pointwise approximation, and older integrated variants use different definitions. Full-text access restrictions for the 2008 and 2000 papers and unindexed historical results leave a residual priority risk.

**Value — PASS.** A classical positive approximation family has a sharp error-budget problem on discontinuous data; here the exact finite coefficients are computable, the low-degree supremum is provably not realized, the first degree that admits an interior jump beating the boundary is identified exactly, and near-worst jumps concentrate at one third at large degree. The boundary transition is not inferred merely from correctness or a numerical table; it is proved for every degree. These distinctions matter when point-sampling smoothing is applied to signals with discontinuities and when endpoint sample conventions affect optimal error.

The relevant limitations are that the input representative is fixed, the finite maximizing index is not explicitly classified for all large degrees, and historical priority is not externally certified.
