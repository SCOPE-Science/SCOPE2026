# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The Poisson-kernel Fourier expansion and Parseval identity reduce the exact variance to a nonnegative sum of weighted root power sums. The regular-polygon upper bound forces every global minimizer into a locally invertible Newton-coordinate chart around the roots of unity. Its positive quadratic core dominates the higher Fourier-tail Hessian, proving a single stationary minimum. Stationary equations yield the exact leading coefficient, and perturbing each simple root verifies that all but the distinguished zero lie strictly inward, making the unconstrained optimizer admissible. This closes the global argument. The finite checker independently tests the exact Taylor coefficients and sampled energies; it is not an infinite-dimensional proof.

## Originality — PASS (best-knowledge)
The primary 2020 study by Kovalev–Yang proves extremal properties of the pointwise minimum and maximum boundary derivatives, not least squared angular-speed variance at fixed maximal zero radius. The 2013 Gluchoff–Hartmann work focuses on interior Hardy means and other zero-pattern conditions; their objective does not give the constrained all-degree sharp minimum. Equal-radius regular-polygon principles cannot imply the result, because the optimizer has unequal zero moduli. Prior related degree-two and equiradial angular-stability findings do not imply the all-degree, radial-relaxation theorem. Risk: older equivalent Poisson-energy formulations may exist beyond the inspected sources.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The fixed outermost zero radius is a natural prescribed aperture for finite circle-covering maps. The problem has a global geometric answer for every fixed degree, a sharp nontrivial two-term energy coefficient, strict inward movement of every unconstrained companion zero, and equality/uniqueness. It distinguishes a fixed-radius constraint from the very different equal-radius constraint. The precise small-radius threshold is not computed.
