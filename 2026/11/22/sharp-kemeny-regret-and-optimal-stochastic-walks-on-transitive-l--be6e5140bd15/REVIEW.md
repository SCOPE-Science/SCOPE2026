# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The claim is a global statement on finite connected vertex- and edge-transitive cores, with symmetric nonnegative stochastic transition matrices and the zero-diagonal Kemeny normalization. Its proof reduces general asymmetric edge weights by strict convexity and graph-automorphism averaging, uses the weighted-corona resistance identity, and optimizes the resulting two-variable objective with exact equality conditions. The regret identity is an exact algebraic square, not an empirical approximation. The independent standard-library verifier executes exact-rational matrix inversions for heterogeneous cores and checks the sharp global lower bound on asymmetric examples. Boundary cases are covered: pendant probabilities are strictly positive in irreducible walks; the optimal core weights are positive; no assumption of aperiodicity is needed for the spectral hitting-time functional.

## Originality — PASS on inspected evidence, qualified
Agharkar–Patel–Bullo (2014) establishes the convex general Kemeny design framework but not the displayed transitive-corona optimizer or global regret. Boyd–Diaconis–Parrilo–Xiao (2007/2009) studies the distinct fastest-spectral-mixing objective using symmetry. Q. Liu (2016, 2020) establishes related corona resistance/Kirchhoff formulas, whose structural content is credited as prior; those identities alone do not assert the optimal transition allocation under vertexwise stochastic constraints or the equality-rigid global Kemeny regret. Durastante–Gnazzo–Meini (2026) addresses general sparse structure-preserving Kemeny perturbations but does not give this corona classification. Closely relevant published fastest-mixing and leader-placement results on coronas concern distinct matrix criteria. The remaining risk is an unindexed equivalent or an earlier combination of these tools; publication priority remains open to independent checking.

## Value — PASS
The theorem is a natural complete design classification across an infinite family of transitive cores, rather than a fixed numerical example. It determines the exact optimum and all equality cases for unrestricted asymmetric edge allocations, and gives a globally sharp quantitative penalty controlled by one observable allocation statistic. The cycle specialization exposes the nontrivial \(m^{-1/2}\) pendant allocation scale and a limiting \(3/2\) average-hitting-time disadvantage of the equal-edge walk. These facts directly quantify a structural difference in network navigation policy design; no downstream general theorem is claimed.

## Boundaries and remaining risk
The transitivity hypotheses cannot simply be dropped from the closed-form optimizer; only the resistance decomposition persists for arbitrary cores. The theorem does not treat nonuniform invariant distributions, general nonsymmetric chains, or finite-horizon mixing. Primary scientific full-text comparison is available for the 2014 general optimization work and the 2026 numerical framework; the 2020 corona paper was read via retrieved PDF text, but visual page retrieval was unavailable. The 2007 mixing paper was checked in its published summary, while direct source access for certain highly relevant published fast-mixing comparisons was unavailable. Thus novelty is supported but not independently certified.
