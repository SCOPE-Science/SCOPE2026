# Scientific review — box-average hat interpolation error frontier

## Correctness: PASS
The real-line operator is defined by positive box averages and cardinal hat reconstruction. A BV function is a signed superposition of unit jumps plus a constant; the constant cancels in the error. Thus exact operator norms are the suprema of single-jump errors. With one jump in the half-cell, only four consecutive nodal levels matter and the output consists of three linear transition pieces. Direct integration gives the explicit expression in `RESULT.md`. Its three possible algebraic branches are monotone with the required directions, so the integrated-error maximum switches from a knot jump to a half-cell jump exactly at box width \(3h/2\). The uniform error is controlled by the reconstructed value at the jump, whose quadratic minimizer changes regime at box width \(2h/3\). The explicit witnesses attain every constant. The nodal-difference averaging identity proves variation diminution for all allowed widths. The final executable verification evaluates these facts with exact rational arithmetic and tests signed superpositions.

## Originality: PASS, with historical-priority qualification
The nearest primary source is Nochetto–Salgado (2012), which constructs a TV-diminishing Cartesian quasi-interpolant using centered box averages (arXiv:1211.1069, §§3–4). Its displayed approximation bound in Theorem 3.9 has an unspecified geometric \(L^1\) constant, and its discussion does not give the one-dimensional variable-box-width exact \(L^1\)/\(L^\infty\) jump-error frontier or the two thresholds. Kristensen–Rindler (2012, arXiv:1211.1792) prove adaptive piecewise-affine BV approximation and trace convergence; their operators and quantifiers differ. Ern–Guermond (2015, arXiv:1505.06931, §§4–5) give abstract Sobolev quasi-interpolation estimates for another averaging mechanism, not the exact BV operator seminorms. Relevant indexed comparisons include histopolation from overlapping averages, spline projection stability and nonlinear box envelopes: none of the inspected statements gives either formula or implies the \(3h/2\) constrained optimality. The searches cannot exclude non-indexed older exact estimates or results in spline monographs.

## Mathematical value: PASS
For discontinuity-bearing BV data, positivity and TV diminution by themselves do not quantify reconstruction error. The complete exact bandwidth frontier shows that increasing the averaging width from \(h\) to \(3h/2\) reduces the worst *pointwise* error from \(9/16\) to \(49/96\) without changing the best worst-case *integrated* error coefficient \(1/2\). The two separate jump-location and integrated-error transitions at \(2h/3\) and \(3h/2\) are structural, not merely a fixed-stencil norm recomputation. The width choice is a natural constrained design within a classical affine-reproducing, variation-diminishing approximation family; smoothing more aggressively incurs strictly greater sharp integrated jump error.

## Risks and limitations
The scope is a scalar, infinite, uniform, one-dimensional grid, with centered boxes of width at most \(2h\). Boundary adaptation, nonuniform grids, other kernels and multivariate interpolation are not covered. The constrained optimum is not a global optimality statement over arbitrary operators. Search-based originality is not a historical uniqueness certificate; older equivalent formulas may exist. Randomized finite checks do not substitute for the universal signed-measure argument.
