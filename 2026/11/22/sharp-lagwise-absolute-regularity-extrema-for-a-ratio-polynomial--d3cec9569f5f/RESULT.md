# Sharp lagwise absolute-regularity extrema for a ratio-polynomial copula chain

## Finding
Let 
\[
C_\alpha(u,v)=uv\frac{(1+\alpha)(1+\alpha uv)}{(1+\alpha u)(1+\alpha v)},\qquad 0\le u,v\le1,
\]
and let \((U_j)_{j\in\mathbb Z}\) be the stationary Markov chain with uniform margins and consecutive copula \(C_\alpha\). Write \(r=(3-\sqrt5)/2\); the exact copula domain is \(\alpha\ge-r\). For each integer \(m\ge1\) let
\[
\beta_m(\alpha)=\bigl\|\mathcal L(U_0,U_m)-\mathcal L(U_0)\otimes\mathcal L(U_m)\bigr\|_{\rm TV}
=\frac12\int_0^1\!\int_0^1|p_m(u,v)-1|\,du\,dv,
\]
where \(p_m\) is the lag-\(m\) joint density and \(\|\cdot\|_{\rm TV}\) is supremum discrepancy over measurable events. For a stationary Markov chain this is also the absolute-regularity coefficient between the complete past through time zero and the complete future from time \(m\).

Set \(s=\sqrt{1+\alpha}\), \(s_0=\sqrt{(\sqrt5-1)/2}\), and define
\[
B_m(s)=\frac{2|s-1|}{(s+1)^3}
\left(\frac{|s^2-1|}{3s^2}\right)^{m-1}.
\]
When \(m=1\), a factor with exponent \(m-1\) is interpreted as the empty product \(1\), including at \(s=1\). Then \(\beta_m(\alpha)=B_m(\sqrt{1+\alpha})\) for every \(\alpha\ge-r\), including \(\alpha=0\). The full parameterwise maxima are **attained uniquely** as follows:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sup_{\alpha\ge-r}\beta_1(\alpha)&=\frac{2(1-s_0)}{(1+s_0)^3}=0.075055351553114962\ldots,
&\alpha_{\rm max,1}&=-r,\\
\sup_{\alpha\ge-r}\beta_m(\alpha)&=\frac{2\sqrt m}{(2+\sqrt m)^3}\left[\frac{m+2\sqrt m}{3(1+\sqrt m)^2}\right]^{m-1},
&\alpha_{\rm max,m}&=m+2\sqrt m\quad(m\ge2).
\end{aligned}
\]
In particular the worst-case parameter changes **from the negative admissibility endpoint at lag one to a positive, lag-dependent interior value at every lag at least two**. The exact large-lag envelope is
\[
\sup_{\alpha\ge-r}\beta_m(\alpha)\sim\frac{2}{e\,m}\,3^{-(m-1)}\qquad(m\to\infty).
\]
A related limiting contrast is \(\beta_m(\alpha)\to0\) for each fixed \(m\) as \(\alpha\to\infty\), even though the maximal correlation of \((U_0,U_m)\) tends to \(3^{-m}\).

## Assumptions and scope
The time index is discrete, the marginal law is continuous uniform on \([0,1]\), \(m\) is any positive integer, and \(\alpha\) varies over the **entire** valid ratio-polynomial copula parameter domain. The statement concerns the homogeneous stationary chain defined by this particular transition copula, not all copula-driven Markov chains. Boundary values are included. Density integrals are ordinary Lebesgue integrals; the total-variation convention includes the factor \(1/2\).

## Proof
**1. Exact admissibility.** Algebra gives
\[
C_\alpha(u,v)=uv+\alpha f_\alpha(u)f_\alpha(v),\qquad
f_\alpha(u)=\frac{u(1-u)}{1+\alpha u},
\]
with derivative \(g_\alpha(u)=[1-2u-\alpha u^2]/(1+\alpha u)^2\). For \(\alpha>-1\) the mixed density is
\[
c_\alpha(u,v)=(1+\alpha)
\frac{1+\alpha uv(2+\alpha u)(2+\alpha v)}{(1+\alpha u)^2(1+\alpha v)^2}.
\]
It is nonnegative for \(\alpha\ge0\). For \(\alpha=-t\in(-1,0)\), the function \(u(2-tu)\) is increasing on \([0,1]\), so nonnegativity everywhere is equivalent to \(1-t(2-t)^2\ge0\). As \(1-t(2-t)^2=(1-t)(t^2-3t+1)\), this is equivalent to \(t\le(3-\sqrt5)/2\). For \(\alpha<-1\) an interior denominator vanishes without general numerator cancellation; at \(\alpha=-1\) the formula collapses in the interior and cannot have continuous uniform margins. The boundary and marginal conditions follow from the displayed formula. Hence the stated domain is exact.

**2. Exact powers and coefficient.** The identities \(\int_0^1g_\alpha=0\) and
\[
J_\alpha:=\int_0^1g_\alpha(u)^2\,du=\frac1{3(1+\alpha)}
\]
hold throughout the domain. For the latter, when \(\alpha\ne0\), insert \(g_\alpha(u)=\alpha^{-1}\bigl[(1+\alpha)(1+\alpha u)^{-2}-1\bigr]\) and integrate the two powers explicitly; at zero the formula follows by continuity. Composing the rank-one kernels gives for **all** integers \(m\ge1\),
\[
p_m(u,v)=1+\alpha\left(\frac{\alpha}{3(1+\alpha)}\right)^{m-1}g_\alpha(u)g_\alpha(v).
\]
The function \(f_\alpha\) is nonnegative with zero endpoints, increases to a single maximum at \(u_*=(1+\sqrt{1+\alpha})^{-1}\), then decreases. Therefore
\[
\int_0^1|g_\alpha(u)|\,du=2f_\alpha(u_*)=\frac{2}{(1+\sqrt{1+\alpha})^2}.
\]
Taking the \(L^1\) norm of the product kernel proves the exact displayed formula for \(\beta_m=B_m(s)\). The equality with the past-future coefficient follows from the Markov property: conditioning the future on its first state gives an upper bound by the \((U_0,U_m)\) joint total variation, and the sub-sigma-field generated by \(U_m\) gives the reverse bound.

**3. Shape optimization.** On \(s_0\le s<1\), both \((1-s)/(1+s)^3\) and \((1-s^2)/s^2\) strictly decrease with \(s\). Consequently the unique negative-parameter maximum is attained at \(s=s_0\) for every lag. On \(s>1\), logarithmic differentiation gives
\[
\frac{d}{ds}\log B_m(s)=\frac{2[m-(s-1)^2]}{s(s^2-1)}.
\]
The unique positive-parameter maximum is at \(s=1+\sqrt m\), corresponding to \(\alpha=m+2\sqrt m\).

**4. Comparing the two branches without floating-point assumptions.** The algebraic bounds
\[
\frac{39}{50}<s_0<\frac{787}{1000}
\]
follow by squaring and using \(s_0^2=(\sqrt5-1)/2\). For \(m=1\), monotonicity and the exact rational inequality \(27(1-787/1000)>(1+787/1000)^3\) imply \(B_1(s_0)>2/27=B_1(2)\), the positive-branch maximum. For \(m\ge2\), monotonicity, \(B_2(39/50)<1/54\), and
\[
\frac{1-s_0^2}{3s_0^2}<\frac14
\]
show \(B_m(s_0)<(1/54)(1/4)^{m-2}=B_m(2)\), the value at the admissible positive parameter \(\alpha=3\). Hence the positive-branch maximizer is strictly globally dominant for all \(m\ge2\). All winners are unique.

**5. Asymptotic and correlation contrast.** At \(s=1+\sqrt m\), the prefactor is asymptotic to \(2/m\), while
\[
\left[1-(1+\sqrt m)^{-2}\right]^{m-1}\longrightarrow e^{-1}.
\]
This proves the sharp asymptotic. On the centered \(L^2([0,1])\) subspace the transition operator has rank at most one, with eigenvalue \(\lambda_\alpha=\alpha/[3(1+\alpha)]\) on the line spanned by \(g_\alpha\) and zero on its orthogonal complement. Thus the lag-\(m\) maximal correlation is \(|\lambda_\alpha|^m\), including zero at independence. As \(\alpha\to\infty\), \(|\lambda_\alpha|\to1/3\) while the formula for \(B_m\) tends to zero. This proves the last assertion.

## Relationship to prior work
Chesneau (2023), Proposition 1 and Lemma 1, introduced the ratio-polynomial copula and calculated its self-fold product, using the sufficient parameter interval \(\alpha\ge-1/4\); its rho-of-Spearman optimization is distinct from absolute regularity. Longla, Mous-Abou and Ngongo (2022) studied mixing classes for copula Markov chains, including the FGM fold-product. Longla (2024a), Theorem 2.1, established the general rank-one fold-product principle, and Longla (2024b) further developed general spectral copula mixing properties. Therefore the rank-one composition itself is **not** presented as a new general discovery. The result here is the exact full-parameter, all-lag total-variation coefficient and the nontrivial *global extremizer switch*, with a sharp lag-dependent optimizer and asymptotic. The full admissibility boundary is a prerequisite, not a separate novelty claim. No examined source states this same family-specific sharp envelope.

## Limitations
Historical originality is conditional on the accessible cited literature and research-record comparisons; absence of a matching result in searches does not prove first discovery. This is a theoretical stationary-chain result, not an empirical mixing-rate estimation claim. The formula uses the exact absolutely continuous copula, not a singular or nonstationary extension.
