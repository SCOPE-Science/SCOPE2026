## Originality (O): PASS, qualified

A crucial closer historical comparison is Henze–Klar (2002), Theorem 3.1, which **already proves large-weight polynomial collapse using an Abelian Laplace argument**, but for a different inverse-Gaussian empirical-Laplace test with a different empirical moment contrast. The present result's precise second-inverse-moment slope, sharp exponential cancellation branch, if-and-only-if sample-mean boundary at size three, second Gaussian weight and explicit fitted null coefficient are not supplied by that theorem. A search in the accessible prior ledger identified analogous high-weight Rayleigh/Weibull work but not the same test. Targeted published-finding corpus and primary-literature queries did not locate stronger direct coverage. This is evidence of best-knowledge originality, not definitive historical priority; general Abelian principles are specifically disclaimed. The 2002 PDF was reviewed via parsed substantive text, but page-image retrieval failed, leaving a modest presentation-access risk.

## Value (V): PASS
The tunable CDF–Stein test has two competing asymptotic decay mechanisms, with an exact sample-mean switching surface at the smallest sample size that admits cancellation. This is a meaningful numerical-conditioning and calibration boundary for a published fit statistic, not just an arbitrary parameter substitution. The explicit rank-one null law identifies the effective asymptotic degrees of freedom for extreme tuning. These conclusions are structural properties of the test; they do not imply a failure of fixed-a omnibus consistency or a tested recommendation about real-world a values.
