# Sharp Lie-jet degree growth forbids polynomial Koopman closure for nonlinear gradient flows

## Finding

Let \(n\ge1\), let \(V\in\mathbb R[x_1,\ldots,x_n]\) be a polynomial of total degree \(d\), and define the Lie generator of its gradient dynamics by
\[
\mathcal L f=-\sum_{j=1}^n\frac{\partial V}{\partial x_j}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_j},\qquad \dot x=-\nabla V(x).
\]
For every \(d\ge3\), **some fixed coordinate index \(i\)** satisfies the exact degree identity
\[
\boxed{\deg(\mathcal L^k x_i)=1+k(d-2)\qquad(k=0,1,2,\ldots).}
\]
Thus the iterated Lie derivatives of that coordinate are linearly independent. No finite-dimensional real polynomial space containing all the coordinate observables is invariant under \(\mathcal L\). In contrast, if \(d\le2\), the affine space generated by \(1,x_1,\ldots,x_n\) is invariant. Hence the dividing line for exact finite polynomial state-inclusive Koopman-generator closure is precisely \(d=2\).

## Assumptions and scope

The polynomial potential is real, the gradient uses the standard Euclidean inner product, and the initial state ranges over all of \(\mathbb R^n\). Global existence of trajectories is not assumed: an exact lift must agree on each common local existence interval near time zero. A **polynomial state-inclusive finite linear lift** means finitely many polynomial initial observables \(p_1,\ldots,p_m\), a constant real matrix \(A\), and a fixed linear output projection such that the solution of \(\dot z=Az\), initialized with the coordinates and the \(p_j\), projects to the original trajectory for every initial state. Affine forcing may equivalently be handled by adjoining the constant observable \(1\). This does not claim anything about embeddings using arbitrary nonpolynomial observables.

## Proof

Write \(V=V_d+V_{<d}\), where \(V_d\not\equiv0\) is homogeneous of degree \(d\). Because \(V_d\) is nonzero and continuous on the unit sphere, an extremizer \(a\in S^{n-1}\) of a nonzero maximum or nonzero minimum satisfies \(V_d(a)\ne0\) and, by Lagrange multipliers plus Euler's homogeneous identity,
\[
\nabla V_d(a)=\lambda a,\qquad \lambda=dV_d(a)\ne0.
\]
Let \(\mathcal D=-\nabla V_d\cdot\nabla\). The homogeneous vector field \(\dot y=-\nabla V_d(y)\) preserves the ray through \(a\) near time zero, with \(y(t)=r(t)a\) and
\[
\dot r=-\lambda r^{d-1},\quad r(0)=1,\quad r(t)=[1+(d-2)\lambda t]^{-1/(d-2)}.
\]
All Taylor coefficients of \(r\) at zero are nonzero. In fact,
\[
\mathcal D^k x_i(a)=a_i(-1)^k\left(\frac1{d-2}\right)_k[(d-2)\lambda]^k
\]
for every integer \(k\ge0\), where \((s)_k=s(s+1)\cdots(s+k-1)\) and \((s)_0=1\). Choose \(i\) with \(a_i\ne0\). Hence \(\mathcal D^k x_i\) is a nonzero homogeneous polynomial of degree \(1+k(d-2)\).

The operator \(\mathcal L\) maps any polynomial of degree \(m\) into degree at most \(m+d-2\). Its degree-\((m+d-2)\) contribution, when applied to a polynomial with top homogeneous part \(f_m\), is exactly \(\mathcal D f_m\); the lower terms in \(V\) cannot contribute there. By induction, the top homogeneous part of \(\mathcal L^k x_i\) equals \(\mathcal D^k x_i\), which has just been shown nonzero. Therefore the displayed exact degree identity holds for all \(k\), with strictly increasing degrees. This yields linear independence of the iterates and excludes any finite-dimensional invariant polynomial space containing all the coordinates.

If a finite linear lift with polynomial initial observables existed, differentiation of the assumed trajectory equality at time zero repeatedly would put every \(\mathcal L^k x_i\) into the finite span of the components of the polynomial initialization map, contradicting independence. Conversely, when \(d\le2\), \(-\nabla V\) is affine and \(\mathcal L\) preserves \(\operatorname{span}\{1,x_1,\ldots,x_n\}\); its restriction is a finite matrix. This completes both directions.

## Verification

Run `python3 gradient_degree_checker.py` with SymPy installed. The script checks exact polynomial identities for nonhomogeneous cubic, strongly convex quartic and quintic potentials to Lie order eight, seven and six, respectively, including the equality of top homogeneous parts and their nonvanishing on the chosen extremal ray; it also checks the affine boundary. All **80** exact rational checks passed. These are supporting computations: the unbounded-iteration assertion is proved in the preceding argument, not by finite tests.

For a concrete smooth globally strongly convex example, \(V(x)=\frac14\|x\|^4+\frac12\|x\|^2\) has Hessian \(I+\|x\|^2I+2xx^\top\succeq I\), but still admits no state-inclusive exact finite polynomial lift.

## Relationship to prior work

Hong, Li, Colbrook and Lyu study how to detect a finite Koopman-invariant dictionary when one exists, and approximate leakage when exact invariance is absent [1]. Their exact zero-block theorem is conditional on the presence of an invariant subspace; it does not classify gradient polynomial generators. Brunton and coauthors provide topological necessary conditions for state-inclusive finite Koopman representations [2], which leave the single-equilibrium strongly convex quartic case undecided. Harshana and Belabbas give polynomial-lift invariance under coordinate automorphisms and cite the classical finite Lie-span criterion [3]; their results do not provide the degree formula or the universal gradient obstruction. Classical polynomial-flow/locally finite derivation studies [4,5] are relevant background and possible historical routes to a qualitative corollary. The noncancelling top-degree computation and exact Lie-jet growth are the specific claims here.

## Limitations

The result concerns Euclidean polynomial **gradient** vector fields and polynomial state-inclusive observables. It does not deny polynomial invariant observables unrelated to the coordinates, arbitrary smooth or nonpolynomial state embeddings, local analytic nonlinear conjugacies, useful approximate finite dictionaries, or state reconstruction by nonlinear output maps. It does not furnish a numerical error lower bound for truncated Koopman regression. Source [5] was not available for complete full-text inspection; historical priority of a broader classification remains an unresolved risk. No independent validation has been performed.
