# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The graph-Lipschitz seminorm is the maximum of hub–leaf differences; fixing the hub value leaves independently adjustable leaf values. The row differences have explicit rigorously signed numerators, and choosing the sign vector achieves each edge norm. Equation (5) is an exact common-denominator identity whose positive-parameter bracket is strictly decreasing with the bandwidth parameter; this gives the unique threshold. A positive definite Gaussian covariance construction proves the spectrum assertion; the diameter-two coupling argument and an explicit asymptotic family prove the unattained universal envelope. The standalone exact-rational test suite passed 870 parameter cases. Its enumeration is supplementary only.

## Originality — PASS (with priority risk)

The Wasserstein-transform paper introduces localization by probability rows and studies a different perturbation question. Montrucchio–Pistone proves the general tree 1-Wasserstein cut formula; this known ingredient is explicitly acknowledged, and neither its statement nor the inspected sections derive the weighted distance-localization norm, the necessary-and-sufficient mass inequality, or the one-interval bandwidth transition. The nearby indexed exact star-heat results concern continuous-time heat semigroups, whereas the present operator is a row-normalized exponential-distance kernel. Multiple statement-, alias-, and parameter-level searches returned no decisive covering formula; failure to find one does not prove absence. Full-text retrieval of those two exact star-heat records was unavailable, which remains a priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The theorem gives an exact stability coefficient and a complete mass–bandwidth phase diagram for a natural geometry-aware Markov smoother, including a sharp uniform branching threshold, a nontrivial three-vertex mass-imbalance phenomenon, and an optimal dimension-free envelope. The positive-spectrum reversible counterexamples distinguish spectral positivity from graph-Lipschitz nonexpansivity. These are natural operator-robustness questions not answered by the cited general tree-distance formula alone.

## Limitations and closest coverage

The graph is a unit-edge star rather than an arbitrary tree. The broad tree transportation identity of Montrucchio–Pistone is prior art and is used as such. Other published work may implicitly entail the final rational formula; the full text of the closest indexed star-heat research records was not accessible. No independent audit has been performed.
