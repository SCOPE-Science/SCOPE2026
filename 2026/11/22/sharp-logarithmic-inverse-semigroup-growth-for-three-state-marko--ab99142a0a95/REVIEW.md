# Scientific assessment

**Correctness: PASS.** Normalizing the largest exit rate converts the three-state characteristic polynomial into a quadratic with a bounded second coefficient. Splitting by the sum of the other exits proves the sharp spectral lower bound. Exact rank-one projection and nilpotent identities prove asymptotic optimality for an irreducible example. Rational matrix computations independently corroborate the explicit certificate; finite tests are not relied upon for universal quantifiers.

**Originality: PASS, qualified.** The accessible full text of Glück (2020), arXiv:2004.05995v1, gives a general existence/boundedness treatment and a finite-dimensional coefficient \(2d\), but not this sharp \(d=3\) coefficient. The published one-way three-state budget result (2026) concerns the slowest decay exponent and not this largest-real-part Markov spectral bound or the inverse norm. Semantically related records and alternate parameterizations were checked. The full text of Cuthbert (1975), DOI 10.1112/jlms/s2-11.1.104, was not available; it could contain an unindexed stronger predecessor, so historical priority is qualified rather than asserted globally.

**Mathematical value: PASS.** This is a sharp, parameter-free finite-dimensional extremal constant for a natural logarithmic inverse-Markov problem, improving an available elementary three-dimensional factor from \(6\) to \(8/3\). It exposes a strict minimal-dimension transition from the two-state constant \(2\), with an explicit defective irreducible extremal family. It does not purport to settle the general decoupled Markov group conjecture.
