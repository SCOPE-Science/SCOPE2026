# Scientific assessment

## Correctness

PASS. The claimed time-lag covariance follows directly from three stationary generator moment equations and a forced scalar first-order covariance equation. At \(r<\gamma\), the slow exponential coefficient is strictly positive; at \(r>\gamma\), a nonzero oscillating exponential dominates; on the critical line an exact trigonometric-amplitude calculation produces both the strictly negative and tangential-zero regimes. The numerical checker independently compares the analytic resolvent to a real matrix exponential and a stationary Laplace integral. Analyticity and positive zero-lag variance exclude an infinite number of compact-interval zeroes.

## Originality

PASS, with residual risk. Gupta (arXiv:1812.07227) supplies one-dimensional position-only inertial resetting and stationary position marginals. Abdoli and Sharma (arXiv:2010.02103) treat a Lorentz-influenced active Brownian system with spatial resetting to an axis, studying one-time profiles and transport rather than a full planar-position-reset autocovariance. Olsen and Löwen (arXiv:2401.12685) reset velocity while leaving position unaffected. Exact comparison of model assumptions and theorems yields no directly covering positive-lag sign classification. The technique uses standard resolvents, and a broader spectral or partial-resetting theorem could implicitly subsume the result; independent priority review is still required.

## Mathematical value

PASS. It gives a sharp physically motivated memory boundary: sufficiently frequent spatial resets do not necessarily suppress long-lag sign reversals if the velocity retains cyclotron dynamics, and an exact frequency condition determines the critical case. The existence of infinitely many negative time-lag intervals and infinitely many *nonnegative tangencies* are differentiated sharply. This is a classification of an invariant observable, not just a numerical example or a generic rate substitution.

## Scope and outstanding issues

The model is idealized and has no position diffusion or hydrodynamic feedback. The number of transient sign reversals below the critical rate is not classified. No experimental signal-to-noise claim is made, and published general spectral approaches may implicitly cover the result. All presented checks are self-review, not independent validation.
