# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS (self-review).** The inverse-row recursion follows from the finite nilpotent Neumann formula; triangular row support yields orthogonality to the new diagonal coordinate. A uniform row-sum bound gives a sharp row-defect recurrence. Summing supplies the exact finite Frobenius defect extremum, with equality for a bidiagonal chain. The operator-norm upper bound and a chain-fed leaf construction give matching sharp limits. The supplied reproducible checker passed 15,117 exact-rational parent-forest row assertions and 369 numerical matrix/comb tests. These computations are supplementary, not a proof for all matrix sizes.

**Originality — PASS, qualified.** Full accessible primary material from Dargaville–Pain (arXiv:2611.00552v1) and Echeverría–Liesen–Nabben (arXiv:1712.05662), together with published result searches, did not exhibit the two sharp row-budget norms. The closest older Peña papers address Skeel or componentwise condition bounds on strictly row-dominant triangular factors, not the unweighted Euclidean inverse amplification law; publisher abstracts were available, not complete articles. A published result about infinite sparse comb parent shifts concerns different resolvent-growth quantifiers, and a tree Cesàro singular-spectrum result concerns different averaging weights. Equivalent unindexed historical bounds and inaccessible details remain priority risks; no independent novelty certification is asserted.

**Value — PASS.** The source paper makes a row-diagonal-dominance target a tunable design parameter for parallel coarse/fine triangular solves. The present sharp mixed-norm law distinguishes favorable row-wise control from worst-case dimension-dependent Euclidean amplification, and identifies a structurally motivated family realizing the exact leading coefficient. This supports precise stability assessment without claiming that actual factors must realize the worst case.

**Closest literature and limits.** Dargaville–Pain (2026), https://arxiv.org/html/2611.00552v1; Echeverría–Liesen–Nabben (2017), https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.05662; Peña (2007), https://doi.org/10.1137/060677562; Peña (2003), https://doi.org/10.1137/S0036142901395163. The theorem assumes unit diagonal, row absolute-sum budget less than one, exact arithmetic and strictly triangular structure. The finite equality is Frobenius; the Euclidean statement is only an asymptotic optimum. It is not a convergence theorem for a complete multilevel solver.
