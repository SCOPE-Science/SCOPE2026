The full order-\(m\) generalized Watson statistic has a sharp moving-frequency detection boundary: against \(1+\varepsilon\cos(2\pi k_n x)\), its null limit is translated by \(\varepsilon^2s/[2(2\pi)^{2m}]\) when \(n/k_n^{2m}\to s\), and its power tends to one when this ratio diverges. At \(k_n\asymp n^{1/3}\), ordinary Watson has asymptotic power one while every fixed higher order has nominal asymptotic power, even though the full sample laws become perfectly distinguishable in total variation.
