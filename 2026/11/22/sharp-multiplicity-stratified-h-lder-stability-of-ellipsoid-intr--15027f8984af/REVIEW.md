# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The claim is strictly local around a fixed full-dimensional ellipsoid. The intrinsic-volume integral formula provides a smooth map from squared-axis elementary symmetric coefficients to normalized Steiner data. The cited strict derivative-convexity lemma implies that the first \(n-1\) Jacobian block is invertible by column diagonal dominance after multiplication by the discrete Laplacian. The last component \(v_n=\sqrt{s_n}\) gives a nonsingular triangular full Jacobian. The inverse-function theorem then gives a coefficient-distance estimate. The proof separately controls the multiset of real polynomial roots using Rouché on collinear disk chains inside separated multiplicity clusters, obtaining exponent \(1/m\). Two explicit polynomial families differing by a constant times the \(m\)-th power of their perturbation parameter show that no larger exponent is possible. This is not merely a finite numerical observation. The dimension-three Jacobian is checked by exact beta integrals, and a standalone program passes 28 independent finite numerical assertions. The critical imported nonsingularity premise is attributed, not silently assumed.

## Originality — PASS, provisional
The 2026-05-12 weighted-\(\ell_p\) intrinsic-volume formulas and 2022 ellipsoid formulas provide forward evaluation but no stability exponent. Petrov--Tarasov (2020) covers unique determination in dimension three. Myroshnychenko--Tatarko--Yaskin (2026-11-18) proves uniqueness in all dimensions and establishes the strict-Jacobian lemma reused here; its full source was examined, including the derivative formulas, convexity lemma and concluding argument. Its stated theorem and proof do not quantify optimal noisy recovery with multiplicity-dependent exponents or construct the exact sharp pairs. The new work supplies the locally optimal all-multiplicity classification rather than restating uniqueness. Published mathematical-research records concerning ellipsoid geodesic-length conditioning and ellipsoid section diameters concern different measurement maps, and their conclusions do not imply stability from the full intrinsic-volume vector. Priority remains provisional, especially because classical polynomial-root sensitivity may make a similar quantitative statement accessible elsewhere.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Recovering shapes from intrinsic volumes is an inverse problem in convex geometry. A qualitative uniqueness theorem cannot by itself prescribe noise amplification near repeated axes. The exact multiplicity index gives a natural complete classification of how well the semiaxes can be recovered: all-distinct configurations are Lipschitz, while the most symmetric configurations lose precisely the optimal \(1/n\) Hölder exponent. The explicit noisy pairs prove sharpness at every stratum; the computed three-dimensional witness shows cubic data deterioration at a round ball. No global center/orientation, arbitrary-body inverse or new uniqueness theorem is asserted.
