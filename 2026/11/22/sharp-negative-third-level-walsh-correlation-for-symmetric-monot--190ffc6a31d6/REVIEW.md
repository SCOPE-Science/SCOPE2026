# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The claim has precise uniform-cube Walsh normalization, integer dimension range, monotonicity and symmetry assumptions, exact finite coefficient formula, and an analytic proof. Nonnegative threshold increments reduce the entire class to an interval of third-level Fourier coefficients. The factored adjacent binomial difference proves both opposite extrema and their tie cases, and interval optimization proves all equality cases. Stirling's local estimate gives the stated Gaussian limit. The embedded exact-arithmetic checker confirms the binomial identities and independent small-cube Walsh sums; these finite checks do not replace the infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS relative to inspected sources

Chang, Li, Wang and Wang (arXiv:2611.08289v1) proved a general third-level correlation lower bound controlled by first-level correlation and variances. Their Section 6 discusses a distinct unrestricted optimal *ratio* and does not compute this normalized bounded symmetric absolute minimum. Bernasconi's 1998 Calcolo article gives the classical Krawtchouk coefficient formula for individual thresholds; the two-opposite-peaks optimization, exact all-dimension pair constant, classification of all extremizers and Gaussian limit were not found in the inspected threshold passage. Chang (arXiv:2606.08958) instead bounds covariance by squared-spectrum products. Targeted published-record and scholarly-literature searches did not find an identical result. This is a best-knowledge novelty comparison, not a proof of uniqueness in all literature.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The first-to-third Walsh-level comparison of increasing cube functions is an active question. Within the natural symmetric probability-bounded class, this theorem completely determines the maximal possible **negative** signed third-level correlation, not merely a coefficient identity or another specific example. It locates the two competing Hamming thresholds on a Gaussian square-root scale and supplies the dimension-free sharp constant and all equality mixtures.
