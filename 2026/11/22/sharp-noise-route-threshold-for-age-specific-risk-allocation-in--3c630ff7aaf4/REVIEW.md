# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** For nonnegative two-stage feedback coefficients summing to a number below one, the companion matrix is strictly stable. A stationary mean and covariance calculation yields the exact positive rational risk function. Differentiation reduces the global optimizer to one increasing rational threshold function. Its derivative is strictly positive by the inequality \(a(z+2)-3z^3\geq2a(1-z)>0\), and the endpoint calculation gives the sharp noise boundary. An independent rational script checks 57,978 finite identities; this is supplementary, not a substitute for the universal proof.

**Originality — PASS on inspected evidence.** The motivating 2024 fishery study analyzes deterministic delayed population stability, the 2018 open-population work gives general covariance/sensitivity formulas, and the 2022 age-structure study gives frequency-domain responses. A 2026 published two-lag noise-weighting result covers the recruitment-noise-only special case exactly, so that case is not claimed as new. The new element is a jointly juvenile/adult noise model, in which innovation variance and stationary mean change with stage allocation, and the sharp phase boundary \(q_c=m^4/(1-2m^2)\) (for \(m<1/\sqrt2\)). This boundary is not implied as a stated theorem by the inspected sources. Residual risk: an equivalent policy-constrained stochastic-age model may have appeared elsewhere, and general covariance formulas can be specialized to the present family.

**Value — PASS.** Independent fluctuations affecting recruitment and adults are a natural distinction in open stock/structured-population monitoring. The theorem shows when preserving some adult retention is optimal for long-run relative abundance risk at fixed replacement margin, and when it is strictly preferable to put all linear response into juvenile recruitment. The structural value is its complete, sharp regime classification, not merely a covariance calculation. This is not a calibrated harvest prescription.
