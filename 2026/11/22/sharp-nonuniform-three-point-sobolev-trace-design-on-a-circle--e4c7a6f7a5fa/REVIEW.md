# Scientific review: sharp three-point periodic Sobolev trace design

## Correctness — PASS
The point-evaluation map on real, zero-mean periodic \(H^1\) has reproducing kernel \(G(u)=B_2(\{u\})/2\), giving an exact three-by-three Gram spectral norm. Every two-by-two principal submatrix yields the universal lower bound \(1/12+\max_i|G(g_i)|\). The convex Bernoulli profile has two admissible gap intervals below the proposed threshold; the sum-one constraint rules out three small gaps, one large plus two small, and two or more large gaps. Equality forces the unique gap profile \((t,t,1-2t)\), where \(t=(9-\sqrt{21})/30\). The corresponding Gram eigenvalues attain the lower bound and classify norming functions. Both strict inequalities and exact boundary equality are proved. Symbolic eigen- and polynomial identities and 551,300 rational triple checks corroborate the proof; finite experiments alone do not establish the continuum statement.

## Originality — PASS, with historical-priority risk
Shadimetov–Hayotov–Mamatova (arXiv:1012.1953v1, 2010) solve optimal **interpolation coefficients** at lattice sites and use the same classical Sobolev/Bernoulli framework. Wang–Zhang (arXiv:1207.5871v1, 2012), inspected through their RKHS reconstruction formulation and numerical examples, optimize the **residual reconstruction loss**, not the spectral norm of the raw three-evaluation map. A published circle Sobolev design finding with positive point masses changes the mean constraint and studies a supremum embedding, so it does not imply the fixed arclength-mean three-output trace design. A prior circular \(W^{1,1}\) sample-trace classification uses a different derivative exponent and a nonspectral cut method. No exact statement or more general theorem implying the present square-root-of-21 three-gap equality classification was identified in the executed relevant searches. Unindexed operator-design literature and inaccessible full public versions remain risks; negative searches are not proof of novelty.

## Value — PASS
Minimizing the worst simultaneous magnitude of three point observations under a fixed derivative-energy budget is a natural exact sensor-design and Sobolev trace problem. The complete classification exhibits a concrete and counterintuitive exception to uniform sampling: the unique optimal geometry consists of two adjacent short gaps and one long gap, while the equally spaced configuration has strictly larger worst-case gain. The exact constant, tightness, norming directions and full geometric rigidity make the result usable as a benchmark for kernel sensor placement and distinguish raw information amplification from reconstruction error. No higher-sample claim is inferred.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest sources are the 2010 periodic lattice interpolation preprint, the 2012 RKHS optimal recovery paper, and a related exact atomic-mean circle Sobolev design result (bibliography in RESULT.md). The general reproducing-kernel reduction is prior art, not a claimed invention. The result treats precisely three unweighted real evaluations, a unit circle, and arclength-zero-mean \(H^1\); it does not solve arbitrary sample counts, weighted observations, or recovery design.
