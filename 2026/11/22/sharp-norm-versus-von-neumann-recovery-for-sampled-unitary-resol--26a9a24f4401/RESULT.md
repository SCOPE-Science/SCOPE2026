# Sharp norm-versus-von-Neumann recovery for sampled unitary resolvents

## Finding

Let \(H=L^2(\mathbb R,dx)\), \((Af)(x)=xf(x)\) on its natural self-adjoint domain, and \(U_t=e^{itA}\). Given any set \(T\subset(0,\infty)\), define \(\mathcal C_T=C^*(I,\{U_t:t\in T\})\), \(\mathcal W_T=W^*(\{U_t:t\in T\})\), and the common alias-period group
\[
P_T=\{p\in\mathbb R:tp\in2\pi\mathbb Z\ \text{for every }t\in T\}.
\]
For every \(\lambda=\alpha+i\beta\) with \(\alpha>0\), put \(R_\lambda=(\lambda-iA)^{-1}\). Then
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{dist}(R_\lambda,\mathcal C_T)=\frac1{2\alpha},\qquad
\operatorname{dist}(R_\lambda,\mathcal W_T)=
\begin{cases}0,&P_T=\{0\},\\ \frac1{2\alpha},&P_T\ne\{0\}.\end{cases}}
\]
Both positive distances are attained by the scalar operator \((2\alpha)^{-1}I\). Moreover \(\mathcal W_T\) consists exactly of multiplication operators by measurable functions invariant almost everywhere under every shift in \(P_T\). In particular, two positive clocks whose ratio is irrational recover the resolvent in the von Neumann algebra, but *no set of clocks, even all positive real times*, gives operator-norm accuracy better than \((2\alpha)^{-1}\) from its unital sampled \(C^*\)-algebra.

There is a strict infinitary transition: with \(T_N=\{1/j!:1\le j\le N\}\) and \(T_\infty=\bigcup_{N\ge1}T_N\), one has \(P_{T_N}=2\pi N!\mathbb Z\), so the \(\mathcal W_{T_N}\)-distance is \((2\alpha)^{-1}\) for every finite \(N\), whereas \(P_{T_\infty}=\{0\}\), and hence the \(\mathcal W_{T_\infty}\)-distance is zero. Even then the sampled \(C^*\)-distance remains \((2\alpha)^{-1}\).

## Assumptions and scope

All algebras act concretely on \(L^2(\mathbb R,dx)\). The notation \(\operatorname{dist}\) refers to the operator norm in \(B(H)\). Both algebras are unital. The set \(T\) may be empty, finite, countably infinite or arbitrary; an empty set gives the scalar algebra and \(P_T=\mathbb R\). The multiplier \(R_\lambda\) has symbol \(r_\lambda(x)=(\alpha+i(\beta-x))^{-1}\). No boundedness of the generator is assumed: \(\sigma(A)=\mathbb R\).

## Proof

**Step 1 (the alias-period classification).** Let \(G_T\) be the additive subgroup of \(\mathbb R\) generated by \(T\) and their negatives. The group \(P_T\) consists exactly of real numbers \(p\) such that \(e^{igp}=1\) for all \(g\in G_T\). Every subgroup of \(\mathbb R\) is either dense, cyclic discrete \(a\mathbb Z\) for some \(a>0\), or zero. Indeed, arbitrarily small positive group elements imply density; otherwise the infimum of positive elements is attained and the Euclidean division argument proves cyclicity. Thus \(P_T=\{0\}\) precisely when \(G_T\) is dense. The other cases are \(G_T=a\mathbb Z\), \(P_T=(2\pi/a)\mathbb Z\), and \(G_T=\{0\}\), \(P_T=\mathbb R\).

For dense \(G_T\), the strong continuity of \(t\mapsto U_t\) implies that \(U_s\in\mathcal W_T\) for all \(s\in\mathbb R\). By the spectral theorem for the multiplication generator, \(W^*(U_s:s\in\mathbb R)=\{M_f:f\in L^\infty(\mathbb R)\}\); hence \(\mathcal W_T=L^\infty(\mathbb R)\) in its multiplication representation. In the discrete case \(G_T=a\mathbb Z\), the sampled algebra is \(W^*(U_a)\). The Borel functional calculus for multiplication by \(e^{iax}\) identifies it with precisely the measurable \((2\pi/a)\)-periodic multipliers. The zero-group case gives scalars. This proves the asserted \(\mathcal W_T\) description and the zero-distance statement when \(P_T=\{0\}\).

**Step 2 (the universal upper bound).** Direct algebra gives the exact circle identity
\[
\left|\frac1{\alpha+i(\beta-x)}-\frac1{2\alpha}\right|=\frac1{2\alpha}\quad(x\in\mathbb R).
\]
The scalar \((2\alpha)^{-1}I\) belongs to both sampled algebras, proving both upper bounds.

**Step 3 (the sampled \(C^*\)-norm obstruction).** By the multiplication representation, the elements of \(\mathcal C_T\) are uniform limits of trigonometric polynomials in \(x\), hence Bohr-almost-periodic continuous functions \(f\). For any such \(f\), Bohr recurrence supplies \(s_n\to+\infty\) with \(\sup_x|f(x+s_n)-f(x)|\to0\). Let \(d=\sup_x|r_\lambda(x)-f(x)|\). At \(x=\beta\), \(|1/\alpha-f(\beta)|\le d\). Since \(r_\lambda(\beta+s_n)\to0\), recurrence also gives \(|f(\beta)|\le d\). By the triangle inequality, \(1/\alpha\le2d\), so \(d\ge(2\alpha)^{-1}\). The operator norm of a continuous multiplier is its supremum norm, giving equality with Step 2.

**Step 4 (the positive-distance von Neumann case).** Suppose \(P_T\ne\{0\}\). Take some nonzero \(p\in P_T\). Every multiplier \(M_f\in\mathcal W_T\) is \(p\)-periodic almost everywhere by Step 1. Write \(d=\|R_\lambda-M_f\|\). For every integer \(n\ge1\) and almost every \(x\),
\[
|r_\lambda(x)-r_\lambda(x+np)|\le2d.
\]
Taking \(n\to\infty\) and using \(r_\lambda(x+np)\to0\) pointwise gives \(|r_\lambda(x)|\le2d\) almost everywhere. Its essential supremum is \(1/\alpha\), hence \(d\ge(2\alpha)^{-1}\). Step 2 gives equality.

**Step 5 (finite versus infinite sampling).** A number \(p\) is an alias period for all \(1/j!\), \(1\le j\le N\), precisely when it is an integer multiple of \(2\pi N!\). Thus \(P_{T_N}=2\pi N!\mathbb Z\); their intersection is \(\{0\}\). All claimed distances now follow from Steps 1--4. If \(P_T=\{0\}\), Kaplansky density also implies the existence of a uniformly bounded net of elements of \(\mathcal C_T\) converging strongly to \(R_\lambda\), despite the fixed operator-norm barrier.

## Verification

The entire result is analytic. The critical facts are the elementary subgroup classification of \(\mathbb R\), strong continuity and spectral multiplication calculus, Bohr recurrence, and the exact circle identity. A standalone `check.py` verifies that identity by exact rational arithmetic for many parameters and verifies the factorial finite-clock period relations; it does not certify the infinite-dimensional conclusions.

## Relationship to prior work

Castillo's 2026 preprint studies the Laplace-resolvent rectangle rules, their alias points and their strong/operator-norm convergence rates; in particular its Section 5.3 treats self-adjoint generators, and Section 9 discusses uniform-clock aliasing. That work motivates the sampled-group question but does not state the two-algebra minimax distances, the criterion for arbitrary clock sets, or the factorial-clock discontinuity proved here. The recurrence property of Bohr almost-periodic functions, the spectral theorem for unitary one-parameter groups and Kaplansky density are classical inputs rather than new results. The result does not claim that sampled data are stably invertible: recovering the resolvent in the von Neumann algebra may require discontinuous measurable functional calculus.

## Limitations

This is a complete theorem only for the multiplication generator on \(L^2(\mathbb R,dx)\), equivalently Lebesgue spectral type with the specified representation, not for every unbounded self-adjoint generator. Its exact half-norm distance can fail for generators with sparse or bounded spectra. No quantitative convergence rate of strong reconstructions is asserted. The classical ingredients imply some parts of the theorem independently; the asserted addition is their joint sharp sampling-algebra distance classification and infinite-clock boundary in this concrete spectral model. An older equivalent formulation in operator-algebra or sampling literature cannot be excluded by the bibliographic checks performed.
