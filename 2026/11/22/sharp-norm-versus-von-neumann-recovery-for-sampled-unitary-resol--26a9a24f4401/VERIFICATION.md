# Verification and reproducibility

The scalar circle identity is checked by multiplying \(1/(\alpha+i(\beta-x))-1/(2\alpha)\) by the conjugate denominator; the result has magnitude exactly \(1/(2\alpha)\). This yields the constructive upper bound. Recurrence for Bohr-almost-periodic approximants gives the exact lower bound at \(x=\beta\) and along far approximate periods. The positive-period W* lower bound is measured in essential supremum and takes the countable intersection of full-measure sets for integer translates before passing to infinity.

For the W* classification, the subgroup generated by the sampling clocks is either dense, discrete cyclic, or zero. In the dense case, strong continuity gives every \(U_t\) in the generated von Neumann algebra, and the spectral theorem gives all multiplication operators. In the cyclic case, Borel functional calculus for a single periodic phase gives exactly the periodic multipliers. Thus no unproved spectral-injectivity assumption is needed. The factorial-period calculation is integer divisibility. Kaplansky density supplies the claimed strong approximants and does not assert norm convergence.

Run `python3 check.py` to reproduce the 9,400 supplementary exact rational/integer assertions. The checker contains no simulation or certification of the subgroup dichotomy, Bohr recurrence, spectral theorem or norm-distance quantifiers; these are handled in the written proof.

All claims assume \(H=L^2(\mathbb R,dx)\), \(\operatorname{Re}\lambda>0\), and the specified sampled multiplication representation. The conclusions do not automatically extend to arbitrary self-adjoint generators or to noisy finite-dimensional recovery.
