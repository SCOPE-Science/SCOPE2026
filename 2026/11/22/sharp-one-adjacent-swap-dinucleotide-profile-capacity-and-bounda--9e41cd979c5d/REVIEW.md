# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. For each interior swap of context \(abcd\), the signed ordered-pair update is \(e_{ac}+e_{cb}+e_{bd}-e_{ab}-e_{bc}-e_{cd}\). Every context uses at most four letters. Full four-letter enumeration yields one zero update and exactly two, fourteen and twenty-four signed updates per specified support of size two, three and four. Endpoint row/column sums distinguish boundary swaps. De Bruijn coverage of all four-letter contexts supplies equality for every sufficiently long word, with a rotation making both boundary pairs unequal; separate binary witnesses reach all lengths from eight. An exhaustive binary census settles shorter lengths. A disjoint-block union bound and independent endpoint indicators establish the stated limiting distribution. Two independent executable calculations reproduce the finite assertions.

## Originality
PASS against inspected evidence, with residual priority uncertainty. The sequence-profile literature treats global observations and coding distances; Damerau-distance papers treat distinct descendant strings and code constructions. Published results on one-deletion/one-insertion dinucleotide-profile ambiguity concern different edit operations. An accessible abstract of the September 2026 substring-count hashing work concerns deletion codes, not the exact adjacent-transposition observation capacity, but its full manuscript was not accessible. No retrieved published-finding corpus record stated an equivalent capacity or its all-alphabet support classification. This does not prove global uniqueness.

## Value
PASS. The exact universal read-profile output capacity, the binary sharp first-attainment threshold, explicit de Bruijn saturation constructions for arbitrary alphabets and the uniform-input limiting law address a natural ambiguity invariant of adjacent-symbol transpositions in substring-count observation channels. The result is not a mere recount of edit descendants: multiple swaps can collapse to one histogram, and their collisions are classified by signed vector support.
