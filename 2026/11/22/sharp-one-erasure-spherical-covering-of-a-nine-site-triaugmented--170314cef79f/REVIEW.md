# Scientific review: one-erasure spherical covering of a triaugmented prism

**Correctness — PASS.** The theorem is confined to the explicit nine-site unit-sphere family and to its full open fourteen-triangle chamber. All eight-site facets are presented, with eleven distinct supporting planes after each deletion type. Every remaining site lies on the asserted interior side for the entire parameter interval, not merely at tested examples. Since each erased polytope has eight vertices and one quadrilateral facet, Euler's formula bounds its number of facets by eleven, proving completeness. Direct cross-product calculations yield the table of squared origin-to-facet distances; exact rational inequalities reduce the minima to the two stated formulas. Their crossing and strict monotonicity produce the unique \(2/3\) robust optimum. The attached exact symbolic verifier passed every determinant, plane-distance and inequality test. Boundary values \(r=1/2\) and \(r=1\) are explicitly excluded.

**Originality — PASS, provisionally.** Hoehner–Ledford (arXiv:2608.04392) prove nine-vertex volume maximality. The closest inspected analyses of this very symmetric family determine the full body's incenter locus and John–Löwner ellipsoids; they do not consider deleting an input site, do not determine the new eight-vertex supporting quadrilaterals, and do not imply the sharp erasure-type exchange at \(r=2/3\). Classical spherical-covering tables optimize ordinary retained-site configurations rather than a fixed nine-site configuration under adversarial one-point deletion. Exact phrase and semantic searches did not locate the same formula. Historical unindexed fault-tolerant covering calculations remain a priority risk; novelty is not certified by a failed search.

**Value — PASS.** Adversarial loss of a single cap center is a natural robustness question for finite spherical coverings. The theorem gives the complete eight-site covering radii for both inequivalent vertex orbits in a continuously varying, recently prominent nine-vertex family and identifies a sharp, unique minimax parameter different from both its volume and inradius optimizers. Its phase boundary and exact attainable radius are structurally informative, not a one-off table entry or a reparameterization of the intact convex-body results.

**Limits and related literature.** The minimax is family-relative, not a globally optimal robust nine-point spherical covering, and independent publication-priority confirmation is outstanding. The ordinary spherical covering radii in numerical archives and the previously published full-polytope inball formula answer other questions.
