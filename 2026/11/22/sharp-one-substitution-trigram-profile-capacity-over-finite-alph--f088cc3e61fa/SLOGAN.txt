For every \(q\ge2\), a single symbol substitution in a linear word of length at least five produces at most \((q-1)(q^5-q^2-4q+4)\) distinct complete trigram-count profiles, and an order-five de Bruijn word attains the bound. An exact seven-template classification of local profile-change collisions proves the bound for all alphabet sizes, with attainment already guaranteed at length \(q^5+4\).
