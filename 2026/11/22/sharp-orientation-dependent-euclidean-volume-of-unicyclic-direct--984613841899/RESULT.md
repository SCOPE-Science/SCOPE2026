# Sharp orientation-dependent Euclidean volume of unicyclic directed-edge polytopes

## Finding
Let \(G\) be any connected simple unicyclic graph with \(n\ge3\) vertices, whose unique cycle has length \(\ell\ge3\), and orient all of its edges arbitrarily. Its canonical directed-edge polytope is
\[
P_G=\operatorname{conv}\{e_{\mathrm{head}(e)}-e_{\mathrm{tail}(e)}:e\in E(G)\}\subset H=\{x\in\mathbb R^n:\sum_vx_v=0\}.
\]
Choose either cyclic traversal and let \(\delta\) equal the number of cycle edges agreeing with this traversal minus the number disagreeing. All volumes below are intrinsic Euclidean volumes in the actual affine span, using the standard orthonormal metric of \(\mathbb R^n\).

**Dimension and volume dichotomy.** If \(\delta\ne0\), the polytope is a simplex of dimension \(n-1\), with
\[
\operatorname{Vol}_{n-1}(P_G)=\frac{\sqrt n\,|\delta|}{(n-1)!}.
\]
If \(\delta=0\), necessarily \(\ell\) is even, and the directed graph has an integer height potential \(h=(h_v)\), unique up to translation, satisfying \(h_{\mathrm{head}(e)}-h_{\mathrm{tail}(e)}=1\) on every edge. Put \(\bar h=n^{-1}\sum_vh_v\). Then the polytope has dimension \(n-2\), with
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Vol}_{n-2}(P_G)
=\frac{\ell}{2(n-2)!}\sqrt{n\sum_v(h_v-\bar h)^2}.}
\]
The second formula is independent of the additive normalization of \(h\).

**Sharp same-graph distortion.** Let \(F_t\), for any integer \(t\ge1\), consist of a four-cycle and \(t\) leaves attached at one of its vertices. Direct its cycle so that the successive cycle heights are \((0,1,2,1)\); of its leaf edges, direct \(a\) toward the leaves and the other \(t-a\) toward the attachment vertex, where \(0\le a\le t\). Write \(n=t+4\) and \(V_{t,a}=\operatorname{Vol}_{n-2}(P_{F_t,a})\). Then
\[
\boxed{V_{t,a}^2=\frac{4}{((t+2)!)^2}\Bigl[(t+4)(t+6)-(4+2a-t)^2\Bigr].}
\]
The minimum occurs uniquely at \(a=t\); all leaves point outward. The maximum occurs exactly at the integers \(a\in[0,t]\) minimizing \(|4+2a-t|\). Put \(\varepsilon_t=\min_{0\le a\le t}|4+2a-t|\); it is \(0\) for even \(t\ge4\), \(1\) for odd \(t\ge3\), and respectively \(3,2\) for \(t=1,2\). The sharp ratio is
\[
\boxed{\frac{\max_a V_{t,a}}{\min_a V_{t,a}}=
\sqrt{\frac{n(n+2)-\varepsilon_t^2}{2n}}\sim\sqrt{\frac n2}\quad(t\to\infty).}
\]
For each fixed \(t\), all these differently oriented directed-edge polytopes are **unimodularly equivalent** in their affine lattice, hence share their face lattice, Ehrhart data, and normalized lattice volume \(2\); nevertheless their intrinsic Euclidean volumes have this unbounded sharp ratio. For \(t=1\), the squared centered-height energies are \(10\) and \(26\), giving volumes \(\sqrt{10}/3\) and \(\sqrt{26}/3\).

## Assumptions and scope
Edges are simple, unit-weight oriented incidence vectors in the fixed canonical Euclidean realization. The volume dimension changes between the non-semi-balanced and semi-balanced cases, and therefore volumes from the two cases are not compared as same-dimensional numbers. The orientation-dependent ratio concerns only the semi-balanced family \(F_t\) with the cycle orientation held fixed. No assertion about general multicyclic graphs, arbitrary root configurations, or weighted incidence vectors is made. Normalized lattice volume always refers to the lattice induced on the polytope's affine span, whereas Euclidean volume refers to the ambient standard metric.

## Proof
**1. Spanning-tree determinant.** Delete any one cycle edge from \(G\). The remaining oriented incidence vectors \(u_1,\ldots,u_{n-1}\) form an integer basis of the root lattice \(A_{n-1}=H\cap\mathbb Z^n\). The Euclidean covolume of this lattice is \(\sqrt n\): the basis \(e_i-e_n\) has Gram determinant \(n\). Consequently the Euclidean parallelotope generated by the \(u_i\) has volume \(\sqrt n\), irrespective of the orientation or the tree.

**2. Nonbalanced simplex.** List the edges in cyclic order and write their oriented roots as \(v_i=s_i(e_{i+1}-e_i)\), with \(s_i\in\{\pm1\}\), and index all bridge vectors separately. The unique linear dependence among the \(n\) incidence roots is
\[
\sum_{i\in C}s_iv_i=0.
\]
The sum of its coefficients is \(\delta=\sum_{i\in C}s_i\). If \(\delta\ne0\), no nontrivial affine dependence exists. Delete a cycle root \(v_j\). The other \(n-1\) vectors form a tree basis \(U\), and in this basis the omitted root has coefficients \(c_i=-s_js_i\) on the remaining cycle roots and zero on bridges. Thus \(\sum_i c_i=1-s_j\delta\). The simplex edge-difference determinant relative to \(U\) is, by the matrix determinant lemma,
\[
\left|\det(I-c\mathbf1^{\mathsf T})\right|=|s_j\delta|=|\delta|.
\]
Multiplying by the tree covolume \(\sqrt n\) and dividing by \((n-1)!\) gives the first formula.

**3. Balanced circuit and its Euclidean simplex volumes.** When \(\delta=0\), the cycle relation is affine. Its positive and negative supports have equal cardinality \(\ell/2\); the bridge roots have zero coefficient. This is the only affine relation, so the dimension is \(n-2\). The zero-cycle-sum condition is exactly the integrability condition for a height potential with every oriented edge increasing height by one. Such an integer potential is constructed along a spanning tree, and consistency on the closing cycle edge follows from balance.

Every oriented root \(v_e\) lies in \(h\cdot x=1\). Because \(v_e\in H\), only \(h^\circ=h-\bar h\mathbf1\) is relevant; the perpendicular distance from the origin to the common affine hyperplane is \(1/\|h^\circ\|\). For each cycle edge \(j\), delete \(v_j\) to obtain an \((n-2)\)-simplex \(S_j\) with the other \(n-1\) roots as vertices. Its pyramid with apex zero is the \((n-1)\)-simplex generated by an oriented spanning-tree basis, so its volume is \(\sqrt n/(n-1)!\). The pyramid height is \(1/\|h^\circ\|\); therefore
\[
\operatorname{Vol}_{n-2}(S_j)=\frac{\sqrt n\,\|h^\circ\|}{(n-2)!}.
\]

The affine circuit triangulation consists of exactly the \(\ell/2\) simplices \(S_j\) obtained by deleting a positive-support cycle root. To see both coverage and nonoverlap, express any point as a convex combination of the \(n\) vertices and shift its barycentric coefficients along the one-dimensional affine dependence \((s_i)\). Decrease the shift until one positive-support coefficient first reaches zero (the negative-support coefficients then increase). This places the point in at least one \(S_j\); for an interior point the first vanishing positive coefficient is unique except on a shared lower-dimensional boundary. Hence the simplex interiors are disjoint and their union is \(P_G\). Summing their equal Euclidean volumes gives
\[
\operatorname{Vol}_{n-2}(P_G)=\frac\ell2\frac{\sqrt n\|h^\circ\|}{(n-2)!},
\]
which is the stated formula.

**4. Same-lattice family and sharp extreme.** For \(F_t\), normalize the height of the attachment vertex to zero. Its four cycle heights are \((0,1,2,1)\); exactly \(a\) leaf heights equal \(1\) and the other \(t-a\) equal \(-1\). Therefore
\[
\sum_v h_v=4+2a-t,\qquad \sum_vh_v^2=t+6,
\]
and so
\[
n\sum_v(h_v-\bar h)^2=(t+4)(t+6)-(4+2a-t)^2.
\]
Substitution into the balanced-volume formula with \(\ell=4\) gives the explicit squared volume. At a fixed \(t\) the bracket is largest precisely when the nonnegative integer \(a\le t\) makes \(|4+2a-t|\) smallest. Its smallest value occurs at the largest attainable \(|4+2a-t|\), which is the unique endpoint \(a=t\), of absolute value \(t+4\), yielding squared energy \(2(t+4)\). Evaluating the other extreme gives the sharp ratio and its asymptotic.

Finally fix any cycle edge and delete it. Its other \(n-1\) incidence vectors form an integer basis of \(A_{n-1}\). Reversing any subset of the bridge vectors amounts to negating the corresponding members of that integer basis, which is an automorphism of \(A_{n-1}\). The omitted cycle root is a linear combination exclusively of retained cycle vectors, so the same automorphism also fixes that omitted root. It consequently maps the entire directed-edge vertex set for one leaf orientation bijectively to that for another. Thus all these polytopes are related by integral unimodular linear maps, proving identical face lattices, Ehrhart data and normalized lattice volumes. The circuit triangulation is by two spanning-tree simplices, each unimodular in the induced affine lattice, so the common normalized volume equals \(2\).

## Verification
The supplied standalone `verify.py` uses only exact integer determinant arithmetic from the Python standard library. It reconstructs the incidence vectors, calculates Gram determinants of the actual simplex vertices without invoking the claimed covolume or height-normalization formulas, and checks the formulas for all \(1,144\) orientations across ten unicyclic graph instances. For balanced cases it checks every cycle-edge deletion. It also tests the four-cycle leaf-family exact energy and its sharp maximum and minimum for every \(1\le t\le15\). A separate numerical convex-hull comparison was carried out for low-dimensional balanced and nonbalanced cases; this is supplementary, not a proof. Run `python verify.py` from a directory containing this file. The all-order statements are established by the analytic proof above; finite computation alone cannot establish them.

## Relationship to prior work
Kálmán and Tóthmérész (2021) introduce the relevant directed root-polytope framework and the semi-balanced dimension dichotomy, and establish spanning-tree dissections and lattice-normalized information. Numata, Takahashi and Tamaki (2022) characterize faces using rank potentials, including balanced cycles. The published oriented-cycle Ehrhart classification gives the normalized lattice volume for a single cycle. These facts motivate and supply the natural comparison class, but they do not state the orientation-dependent **intrinsic Euclidean** volume formula for arbitrary attached trees or the sharp, unbounded same-graph orientation ratio. The argument explicitly retains the known lattice-normalized-volume and rank-potential ingredients and adds the Euclidean normal-norm calculation together with the fixed-graph extremal optimization. A general lattice-covolume identity might permit some of these evaluations as deductions; no inspected equivalent statement of the sharp family theorem was located.

## Limitations
The unbalanced formula and balanced formula concern different intrinsic dimensions; comparing their numerical values is not geometrically meaningful without normalizing dimensions. The sharp extremal ratio is proved for the four-cycle with all leaves attached to a single vertex, not asserted as an optimum over all unicyclic graphs. The claim establishes a sharp separation between Euclidean and lattice geometry but does not imply an extremal universal isoperimetric or Mahler inequality. The priority assessment is provisional: it remains possible that a general theorem elsewhere already implies the full same-graph sharp extremal statement. The verifier's finite tests support, but do not replace, the proof.
