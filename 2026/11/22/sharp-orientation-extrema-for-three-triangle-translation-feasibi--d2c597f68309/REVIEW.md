# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS

The target is the four-dimensional Lebesgue measure of all two-translation pairs giving nonempty triple intersection. The proof establishes a general three-body mixed-area identity by integrating the area of each feasible second-translation section. The boundary integration uses each contributing boundary arc exactly once, except for null-translation coincidences. The equilateral mixed area is computed directly from its three support normals, and the global equality configurations follow by strict concavity of weighted cosines on two complete gap cells of the orientation circle. The proof handles positive but otherwise arbitrary scales, orientation wraparound, tied smallest scales, and both extrema. An independent twenty-seven-vertex convex-hull computation matched 250 random parameters and eight boundary configurations. These finite tests are checks, not replacements for the analytic arguments.

## Originality — PASS (provisional priority)

Kweon–Zhu (arXiv:2301.02949, Section 5; arXiv:2307.00409, introductory theorems and methods) optimize planar overlap area among translations; that maximum is distinct from the four-dimensional measure of the complete feasible-translation set. Haddad–Langharst–Putterman–Roysdon–Ye (arXiv:2304.07859, introduction and higher-order difference-body sections) treat Schneider's identical-body higher-order difference bodies and the established simplex upper value. The known value for aligned equilateral triangles is explicitly not claimed as new. The distinct-template weighted mixed-area profile and exact opposite-orientation minimum are not implied by those statements. Focused searches across published mathematical findings and primary literature located no exact or stronger covering theorem. Priority remains provisional: an older unindexed mixed-area or translative-integral formula may implicitly cover the profile, and the full classification may have appeared without the same terminology.

## Value — PASS

The feasible-translation volume is a natural measure of geometric tolerance in a three-shape matching problem: it quantifies the size of the translation domain in which a common point exists, unlike the best overlap at one translation. The theorem supplies the complete orientation profile for independently scaled equilateral templates; more substantially, it determines two distinct sharp orientation shapes, with a weighted allocation rule identifying the smallest triangle as the phase-opposed singleton. The \(11A^2\) lower value and weighted equality classification are not obtained from the known identical-body Rogers–Shephard maximum. The result is exact, structural, and useful as a benchmark for higher-order translated-convex-body geometry. Broader shapes and more templates remain open outside the theorem's scope.
