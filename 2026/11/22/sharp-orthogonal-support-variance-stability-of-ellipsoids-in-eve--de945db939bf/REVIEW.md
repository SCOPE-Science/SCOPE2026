# Scientific review: sharp orthogonal-support variance stability

**Correctness — PASS.** The supporting-hyperplane intersection is exactly the squared-support Haar frame sum. Orthogonal equatorial averaging has Gegenbauer multipliers \(p_{d,2m}(0)\). Their absolute ratios decrease strictly in dimension \(d\ge3\), and the degree-\(6\) and degree-\(4\) terms are the sharp minimum and maximum nonzero spectral values. Tiny smooth harmonic perturbations of the ball are honest strictly convex support functions, so both constants are attained. Exact rational moment tests and seeded random-frame checks corroborate, but do not replace, the all-dimensional analytic proof.

**Originality — PASS (provisional priority).** Classical real frame-function theory proves that exact constant sums are quadratic; the zero-variance characterization is therefore not new. The literature read supplies general harmonic methods, not the full claimed quantitative random-support-plane stability with exact two-sided dimensional constants and smooth convex equality examples. The possibility of an implicit or unindexed sharper frame-operator theorem remains.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** It converts the classical director-sphere/orthogonal-support characterization into an optimally conditioned stochastic shape test, complete for all dimensions, with meaningful equality geometry. This identifies the most and least detectable nonellipsoidal harmonic deformations, rather than just evaluating isolated samples.

**Closest prior literature.** Cooke–Keane–Moran (1985) and Moretti–Pastorello (2012) establish qualitative frame-function rigidity and harmonic methods. The later Benedetto–Koprowski–Nolan work develops generalized Parseval-frame characterization, not the same random-frame support statistic. The exact Funk/Gegenbauer multipliers are known and are explicitly used; novelty is not claimed for them. Full independent priority and proof review remain outstanding.

**Scope and limits.** Origin-symmetric convex bodies, Haar orthonormal frames, spherical \(L^2\) error of squared supports versus general quadratic forms; no asserted global Hausdorff stability. The least-squares quadratic need not be positive definite far from a ball.
