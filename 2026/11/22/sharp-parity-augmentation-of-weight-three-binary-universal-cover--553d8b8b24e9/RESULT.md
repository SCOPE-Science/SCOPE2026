# Sharp parity augmentation of weight-three binary universal covers

## Finding
For every even integer \(n\ge 6\), consider oriented circular binary words \(C\) in which every cyclic length-\(n\) factor has Hamming weight either two or three, and **every** binary length-\(n\) word of weight three appears **exactly once** as a cyclic factor. Weight-two factors may be repeated. The minimum possible circumference is
\[
L_n=\binom{n}{3}+\frac{n(n-2)}4.
\]
Moreover, the multiset of cyclic length-\(n\) factors of every minimum-length cover is uniquely specified: each weight-three word occurs once, and each weight-two word with its two one-positions of the same parity occurs once, with no other factors. This is uniqueness of the *factor multiset*, not uniqueness of the circular word up to rotation. A constructive directed Euler circuit realizes the bound.

## Assumptions and scope
A binary circular word of circumference \(L\) has \(L\) indexed cyclic windows of any given length \(n\), read with wrapping; repeated windows count with multiplicity. Position indices inside a window are \(0,1,\ldots,n-1\), and \(\operatorname{wt}(w)\) is the number of one-bits. Define the overlap digraph \(D_n\) with vertices binary words of length \(n-1\), and one directed arc for each binary length-\(n\) word, directed from its length-\((n-1)\) prefix to its length-\((n-1)\) suffix. All claims hold for every even integer \(n\ge6\) under the stated **two-or-three-weight-only** restriction; no claims are made about odd orders or covers that use auxiliary windows of weights outside \(\{2,3\}\). The length-four boundary is excluded because its weight-three rotation subsystem is already one cycle and requires no augmentation.

## Proof
**1. The fixed-weight arcs split into rotation cycles.** For any weight \(k\), arcs of \(D_n\) of weight \(k\) form a balanced directed graph whose active vertices have weight \(k\) or \(k-1\). An active vertex of weight \(k\) has one possible incoming and outgoing weight-\(k\) arc, each using a zero at the corresponding end; a vertex of weight \(k-1\) has the analogous unique arcs using one. Consequently each active vertex has indegree and outdegree exactly one. Thus these arcs split into vertex-disjoint directed cycles; following an arc rotates its length-\(n\) binary label by one bit, and the cycles correspond exactly to rotation necklaces of fixed-weight words.

**2. Every possible auxiliary multiset contains complete weight-two rotation classes.** A covering circle is a closed directed walk using every weight-three arc once and some weight-two arcs, perhaps repeatedly. Since the full weight-three arc set is balanced, the multiset of added weight-two arcs is balanced on its own. At each active vertex of the weight-two-only graph there is exactly one entering and one leaving weight-two arc. Their multiplicities must agree; propagating equality around its directed cycles shows that on each weight-two rotation orbit all arcs have the same nonnegative multiplicity. In particular, its *support* is invariant under cyclic rotation of the \(n\) positions.

**3. Connected coverage forces a triangle-free complement.** Form a simple graph \(J\) on the \(n\) position indices: the pair \(\{i,j\}\) belongs to \(J\) exactly when the length-\(n\) word with ones precisely at \(i,j\) belongs to the auxiliary support. Each weight-three necklace cycle has no overlap-graph vertices in common with any other weight-three necklace cycle, by Step 1. Such a necklace indexed by a triple \(T\) can share a vertex with an auxiliary weight-two necklace exactly when at least one two-element subset of \(T\) belongs to \(J\): to see this, rotate a selected three-one word to put its third one at either end, leaving a length-\((n-1)\) word with the chosen two one-positions, and conversely every shared vertex arises by deleting such an endpoint one. If a triple \(T\) has no selected pair, its whole required necklace cycle is an isolated component of the union digraph. A single Euler tour cannot cover it together with other required cycles. Since \(n\ge6\) supplies more than one weight-three necklace, every triple must contain a pair of \(J\). Equivalently, the complement graph \(\overline J\) contains **no triangle**.

**4. Sharp lower bound.** Mantel's triangle-free graph theorem gives \(|E(\overline J)|\le n^2/4\). Indeed, for every edge \(uv\) of a triangle-free graph, \(\deg(u)+\deg(v)\le n\); summing gives \(\sum_v\deg(v)^2\le n|E|\), whereas Cauchy--Schwarz gives \(\sum_v\deg(v)^2\ge4|E|^2/n\). Hence
\[
|J|\ge \binom n2-\frac{n^2}4=\frac{n(n-2)}4.
\]
Each pair of \(J\) corresponds to a distinct auxiliary weight-two length-\(n\) factor, occurring at least once. Thus every cover has at least \(\frac{n(n-2)}4\) extra factors, in addition to its exactly \(\binom n3\) weight-three factors.

**5. Construction and connectedness.** Select each weight-three arc once, together with each weight-two arc whose two one-positions have the same parity, once. The auxiliary arcs are unions of full rotation necklaces since \(n\) is even, and the combined digraph is balanced. There are exactly \(2\binom{n/2}{2}=\frac{n(n-2)}4\) auxiliary arcs. Every triple of indices has two of the same parity, so each weight-three necklace shares a vertex with an auxiliary necklace. To connect all auxiliary necklaces to one another, classify a selected weight-two necklace by its smaller circular one-position separation \(d\), which is even; the separations are \(d=2,4,\ldots\), up to \(n/2\) when that endpoint is even. For each \(d>2\), the weight-three necklace of the triple \(\{0,2,d\}\) meets both the separation-\(2\) auxiliary cycle and the separation-\(d\) auxiliary cycle. Consequently all active cycles of the combined graph lie in one weakly connected component. A finite balanced directed graph with one weakly connected active component is Eulerian. Traverse its Euler circuit, writing the appended bit of each arc to obtain the required circular word of length \(L_n\).

**6. Uniqueness of the minimizing factor multiset.** At equality, \(|J|=\frac{n(n-2)}4\), all selected arcs occur once, and \(\overline J\) attains the equality case of Mantel's theorem. For even \(n\), this forces \(\overline J\) to be a complete bipartite graph with two classes of size \(n/2\). The one-step cyclic shift of the \(n\) indices preserves \(J\) by Step 2, hence preserves this complete-bipartite graph. It must exchange its two nonempty classes: if it preserved each class, its transitive action on the full set of indices would keep the orbit of each index inside one class, impossible. Thus these classes are precisely the even and odd positions. Therefore \(J\) is exactly the same-parity-pair graph. No auxiliary multiplicity can exceed one at the optimum, proving the claimed unique factor multiset.

## Verification
The standalone Python 3 program `verify.py`, using only the standard library, reproduces the combinatorial checks by constructing the prescribed arcs and their overlap graph, enumerating all rotation-closed selections of weight-two necklaces, finding the connected balanced selections of least size, and reconstructing an Euler circuit whose cyclic window multiset is checked explicitly. Running `python3 verify.py` verifies every even order \(n\in\{6,8,10,12,14\}\), with respective optimal lengths \(26,68,140,250,406\); in each case the same-parity set is the **unique** optimal auxiliary support. The length-six circular witness printed by the verifier is `11001010100110100011100010`. These finite checks corroborate, but do not replace, the all-order graph-theoretic proof above.

## Relationship to prior work
Ruskey, Sawada and Williams (2012) established Eulerian constructions for the **entire** fixed adjacent weight range, including every weight-two and weight-three factor, not the minimum subset of weight-two factors needed to connect all weight-three necklaces. Sawada, Williams and Wong (2013) obtained universal cycles and gluing lemmas for arbitrary complete contiguous weight ranges; the 2016 greedy-and-necklace work treats a separate language closure criterion. The present theorem instead fixes exact once-only coverage at weight three, permits a selected and potentially repeated auxiliary weight-two family, minimizes its cardinality, and classifies its uniquely forced optimal support. Earlier equivalent sparse augmentation results may exist outside the literature inspected; no priority claim is made beyond the checked comparisons.

## Limitations
The theorem covers even \(n\ge6\), not odd \(n\), and prohibits auxiliary factors of weights outside two and three. It characterizes the optimal factor multiset, not the number or isomorphism classes of Eulerian circles realizing it. The exhaustive program checks only five even orders; universal validity rests on the proof. The primary antecedent's first verified public availability is May 8, 2012; this is a source date, not a claimed date for the present result.
