# Sharp parity cusps for exact-distance copula capacity

## Finding

For two random variables with **prescribed uniform marginals**, but arbitrary dependence, the largest possible atom at a prescribed nonzero absolute difference is explicit. For every \(0<d<1\), set
\[
M(d)=\max_{U,V\sim\operatorname{Uniform}[0,1]}\Pr(|U-V|=d),\qquad m=\lfloor 1/d\rfloor,\qquad r=1-md\in[0,d).
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{M(d)=\begin{cases}md,&m\text{ even},\\2-(m+1)d,&m\text{ odd}.\end{cases}}
\]
Thus the optimal *failure probability* is \(r\) when \(m\) is even and \(d-r\) when \(m\) is odd. Each maximum is attained by an **exchangeable deterministic copula** generated by a Lebesgue-preserving involution.

For \(j=1,2,\ldots\), the exact-distance event is achievable almost surely precisely at \(d=1/(2j)\). Every odd reciprocal \(d=1/(2j+1)\), for \(j\ge1\), is a strict local minimum of \(M\), with value \(1-1/(2j+1)\). Between reciprocal distances the envelope is linear, with alternating slopes. The boundary cases are \(M(0)=1\) and \(M(d)=0\) for \(d\ge1\).

## Assumptions and scope

All random variables are Borel-measurable and have marginal law Lebesgue measure on \([0,1]\). In particular their joint law need not have a density and may concentrate on graphs. No exchangeability is required of a competitor. The construction attains the supremum; the theorem does **not** assert uniqueness of optimal couplings.

The one-dimensional distance \(d\) is Euclidean absolute difference on an ordinary interval, **not** circular distance modulo one. Null endpoints can be assigned arbitrarily.

## Proof

**1. Decompose the interval into finite arithmetic chains.** Fix \(0<d<1\) and write \(m=\lfloor1/d\rfloor\), \(r=1-md\). Every \(x\in[0,1)\) is uniquely \(x=t+kd\), where \(0\le t<d\) and \(k\) is a nonnegative integer for which \(t+kd<1\). For almost every residue \(t\in[0,d)\), the associated chain has length
\[
n(t)=\begin{cases}m+1,&0\le t<r,\\m,&r\le t<d.\end{cases}
\]
The map \((t,k)\mapsto t+kd\) preserves Lebesgue measure as a disjoint union of intervals of measure \(dt\); therefore the measure of any union of specified positions in these chains is the integral of the number of chosen positions over \(t\).

**2. Every coupling has a forced loss.** On each *odd*-length chain, mark vertices of even position \(k=0,2,4,\ldots\) as \(S\), and odd positions \(k=1,3,5,\ldots\) as \(B\). Do not mark vertices on even-length chains. Whenever \(x\in S\) and \(|x-y|=d\), necessarily \(y\in B\), because the successful edges connect adjacent positions of the same chain. Thus for **every** coupling of the uniform marginals,
\[
\Pr(|U-V|=d)\le 1-\Pr(U\in S)+\Pr(V\in B)=1-(\lambda(S)-\lambda(B)).
\]
An odd chain has one more \(S\)-vertex than \(B\)-vertex, while even chains contribute neither set. Hence
\[
\lambda(S)-\lambda(B)
=\int_0^d\mathbf 1_{\{n(t)\text{ odd}\}}\,dt
=\begin{cases}r,&m\text{ even},\\d-r,&m\text{ odd}.\end{cases}
\]
This proves the stated universal upper bound without assuming a Monge coupling or a transport duality theorem.

**3. A sharp coupling is an involution.** For each fixed residue \(t\), swap the adjacent pairs of chain positions \((0,1),(2,3),\ldots\). When the number of positions is odd, fix the unpaired terminal position. This defines \(T_d:[0,1)\to[0,1)\), with \(T_d(T_d(x))=x\). On each swapped interval it translates by \(+d\) or \(-d\); on each unpaired interval it is the identity. Therefore it is a Borel Lebesgue-preserving bijection, up to irrelevant endpoints. If \(U\) is uniform and \(V=T_d(U)\), then \(V\) is uniform and the joint law is exchangeable, since \(T_d\) is an involution. A displacement fails to have absolute value \(d\) exactly on the one unpaired vertex of each odd chain, whose total Lebesgue measure equals the upper-bound deficit. This attains equality and completes the proof of the formula.

**4. Sharp parity transitions.** If \(d=1/k\) for an integer \(k\ge2\), all chains have length \(k\). For even \(k\) they admit perfect adjacent pairings, giving \(M(d)=1\). For odd \(k\) one vertex per chain must remain unmatched, giving \(M(d)=1-1/k\). At a reciprocal with even denominator the slopes on either side have opposite signs toward the maximum, and at an odd reciprocal they have opposite signs away from a strict minimum. Away from reciprocal points, the explicit formula is linear. As \(m\to\infty\), the deficit is bounded by \(d\), so \(M(d)\to1\) as \(d\downarrow0\). For \(d=0\) use \(V=U\); for \(d\ge1\), the only possible distance-one endpoint pairs have zero marginal probability, and larger distances are impossible.

## Verification

The analytic argument in Steps 1–4 proves the claim for **all real** \(0<d<1\). Run `python3 checker.py` using Python's standard library to reproduce independently:

- An exhaustive search over all permutation couplings on uniform grids of orders \(2\) through \(8\), testing all nonzero grid distances; Birkhoff's theorem justifies restricting the finite grid optimum to permutations.
- Exact rational checks of the constructed involution and formula for every grid distance through order \(128\).
- Exact rational range/boundary checks for fractions of denominator at most \(59\).

These are finite diagnostic tests, not a proof that the formula holds for all real distances. No computation is required for the measure-theoretic argument.

## Relationship to prior work

Ansari (2026) solves a different prescribed-dependence problem involving the distribution of a *maximum* and increasing copula tracks. Mroz and coauthors (2016) provide sharp mass bounds for the graph or endograph of a **single** measurable mapping, which does not itself solve the maximum mass of the union of the two disjoint graphs \(y=x+d\) and \(y=x-d\). Iacò, Thonhauser and Tichy (2015) relate copula optimization to optimal transport and assignments, without giving the alternating exact-distance envelope.

Kellerer's general two-marginal support criterion, discussed for example by Côté and Wang (2026), provides a general variational characterization of the upper capacity of an arbitrary closed set. It encompasses the *optimization problem* abstractly; the present contribution is the **evaluated, sharp two-line formula, involution, and parity classification**, rather than the general support criterion. The chain imbalance above is an explicit dual witness specialized to this geometry.

## Limitations

The result concerns exactly equal **uniform marginals on an interval** and an **exact**, not approximate, distance. For independent uniforms the probability of any fixed nonzero exact distance is zero; attaining the bound uses a singular copula. No generalization to different marginals, higher-dimensional metrics, multiple permitted distances, or unique optimal couplings is asserted. A historically prior equivalent specialization of a general transport theorem may exist; none was identified in the inspected materials. The proof has not received independent mathematical validation.
