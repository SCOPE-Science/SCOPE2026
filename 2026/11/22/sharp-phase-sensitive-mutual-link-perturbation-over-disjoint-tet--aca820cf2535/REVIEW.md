# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
All admissible extension diagrams reduce exactly to local forbidden four-sets; the four edge-block weights are \((49,49,40,25)\). Conditioning on a chosen mutual-link subset gives a factorization, and elementary sampling-without-replacement probabilities yield a uniform double-hit expansion. Sub-Gaussian localization of the empty-base measure, with a stronger exponentially suppressed perturbed tail, proves both the type-count asymptotic and the first nonzero total-variation coefficient. Standard-library direct diagram enumeration matches each local formula up to four parameters; coefficient and numerical regression checks support, but do not prove, the unrestricted asymptotics.

## Originality — PASS (best-knowledge, provisional)
Conant's free-amalgamation paper sets up the ambient generic tetrahedron-free structure, without the nonempty-matched-base two-point enumeration. A previous exact empty-base pair-link theorem gives the binomial quadratic weights and lattice phases, but its base has no named hyperedges, so it does not imply the matched-base local weights or \(13/49\) perturbation. A previous overlap-matching theorem studies one-point extensions only. Actual published-finding corpus and literature searches found no statement containing the matched-base exact factorization or uniform phase-sensitive rate. An unindexed reduction to a known hypergraph/cluster polynomial remains a possibility; this review does not certify historical priority.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The induced matching is the natural first nontrivial parameter family for two-point extension constraints. The result quantifies a structural separation between exponentially strong suppression of total numbers of types and a sharply vanishing, oscillatory change in the observable mutual-link statistic. It pinpoints a model-specific finite hyperedge defect and an all-size first-order coefficient; this is more than a formal restatement of the empty-base distribution.

## Limits
The base has no intersecting hyperedges. This is a theorem about uniformly chosen complete types, not random actual vertices or full-distribution total variation. All-size correctness has not been independently assessed; a direct prior-work equivalence may yet be found.
