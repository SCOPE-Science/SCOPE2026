In attractive dipolar beta-biased continuum parking, the probability of attaining the geometric maximum of \(n\) unit rods in an interval of length \(n+\varepsilon\) has a sharp power-law exponent for every fixed integer \(n\), and that exponent changes its history-tree minimizer at \(\lambda=1/2\). Strong attraction favors near-boundary cascades, while weaker attraction favors maximally branching placement histories.
