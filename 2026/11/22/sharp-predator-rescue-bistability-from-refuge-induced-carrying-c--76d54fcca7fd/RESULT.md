# Sharp predator-rescue bistability from refuge-induced carrying capacity

## Finding

The spatially homogeneous reaction subsystem of Bisi–Bondesan–Groppi–Soresina's predator–prey model admits a sharp and nonempty region in which **three distinct nonnegative equilibria are locally organized as a stable predator-free state, a positive saddle, and an attracting positive coexistence state**, despite a negative predator invasion eigenvalue at the predator-free equilibrium. The exact dimensionless inequalities determining this coexistence/bistability region and the complete count of positive equilibria are given below. The mechanism is the predator-dependent carrying capacity generated by separate crowding of active and hidden prey, not cooperation in predator hunting.

When the predator nullcline exists, define
\[
T=\frac{\mu}{h-\mu\delta},\qquad \rho=\frac{T}{K},\qquad \eta=\frac{a\mu}{\alpha\beta hT}.
\]
The **strict bistability wedge** is
\[
\boxed{1<\rho<\frac{1+\sqrt2}{2},\qquad
 0<\eta<1-2\sqrt{\rho(\rho-1)}.}
\]
Inside the wedge the two strictly positive equilibria have predator coordinates
\[
Y_{\pm}=\frac{1-\eta\pm\sqrt{(1-\eta)^2-4\rho(\rho-1)}}{2\rho\beta},
\qquad X_{\pm}=T(1+\beta Y_{\pm}).
\]
The smaller branch is a hyperbolic saddle, the larger a hyperbolic sink, and the predator-free equilibrium \((K,0)\) is a hyperbolic sink. The associated equality \(\eta=1-2\sqrt{\rho(\rho-1)}\) is a nondegenerate saddle-node of the positive-equilibrium branches when the other parameters are fixed and predation coefficient \(a\) varies.

## Assumptions and scope

Use the spatially uniform, nonnegative-population ordinary differential equations obtained from the prey reaction in equations (41)–(42) and the predator reaction in equation (43) of the originating article, under its equal-reproduction/equal-carrying-capacity assumptions for active and refuge prey:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\dot X&=X\left[\alpha\left(1-\frac{X(1+(\beta Y)^2)}{K(1+\beta Y)^2}\right)-\frac{aY}{1+\delta X+\beta Y}\right],\\
\dot Y&=Y\left[-\mu+\frac{hX}{1+\delta X+\beta Y}\right].
\end{aligned}
\]
Here \(X\) is the total prey population and \(Y\) total predator population. Parameters \(\alpha,K,a,\beta,\delta,\mu,h\) are arbitrary *strictly positive* constants. The notation \(a\) denotes the prey-removal coefficient and \(h\) the effective predator-conversion coefficient. The equations agree with the originating model after setting \(a=c_{SP}^{H}\), \(h=c_{HE}^{HS}\delta\). Population stability always means local stability of the **two-dimensional homogeneous ODE**, not the spatial cross-diffusion PDE.

If \(h\le\mu\delta\), no strictly positive equilibrium exists. If \(h>\mu\delta\), define \(T,\rho,\eta\) as above. The complete census of strictly positive equilibria, counting distinct states (not multiplicity), is:

* For \(0<\rho<1\), exactly one positive equilibrium for every \(\eta>0\).
* For \(\rho=1\), exactly one if \(0<\eta<1\), and none if \(\eta\ge1\).
* For \(\rho>1\), exactly two if \(1<\rho<(1+\sqrt2)/2\) and \(0<\eta<1-2\sqrt{\rho(\rho-1)}\); exactly one double-root equilibrium at the positive equality \(\eta=1-2\sqrt{\rho(\rho-1)}\); and none otherwise.

The one-equilibrium fold statement requires \(\rho\) within the displayed *strict* range, so the equality corresponds to a strictly positive \(\eta\). Nothing here asserts a global basin classification or PDE spatial stability.

## Proof

**1. Reduction and root count.** Set \(q=\beta Y\). At any strictly positive equilibrium the predator equation gives
\[
(h-\mu\delta)X=\mu(1+q).
\]
Thus \(h>\mu\delta\), and the predator-nullcline equation is \(X=T(1+q)\). There \(1+\delta X+q=hT(1+q)/\mu\), and the prey per-capita growth becomes
\[
G(T(1+q),q)=\frac{\alpha}{1+q}F(q),\qquad
F(q)=1-\rho+(1-\eta)q-\rho q^2.
\]
All denominators are strictly positive in the physical quadrant, so positive equilibria correspond bijectively to positive roots of \(F\). If \(\rho<1\), \(F(0)>0\), \(F\) tends to minus infinity, and its strictly concave quadratic has exactly one positive root. If \(\rho=1\), \(F(q)=q(1-\eta-q)\), giving precisely the stated cases. For \(\rho>1\), \(F(0)<0\); two positive roots occur precisely when its vertex is positive and lies above zero, equivalently \(\eta<1\) and \((1-\eta)^2>4\rho(\rho-1)\). With \(\eta>0\), this reduces to the strict wedge in the Finding. Equality yields one repeated positive root and the remaining choices yield none. The positive roots follow from the quadratic formula.

**2. Jacobian determinant and the saddle branch.** Write \(D=1+\delta X+q\) and
\[
G(X,q)=\alpha\left(1-\frac{X(1+q^2)}{K(1+q)^2}\right)-\frac{aq}{\beta D},\qquad
H(X,q)=-\mu+\frac{hX}{D}.
\]
The original dynamics are \(\dot X=XG(X,\beta Y)\) and \(\dot Y=YH(X,\beta Y)\). On the predator nullcline, \(H_X=h(1+q)/D^2>0\) and, differentiating \(H(T(1+q),q)=0\), \(H_q=-TH_X\). At a positive equilibrium differentiation of \(G(T(1+q),q)=\alpha F(q)/(1+q)\) yields
\[
\det J=-\frac{\alpha\beta XYH_X}{1+q}F'(q).
\]
The lower positive root has \(F'>0\), hence negative determinant and one stable and one unstable eigenvalue. It is a hyperbolic saddle. The upper root has \(F'<0\), hence positive determinant.

**3. Negative trace on the upper branch.** At any positive equilibrium put
\[
\phi=\rho\frac{1+q^2}{1+q}=\frac{X(1+q^2)}{K(1+q)^2}.
\]
From \(G=0\) and strict positivity of predation, \(0<\phi<1\). The prey diagonal entry of the Jacobian is
\[
XG_X=\alpha\left[-\phi+(1-\phi)\frac{\delta X}{D}\right]
<\alpha(1-2\phi).
\]
For \(q\ge0\), the elementary minimum \(\min (1+q^2)/(1+q)=2(\sqrt2-1)>1/2\) occurs at \(q=\sqrt2-1\). Throughout the two-root wedge \(\rho>1\), so \(\phi>1/2\), and consequently \(XG_X<0\). Moreover \(YH_Y=-\beta hXY/D^2<0\). Thus \(\operatorname{tr}J<0\) for both positive branches in the wedge; combined with positive determinant, the upper root is a hyperbolic sink.

**4. Predator-free state and fold.** At \((K,0)\), the Jacobian is triangular with eigenvalues \(-\alpha\) and \(-\mu+hK/(1+\delta K)\). In the region \(h>\mu\delta\), the second eigenvalue is negative exactly when \(K<T\), or \(\rho>1\). Hence the predator-free state is a hyperbolic sink throughout the wedge. At its upper boundary, the positive root is repeated, \(F''=-2\rho\ne0\) and \(\partial_\eta F=-q\ne0\). Since \(H_X>0\) and the other Jacobian eigenvalue is strictly negative, these are the ordinary nondegeneracy and transversality conditions for a one-parameter saddle-node of positive equilibria as \(a\), hence \(\eta\), varies.

**5. Exact positive-parameter witness.** Take
\[
(\alpha,K,\beta,\delta,a,\mu,h)=\left(1,\frac{10}{11},1,1,\frac15,1,2\right).
\]
Then \(T=1\), \(\rho=11/10\), \(\eta=1/10\), and \(F(q)=(-1+9q-11q^2)/10\). Its roots are \(q_{\pm}=(9\pm\sqrt{37})/22\), both positive. The predator-free eigenvalues are \(-1\) and \(-1/21\). The positive-state Jacobian determinants are, respectively, \((37-11\sqrt{37})/840<0\) and \((37+11\sqrt{37})/840>0\); their traces are \((-131+\sqrt{37})/120<0\) and \((-131-\sqrt{37})/120<0\). These exact signs prove all three local types without numerical eigenvalue approximations.

## Relationship to prior work

Bisi, Bondesan, Groppi and Soresina (2026), equations (41) and (43), and Remark 6 establish the model and identify the non-monotone carrying capacity. Their conclusion explicitly lists investigation of the reduced model's equilibria and stability as future work. The present result is the exact homogeneous-equilibrium answer for the equal-prey-growth subcase; it neither derives the kinetic limit nor asserts a cross-diffusion stability theorem. Earlier literature including Liu et al. (2019) establishes saddle-node bistability in **different** predator-dependent-carrying-capacity systems; this work does not claim the phenomenon itself is new. Other fear/refuge and cooperative-predation models have analogous bistability by distinct mechanisms, but those theorems do not imply this model's explicit dimensionless wedge and stable-branch classification.

## Limitations

The result is a theorem only about homogeneous states and their response to homogeneous perturbations. Cross-diffusion can create spatial instabilities even when the homogeneous Jacobian is Hurwitz. The model assumes positive constant parameters and equal intrinsic growth and carrying capacities of the active and hidden prey subpopulations; the non-reproducing-refuge variants in the source are not covered. The fold describes coexistence equilibria, not the threshold for an arbitrary finite predator introduction. The original mesoscopic-to-macroscopic derivation is formal. The exact historical priority of the specialized parameter inequality remains subject to independent review, particularly among older paywalled models of state-dependent carrying capacity.
