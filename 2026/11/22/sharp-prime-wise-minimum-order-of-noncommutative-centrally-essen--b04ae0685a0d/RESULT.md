# Sharp prime-wise minimum order of noncommutative centrally essential rings

## Finding

For **every odd prime** \(p\), the smallest order of a finite associative unital **noncommutative centrally essential ring whose cardinality is a power of \(p\)** is exactly

\[
\boxed{p^6}.
\]

More precisely, no centrally essential noncommutative ring has order \(p^n\) for \(n\le5\), and an explicit six-dimensional unital algebra over \(\mathbb F_p\) attains the bound. The same statement for \(p=2\) was already established by Abyzov, Lyubimtsev and Tuganbaev [1, 2], and is not claimed as new. The addition here is the sharp prime-by-prime result for **all odd characteristics**.

## Assumptions and scope

A ring \(R\) is centrally essential if, for every nonzero \(r\in R\), there are nonzero central elements \(x,y\) with \(rx=y\). All rings are associative with identity. A finite \(p\)-ring here means a ring of cardinality \(p^n\), without assuming its characteristic equals \(p\) or that it is an algebra over \(\mathbb F_p\).

## Proof

**Reduction to a local ring.** Any finite centrally essential ring decomposes as a product of finite local rings: idempotents are central and lift modulo the nilpotent Jacobson radical [1, 3]. Every direct factor is centrally essential, and if the product is noncommutative then at least one factor is noncommutative. It is therefore enough to exclude noncommutative centrally essential *local* rings of prime-power order at most \(p^5\).

Fix such a local ring \(R\), with radical \(J\), center \(C\), and \(C_0=C\cap J\). Its residue division ring is a finite field \(R/J\cong\mathbb F_{p^f}\), and \(f\) divides \(n=\log_p|R|\), since each radical filtration quotient \(J^i/J^{i+1}\) is a vector space over the residue field. For \(n\le5\), the only possibilities with \(f>1\) and \(J\ne0\) are \((n,f)=(4,2)\). In this case \(|J|=p^2\). The radical is nilpotent, so \(J/J^2\) is a nonzero left vector space over \(\mathbb F_{p^2}\); thus \(|J/J^2|\ge p^2=|J|\), forcing \(J^2=0\). Central essentiality implies \(C_0\ne0\): otherwise no noncentral radical element could have a nonzero central multiple. Choose \(0\ne z\in C_0\). As \(J\) is one-dimensional over \(R/J\) on the left, \(z\) generates \(J\) as a left ideal. Choose \(t\in R\) lifting a generator of the finite-field extension \(\mathbb F_{p^2}/\mathbb F_p\). Every element of \(J\) is an additive combination of \(z,tz\); these commute with \(t\) because \(z\) is central, and commute with \(J\) because \(J^2=0\). Hence \(J\) is central. Since every element of \(R\) is an integer polynomial in \(t\) modulo \(J\), the ring \(R\) is generated by the single element \(t\) together with central elements. It is commutative, a contradiction.

It remains to rule out the prime-residue case \(R/J=\mathbb F_p\). As a noncommutative ring has additive center index at least \(p^2\), while the image of \(C\) in \(R/J\) is all of \(\mathbb F_p\),

\[
|C_0|=|C|/p\le p^{n-3}\le p^2.
\]

Also \(C_0\ne0\): otherwise every nonzero central element would be a unit, so a noncentral element of \(J\) could not have a nonzero central multiple. Thus \(|C_0|\) is \(p\) or \(p^2\).

If \(|C_0|=p\), choose its nonzero generator \(z\). For any noncentral \(j\in J\), central essentiality forces \(jz\) to be a **nonzero element of \(C_0\)**, since any central multiplier outside \(J\) is a unit. Thus \(jz=\lambda z\) with \(\lambda\in\mathbb F_p^\times\). But \(j\) is nilpotent and so \(j^kz=0\) for some \(k\), whereas \(j^kz=\lambda^kz\ne0\), a contradiction.

Finally suppose \(|C_0|=p^2\). Necessarily \(n=5\), \(|J|=p^4\), and \(|J/C_0|=p^2\). The quotient cannot be cyclic, or the same one-generator-plus-center argument would make \(J\), and hence \(R\), commutative. Therefore \(J/C_0\cong C_p^2\). We first prove

\[
JC_0\subseteq C_0.
\]

If \(j\in J\setminus C_0\), \(z\in C_0\), and \(jz\notin C_0\), then the cosets \(j+C_0\), \(jz+C_0\) are linearly independent in \(J/C_0\). Indeed, an equality \(jz\equiv\lambda j\pmod{C_0}\) with \(\lambda=0\) contradicts the choice; if \(\lambda\ne0\), then \(j(z-\lambda1)\in C_0\). The element \(z-\lambda1\) is an invertible central element, implying \(j\in C_0\), another contradiction. Thus \(j,jz\) span \(J/C_0\); yet they commute, because \(z\) is central. Every pair of elements of \(J\) must then commute, making \(R\) commutative. This contradiction proves \(JC_0\subseteq C_0\).

Hence left multiplication induces an additive homomorphism

\[
\rho:J\longrightarrow\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb Z}(C_0),\qquad \rho(j)(z)=jz.
\]

Every \(\rho(j)\) is nilpotent, since \(J\) is nilpotent. The operators commute: for \(j,k\in J,z\in C_0\), the element \(kz\) is central, so \(j(kz)=(kz)j=k(zj)\). There are at most \(p\) **commuting nilpotent additive endomorphisms** on any abelian group of order \(p^2\). For \(C_0\cong C_{p^2}\), all nilpotent endomorphisms are multiplication by multiples of \(p\), giving exactly \(p\) choices. For \(C_0\cong C_p^2\), if one image operator is nonzero, it is a rank-one nilpotent matrix \(N\); every commuting nilpotent matrix lies in the one-dimensional space \(\mathbb F_pN\), because the full centralizer of \(N\) consists of matrices \(aI+bN\). If the image consists only of zero, its order is even smaller. Therefore

\[
|\operatorname{im}\rho|\le p,
\qquad |\ker\rho|\ge |J|/p=p^3>|C_0|.
\]

But every noncentral \(j\in J\) must satisfy \(jC_0\ne0\), by central essentiality, since any nonzero central multiple of such \(j\) must use a central nonunit. Thus \(\ker\rho\subseteq C_0\), contradicting the displayed inequality. This excludes every prime-residue noncommutative local ring of order below \(p^6\).

**Construction reaching the bound.** Let \(A_p\) be the six-dimensional \(\mathbb F_p\)-algebra with basis \(1,a,b,c,s_1,s_2\), where \(1\) is the identity and the only nonzero products of basis elements in the radical \(J=\langle a,b,c,s_1,s_2\rangle\) are

\[
ac=ca=s_1,\qquad bc=cb=s_2,\qquad ab=s_1.
\]

All products containing \(s_1\) or \(s_2\) and another element of \(J\) vanish; all other unspecified products in \(J\) vanish. Then \(J^2=\langle s_1,s_2\rangle\), \(J^3=0\), so associativity is immediate. We have \([a,b]=s_1\ne0\) and center \(\mathbb F_p1\oplus\langle c,s_1,s_2\rangle\). Every noncentral element \(j=ua+vb+z\in J\), where \((u,v)\ne(0,0)\) and \(z\) is central, satisfies \(jc=us_1+vs_2\ne0\), which is central. Central nonzero elements are centrally essential by multiplying by \(1\), and each unit has a nonzero central multiple obtained by multiplying by \(s_1\). Thus \(A_p\) is centrally essential and noncommutative, and \(|A_p|=p^6\). This proves sharpness. \(\square\)

## Verification

The lower bound is a structural proof for all primes and all finite unital rings of prime-power order, with no numerical enumeration. The construction can be checked directly from its multiplication table: products of three radical elements vanish, the central radical has basis \(c,s_1,s_2\), and multiplication by \(c\) is injective on the noncentral quotient spanned by \(a,b\). The existing characteristic-two minimum \(64\) is due to [1, 2], not an independently asserted discovery.

## Relationship to prior work

Abyzov, Lyubimtsev and Tuganbaev [1] proved that the **absolute** smallest finite noncommutative centrally essential ring has order \(64\), and supplied binary six-dimensional examples. Their follow-up [2] classifies all order-64 examples. The present statement refines absolute minimality to a **sharp bound within each odd-prime cardinality class**, using a nilpotent-endomorphism obstruction that rules out order \(p^5\) uniformly. The construction is a scalar-field version of their earlier characteristic-two radical table and is credited as such. A 2022 survey [3] provides older general theory but does not state this prime-wise sharp cardinality.

## Limitations

The theorem concerns finite rings whose sizes are powers of one prime; it does not classify all minimal examples for odd primes or address composite sizes. The residue-degree-two boundary is treated explicitly using radical square-zero and the central radical generator; no classification of odd-prime equality cases is attempted. Independent review has not been performed.
