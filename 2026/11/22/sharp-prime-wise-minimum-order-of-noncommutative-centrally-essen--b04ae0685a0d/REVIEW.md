# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The proof first reduces any prime-power example to a local factor. For residue degree two at order four, the radical is square-zero and cyclic over the residue field; a nonzero central radical element then generates the entire radical, making the ring commutative. For prime residue fields, centrality-index bounds restrict the central radical to order at most the square of the prime; its order-one case contradicts nilpotence, while the order-two case contradicts the fact that commuting nilpotent endomorphisms of a two-dimensional prime-group module form at most a one-dimensional family. All hypotheses, both abelian-group types of the central radical, and the equality construction are covered.

## Originality — PASS (historical priority provisional)
Abyzov–Lyubimtsev–Tuganbaev (arXiv:2610.36174, arXiv:2611.16086) establish the absolute minimum 64 and classify order-64 binary rings, respectively. Neither establishes sharp lower bounds at every odd prime: their order-based exclusions stop below 64, whereas the new obstruction excludes odd-prime powers through exponent five. The earlier six-dimensional radical multiplication pattern is credited. Search of the relevant older survey and claim-level public research records identified no prime-wise theorem. An unindexed source containing the same bound is an unresolved historical risk.

## Value — PASS
The smallest noncommutative centrally essential finite ring is known absolutely, but this does not answer the more refined natural question with residue characteristic fixed. The exact exponent six for every odd prime is an infinite family of sharp extremal results with a structural obstruction, not a finite known-table recomputation.
