# Sharp quadratic maximum-entry sensitivity of matrix sine on the two-dimensional positive cone

## Finding
For \(b>0\) define the compact convex spectral interval
\[
\mathcal K_b=\{A\in\mathbb R^{2\times2}:A=A^{\mathsf T},\ 0\preceq A\preceq bI\},
\qquad \|H\|_{\max}=\max_{i,j}|H_{ij}|.
\]
Let \(\mathcal L(b)\) be the least constant such that
\[
\|\sin A-\sin C\|_{\max}\le\mathcal L(b)\|A-C\|_{\max}
\quad\text{for all }A,C\in\mathcal K_b.
\]
Then, as \(b\downarrow0\),
\[
\boxed{\mathcal L(b)=1+\kappa b^2+O(b^4),\qquad
\kappa=\frac{-2t_*^2-t_*+2(1+t_*)\sqrt{t_*(1-t_*)}}6
=0.118782435095756\ldots.}
\]
Here \(t_*=0.283554610289061\ldots\) is the unique real root in \((0,2/3)\) of
\[
32t^4-16t^3-14t^2+t+1=0.
\]
In particular, despite the scalar bound \(|\sin u-\sin v|\le|u-v|\), the matrix sine is strictly **expansive in the maximum absolute-entry norm** for every sufficiently small positive spectral width. The sharp leading excess is generated by a rank-one endpoint spectral direction (approached from the interior).

## Assumptions and scope
Matrices are real symmetric of order exactly two; their eigenvalues lie in the **nonnegative spectral interval** \([0,b]\). The trigonometric matrix function is defined by its absolutely convergent power series, and perturbations are measured by the largest absolute entry, not the Euclidean, Frobenius or spectral norm. The big-\(O\) constant is absolute for \(0<b\le b_0\) with fixed small \(b_0\). The statement determines a sharp second-order coefficient, **not** the complete fixed-\(b\) Lipschitz function or an attained finite-pair maximizer.

## Proof
**1. Derivative reduction and uniform expansion.** The set \(\mathcal K_b\) is convex with nonempty interior in the three-dimensional space of symmetric matrices. On a convex domain, the global Lipschitz coefficient of a continuously differentiable function equals the supremum of the induced derivative norms over interior points: the fundamental theorem of calculus gives the upper bound and short interior line segments give the reverse; boundary derivatives are obtained by continuity. Write \(A=bB\), where \(0\preceq B\preceq I\). The Fréchet derivative of the matrix-sine series is, uniformly over these \(B\),
\[
D\sin_{bB}[H]=H-\frac{b^2}{6}(B^2H+BHB+HB^2)+\mathcal R_b(B)[H],
\qquad \|\mathcal R_b(B)\|_{\max\to\max}=O(b^4).
\]
Uniformity follows directly from the absolutely summable odd-power series: for a monomial of degree \(2k+1\) the derivative is a sum of \(2k+1\) terms, with all factors bounded in spectral norm and all norms equivalent in the fixed three-dimensional matrix space.

**2. Coefficient rows in the independent-entry norm.** Write
\[
B=\begin{pmatrix}x&y\\y&z\end{pmatrix},\qquad
H=\begin{pmatrix}p&q\\q&r\end{pmatrix}.
\]
A direct multiplication gives
\[
(B^2H+BHB+HB^2)_{11}=(3x^2+2y^2)p+2y(2x+z)q+y^2r,
\]
\[
(B^2H+BHB+HB^2)_{12}=y(2x+z)p+
(x^2+xz+z^2+3y^2)q+y(x+2z)r.
\]
The \(22\) output is obtained by interchanging \(x\) and \(z\). The induced maximum-entry norm of a linear operator on symmetric \(2\times2\) matrices is the largest absolute row sum of the corresponding \(3\times3\) independent-entry coefficient matrix. Since the diagonal coefficients at order zero equal one and remain positive for small \(b\), the diagonal-row second-order contribution is
\[
C_d(B)/6,\qquad C_d(B)=-3x^2-y^2+2|y|(2x+z).
\]
The off-diagonal row has contribution
\[
C_o(B)/6,\qquad C_o(B)=-(x^2+xz+z^2+3y^2)+3|y|(x+z).
\]
Consequently the sharp coefficient is \(\frac16\max_{0\preceq B\preceq I}\max\{C_d(B),C_d(B^{\mathrm{swap}}),C_o(B)\}\).

**3. Off-diagonal rows cannot produce positive excess.** By diagonal sign conjugation we may take \(y\ge0\). If the eigenvalues of \(B\) are \(s\ge0\) and \(sr\), with \(r\in[0,1]\), every expression \(C_d,C_o\) is homogeneous quadratic in \(s\), so positive maxima can be sought at \(s=1\). For a contraction with eigenvalues \(1,r\), put \(y=(1-r)u\) with \(0\le u\le1/2\), and observe \(xz=r+y^2\). Then
\[
C_o=-1-r-r^2-2y^2+3(1+r)y.
\]
This is strictly increasing for \(0\le y\le(1-r)/2\), and its endpoint value is \(-3r^2\le0\). Thus off-diagonal rows have no positive quadratic amplification.

**4. Rank-one reduction for diagonal rows.** Any positive-semidefinite contraction has eigenvalues \(s,sr\) with \(s,r\in[0,1]\). For \(s=1\), after a diagonal sign conjugation, parameterize it as
\[
B=rI+(1-r)vv^{\mathsf T},\qquad
v=(\sqrt t,\sqrt{1-t})^{\mathsf T},\quad
u=\sqrt{t(1-t)},\quad0\le t\le1.
\]
Substitution, using \(2x+z=1+r+x\), yields the exact identity
\[
 C_d(B)=C(t)+a(t)r-D(t)r^2,
 \quad C(t)=-2t^2-t+2(1+t)u,
 \quad a(t)=2u(1-2t-2u),\quad D(t)=(1-t)(3-2t+2u)+2u.
\]
For \(3/20\le t\le 1/2\), we have \(t(1-t)\ge51/400>1/8\), so \((1-2t)^2<4u^2\); for \(1/2\le t\le1\), we have \(1-2t\le0\). Thus \(a(t)\le0\) on \([3/20,1]\), and with \(D(t)\ge0\), the maximum over \(r\) there is \(C(t)\).

It remains to rule out an improved maximum in \([0,3/20]\), where the linear term can be positive. On this interval \(u\le\sqrt{51}/20<9/25\), \(D(t)\ge(17/20)(27/10)>2\), and \(a(t)\le18/25\). The derivative formula in Step 5 implies \(C\) is increasing there: \(1+t-4t^2\ge1\) and \((4t+1)u\le(8/5)(9/25)<1\). Therefore
\[
 C_d(B)\le C(3/20)+\frac{(18/25)^2}{8}
 < \frac{633}{1000}+\frac{81}{1250}<\frac{7}{10}<C(1/4)=\frac{5\sqrt3-3}{8}.
\]
The quadratic bound follows by completing the square in \((18/25)r-2r^2\). The other diagonal row corresponds to \(t\mapsto1-t\). Hence the maximum is \(\max_{0\le t\le1}C(t)\), reached at the rank-one spectral boundary \(r=0,s=1\); at the maximizing \(t\), \(D(t)>0\).

**5. Uniqueness and exact algebraic coefficient.** For \(0<t<1\), differentiation gives
\[
\sqrt{t(1-t)}\,C'(t)
=1+t-4t^2-(4t+1)\sqrt{t(1-t)}.
\]
Any stationary point must satisfy \(1+t-4t^2>0\), hence \(t<(1+\sqrt{17})/8<2/3\). Squaring its equation gives precisely
\[
P(t)=32t^4-16t^3-14t^2+t+1=0.
\]
An exact rational Sturm-sequence count gives **one** root of \(P\) in \((0,2/3)\), and the signs at \(t=0\) and \(t=1/2\) locate it in \((0,1/2)\); the checker reproduces that count. Thus \(C\) has exactly one interior critical point before the sign restriction ends, while its derivative is negative thereafter. Since \(C(0)=0\), \(C(1)=-3\), and \(C(1/4)>0\), that critical point \(t_*\) is the unique global maximum. This establishes the claimed formula for \(\kappa\).

**6. Sharp realization by finite matrix pairs.** For the rank-one projection \(P=vv^{\mathsf T}\) with \(t=t_*\), choose interior approximants \(B_\varepsilon=(1-2\varepsilon)P+\varepsilon I\), with eigenvalues \(\varepsilon\) and \(1-\varepsilon\). The symmetric perturbation \(H=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&-1\\-1&-1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) realizes the absolute coefficient row for the \(11\) output as \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\). For each fixed interior \(bB_\varepsilon\), arbitrarily short segments in direction \(H\) remain inside \(\mathcal K_b\), so their finite two-input ratios approach the derivative. Together with the uniform derivative upper bound, this proves sharpness of the second-order coefficient. The remainder remains \(O(b^4)\) uniformly. \(\square\)

## Verification
The included standalone `verify_sine_b2.py` confirms the exact cubic derivative coefficient identities symbolically, the rank-one reduction, the quartic elimination and the exact Sturm root count in \((0,2/3)\). It also tests interior finite differences at three positive band widths and evaluates 400 independent random Taylor-derivative comparisons. Running `python verify_sine_b2.py` with NumPy, SciPy and SymPy prints `PASS`. The *proof*, not the finite tests, establishes the infinite-parameter assertion.

## Relationship to prior work
Crouzeix and Kressner [1] give general spectral-norm estimates for matrix-function Fréchet derivatives, whereas the present result optimizes a nonunitarily invariant **maximum-entry norm** over symmetric positive-semidefinite \(2\times2\) spectral intervals and determines an exact second-order constant. Al-Mohy, Higham and Liu [2] provide computational algorithms and derivative evaluation for matrix sine and cosine, but their relevant Sections 1 and 5.2 address analytic and forward-error methods rather than this small-band extremal coefficient. The elementary power series, spectral theorem, and Fréchet derivative expansions used here are standard ingredients; novelty is claimed only for the optimized sharp coefficient and its rank-one extremal structure. Public-record and prior-ledger comparisons found no matching parameterized maximum-entry coefficient, but cannot establish exhaustive historical priority.

## Limitations
The theorem is local in spectral width and uses precisely real symmetric \(2\times2\) positive-semidefinite matrices. It neither determines \(\mathcal L(b)\) exactly for arbitrary \(b\), nor asserts the same coefficient for higher dimensions, indefinite matrices, other norms, or operator sine on infinite-dimensional spaces. The supremum need not be attained by a pair of distinct matrices. No independent validation has been performed. A historical priority risk remains for unindexed specialty literature on entrywise matrix-function conditioning.
