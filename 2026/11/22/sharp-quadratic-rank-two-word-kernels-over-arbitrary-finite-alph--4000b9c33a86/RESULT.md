# Sharp quadratic rank-two word kernels over arbitrary finite alphabets

## Finding
Let \(\Sigma\) be a fixed nonempty finite alphabet with \(k=|\Sigma|\), and represent each word \(w=w_1\cdots w_n\) as a finite ordered structure with unary predicates naming precisely the letter at each position. Write \(\equiv_2\) for equivalence under all first-order **sentences of quantifier rank at most two** in the language \(\{<,(P_a)_{a\in\Sigma}\}\); there is no successor predicate. For every position \(i\), define
\[
L_i=\{w_j:j<i\},\qquad R_i=\{w_j:j>i\},\qquad S(w)=\{(w_i,L_i,R_i):1\le i\le n\}.
\]
Then the following statements hold, including for the empty word:

1. \(u\equiv_2 v\) if and only if \(S(u)=S(v)\).
2. The minimum length of a word \(v\equiv_2 w\) is exactly \(|S(w)|\). Such a shortest equivalent word is an **order-preserving subsequence** of \(w\), obtained by retaining one occurrence of each different triple in \(S(w)\).
3. For every \(k\ge1\), among words which use all \(k\) letters, the sharp largest minimal equivalent-word length is
\[
\boxed{\max_{\operatorname{alph}(w)=\Sigma}\min_{v\equiv_2 w}|v|=k(k+2).}
\]
For example, the extremal lengths for alphabets of sizes \(1,2,3,4\) are \(3,8,15,24\). The number \(k(k+2)\) is not just an upper bound: an explicit family realizes it with every position having a different rank-one pointed type.

## Assumptions and scope
All words are finite, and positions satisfy exactly one of the named unary letter predicates. The empty word is permitted and has empty signature and shortest representative of length zero. The maximization in statement 3 uses **all** letters of \(\Sigma\); if a particular word uses only \(r\) of the named letters, the appropriate bound is \(r(r+2)\) for \(r\ge1\). Quantifier rank means nesting depth, independent of formula length or quantifier alternations. Symbols, orientation, and predicate names are fixed; no quotient by renaming or reversal is taken. The theorem applies equally to two-variable first-order logic at rank two, because each rank-two sentence has an equivalent formulation using two variables.

## Proof
**Step 1: Complete rank-two signature.** Fix one distinguished position \(x\). A rank-one formula \(\theta(x)\) is a Boolean combination of the current letter test and elementary statements about whether a chosen letter \(a\) occurs to the left or right of \(x\), written \(\exists y(y<x\land P_a(y))\) and \(\exists y(x<y\land P_a(y))\). Any occurrence of \(y=x\) is resolved by the letter at \(x\); all other quantifier-free conditions on \(x,y\) reduce to their order type and letters. Closed rank-one subformulas are determined by which letters occur and hence by \(S(w)\). Therefore the truth of a rank-one formula at \(x\) is determined by \((w_x,L_x,R_x)\) and the overall signature set. A rank-two sentence is a Boolean combination of existential claims about these pointed rank-one types, together with lower-rank sentences. Conversely, for each triple \((a,B,C)\) there is a rank-one formula \(\vartheta_{a,B,C}(x)\) asserting the current letter is \(a\), with exactly the letters \(B\) strictly left and exactly \(C\) strictly right. The rank-two sentence \(\exists x\,\vartheta_{a,B,C}(x)\) says this triple occurs. Thus \(u\equiv_2 v\iff S(u)=S(v)\).

**Step 2: Exact shortest subsequence.** Every word with signature \(S(w)\) must contain at least \(|S(w)|\) positions, as one position realizes just one triple. Select one original occurrence of each triple, and keep these positions in their original order, obtaining a subsequence \(v\). The *first* occurrence of each letter \(a\) is retained: it is the unique \(a\)-position whose left-letter set excludes \(a\); likewise the *last* occurrence of \(a\) is retained, because it is the unique \(a\)-position whose right-letter set excludes \(a\). (If \(a\) occurs only once, the same position serves both roles.) Consequently, for each retained position and each letter \(a\), that letter occurred to its left in \(w\) precisely when the retained first \(a\) is to its left, and occurred to its right precisely when the retained last \(a\) is to its right. The triples of retained positions remain unchanged, so \(S(v)=S(w)\). Its size is \(|S(w)|\); this meets the lower bound.

**Step 3: Quadratic upper bound.** For each of the \(k\) letters mark its first and last occurrence. There are at most \(2k\) marked event positions. These contribute at most \(2k\) triples. Between consecutive marked positions, both \(L_i\) and \(R_i\) are constant, so every *unmarked* position in a fixed gap contributes at most one new triple per **active letter**, meaning a letter already seen but whose last occurrence is still to come. Splitting any singleton first/last coincidence into consecutive formal open and close events can only enlarge the upper bound. In the resulting \(2k\)-event sequence, every letter opens once and closes once and remains active between those two events. The number of pairs (active letter, intervening gap) is exactly the sum of the \(k\) distances between each letter's opening and closing in the event-order sequence, minus \(k\). This sum is maximal when all opening events precede all closing events: swapping an adjacent closing event followed by an opening event to opening then closing weakly increases the sum of active-gap counts. In that order the active counts in the \(2k-1\) gaps are \(1,2,\ldots,k-1,k,k-1,\ldots,2,1\), summing to \(k^2\). Hence \(|S(w)|\le 2k+k^2\).

**Step 4: Simultaneous sharpness.** Name the symbols \(a_1,\ldots,a_k\). Put down an opening occurrence of \(a_1,\ldots,a_k\) in order and then a closing occurrence of \(a_1,\ldots,a_k\) in order. After each event except the last, insert **one occurrence of every currently active symbol**, in any fixed order. There are \(2k\) event occurrences and \(k^2\) inserted occurrences. The first and last occurrences of each symbol are indeed its designated event occurrences. Every opening/closing occurrence has a profile not shared by any other occurrence of that symbol, because its left or right set respectively excludes that symbol. Each inserted occurrence has that symbol present on both sides. For two inserted occurrences of the same symbol in different gaps, at least one opening or closing of another letter intervenes; therefore their left or right letter sets differ. Inserted occurrences of different symbols obviously have different first coordinates. Thus every one of the \(k(k+2)\) positions has its own profile, and Step 2 shows no shorter rank-two-equivalent word exists.

## Verification
The standalone Python 3 script `verify.py` checks all \(2^n\) binary words at every length \(0\le n\le11\), verifies each minimum-profile subsequence preserves the signature and has exactly one position per profile, and confirms the binary quotient contains \(97\) classes (one empty, six nonempty monochromatic, ninety mixed), with sharp minimum length \(8\). It also independently plays the two-round Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé game for all word pairs of length at most five and checks agreement with signature equality. Finally it constructs the explicit extremizers for each alphabet size \(1\le k\le9\), checking the predicted length and pairwise distinct profiles. Run `python3 verify.py` from the extracted directory. All these tests are finite corroboration; Steps 1–4, not the tests, prove the statements for all finite words and alphabet sizes.

## Relationship to prior work
Weis and Immerman established a complete ranker characterization of two-variable first-order logic on words, including the underlying bounded-depth expressivity principle, and a general small-model theorem over bounded alphabets. Their Theorem 25 gives an \(O(n^k)\) model bound at quantifier depth \(n\), not this sharp all-alphabet depth-two representative identity or its coefficient \(k(k+2)\). The rank-one pointed type characterization in Step 1 is a direct low-depth specialization of established model-theoretic machinery; it is not claimed as a novel general characterization. Bazarova and Alpay investigate finite-horizon rank growth for regular languages in full first-order logic and explicitly distinguish the two-variable fragment as a separate direction. Earlier exact binary rank-two word-theory counts, and the richer successor-vocabulary counts, also do not supply the new general sharp \(k\)-letter minimal-representative formula. The claimed contribution is the exact word-by-word shortening and **uniform sharp quadratic extremal law**.

## Limitations
The result is specific to quantifier rank two and strict order without successor. The proof does not extend the sharp constant to rank three or to the successor vocabulary, nor does it count the number of all rank-two theories over a variable-sized alphabet. General ranker expressivity and model-counting context are prior results. There remains historical-priority risk from an earlier unlocated derivation of the same quadratic constant or equivalent subsequence kernel. No independent audit of the theorem or its literature priority has been performed.
