# Scientific review of the constant–quadrupole quartic extension profile

**Correctness — PASS.** The stated Fourier convention fixes the Bessel signs and the factor of \(4\pi\). Exact one-dimensional convolution identities give the four radial moment values, including the negative cubic radial coupling and positive pure-quadrupole moment. A derived identity, not an estimate, reduces the complex problem to two real quadrupoles. Matrix trace identities, Cauchy–Schwarz, and the sharp cubic trace yield the global profile and all equality conditions. The standalone symbolic checker reproduces the nontrivial numerical constants. Risk: the analytical equality argument, rather than computer enumeration, must be independently checked before reliance.

**Originality — PASS relative to investigated coverage.** Tang and Xu (arXiv:2611.20898v1, sections 4.5 and Appendix B) provide important components—degree-two quadratic and cubic couplings and angular fourth moments—but neither state nor give the exact fixed-fraction ordinary \(L^4\) extension theorem or classify complex extremizers across both interior and endpoints. The previously published conditional fourth-power profile is for a distinct mixed radial–angular target norm, with degree-one rather than degree-two extremizers. The published pure harmonic \(L^4/L^2\) constant is on the input sphere, not the ordinary Euclidean extension output. Main risk: older unindexed finite-band or phase-retrieval calculations might imply an equivalent statement.

**Value — PASS.** This is a sharp natural one-parameter transition in a complete low-degree harmonic band, motivated by the degree-two bottleneck in recent Fourier-extension stability analysis. The explicit coefficient of the cubic interaction, exact pure-quadrupole endpoint, and changed equality geometry distinguish mixed input from pure degree two. The result does not solve the unrestricted conditional extension problem, and it makes no such claim.
