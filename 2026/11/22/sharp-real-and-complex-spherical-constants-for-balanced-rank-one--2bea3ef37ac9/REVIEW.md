# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS
For every finite matched-length rank-one decomposition, optimization of unimodular phases yields the exact two-unit-vector expression for the unconditional norm. Averaging one unit vector over the real or complex Hilbert sphere gives the reciprocal gamma constant, and the scalar inequality between the linear and quadratic absolute projections yields both Bessel estimates. Finite Haar-approximating equal-mass measures, upgraded uniformly using Lipschitz equicontinuity, prove sharpness; replacing only finitely many analysis vectors enforces spanning without changing the limiting ratio. A separate cusp argument at the orthogonal hyperplane of any positive term proves strict finite nonattainment in dimension at least two. The planar regular-polygon family has an exact geometric-sum formula. Limits and fields are kept separate, and all exponent asymptotics follow from Stirling's gamma ratios.

## Originality — PASS (with historical-priority risk)
The closely matched paper by Balazs, Freeman, Popescu and Speckbacher, arXiv:2212.00947v1, Theorem 4.6, proves a bound of order \(\beta^2 C\) conditional on the frame-operator eigenvalue imbalance \(\beta\). Their Theorem 4.6 and Example 3.2 do not give the exact unconditional, dimension-only constants, simultaneous real/complex sharpness, or finite nonattainment. The bounded-conditioning estimate may be better for nearly tight frames and is not contradicted or subsumed wholesale. Bemrose, Casazza, Kaftal and Lynch, arXiv:1405.6656, study unconditional constants of *reconstruction* frame expansions, not arbitrary matched two-family multipliers. Targeted database queries for spherical gamma constants, Bessel bounds and rank-one multiplier norms yielded nearby dual-frame weaving and frame-perturbation records, neither covering this statement. Unindexed equivalent results in older absolutely summing or frame multiplier literature remain possible.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The sharp constant determines the unavoidable dimension loss when an unconditional phase bound controls **both original** Bessel/frame operator bounds, without tightness, conditioning or rescaling assumptions. It supplies different exact real and complex constants, the two distinct square-root growth coefficients, strict finite nonattainment, genuine-frame asymptotic extremizers, and a completely explicit two-dimensional sharpness sequence. This resolves a natural norm-comparison extremal problem adjacent to the quantitative finite-dimensional multiplier framework, rather than merely evaluating a special matrix.

## Limitations and citations
The result does not solve coefficient-rescaling conjectures or improve every existing conditioned estimate. The approximating sharpness family requires increasing sample length at fixed dimension. The standalone checker verifies only finite consequences.
