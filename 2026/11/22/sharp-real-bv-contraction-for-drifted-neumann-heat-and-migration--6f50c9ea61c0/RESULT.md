# Sharp real-BV contraction for drifted Neumann heat and migration of the optimal jump

## Finding

For real drift \(b\in\mathbb R\), let \(P_t^{(b)}\), \(t>0\), be the heat semigroup on \((0,1)\) generated by \(Lf=f''+bf'\) with the reflecting (ordinary derivative-zero) conditions \(f'(0)=f'(1)=0\). On real \(BV(0,1)\) modulo constants, write \(V(f)=|Df|((0,1))\), with distributional derivative and boundary-point representatives irrelevant.

For every \(b\in\mathbb R\) and \(t>0\), the exact, attained operator seminorm is
\[
 K_b(t):=\sup_{V(f)>0}\frac{V(P_t^{(b)}f)}{V(f)}
 =\max_{0<y<1} S_b(t,y),
\]
where \(S_b(t,y)=\mathbb P_y\{0<y-bs+\sqrt2 B_s<1\text{ for all }0\le s\le t\}\) is the survival probability of a Brownian motion with drift \(-b\), killed at \(0,1\). It has the absolutely and smoothly convergent expansion
\[
S_b(t,y)=2e^{by/2-b^2t/4}\sum_{k=1}^\infty
\frac{k\pi[1-(-1)^k e^{-b/2}]}{k^2\pi^2+b^2/4}
 e^{-k^2\pi^2t}\sin(k\pi y).
\]
For each positive time, \(S_b(t,\cdot)\) has **exactly one** maximizer \(y_b(t)\in(0,1)\), and \(0<K_b(t)<1\). Every nonconstant equality case in the BV inequality is, modulo almost-everywhere equivalence, a nonzero multiple plus a constant of the single-jump indicator \(\mathbf1_{(y_b(t),1)}\). Also \(K_b(s+t)<K_b(s)K_b(t)\) whenever \(s,t>0\).

The maximizing jump migrates between two explicit asymptotic locations. For each fixed real \(b\), as \(t\downarrow0\),
\[
 y_b(t)=\frac12+bt+O_b(t^2),\qquad
 1-K_b(t)\sim\frac{8\sqrt t}{\sqrt\pi}\exp\!\left(-\frac{1}{16t}\right).
\]
The leading exponentially small norm deficit is therefore **independent of drift**, despite the drift-dependent optimal jump displacement. As \(t\to\infty\), define
\[
 y_\infty(b)=\frac12+\frac1\pi\arctan\frac{b}{2\pi},\qquad
 c_b=-\frac{4\pi\tanh(b/4)}{(4\pi^2+b^2/4)\sqrt{1+(b/(2\pi))^2}}.
\]
Then
\[
 y_b(t)=y_\infty(b)+c_b e^{-3\pi^2t}+O_b(e^{-6\pi^2t}),
\]
and
\[
 K_b(t)=A_b\exp[-(\pi^2+b^2/4)t]\bigl(1+O_b(e^{-3\pi^2t})\bigr),
\qquad A_b=\frac{2\pi(1+e^{-b/2})}{\pi^2+b^2/4}
 e^{b y_\infty(b)/2}\sin(\pi y_\infty(b)).
\]
In particular \(y_0(t)=1/2\) for all positive times and \(K_b(t)=K_{-b}(t)\).

## Assumptions and scope

The spatial interval is the open unit interval; diffusion coefficient equals one (the Brownian variance rate is two), and \(b\) is a fixed real drift coefficient. The Neumann boundary condition applies to the backward semigroup on functions, not to a Lebesgue-density forward equation. Total variation is the distributional variation on the *open* interval, not a variation with prescribed boundary traces. For a BV function, \(Df\) may be an arbitrary finite real signed Radon measure. All norm and extremizer assertions are for real-valued functions, not complex-valued BV.

## Proof

**1. Differentiation and the positive Dirichlet kernel.** For smooth initial data, \(u=P_t^{(b)}f\) satisfies \(u_t=u_{xx}+bu_x\), \(u_x(t,0)=u_x(t,1)=0\). Consequently \(v=u_x\) solves the **Dirichlet** problem \(v_t=v_{xx}+bv_x\), \(v(t,0)=v(t,1)=0\). The measure-valued initial derivative passes through by approximation in BV. Thus, at positive times,
\[
 (P_t^{(b)}f)'(x)=\int_{(0,1)}q_b(t,x,y)\,dDf(y),
\]
where \(q_b\) is the strictly positive Dirichlet kernel for \(\partial_x^2+b\partial_x\). A gauge transformation gives
\[
 q_b(t,x,y)=e^{b(y-x)/2-b^2t/4}\,q_0(t,x,y),\quad
 q_0(t,x,y)=2\sum_{k\ge1}e^{-k^2\pi^2t}\sin(k\pi x)\sin(k\pi y).
\]
Strict positivity holds for \(t,x,y>0\) with \(x,y<1\), by the killed Brownian transition density or the strong maximum principle.

**2. Exact norm and equality.** The measure triangle inequality and Tonelli give
\[
 V(P_t^{(b)}f)\le\int_{(0,1)}\!\left(\int_0^1q_b(t,x,y)\,dx\right)d|Df|(y)
 \le K_b(t)V(f).
\]
The column integral equals \(S_b(t,y)\): indeed it is the action of the adjoint Dirichlet kernel, which has generator \(\partial_y^2-b\partial_y\), on the constant initial function one. It is exactly the probability of avoiding both absorbing boundaries for the drift \(-b\). With \(Df=a\delta_y\), i.e. a single jump, equality in the first inequality holds, proving sharpness. Once uniqueness of the maximizing \(y\) is known, equality in the second inequality forces \(|Df|\) to be supported on that one point, establishing the claimed complete equality classification. Since \(q_b\) is everywhere positive, no cancellation loophole arises.

**3. Unique maximum.** For any finite observation grid \(0<s_1<\dots<s_m\le t\), the joint density of Brownian increments is strictly Gaussian log-concave. The condition \(0<y-bs_j+\sqrt2B_{s_j}<1\) for every grid time is a convex set in the joint variables \((y,B_{s_1},\dots,B_{s_m})\). Its Gaussian marginal over the Brownian coordinates is log-concave in \(y\) by the Prékopa marginal theorem. Taking nested grids dense in \([0,t]\), continuity of Brownian sample paths shows that these finite-grid survival probabilities decrease to \(S_b(t,y)\). Log-concavity survives their limit. On \((0,1)\), \(S_b\) is positive and real-analytic, tends to zero at both endpoints, and is not constant. A positive analytic log-concave function cannot have two distinct global maxima without being constant on the intervening interval. Therefore the maximum is unique and interior. Absorption gives \(K_b(t)<1\).

**4. Spectral series and strict semigroup inequality.** Write \(S_b(t,y)=e^{by/2-b^2t/4}w(t,y)\); the sine coefficients of the initial datum \(e^{-by/2}\) are
\[
2\int_0^1e^{-bz/2}\sin(k\pi z)\,dz
=\frac{2k\pi[1-(-1)^k e^{-b/2}]}{k^2\pi^2+b^2/4}.
\]
This gives the displayed series and its uniform differentiability for every positive time. The sub-Markov semigroup has the strictly positive kernel \(q_b\). At the maximizer of its time \(s+t\) column mass, integrating the time-\(s\) column masses against the positive time-\(t\) kernel is strictly below \(K_b(s)\) times the time-\(t\) column mass, since the time-\(s\) mass equals its unique maximum at only one location. This proves \(K_b(s+t)<K_b(s)K_b(t)\). Reflection gives \(S_b(t,y)=S_{-b}(t,1-y)\).

**5. Short-time migration and drift-neutral leading deficit.** Fix \(b\). The two unrestricted one-sided first-passage probabilities for the drift \(-b\), at starting point \(y\), are explicitly
\[
H_L=\Phi\left(\frac{bt-y}{\sqrt{2t}}\right)
  +e^{by}\Phi\left(\frac{-bt-y}{\sqrt{2t}}\right),\qquad
H_R=\Phi\left(\frac{-bt-(1-y)}{\sqrt{2t}}\right)
  +e^{-b(1-y)}\Phi\left(\frac{bt-(1-y)}{\sqrt{2t}}\right).
\]
Here \(\Phi\) denotes the standard Gaussian CDF. The difference between \(H_L+H_R\) and the actual exit probability is a double-boundary crossing contribution; for \(y\) in a fixed neighborhood of \(1/2\), its probability and first spatial derivative are exponentially smaller than the displayed leading terms, as follows from reflection/image expansion (a double crossing requires a travel distance asymptotically at least \(3/2\)). Gaussian tail expansion, uniformly for \(|y-1/2|=O(t)\), yields leading derivative terms of equal algebraic size and exponents
\[
-\frac{(y-bt)^2}{4t},\qquad -\frac{(1-y+bt)^2}{4t},
\]
with relative errors \(O_b(t)\). The unique critical point therefore obeys \(y_b(t)=1/2+bt+O_b(t^2)\). To localize the global maximizer first, compare the exit exponent at fixed positions away from the midpoint with the midpoint: either nearer boundary produces an exponentially larger exit probability. At the maximizing point both Gaussian exponents equal \(-1/(16t)+O_b(t)\); each one-sided crossing probability is asymptotic to \(4\sqrt{t/\pi}\,e^{-1/(16t)}\). Their sum gives the stated universal deficit.

**6. Long-time migration.** Uniform termwise differentiability of the series shows that the first Dirichlet eigenfunction controls the location: \(e^{by/2}\sin\pi y\) has its unique maximum at \(y_\infty(b)\), solving \(\cot(\pi y)=-b/(2\pi)\). Its second derivative there is \(-(\pi^2+b^2/4)\) times itself. The second sine mode is separated from the first by \(3\pi^2\); implicit expansion of the unique critical-point equation gives
\[
 y_b(t)-y_\infty(b)=
 \frac{a_2\phi'_2(y_\infty(b))}{a_1(\pi^2+b^2/4)\phi_1(y_\infty(b))}
 e^{-3\pi^2t}+O_b(e^{-6\pi^2t}),
\]
where \(a_k=2\int_0^1e^{-bz/2}\sin(k\pi z)\,dz\) and \(\phi_k(y)=e^{by/2}\sin(k\pi y)\). Elementary trigonometric simplification produces the displayed \(c_b\). Evaluating the first eigenmode at its maximum gives \(A_b\) and the sharp long-time exponential law. For \(b=0\), the even sine coefficients vanish and exact reflection fixes the maximizer at \(1/2\).

## Relationship to prior work

Wang and Yan (2010) establish gradient bounds and reflecting-Brownian representation for Neumann semigroups on convex domains; their general gradient estimates are not a sharp real-BV operator norm or an optimizing-jump classification for drifted intervals. Guillaume (2015) develops explicit survival formulae for drifted Brownian motion with piecewise two-sided absorbing barriers, addressing *fixed initial conditions* and boundary schedules; that work does not optimize starting position for the present sharp BV norm or give the drift-neutral onset and optimizer-migration asymptotics. Keady's earlier work addresses preservation of log-concavity for the one-dimensional Dirichlet heat equation, a key ingredient rather than the complete conclusion. The Brownian exit and spectral identities themselves are classical. Novelty is claimed only for the combined **sharp BV norm, unique equality classification and two-end optimizer asymptotics**; the underlying survival representation and log-concavity are not claimed new.

## Limitations

The result is one-dimensional and real-valued, with constant drift, homogeneous diffusion, and two reflecting endpoints. The late-time expansion holds with drift fixed; there is no claimed uniformity as \(|b|\to\infty\). The short-time \(O_b(t^2)\) position remainder and the asymptotic ratio are not stated with globally uniform constants in \(b\). No arbitrary interval drift-field, Robin, nonlocal, complex-BV, higher-dimensional, or operator-eigenfunction classification is asserted. External historical priority cannot be fully guaranteed, especially for older treatments of drifted Brownian exit optimization.
