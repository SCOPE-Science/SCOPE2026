# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

The real-BV seminorm reduction is exact because the distributional derivative of the reflecting semigroup evolves by the positive Dirichlet kernel for the differentiated generator. Integrating positive kernel columns yields the adjoint killed-drift survival function, with single-jump functions attaining each column mass. Prékopa log-concavity and analyticity give a unique maximizing jump for every positive time, and support of the derivative measure then completely classifies equality. Sine-mode coefficient normalization, first-passage tails, and the principal/second eigenmode expansion give the stated short- and long-time laws. The standalone checker independently compares kernel quadrature to the spectral series and checks representative asymptotic consequences. A finite checker is not treated as proof of the uniform assertions.

## Originality — PASS, with historical-priority uncertainty

The nearest primary semigroup work, Wang–Yan (2010, arXiv:1009.1965), proves gradient estimates and a reflecting-Brownian representation, not this exact BV seminorm or its unique time-dependent optimizing jump. Guillaume (2015, DOI 10.1155/2015/391681), inspected in full available HTML including its two-sided extension, computes fixed-input survival probabilities for time-dependent barriers; the present norm optimization, uniqueness of equality and two-scale optimizer expansion do not follow as stated from its formulae. Keady's 1990 log-concavity and quasiconcavity theorems are acknowledged as inputs; the complete public 18-page PDF was compared, including its main theorem statements and proofs. Targeted semantic searches of indexed mathematical findings and external literature produced no equivalent norm-and-optimizer theorem. A negative search is not a proof of historical novelty. Inaccessible full older literature remains a residual risk, and independent literature priority review is not performed.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Reflecting drift diffusion is a canonical positive smoothing semigroup, but qualitative BV contraction alone does not specify the **exact positive-time loss**, identify equality cases, or determine the worst discontinuity location. The survival reduction translates the sharp norm into a natural exit-probability optimization. Its drift-independent nonperturbative short-time deficit, moving optimizing jump, and explicit second-eigenmode relaxation are quantitative stability features with a clear role in BV estimates and validation of diffusion schemes. The contribution is the complete combined classification under fixed constant drift, not a claim that Gaussian first-passage formulas or log-concavity are themselves new.

## Scope and risks

The theorem is for real functions on a unit interval, constant drift, unit diffusivity and reflecting derivative-zero boundaries. Asymptotic remainders are for fixed drift. Extension to nonconstant coefficients and general boundary conditions is unproved. Older source coverage is not exhaustive; in particular not every historical drift-survival optimization paper is available in full text. There is no independent audit.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
