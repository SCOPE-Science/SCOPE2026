# Sharp robustness and finite-input nonattainment for Shannon–quadratic simplex prediction

## Finding

Fix an integer \(n\ge2\) and real parameters \(\tau>0\), \(\kappa>0\). Write
\[
\Delta_{n-1}=\{p\in\mathbb R^n:p_i\ge0,\ \sum_{i=1}^np_i=1\}
\]
and define the Shannon–quadratic regularized prediction operator
\[
T_{\tau,\kappa}(z)=\operatorname*{argmax}_{p\in\Delta_{n-1}}
\left\{\langle z,p\rangle-\tau\sum_{i=1}^np_i\log p_i
-\frac\kappa2\sum_{i=1}^np_i^2\right\}.
\]
Here \(0\log0=0\). Set \(m=\lfloor n/2\rfloor\) and \(\ell=\lceil n/2\rceil\). Then the exact global Lipschitz coefficient between the input maximum norm and output sum norm is
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Lip}_{\infty\to1}(T_{\tau,\kappa})
=\left(\tau+\frac{\kappa n}{4m\ell}\right)^{-1}.}
\]
Equivalently, the regularizer \(\tau\sum_i p_i\log p_i+\frac\kappa2\sum_i p_i^2\) has *exact* strong-convexity modulus \(\tau+\kappa n/(4m\ell)\) on the simplex relative to \(\ell_1\).

For even \(n\), the coefficient equals \((\tau+\kappa/n)^{-1}\); for odd \(n\) it is strictly smaller than this dimension-only upper bound. All probabilities where the local cross-norm derivative achieves the global coefficient are permutations of
\[
\left(\underbrace{\frac1{2m},\ldots,\frac1{2m}}_{m\text{ entries}},
\underbrace{\frac1{2\ell},\ldots,\frac1{2\ell}}_{\ell\text{ entries}}\right).
\]
In particular, the sharp local probability is uniform exactly in even dimensions. Despite sharpness, *no two distinct finite score vectors attain the global Lipschitz ratio*.

## Assumptions and scope

The parameters are strictly positive and the domain of scores is all of \(\mathbb R^n\). Both norms are unweighted. The result concerns the mixed regularizer, not convex combinations of the outputs of independent softmax and quadratic projection maps. No assertion of globally attained finite-score secant equality is made; in fact it is excluded by the theorem. The case \(n=1\) is the constant probability map and is excluded from the formula. The singular endpoints \(\tau=0\) and \(\kappa=0\) are not part of the stated theorem.

## Proof

**1. Full support, differentiability, and the derivative.** Strict convexity of the objective's negative gives a unique optimizer. Since the derivative of \(\tau p\log p\) tends to \(-\infty\) as \(p\downarrow0\), every optimum is strictly positive. The Lagrange conditions are, for a scalar \(c\),
\[
z_i=\tau(1+\log p_i)+\kappa p_i+c.
\]
Conversely, every strictly positive probability \(p\) is attained by some score \(z\), for example by taking \(c=0\) in this formula. Differentiation along \(\sum_i dp_i=0\) gives
\[
D T_{\tau,\kappa}(z)=J(p)
=\operatorname{diag}(d_i)-\frac{dd^{\mathsf T}}{D},
\qquad d_i=\frac{p_i}{\tau+\kappa p_i},\quad D=\sum_i d_i.
\]
This symmetric matrix is positive semidefinite, since
\(v^{\mathsf T}Jv=\sum_i d_i(v_i-\bar v_d)^2\), where
\(\bar v_d=\sum_i d_i v_i/D\). The implicit function theorem shows that the map is continuously differentiable.

**2. Reduction of the cross-norm.** For a symmetric positive-semidefinite matrix \(J\),
\[
\|J\|_{\infty\to1}
=\max_{s\in\{-1,1\}^n}s^{\mathsf T}Js.
\]
Indeed the induced norm is \(\max_{s,t\in\{-1,1\}^n}t^{\mathsf T}Js\), and Cauchy–Schwarz for the seminorm generated by \(J\) bounds any cross term by the larger of the two corresponding diagonal terms. For a nonempty proper cut \(I=\{i:s_i=1\}\), let \(a=\sum_{i\in I}d_i\), \(b=\sum_{i\notin I}d_i\). Then
\[
s^{\mathsf T}Js=a+b-\frac{(a-b)^2}{a+b}=\frac{4ab}{a+b}
=\frac4{a^{-1}+b^{-1}}.
\]
Constant signs give zero and cannot maximize.

**3. Optimization for a fixed cut.** Put \(k=|I|\), \(h=n-k\), and \(x=\sum_{i\in I}p_i\). The function \(u\mapsto u/(\tau+\kappa u)\) is strictly concave on \((0,\infty)\). Jensen's inequality therefore yields
\[
a\le\frac{x}{\tau+\kappa x/k},\qquad
b\le\frac{1-x}{\tau+\kappa(1-x)/h}.
\]
Equality in both is possible exactly when the probabilities are constant within each side of the cut. Since \(4ab/(a+b)\) is strictly increasing in \(a,b>0\), the maximum for this cut is bounded by
\[
\frac4{\tau/x+\tau/(1-x)+\kappa/k+\kappa/h}.
\]
The elementary inequality \(x^{-1}+(1-x)^{-1}\ge4\), with equality only at \(x=1/2\), shows that the exact fixed-cut maximum is
\[
\left(\tau+\frac{\kappa n}{4k(n-k)}\right)^{-1}.
\]
The balanced cut sizes \(k=m\) and \(k=\ell\) uniquely maximize \(k(n-k)\). The equality conditions are exactly the displayed two-block probabilities (up to permutation). Thus the supremum of all Jacobian cross-norms is the claimed value, and it is achieved at finite scores via step 1. Integrating the derivative over each finite line segment proves the global Lipschitz upper bound; infinitesimal sign directions at the local maximizers prove sharpness.

**4. Exact strong convexity.** The regularizer's quadratic second variation at interior \(p\) along a nonzero tangent direction \(u\) with \(\sum_i u_i=0\) is
\[
\sum_i\left(\frac\tau{p_i}+\kappa\right)u_i^2.
\]
Cauchy–Schwarz gives \(\sum_i u_i^2/p_i\ge\|u\|_1^2\). If the positive and negative supports of \(u\) have sizes \(k,h\), their sums have equal absolute value \(\|u\|_1/2\), hence
\[
\sum_i u_i^2\ge\frac{\|u\|_1^2}{4}\left(\frac1k+\frac1h\right)
\ge\frac n{4m\ell}\|u\|_1^2.
\]
Equality in both inequalities holds for the balanced two-block \(p\) above and tangent \(u_i=p_i\) on one block, \(u_i=-p_i\) on the other. Integrating second variations proves strong convexity on the closed simplex, with its exact modulus determined by these arbitrarily short interior segments.

**5. No finite-input attainment.** The equality characterization in step 3 says the set of probabilities satisfying \(\|J(p)\|_{\infty\to1}=\operatorname{Lip}_{\infty\to1}(T)\) is finite (the permutations of one specified vector). If two distinct scores achieved equality in the global secant bound, the fundamental theorem of calculus and triangle inequality would force \(\|J(T(z_t))\|_{\infty\to1}\) to equal the global coefficient for almost every \(t\in[0,1]\) on the segment \(z_t\). Continuity then places the entire probability path in that finite set, hence makes it constant. Its output difference would be zero, contradicting equality for distinct inputs because the sharp coefficient is positive. Therefore the sharp coefficient is locally attained but no nontrivial finite secant realizes it.

## Verification

The exact-rational standalone checker `checker.py` recomputes the Jacobian and enumerates every sign vector for several dimensions and rational parameter pairs. It checks the balanced extremizer and uniformly sampled rational interior probabilities, including the strict odd-dimensional gap at the uniform probability. The analytic inequalities above, not the finite checker, establish the all-dimension theorem.

For example, with \(n=3\), \(\tau=\kappa=1\), the sharp coefficient is \(8/11\) at permutations of \((1/2,1/4,1/4)\), whereas at the uniform probability the local cross-norm is only \(2/3\).

## Relationship to prior work

The regularized-prediction and Fenchel–Young viewpoint is due to Blondel, Martins, and Niculae, including its smoothness consequences for strongly convex regularizers. Martins and collaborators further develop generalized Shannon and Tsallis prediction maps. Those frameworks motivate the mixed penalty but do not, in the inspected relevant sections and appendices, give the specific sharp mixed cross-norm constant, its parity-rigid maximizers, or finite-secant nonattainment. Existing exact results for the pure quadratic and pure Tsallis cases do not themselves state the mixed-penalty theorem. The claim should not be read as establishing novelty for the general convex-duality construction, which is prior work.

## Limitations

This is a finite-dimensional deterministic stability theorem for positive \(\tau\) and \(\kappa\), not a statistical generalization guarantee or an assessment of model calibration. The result does not address weighted quadratic regularization, general entropy mixtures, infinite-dimensional simplices, or any practical optimization algorithm's convergence. Priority against inaccessible or differently phrased older literature is not exhaustively certified, and independent validation has not been performed.
