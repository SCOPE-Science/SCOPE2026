In a smooth time-varying Levins metapopulation with increasingly long favorable and hostile seasons, the two Bohl growth extremes stay fixed at \(-1\) and \(+1\), yet the long-run outcome depends sharply on the asymptotic difference \(\delta\) between the season lengths. Positive \(\delta\) gives weak persistence with an exact occupancy peak \(\frac12\tanh(\delta/2)\); nonpositive \(\delta\) gives extinction, with a \(1/(4n)\) critical season-boundary law in the balanced linear-length example.
