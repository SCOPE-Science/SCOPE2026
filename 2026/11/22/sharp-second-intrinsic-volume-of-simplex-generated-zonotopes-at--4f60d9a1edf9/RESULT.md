# Sharp second intrinsic volume of simplex-generated zonotopes at fixed mean width

## Finding

**Theorem.** Let \(d\ge3\) be an integer. For vectors \(p_1,\ldots,p_{d+1}\in\mathbb R^d\), suppose the Minkowski sum \(Z=\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}[0,p_i]\) is full dimensional. Write \(L=\sum_i\lVert p_i\rVert\), and use the intrinsic-volume normalization \(\operatorname{Vol}_d(Z+tB_d)=\sum_{j=0}^d\kappa_{d-j}V_j(Z)t^{d-j}\). Then

\[
\boxed{V_2(Z)\le\frac{L^2}{2}\sqrt{\frac{d-1}{d+1}}.}
\]

Equality holds if and only if the nonzero vectors have a common length and, after choosing their signs and a common orthogonal transformation, point along the vertices of a centered regular simplex. In geometric terms, the unique maximizing zonotope, up to congruence and scale, is the regular generalized rhombic dodecahedron. The theorem allows **all** positive or unequal generator lengths, not merely equilateral presentations.

There is also an exact quantitative deficit decomposition. For \(a_i=\lVert p_i\rVert\), choose unit \(u_i=p_i/a_i\) when \(a_i>0\), and arbitrary unit \(u_i\) for a zero vector; set

\[
T=\sum_i a_i u_i u_i^{\mathsf T},\quad
Q=\sum_i\bigl(a_i-L/(d+1)\bigr)^2,\quad
E=\bigl\lVert T-(L/d)I_d\bigr\rVert_F^2.
\]

For \(i<j\) put \(w_{ij}=a_i a_j\), \(s_{ij}=\sqrt{1-\langle u_i,u_j\rangle^2}\), \(S=V_2(Z)\), and \(A=\sum_{i<j}w_{ij}\). Set \(H=\sum_{i<j}w_{ij}(s_{ij}-S/A)^2\) if \(A>0\), and \(H=0\) otherwise. With

\[
A_*=\frac{L^2d}{2(d+1)},\qquad B_*=\frac{L^2(d-1)}{2d},
\]

one has the **exact identity**

\[
\boxed{A_*B_*-S^2=\frac{A_*E+B_*Q}{2}-\frac{QE}{4}+AH.}
\]

Consequently the squared-value deficit is at least each of \(A_*E/2\) and \(B_*Q/2\), simultaneously. Thus nearly maximizing intrinsic volume forces both nearly equal generating lengths and a nearly isotropic length-weighted frame operator.

## Assumptions and scope

All geometry uses the standard Euclidean metric. The body is generated by **at most** \(d+1\) segments; zero-length generators are permitted and strict equality cannot occur if any is zero. Its intrinsic volume \(V_2\) is the sum of Euclidean areas of all generating parallelograms, with the standard Steiner normalization above. Let \(\kappa_j\) denote the volume of the unit ball in \(\mathbb R^j\). The mean width satisfies

\[
w(Z)=\frac{2\kappa_{d-1}}{d\kappa_d}L.
\]

Hence fixing mean width is exactly equivalent to fixing \(L\). The statement concerns only the second intrinsic volume, not arbitrary higher intrinsic volumes or fixed-circumradius optimization.

## Proof

The standard zonotope intrinsic-volume formula (Joós–Lángi, Corollary 1; also Brazitikos–McIntyre) gives

\[
S=V_2(Z)=\sum_{i<j}\lVert p_i\wedge p_j\rVert
 =\sum_{i<j}a_i a_j\sqrt{1-\langle u_i,u_j\rangle^2}
 =\sum_{i<j}w_{ij}s_{ij}.
\]

Denote \(B=\sum_{i<j}w_{ij}s_{ij}^2\). The positive semidefinite matrix \(T=\sum_i a_i u_i u_i^{\mathsf T}\) has trace \(L\). Expanding squares gives two **exact**, independent identities:

\[
A=\frac{L^2-\sum_i a_i^2}{2}=A_*-\frac Q2,
\qquad
B=\frac{L^2-\operatorname{tr}(T^2)}{2}=B_*-\frac E2.
\]

The first follows by expanding \(L^2\). For the second, expand \(\operatorname{tr}(T^2)=\sum_{i,j}a_ia_j\langle u_i,u_j\rangle^2\), subtract it from \(L^2=\sum_{i,j}a_ia_j\), and cancel all diagonal terms. The identities \(Q=\sum_i a_i^2-L^2/(d+1)\) and \(E=\operatorname{tr}(T^2)-L^2/d\) use \(\operatorname{tr}T=L\). Both \(Q,E\) are manifestly nonnegative.

Weighted Cauchy–Schwarz yields \(S^2\le AB\), even if some generator vanishes. Since \(A\le A_*\) and \(B\le B_*\),

\[
S^2\le AB\le A_*B_*=\frac{L^4}{4}\frac{d-1}{d+1},
\]

proving the stated sharp inequality. If \(A>0\), expanding the weighted angular variance shows \(AB-S^2=AH\). If \(A=0\) there is at most one nonzero generator, \(S=B=0\), and the same equality holds with \(H=0\). Finally,

\[
A_*B_*-AB=\frac12(A_*E+B_*Q)-\frac14QE.
\]

Combining these exact identities proves the deficit formula. Since alternatively

\[
A_*B_*-AB=\frac{A_*E}{2}+\frac{BQ}{2}
=\frac{B_*Q}{2}+\frac{AE}{2},
\]

and all terms are nonnegative, both claimed rigidity lower bounds follow.

**Equality classification.** If \(S^2=A_*B_*\), the two rigidity bounds give \(Q=E=0\) since \(L>0\), \(d\ge3\). Thus every length is \(a=L/(d+1)>0\) and \(\sum_i u_i u_i^{\mathsf T}=\frac{d+1}{d}I_d\). Let \(U\) be the \(d\times(d+1)\) matrix with columns \(u_i\). Its Gram matrix \(G=U^{\mathsf T}U\) is \(\frac{d+1}{d}(I-vv^{\mathsf T})\), where \(v\) is a unit kernel vector, because \(UU^{\mathsf T}=\frac{d+1}{d}I_d\). Every diagonal entry of \(G\) is one, so \(v_i^2=1/(d+1)\) for all \(i\). Independently changing the signs of columns makes \(v\) proportional to the all-ones vector. The resulting Gram matrix has diagonal one and off-diagonal entries \(-1/d\), precisely that of the centered regular simplex. Sign changes only translate a Minkowski sum of unoriented segments, so the maximizing bodies are congruent. Conversely, these simplex directions have \(\lVert p_i\wedge p_j\rVert=a^2\sqrt{1-1/d^2}\), and summing over all pairs gives equality.

## Verification

The proof is valid for every integer \(d\ge3\) and does not infer an infinite statement from finite sampling. The accompanying standalone `verify.py` uses the standard Python library only. It independently checks one exact rational, nonregular configuration; the exact regular-simplex equality formula through eleven dimensions; and 900 seeded numeric configurations, including degenerate choices, against the inequality and deficit identities. Run with `python verify.py`; the expected output begins `PASS:`. The exact-rational calculations are corroboration, not a replacement for the symbolic argument.

## Relationship to prior work

Joós and Lángi [1] established the precise zonotope intrinsic-volume formula and the fixed-mean-width maximum for **equal-length** generalized rhombic-dodecahedron generators (Theorem 6, Section 3.3). They explicitly posed the unrestricted fixed-mean-width maximizing problem for \(1<k<d\) (Problem 3, Section 5). The displayed theorem resolves that question for \(k=2\) without the equal-length restriction; it does **not** resolve the other indices.

Brazitikos and McIntyre [2] developed vector-valued Maclaurin comparisons, including results for \(d\) generating vectors; their inspected statements do not supply the \(d+1\)-vector, fixed-length sharp bound and equality classification. Ivanov [3] studied volume optimization for orthogonal projections of cubes under a *tight-frame constraint*, rather than the present unconstrained fixed-sum-of-lengths problem. Regular-simplex equiangular frames are standard [4]; the new inequality and its exact three-defect identity, not this frame terminology, constitute the asserted result.

## Limitations

No priority guarantee is asserted: an equivalent inequality may occur under a different formulation in earlier convex-geometry or frame-theory literature. The bound does not classify optimizers for other intrinsic-volume indices, for arbitrarily many generators without appropriate equiangular frames, or under different normalizations. The reproducibility script tests only finite configurations. Independent validation has not been performed.
