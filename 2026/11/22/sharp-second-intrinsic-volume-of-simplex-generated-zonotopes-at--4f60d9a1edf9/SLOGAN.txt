Among all \(d\)-dimensional zonotopes generated by \(d+1\) segments at fixed mean width, the regular generalized rhombic dodecahedron uniquely maximizes \(V_2\): \(V_2\le\frac{L^2}{2}\sqrt{\frac{d-1}{d+1}}\). An exact nonnegative-defect decomposition controls both segment-length imbalance and frame anisotropy, settling the second-intrinsic-volume case of a previously posed unrestricted optimization problem.
