# Sharp seven-point failure of graded singleton-type reconstruction in the generic poset

## Finding
Let \(\mathbb P\) be the countable universal homogeneous partial order, and let \(P\) be any finite poset whose elements are individually named in \(\mathbb P\). The complete nonalgebraic singleton types over \(P\) form a finite graded lattice \(\mathcal T(P)\) under the natural *possible-below* order of extension points. For a type \(\tau\), write \(L_\tau\) for the named elements strictly below its realization and \(U_\tau\) for those strictly above it. Put \(n=|P|\) and define the graded type polynomial
\[
 R_P(z)=\sum_{\tau\in\mathcal T(P)}z^{\rho(\tau)},
 \qquad \rho(\tau)=|L_\tau|+n-|U_\tau|.
\]
For every two nonisomorphic finite posets \(P,Q\) of the same order at most six, \(R_P(z)\ne R_Q(z)\). The bound is sharp: **at order seven**, there are exactly **six unordered pairs** of nonisomorphic posets with equal polynomials, and no larger collision fiber. Of the \(2045\) isomorphism classes of seven-element posets, precisely \(12\) belong to these six size-two fibers; there are \(2039\) distinct polynomials. All six pairs, with Hasse-cover certificates and complete coefficient vectors, are included in `collision_pairs.json`. In contrast, the stronger **bivariate** profile by the two separate lower/upper cardinalities is injective on all \(2045\) seven-element classes, as independently reconfirmed here; our new result locates exactly when merging those two coordinates into the natural lattice rank loses reconstruction power.

For instance, the first certified pair has Hasse-cover sets, on vertices \(0,\ldots,6\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 E_A&=\{(0,2),(0,3),(1,3),(1,6),(2,4),(2,5),(4,6)\},\\
 E_B&=\{(0,2),(0,3),(1,3),(2,4),(2,5),(2,6),(3,6)\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The pairs are directed upward, so each defines the transitive partial order generated by the listed covers. They are nonisomorphic, but both give the same coefficient vector in degrees \(0,\ldots,14\):
\[
 (1,2,2,5,6,7,9,9,6,4,5,4,4,3,1).
\]
Thus each has exactly \(68\) nonalgebraic singleton types. The first-sharp-failure classification, not the established description of types by lower and upper sets or the lattice operations on types, is the claimed contribution.

## Assumptions and scope
All posets are finite, with a reflexive antisymmetric transitive order; their strict comparabilities are denoted \(<\). Isomorphism preserves direction, and dual posets are not identified unless actually isomorphic. A one-point type is *nonalgebraic*, so no equality to a named parameter is allowed. Every finite extension occurs in the generic poset by its Fraïssé extension property; homogeneity identifies complete types with extension patterns. The grading uses the possible-below lattice order, which may be defined equivalently by simultaneous inclusion of lower sets and reverse inclusion of upper sets. The theorem concerns arbitrary posets of **at most seven** named elements. It makes no claim that these are the only collisions at greater orders, or that the entire type lattice is unreconstructive.

## Proof
**1. Admissible cut pairs and rank.** An extension point \(x\) determines \(L=\{p\in P:p<x\}\) and \(U=\{p\in P:x<p\}\). These are respectively an order ideal and a filter, and \(\ell<u\) holds for every \(\ell\in L\), \(u\in U\). Conversely those three requirements make a valid one-point partial-order extension; each is realized, and the realization has a unique nonalgebraic type. The partial order of possible-below extension points on these types is
\[
(L,U)\preceq(L',U')\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad L\subseteq L'\text{ and }U\supseteq U'.
\]
The coordinatewise operations make this a distributive lattice. This fact and the admissible-pair criterion are already part of the literature and are **not** claimed as original.

**2. Independent order-ideal representation.** Define a doubled \(2n\)-element poset \(D(P)\) with elements \(p^0,p^1\) for each \(p\in P\), with within-copy orders \(p^i\le q^i\) whenever \(p\le q\), and cross-copy strict comparisons \(q^0<p^1\) precisely when it is *not* the case that \(p<q\) in \(P\); no edges point from copy \(1\) to copy \(0\). This is transitive: for \(a\le b\), the implications \(p\not<b\Rightarrow p\not<a\) and \(a\not<q\Rightarrow b\not<q\) ensure closure of every cross comparison. In particular no extra relation arises under transitive closure.

There is an explicit rank-preserving order isomorphism
\[
 \Phi:\mathcal T(P)\longrightarrow J(D(P)),\quad
 \Phi(L,U)=\{p^1:p\in L\}\cup\{q^0:q\notin U\}.
\]
The within-copy conditions assert that \(L\) and \(P\setminus U\) are down-sets. A cross relation \(q^0<p^1\) forces \(q^0\) into \(\Phi\) whenever \(p\in L\) and \(p\not<q\), exactly forbidding the simultaneous occurrence of \(p\in L\) and \(q\in U\) unless \(p<q\). This proves both directions of the bijection. A rank-one step adds one element of the doubled ideal; thus \(\rho(L,U)=|L|+n-|U|\) and the polynomial equals the ordinary size enumerator of ideals of \(D(P)\). The representation is used as a second, independently implemented counting route; novelty is not attributed to generic distributive-lattice representation theory.

**3. Exhaustive finite census with a proved coverage invariant.** Every nonempty finite poset has a maximal element. Removing one gives a smaller poset, and the predecessors of that removed element form an order ideal. Conversely, adjoining a new maximal element above *exactly* an ideal produces a finite poset. Starting with the empty order, the verifier generates all isomorphism classes through seven by this recursion. For deduplication, it computes an isomorphism-complete canonical signature: partition vertices by their pair of strict in- and out-degrees, list all permutations within each degree class, and take the least directed adjacency encoding across these permutations. Equal signatures are equivalent to an order isomorphism, because every isomorphism preserves those degree pairs and a shared labeled adjacency matrix is an isomorphism witness. No approximate hashing or arbitrary cutoff enters this deduplication.

The verifier enumerates every admissible \((L,U)\) by order-ideal and filter masks, calculates \(R_P\) directly, and independently enumerates the down-sets of \(D(P)\) by layerwise addition of available minimal outside vertices. Both calculations agree, coefficient by coefficient, for **every** generated poset. A separate bivariate counter independently checks that every one of the \(2045\) seven-element classes still has a distinct full two-coordinate fingerprint. The exact isomorphism-class counts at orders \(0,1,2,3,4,5,6,7\) are \(1,1,2,5,16,63,318,2045\). Grouping by the complete rank coefficient vector yields no collisions through six vertices and exactly six pairwise fibers at seven vertices. The full cover lists and their coefficients in `collision_pairs.json` are asserted in the verifier, so the six-pair classification is exhaustively certified within the stated finite scope.

## Verification
Run `python3 checker.py` with Python 3.10 or later in the directory containing `collision_pairs.json`. It uses only the Python standard library. Two separate algorithms calculate each polynomial: direct allowed lower-ideal/upper-filter pairs and ideals of the doubled poset. The canonicalization step checks **all** degree-compatible relabelings, not a probabilistic graph hash. The complete run takes a finite enumeration through the \(2045\) seven-element isomorphism classes, checks the \(2045\) distinct bivariate profiles, and checks every rank-collision certificate. Recorded output in `verification_output.txt` ends with `VERIFY_OK`. Enumeration here is an exact finite proof of a claim whose domain ends at seven, not evidence extrapolated to all larger orders. The structural correspondence has the separate all-order proof above.

## Relationship to prior work
Pach, Pinsker, Pluhár, Pongrácz and Szabó establish the classical homogeneous random-poset setting [1]. Dolinka and Mašulović's finite-poset extension description [2] supplies the established lower/upper cut correspondence. A general published finite-parameter one-type counting result [3] states the same correspondence and proves \(\#\mathrm P\)-completeness; its exact total-count identity does not distinguish the *graded* rank profiles or determine the first collision. Kwiatkowska, Sullivan and Winkel [4], §2, especially Definition 2.12 and Lemma 2.13, explicitly establish the possible-below type order and its meet/join operations; hence lattice structure itself is **prior**, not new. Their full paper addresses embedding and automorphism extension, and does not tabulate these finite rank polynomials or prove their seven-point reconstruction boundary.

A separate complete bivariate finite census identifies its first failures at eight elements; this stronger invariant does **not** determine the equality fibers of the one-variable rank specialization \(R_P(z)=z^n\Phi_P(z,z^{-1})\). The new six-pair calculation identifies the precise earlier loss of information under that specialization. The closest concrete finite-collision work [5] concerns predecessor/successor degree multisets of width-two *parameter posets* and has a different statistic and domain. Another [6] concerns graded scores over locally transitive *tournaments*. A published result for rectangular chain-product bases [7] counts a restricted family of types, not the rank-spectrum reconstruction question over **all** posets of a given small order. The literature on poset doppelgängers [8] concerns order-polynomial equality for arbitrary posets; the present rank enumerator is the ideal-size polynomial of the **specific doubled poset** \(D(P)\), not that classical order polynomial, and the claimed sharp classification is not an immediate implication of the cited general doppelgänger theory.

## Limitations
The bound seven is essential to the exhaustive classification; nothing about collision fibers for larger posets is established. Equality of graded polynomials does **not** imply equality of complete type lattices. The doubled-poset representation and established type-lattice operations serve as counting infrastructure and are not offered as independent novelty claims. Search and source access cannot rule out equivalent seven-element tables in obscure or unindexed order-polynomial or finite-insertion literature; historical priority needs independent review. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
