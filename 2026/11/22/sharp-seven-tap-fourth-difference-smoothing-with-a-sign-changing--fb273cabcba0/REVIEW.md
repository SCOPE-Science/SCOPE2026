# Mathematical assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The Fourier multiplier reduction is exact with normalization \(\sum_j u(j)=1\); its factor is \(16\). A three-dimensional Haar-space alternation argument proves existence, four-contact structure, and uniqueness for the degree-five weighted minimax problem. The prescribed double zero at the origin and degree-five upper bound force exactly three positive interior critical points and one endpoint contact. A signed four-node Lagrange identity independently lower-bounds the optimal norm. A rational test polynomial and exact critical-root isolation give a matching enclosure, and an exact dual-stability interpolation bounds the unknown exact minimizer's coefficients closely enough to prove positive time-domain taps and a negative Fourier-symbol witness. The embedded checker replays these inequalities using rational arithmetic; finite sampling is not used as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS relative to checked sources
Gaitán–Garzón–Madrid, arXiv:2604.25074 (April 28, 2026), Theorem 1.4 treats fourth differences only when the multiplier is everywhere nonnegative. Its Table 2 calls the unrestricted fourth-difference optimum open. Richardson, arXiv:2310.09713, proves the unrestricted second-difference case. Related published database claims deal with radius-one all-order formulas and fixed-radius high-order asymptotics; neither yields an explicit fixed radius-three fourth-difference optimizer or its positive-tap/sign-changing-symbol phenomenon. Recovered earlier mathematical records include an unrestricted radius-two result but do not assert the seven-tap result. A classical or unindexed fixed-degree Chebyshev calculation could still subsume the theorem; database nonhits are not proof of novelty.

## Value — PASS
The seven-tap case solves a natural finite instance of a specifically identified open sharp Fourier inequality. It gives the unique real minimizer, an exact algebraic norm, all-positive averaging taps, and a rigorous \(24.8944\ldots\%\) gain over the Fourier-positive optimum. The unexpected compatibility of positive time-domain weights with a necessarily negative frequency multiplier distinguishes it from a routine numerical table entry. The finding does not claim all-radius formulas, optimality for different difference orders, or stability under parameter perturbations.
