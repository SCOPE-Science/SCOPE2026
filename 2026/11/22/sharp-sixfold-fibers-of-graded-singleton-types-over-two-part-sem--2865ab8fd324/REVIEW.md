# Scientific review

## Correctness
The argument explicitly classifies all one-point extensions as either a new class or one of the two existing classes and invokes the defining rectangle-parity condition. Its polynomial identity is exact for every pair of positive class sizes. Each residual exponent pairing gives an admissible matrix by the binary rank-one representation. A normalized row/column weight argument bounds each pairing by two isomorphism types, and a side-swap check treats equal class sizes. Four distinct exponents are necessary for three distinct profile sums, which proves that the sixfold example is first possible at six points. The finite checker independently verifies rectangle parity, actual extension diagrams, canonical isomorphism orbits and inverse matchings for all two-part bases through seven points. Finite checks corroborate, rather than establish, the unbounded claims.

## Originality
Jasiński, Laflamme, Nguyen Van Thé and Woodrow's 2013 full-text Section 10 establishes the semigeneric class; the full public *Cycle-parity symmetry character for two-part semigeneric digraphs* derives its fixed-point character and unlabeled census and explicitly credits the known binary factorization. Neither addresses the out-neighbor-graded one-type polynomial's complete inverse matching fibers. The published 2025 open-access EPPA paper is about extension of partial automorphisms, not these finite-base graded invariants. The ungraded tuple-orbit formula in *Exact Stirling-layer orbit profile* also does not imply the score-specific inverse thresholds. Searches using aliases (score, one-point extensions, rank-one parity matrices, matching reconstruction) found no statement implying the claimed classification. Specialized unindexed or unpublished enumeration remains a residual historical-priority risk.

## Mathematical value
The scalar number of types over a two-part base is too coarse to recover orientations. Grading by out-neighbors is a natural logical statistic, and this theorem completely determines its information loss via a finite inverse algorithm, sharp uniform six-element fiber bound, and exact earliest witnesses. The first point at which the polynomial loses the class-size profile is also classified. This is a structural, all-size reconstruction statement rather than an arbitrary small-order census.

## Scope and limitations
The proof applies to exactly two nonempty nonadjacency classes. It neither extends to three-part bases nor claims that independent mathematical validation or definitive historical priority has occurred.
