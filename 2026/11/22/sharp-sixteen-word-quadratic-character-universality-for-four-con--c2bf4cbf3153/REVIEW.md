# Review of the sixteen-word Gaussian-norm threshold

**Correctness — PASS.** The theorem quantifies over all odd primes, every residue modulo the prime, and every four-sign word, with zeros excluded. For \(p>13\), the eight roots of the four quadratic norm polynomials are distinct over the algebraic closure because their pairwise resultant primes are confined to \(5,13\) (and the value \(8\)); hence nonempty products are squarefree. The classical Weil bound gives \(16N_\varepsilon\ge p-49\sqrt p-64>0\) for \(p\ge2529\). The other 368 odd primes are exhausted by two independently coded residue tests with identical histograms; every relevant small-prime boundary was included. Risk: the small-prime portion is computer-assisted, not a closed-form symbolic classification.

**Originality — PASS on inspected evidence.** Liu and Meng (2023) count a three-term rational transformation of a single residue class. Wang and Fang (2023) treat consecutive *linear* quadratic-residue patterns. A four-norm quadratic character word is neither those rational triples nor a four-term linear pattern. Relevant published records on six consecutive linear quadratic characters and four consecutive quartic cosets concern different arguments/character orders; neither their reported statements nor the two inspected full texts implies a sharp Gaussian-norm cutoff \(67\). Prior mathematical statements were compared by implication, not merely by title. Risk: generic Weil estimates are known, and a separately indexed or unindexed finite-exception census could cover the same conclusion; no literature search proves uniqueness.

**Value — PASS.** The sign word of four consecutive Gaussian norms is a canonical finite-field pattern invariant, not an arbitrary parameter slice: it probes four-point correlation of quadratic character along the norm polynomial. The classification determines the *entire exceptional prime spectrum*, including a nonmonotone transition (\(59\) complete, \(61\) deficient), and a smallest universal cutoff. A generic asymptotic existence bound alone does not supply these exact finite exceptions. Limitation: only length four is settled, and the small exceptions are computationally certified.
