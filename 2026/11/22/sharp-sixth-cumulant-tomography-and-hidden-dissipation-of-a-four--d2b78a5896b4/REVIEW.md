## Originality

**PASS, qualified.** Smith, Le Doussal, Majumdar and Schehr (arXiv:2207.10445) establish the full forward Beta-product and Gaussian-convolved stationary law, anisotropy, and an entropy-production expression; these are fully credited. Frydel (arXiv:2309.12537) establishes relevant moments and Gaussian thermal-convolution machinery. The new claim is not the forward density, Beta moments or thermodynamic flux alone: it is the simultaneous *unknown-orientation*, *unknown-thermal-intensity* and *unknown-speed* inverse, with exact cumulant feasibility, a global one-to-one formula, sixth-order minimality, and position-only reconstruction of dissipation. A published three-state finite-rest model has a sixth-moment identifiability result under a different generator and calibration, while a published switched-diffusivity model requires eighth-order moments. A published biased-four-state theorem detects chirality through mixed fourth moments but does not solve the symmetric equal-turn thermal inverse. Their precise objects and parameter inferences do not imply this model-specific feasible region. Broader algebraic coverage of the inversion by unindexed statistical literature remains the main publication-priority risk; the exact forward law makes the calculation accessible but does not itself state the strict feasibility and minimality theorem.

## Value

**PASS.** The mathematical gap is the exact structural identifiability of an experimentally meaningful hidden turning rate, propulsion amplitude, thermal broadening and entropy production from *stationary position snapshots alone*, without observing orientations or resetting histories. Fourth cumulants locate otherwise covariance-invisible propulsion axes; the sixth cumulant separates a proven continuum of covariance/kurtosis twins. A sharp and constructive admissible-data corridor distinguishes realizable observations from inconsistent ideal model moments. These are natural observables and parameter questions for a tractable active-matter model, rather than arbitrary coefficient evaluations. This does not purport to quantify finite-sample uncertainty, estimation robustness or entropy under alternative time-reversal conventions.
