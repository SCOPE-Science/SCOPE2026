# Sharp skew-norm rigidity of equality in Widlund–Rapoport near-optimality

## Finding
For every integer \(k\ge1\), exact equality in the Widlund–Rapoport near-optimality inequality on a shifted skew-Hermitian linear system \(A=I+\mathrm iJ\) forces the first \(k\) Lanczos couplings to grow at least linearly. In particular, the sharp minimum possible skew-part spectral norm is \(\rho_k\), the largest eigenvalue of the \((k+1)\)-node Jacobi path with consecutive couplings \(1,2,\ldots,k\). In minimal dimension, the minimizing Lanczos matrix is unique.

## Assumptions and scope
Let \(J=J^*\) be any finite-dimensional Hermitian matrix, with a unit initial residual \(v\), and let \(A=I+\mathrm iJ\). Let \(r_j^{\mathrm W}\) and \(r_j^{\mathrm R}\) be the residuals of the exact Galerkin/Widlund and minimal-residual/Rapoport methods from the same initial iterate, using the Krylov spaces generated by \(J\). Suppose that the first \(k\) Widlund iterates exist and that Lanczos has not broken down through coupling \(\\beta_k\). In an orthonormal Lanczos basis, the principal block of \(J\) is the real symmetric Jacobi matrix with real diagonal \(a_1,\ldots,a_k\), and positive off-diagonals \(\\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_k\). Let \(B_k\) be the \((k+1)\)-square real symmetric zero-diagonal matrix with \((B_k)_{j,j+1}=(B_k)_{j+1,j}=j\), \(1\le j\le k\), and define \(\rho_k=\|B_k\|_2\). The claim concerns exact equality in the published comparison, not near-equality or general convergence rates.

## Proof
The orthogonal-residual/minimum-residual inverse-square identity gives
\[
\|r_k^{\mathrm R}\|^{-2}=\sum_{j=0}^k\|r_j^{\mathrm W}\|^{-2}.
\]
Equality in \(\min_{0\le j\le k}\|r_j^{\mathrm W}\|\le\sqrt{k+1}\|r_k^{\mathrm R}\|\) therefore holds exactly when every \(\|r_j^{\mathrm W}\|\) equals the initial norm, here one. Let \(T_j=I_j+\mathrm iJ_j\), where \(J_j\) is the leading Jacobi block, and set \(D_0=1\), \(D_j=\det T_j\). Tridiagonal cofactor expansion shows
\[
\|r_j^{\mathrm W}\|=\frac{\prod_{\ell=1}^{j}\beta_\ell}{|D_j|},\quad D_1=1+\mathrm i a_1,\quad D_j=(1+\mathrm i a_j)D_{j-1}+\beta_{j-1}^2D_{j-2}.
\]
All \(D_j\) are nonzero: the real part of \(T_j\) is \(I_j\). Under equality of residuals, the determinant quotients \(z_j=D_j/D_{j-1}\) satisfy \(|z_j|=\beta_j\) for \(1\le j\le k\). The continuant recurrence becomes
\[
z_1=1+\mathrm i a_1,\qquad z_j=1+\mathrm i a_j+\frac{\beta_{j-1}^2}{z_{j-1}}.
\]
As \(|z_{j-1}|^2=\beta_{j-1}^2\), its real part obeys \(\operatorname{Re}z_j=1+\operatorname{Re}z_{j-1}=j\). Hence \(\\beta_j=|z_j|\ge j\). In particular, equality \(\\beta_j=j\) for every \(j\) forces all these quotients to be real and, successively, \(a_1=\cdots=a_k=0\).

For arbitrary real diagonal entries in the leading \((k+1)\)-node Hermitian Jacobi block, let \(w>0\) be the normalized Perron eigenvector of its zero-diagonal comparison path. Rayleigh quotients at \(w\) and the alternating-sign vector \(((-1)^j w_j)_j\) have a common diagonal contribution but opposite coupling contributions. Their maximum absolute value is at least \(2\sum_j\beta_jw_jw_{j+1}\ge\rho_k\), by \(\\beta_j\ge j\). Thus the spectral norm of the full Hermitian \(J\) is at least \(\rho_k\) (principal-block compression cannot increase the norm).

For \(J=B_k\), the determinant recurrence is \(D_j=j!\), the residuals are all one through step \(k\), and the equality case of the near-optimal comparison holds, establishing sharpness. If \(J\) has exactly \(k+1\) dimensions and its norm equals \(\rho_k\), strict positivity of \(w\) and comparison of couplings imply \(\\beta_j=j\) for every \(j\), hence \(a_1=\cdots=a_k=0\). For the last diagonal \(a_{k+1}\), the Rayleigh quotients of \(B_k\)'s positive and alternating Perron vectors are \(\rho_k+a_{k+1}w_{k+1}^2\) and \(-\rho_k+a_{k+1}w_{k+1}^2\); the norm cannot equal \(\rho_k\) unless \(a_{k+1}=0\). This proves rigidity up to Lanczos-basis equivalence.

## Verification
The exact determinant recurrence, Widlund residual cofactor formula, Perron comparison, and equality conditions above are an infinite-family proof. The accompanying standalone Python/SymPy checker verifies the determinant and residual identities for the canonical matrices through \(k=5\), checks the first four explicit norm polynomials, and verifies the positive Rayleigh comparison for representative perturbed Jacobi matrices. The finite checks are supplemental, not the basis of the all-\(k\) theorem.

## Relationship to prior work
Higgins, Reed, and Szyld (2026), Theorems 1–3, give the near-optimal comparison, the inverse-square residual identity and the canonical Jacobi construction \(\\beta_j=j\) that attains equality. These are existing results. The new claim is a **necessity and optimal-norm rigidity theorem**: any equality case must have \(\\beta_j\ge j\), the example has the smallest possible skew-part norm, and in minimal dimension it is the unique norm minimizer. The 2020 OR/MR theory and earlier Galerkin/minimum-residual relations underlie the residual identity; they are not claimed as new.

## Limitations
The result addresses **exact** saturation and exact arithmetic. It does not give a quantitative stability estimate for near-saturation, an improved comparison factor under a strict norm cap, or bounds for a general nonsymmetric system without positive-definite Hermitian part. Priority beyond the sources inspected cannot be guaranteed without independent review.
