# Scientific review: Sharp Sobolev threshold and near-Lipschitz exceptional coboundaries for constant-type rotations

## Correctness
PASS within the explicitly stated model. For bounded-type irrational rotations, dyadic separation of the rotation frequencies bounds the inverse squared small-divisor sum on a block by the square of its scale; Fourier Cauchy–Schwarz then produces an absolutely summable transfer for every real mean-zero Sobolev observable of order strictly above one. At the endpoint, even convergent denominators have bounded growth ratios, positive errors comparable to inverse denominators, and permit an exact lacunary Fourier construction. The transfer is in square-integrable class but unbounded by positivity of its Fejér means. Ergodicity excludes all alternative continuous real transfers. Positive trigonometric increments and a controlled high-frequency tail give matching modulus upper and lower bounds. Blockwise coefficients yield every divergent gauge. The finite checker passed 186 tests, but the proof, rather than numerical testing, carries infinite quantifiers.

## Originality
PASS for the composite quantitative claim within examined evidence, with a priority qualification. Akhmedli (2026, Lemma 3.3) constructs measurable noncontinuous real coboundaries over every irrational rotation and thus entirely covers the qualitative phenomenon. This is acknowledged, not recast as new. Neither its inspected statement nor the earlier BV construction of Volný (1997) supplies the joint bounded-type universal Sobolev threshold, the two-sided modulus classification for arbitrary square-summable but not summable positive Fourier weights, and the arbitrary near-Lipschitz gauge construction. Forni's survey concerns higher-genus translation flows. Volný's 1995 smooth/analytic paper was accessible in abstract and is a remaining historical priority risk; its stated nonintegrable-transfer constructions differ from the present square-integrable endpoint. Standard small-divisor arguments account for the positive half of the theorem, so the claimed advancement rests on the combined sharp endpoint classification and gauge flexibility, not a novel Fourier inequality in isolation. Full historical novelty is not certified.

## Mathematical value
PASS: the theorem gives a universal sharp regularity boundary for a natural linear cohomological equation and quantifies how close to a Lipschitz modulus a pathological observable can be. The matched upper and lower moduli are useful for testing regularity criteria for real cohomological transfers, not merely for exhibiting one more pathological series. It does not extend to all rotations or force any assertion exactly at the Lipschitz endpoint.

## Closest literature and limitations
Akhmedli (arXiv:2611.12722v1, 2026) supplies the qualitative measurable/noncontinuous mechanism. Volný (Studia Mathematica 126, 1997) concerns BV and jump cocycles. Volný (Commentationes Mathematicae Universitatis Carolinae 36, 1995) studies smooth and analytic cocycles; only its abstract was inspected, so a broad historical implication remains possible. Forni (arXiv:0707.0940) addresses a different flow setting and was inspected at the abstract level. Independent priority verification remains necessary.
