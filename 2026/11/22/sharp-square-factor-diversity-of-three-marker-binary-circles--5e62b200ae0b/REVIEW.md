# Scientific review of the three-marker circular-square extremum

**Correctness — PASS.** The argument reconstructs the complete gap-dependent pointwise count, separating unary squares from the root-offset intervals for squares containing two ones. For strictly distinct gaps in the feasible central-gap regime, the objective becomes \(n-2(x+y)+\lfloor c/2\rfloor\). The integer congruence \(S=3a+2x+y\) and the bound \(-2h+\lceil(2h+1)/6\rceil<0\) uniquely select the claimed balanced gap differences. All remaining distinct and repeated-gap patterns have uniform bounds strictly below this candidate for \(n\ge25\). The finite interval \(3\le n\le24\) is exhausted by a complete composition enumeration. The separate argument for at most two ones excludes smaller-marker above-circumference examples. Literal-factor enumeration and arithmetic extrema checks independently corroborate the proof, without being substituted for the unrestricted proof.

**Originality — PASS, with a specific parent-result risk.** The existing three-marker circular-square theorem already provides an exact count for each chosen gap triple and a limit law. Its complete pointwise classification is acknowledged, not rebranded as new. Its available claim and result text do **not** provide the fixed-circumference sharp maximum, the unique sorted optimizing gaps in all three residue classes, the complete two-necklace classification, or the first above-circumference instance. Those require a nontrivial integer optimization with separate repeated-gap and nonbalanced regimes. The four-run circular square extremum covers different symbol-run constraints, while Hamai's universal \(3(n-\sigma)/2\) upper bound and the CPM 2026 bounds provide no fixed-three-one optimizer classification. A formula known pointwise is logically a finite search oracle for its maximum: an unpublished optimization of it is the central residual priority risk, and this result's originality should not be confused with discovery of the pointwise formula.

**Value — PASS.** Determining sharp extrema and every optimizer is a motivated problem in circular-word repetition diversity, particularly in view of the recent universal \(3n/2\) square bound. The result locates the sparse-content extremal density \(7/6\), identifies the first sparse three-marker word exceeding one square per position at \(n=30\), and proves two oriented necklace classes are the only sharp constructions at each \(n\ge25\). The repeated-gap exclusion and residue-sensitive optimizer uniqueness are structural rather than a pointwise recalculation.

The claim concerns only distinct literal squares of total length at most the circle's length. Its pointwise counting lemma is prior work, its finite verification does not replace infinite proof, and priority is not guaranteed against unindexed extrema. The current assessment is internal scientific review, not independent validation.
