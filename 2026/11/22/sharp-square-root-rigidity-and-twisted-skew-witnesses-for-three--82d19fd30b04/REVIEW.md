# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The claim concerns three externally tangent infinite cylinders with positive fixed radius ratios. The proof passes from each cylinder to its contained tangent ball, converts pairwise nonoverlap to a spherical angular constraint, proves the exact three-point perimeter bound and its equality case, and obtains the weighted threshold from Descartes' classical circle identity. For stability it separately bounds all angular slack by the radius deficit, proves square-root contact-normal closeness by a two-coordinate Gram inversion, and bounds axis tilts through the squared point-to-line clearance. The exact skew-line cross-product formula and positive first derivative of each planar clearance give packings with pairwise nonparallel axes and square-root-scale angular spread. The standalone program passed 205 distinct positive weight tests, including triple contacts, derivatives, and skew-line distances; finite tests do not replace the analytic proof. Constants depend on positive weights, and no uniform stability at vanishing weights is asserted.

## Originality — PASS (historical priority provisional)
The exact equal-radius three-cylinder packing optimum is explicitly known in Glazyrin (2026); Descartes' curvature identity is classical. Ogievetsky–Shlosman (2019) already describe quadratic decay of a three-equal-ball perturbation. None is claimed new. The assessed assertion is the simultaneous global free-axis stability estimate for every fixed unequal/equal triple and its optimality by an explicit three-axis skew-cylinder family. The closest indexed three-cylinder theorem fixes nonparallel cone directions, a different optimization domain; its full publicly available theorem, proof and limitations were read. Searches for weighted cylinder rigidity, Descartes aliases, stability and skew-line packings did not locate a stronger implication. Remaining risk: older unindexed contact-geometry stability may contain a disguised formulation.

## Mathematical value — PASS
This is a natural quantitative refinement of the three-cylinder extremal packing, addressing geometric instability under skew axis perturbations rather than only the circle-packing radius. It treats every fixed positive unequal radius ratio, gives the full-space geometric stability exponent, and exhibits nonparallel extremizers of the sharp order. The axis-direction conclusion does not follow merely from the classical Descartes radius formula or the cited three-ball perturbation, and supports geometric control of near-critical cylinder clusters.
