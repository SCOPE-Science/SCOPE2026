# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The 36 directed three-mers and 18 reverse-complement classes are generated from four fixed nucleotides. The undirected quotient overlap graph has four odd-degree and four even-degree vertices, forcing the exact necessary repeat classes and lower bounds for open and closed walks. The provided 21-base and 20-base hosts meet those bounds. A finite-state exact-integer completion recurrence, plus independent memoization-free C++ trail enumeration, both give 57,984 shortest linear words and 289,920 rooted shortest circles, or 14,496 rotational classes. The proof of dividing by 20 checks that all circular optima are primitive. Limitations: the finite census is not a higher-order theorem, and biological performance is unmodeled.

**Originality — PASS, qualified.** The full open-access Orenstein–Shamir 2013 paper was examined for its unrestricted reverse-complement completion theorem, graph construction, tables, and discussion of optimal-sequence counting. It does not state the no-homopolymer three-mer optimum, the parity obstruction or either equality count. Burris–Motta–Shipman's 2016 unoriented de Bruijn paper uses an analogous quotient/parity tool for ordinary reversal, a distinct involution and target family; that general technique is acknowledged, not claimed as novel. Full relevant published small-case research documents on unconstrained reverse-complement dinucleotide cycles, four-mer reverse-complement palindrome covers and single-symbol zero-run-free universal cycles were compared for implication; their objects differ materially. Targeted indexed similarity and web searches returned no same-statement census. This is a qualified originality judgment, not a guarantee against unindexed historical calculations.

**Value — PASS.** The case is directly motivated by shortest double-stranded DNA motif libraries with a strict homopolymer-exclusion design constraint. The key surprise is that the odd-order unrestricted reverse-complement construction can meet its raw orbit count, whereas an all-letter homopolymer prohibition creates an exact one-window linear and two-window circular penalty. Classification of all optimal circular and linear covers quantifies design diversity and gives independent benchmarks, not merely a lone example. The short order-three case is a natural first nontrivial test rather than an arbitrary slice; no claim of in vivo efficacy is made.

**Closest literature and residual risks.** Orenstein–Shamir (2013), https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btt230; Burris–Motta–Shipman (2016), https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.08480. One restricted zero-run universal-cover result allows a distinguished forbidden symbol rather than all four and requires the full oriented target set. The nearest reverse-complement dinucleotide census is order two and unrestricted. An unindexed equivalent three-mer enumeration remains possible. The exact problem is finite and exhaustively verified, but independent mathematical validation remains outstanding.
