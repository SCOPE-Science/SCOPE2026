# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The starting Minkowski presentation is the \(m=0\) specialization of the explicit simplex coefficients in Pilaud–Poliakova, Remark 61. The all-composition vertex list comes from winners in nested suffixes. Its quadratic cosphere identity is preserved by adjacent-block merging with an explicit cancellation. Euclidean projection onto the nonnegative fixed-sum simplex gives the active-set parameter and exact center; a transportation Hall argument independently proves that projected point lies inside the actual polytope. A supporting-face convex-combination identity then certifies global minimality and uniqueness, not merely an upper bound. The contact face, tie convention, finite-sum radius and \(n^{2/3}\) asymptotics follow without numerical assumptions. Reproduced exact-rational checks exhaust every vertex for dimensions 2 through 12. Residual risk: a hidden transcription or normalization error in applying the published Minkowski coefficients, although the displayed coefficients were checked in the source and the low-dimensional vertices agree with the listed formula.

## Originality — PASS, with provisional historical priority

The primary 2023 source supplies vertex and Minkowski data and calls the \(m=0\) polytope a skew cube; its relevant geometric construction and discussion contain no claim of the exact Euclidean minimum circumsphere or the sublinear contact-face law. The closest indexed public inradius claims involve \((1,n)\) or higher \((m,n)\), not \((0,n)\) outer radius. Related minimum-sphere results for partial permutohedra, terminal simplices, capped cubes and two-part composition polytopes are not all-composition nested suffix-simplex sums and do not imply the present contact count. Searches of the exact family, its defining Minkowski formula, water-filling aliases and asymptotic exponent found no covering result. Search failure does not establish first priority. Some potentially related indexed records could not be read beyond summaries; no claim of a comprehensive review of those texts is made.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The original skew-cube realization is a natural, explicitly defined family with exponentially many vertices. The exact smallest ball and its complete exposed contact face are intrinsic Euclidean invariants of this realization, not arbitrary point samples. The key structural fact is that the circumball screens out all but \(2^{\Theta(n^{2/3})}\) vertices, giving a nontrivial sublinear contact dimension and a precise transition from the preliminary all-vertex sphere to the optimal one. The unique center and all-dimensional closed formula make the result useful for comparing geometric realizations of combinatorially identical cubes. It is more than a routine formula substitution or finite computation.
