For every Euclidean simplex in dimension at least three, its largest surface-versus-volume centroid displacement relative to directional width is exactly the total-variation imbalance of the normalized facet areas divided by the dimension. The sharp supremum is \((d-1)/(d(d+1))\), approached only through degeneration, and a sufficiently small deficit forces all but two facet areas to carry exactly the deficit mass while the dominant outward normals become nearly antiparallel.
