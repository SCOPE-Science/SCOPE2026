# Sharp ten-cycle onset and all small edge-metric bases of double generalized Petersen graphs

## Finding

Let \(G_n=DGP(n,1)\) for every integer \(n\ge3\). The previously unclassified initial values satisfy
\[
\beta_e(G_n)=\begin{cases}3,&n=10,\\4,&3\le n\le12,\ n\ne10.\end{cases}
\]
The only size-three edge-metric generators in this range occur at \(n=10\), exactly \(160\) of them. They comprise exactly six orbits of cardinalities \(20,20,20,20,40,40\) under a natural group of graph automorphisms. The number of *all* four-vertex edge-metric generators for every finite initial index is also determined:

| \(n\) | Resolving triples | Resolving four-sets |
|:--:|--:|--:|
| 3 | 0 | 141 |
| 4 | 0 | 224 |
| 5 | 0 | 590 |
| 6 | 0 | 1,644 |
| 7 | 0 | 2,380 |
| 8 | 0 | 7,536 |
| 9 | 0 | 5,202 |
| 10 | 160 | 21,600 |
| 11 | 0 | 11,308 |
| 12 | 0 | 42,768 |

Together with the theorem for \(n\ge13\) of Iqbal et al. [1], this yields the complete all-index law
\[
\boxed{\beta_e(DGP(n,1))=\begin{cases}3,&n\ge10\text{ and }n\equiv2\pmod4,\\4,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}\quad n\ge3.}
\]
Thus the first occurrence of a three-landmark edge generator is exactly at \(n=10\), while the smaller congruent index \(n=6\) requires four. The infinite tail of this formula is credited to [1]; only the finite boundary and exact multiplicities are claimed as new.

## Assumptions and scope

The graph \(G_n\) has disjoint vertex layers \(X=\{x_i:i\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\}\), \(U=\{u_i:i\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\}\), \(V=\{v_i:i\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\}\), and \(Y=\{y_i:i\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\}\), with the edge set
\[
E(G_n)=\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z}\{x_ix_{i+1},x_iu_i,u_iv_{i+1},v_iu_{i+1},v_iy_i,y_iy_{i+1}\}.
\]
This graph has \(4n\) vertices and \(6n\) distinct edges for \(n\ge3\). A vertex-to-edge distance is \(d(s,ab)=\min\{d(s,a),d(s,b)\}\). A set \(S\subseteq V(G_n)\) is *edge resolving* if the signatures \((d(s,e))_{s\in S}\) of all edges \(e\in E(G_n)\) are pairwise distinct. The edge metric dimension \(\beta_e(G_n)\) is the smallest size of such a set. Vertex names and edges are labeled, whereas vertex subsets are counted without ordering.

For executable checks, the vertices use zero-based consecutive integer blocks: \(x_0,\ldots,x_{n-1}\), then \(u_0,\ldots,u_{n-1}\), then \(v_0,\ldots,v_{n-1}\), then \(y_0,\ldots,y_{n-1}\).

## Proof

**Lemma 1 (finite counting certificate).** For each \(n=3,4,\ldots,12\), construct the six types of edges specified above, deduplicate unordered pairs, and confirm the graph has \(4n\) vertices, \(6n\) edges, and degree three. Compute the entire unweighted shortest-path distance matrix \(D\). For each vertex \(s\) and graph edge \(e=ab\), form \(c_s(e)=\min\{D_{s,a},D_{s,b}\}\). A chosen \(k\)-set \(S\) resolves exactly when the map \(e\mapsto(c_s(e))_{s\in S}\) is injective. Exhaustively visit *all* \(\binom{4n}{k}\) unordered choices for \(k\in\{2,3,4\}\), with no sampling and no symmetry pruning. The two supplied fully specified algorithms produce identical tables: zero resolving pairs for all ten values, zero resolving triples except \(160\) at \(n=10\), and the strictly positive four-set counts displayed under Finding. Their finite loops exhaust the stated parameter and subset domains. This directly establishes every claimed initial edge metric dimension, since a set of size one resolving would imply an extending resolving pair.

**Witnesses for the upper bounds.** In the integer block order just defined, representative resolving four-sets for \(n=3,\ldots,12\), omitting \(n=10\), are respectively
\[
\begin{gathered}
(0,1,6,7),\ (0,1,8,9),\ (0,1,2,3),\ (0,1,2,9),\\
(0,1,3,5),\ (0,1,3,16),\ (0,1,5,13),\\
(0,1,5,22),\ (0,1,5,24).
\end{gathered}
\]
At \(n=10\) a resolving triple is \((0,4,12)=(x_0,x_4,u_2)\). Witness validity is tested by the same injectivity criterion, not inferred from numerical totals.

**Lemma 2 (six-orbit certificate).** For \(n=10\), the triple enumeration outputs exactly \(160\) admissible subsets. Let transformations of the vertex labels be generated by \(i\mapsto i+a\), \(i\mapsto-i\) modulo \(10\), and simultaneous layer exchange \(x\leftrightarrow y\), \(u\leftrightarrow v\). Each preserves the stated six edge families. Transforming the enumerated triples and comparing their complete orbits against the full admissible family gives six disjoint orbits with representatives and sizes:

| Representative in integer blocks | Orbit size |
|---|---:|
| \((0,4,12)\) | 20 |
| \((0,4,17)\) | 20 |
| \((0,4,26)\) | 40 |
| \((0,12,18)\) | 20 |
| \((0,22,26)\) | 40 |
| \((0,23,27)\) | 20 |

Their cardinalities add to \(160\), proving the stated orbit count exhaustively. The standalone `check_orbits.py` reconstructs the graph and verifies every group image is in the enumerated set.

**Corollary (all indices).** Apply Lemma 1 at \(3\le n\le12\) and the published Theorem 1 of [1] at \(n\ge13\). These disjoint ranges comprise every integer \(n\ge3\), proving the displayed combined formula without extending a previously stated hypothesis beyond its range.

## Verification

`check_bfs.cpp` constructs the graph as undirected ordered pairs, computes distances via breadth-first search, and searches for pairwise collisions of edge signatures. `check_floyd.py` independently constructs symbolically labeled vertices and unordered edges, computes distances via Floyd–Warshall, and checks injectivity by sets of complete signatures. Each enumerates every vertex pair, triple, and four-set for all ten orders, and their saved TSV outputs match exactly, including every coefficient.

`check_orbits.py` independently constructs \(G_{10}\), enumerates the \(160\) bases, and computes six symmetry orbits without relying on the TSV outputs. The additional `verify_independent.py` lists exact first witnesses for all three- and four-landmark strata and its output is saved as `verify_py.out`. The supplied source programs use only standard C++17 and Python 3 and require no external mathematical software. Commands: `g++ -std=c++17 -O2 check_bfs.cpp -o check_bfs && ./check_bfs`, `python3 check_floyd.py`, `python3 check_orbits.py`, and `python3 verify_independent.py`. Compare terminal outputs to `bfs_output.tsv`, `floyd_output.tsv`, `orbit_output.txt`, and `verify_py.out`, respectively. Exhaustive finite computation proves the new finite statements *provided the short code is inspected for faithful implementation of the definitions*; no finite enumeration is used to infer the infinite range.

## Relationship to prior work

Iqbal, Rafiq, Azhar, Salman and Khalid [1] introduced the explicit edge-distance treatment of this double generalized Petersen family. Their **Theorem 1 is explicitly scoped to \(n\ge13\)**, with an upper bound for the \(2\bmod4\) class beginning at \(n\ge14\). The finite orders \(3\le n\le12\), their basis counts, and their orbit classification are not given by that theorem. The abstract states an unqualified parity pattern, but does not establish these excluded cases; the computed \(n=6\) boundary differs from an unqualified extrapolation. Another article [2] concerns Wiener and Wiener-polarity indices for a family of the same name, which do not determine edge-resolving bases. Records concerning ordinary generalized Petersen graphs, the Pappus graph, and other metric generators treat different graph domains or parameters and do not imply this finite list. No exact covering enumeration was identified, but this search is not a proof of historical priority.

## Limitations

The new exhaustive proof concerns the finite initial values \(3\le n\le12\), plus the full orbit census at \(n=10\). The statement for \(n\ge13\) is a cited previously published theorem and is not independently reproved here. Symmetry orbits are under the explicitly generated natural subgroup, with no claim to classify the entire automorphism group. Full priority clearance and an external independent mathematical audit have not been performed. The method supplies exhaustive finite answers, not an explicit formula for all higher-order basis counts.
