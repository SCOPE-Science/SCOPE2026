# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The claim defines an explicit unweighted map on a fully normalized family. The direct chord-length derivative gives two Fourier rows and hence an exact complete singular-spectrum decomposition. The displayed positive parity-separated determinants prove exactly three rigid-motion null directions. Fixed-mode limits and a uniform frequency-escaping lower bound identify third harmonic as the eventual unique lowest nonzero mode, yielding the exact limit. Re-evaluation of the matrix algebra and independent direct coordinate spectrum tests supports the proof. Tests are finite and are not substituted for the universal proof.

## Originality — PASS, provisional
The closest publicly inspected work is Guo–Luo (arXiv:2608.08001), which has a discrete Fourier/Dirichlet bound but on an angular sign-code operator and only at selected even powers of two. It neither states nor implies the singular spectrum of a 2n-output Euclidean side-plus-diameter rigidity operator on odd regular polygons. The zonogon method of Mulansky–Potschka (arXiv:2404.01841) addresses perimeter optimization and encoding, not singular-value conditioning; the later qualitative active-equality Jacobian of Ayala Hoffmann–Ayala Coppola (arXiv:2611.23066) has a different variable and no sharp third-mode spectral result. published-finding corpus searches on aliases, graph rigidity spectra, parameters and limits found no equivalent indexed statement. Historical spectral-rigidity literature may contain a special case and remains a genuine priority risk; lack of search hits is not proof of novelty.

## Value — PASS
The tight first-order stability of a constrained Euclidean polygon is measured by its least nonzero edge-length Jacobian singular value. The result proves an exact sharp \(1/n\) deterioration rate, identifies the least-detectable harmonic and supplies the exact leading constant, giving a meaningful quantitative benchmark for diameter-graph conditioning that qualitative rigidity alone cannot supply. It does not merely restate known global odd-polygon optimality.

## Limitations
The conclusion is linearized and specific to equally weighted side and diameter derivatives. The exact finite onset index is not characterized. Priority and correctness have not been independently certified.
