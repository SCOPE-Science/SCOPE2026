# Review of the sharp minimum-degree-two triameter theorem

## Correctness — PASS

The canonical metric projection is stated and proved in Hak–Kozerenko, Lemma 3.2. Given the cited lemma, edge coverage and a bijection force identical adjacency in the zero-extra-fibre cases. At exactly one extra vertex, every model leaf has a degree-one preimage unless the unique doubled fibre lies within one model edge; two leaves separated by at least three edges make this impossible. The barbell witnesses and the small-order graph checks agree with the analytic formula. There are no unproved finite-to-infinite extrapolations.

## Originality — PASS, provisional historical priority

The motivating paper poses the fixed-order, fixed-minimum-degree extremum as an explicit question rather than providing this formula. The earlier \(2n-3\) classification does not exclude \(2n-4\), which is essential to the sharp upper bound. The spider-fibre obstruction and complete \(\delta\ge2\) answer were not identified in checked mathematical records or relevant earlier local findings. Unindexed or differently phrased historical work remains possible.

## Value — PASS

The result answers the first nontrivial minimum-degree case of a currently posed precise extremal problem, with an infinite sharp family and a structural proof. The threshold at order six is intrinsic to the geometry of triple-point quotients, not an arbitrary finite slice. It can inform the still-open higher-degree and equality-graph classifications.

## Scientific limitations

The formula is an extremal maximum across admissible graphs; it does not describe every extremizer, and it does not settle fixed higher minimum degree. The proof invokes an inspected established lemma. Independent mathematical auditing has not been performed.
