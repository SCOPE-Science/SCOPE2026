# Sharp two-block locality for correcting two successive adjacent swaps in binary block codes

## Finding
Let \(b\ge1\) be any integer and let \(\varnothing\ne P\subseteq\{0,1\}^b\). For \(m\ge1\), let \(P^m\) consist of all concatenations of exactly \(m\) words from \(P\). An adjacent-swap error exchanges the symbols at consecutive positions, and successive swaps may overlap. A code corrects at most two such errors when no received word can be obtained from two different codewords by at most two swaps each.

**Theorem.** Every \(P^m\), for all \(m\ge1\), corrects at most two successive adjacent swaps **if and only if** \(P^2\) does. Equivalently, the first collision power is either \(1\), \(2\), or infinite; the optimal universal finite-test horizon is
\[
D_2(2)=2.
\]
The horizon is sharp: \(P=\{\mathtt{0},\mathtt{1}\}\) at block length one corrects two errors at the first power (indeed its codewords have different compositions), but not at the second power, because \(\mathtt{01}\) and \(\mathtt{10}\) are one adjacent swap apart.

## Assumptions and scope
A fixed, nonempty binary block set \(P\) has a fixed positive integer block length \(b\); repetition is permitted among blocks in a concatenation. There are no block-composition restrictions and no assumption on \(|P|\). The error model is the graph metric generated by **sequential** adjacent transpositions, with each transposition counting one error, including those across block boundaries. Swapping equal symbols does not change a word and is immaterial. This differs from a channel that permits an arbitrary number of pairwise disjoint swaps in a single error.

For binary words \(x,z\) of the same length, set the distance to infinity if their Hamming weights differ. Otherwise \(d(x,z)\) is the minimum number of sequential adjacent swaps taking \(x\) to \(z\). Reversibility of swaps and the graph-metric midpoint argument show that two-error balls of distinct codewords intersect exactly when their distance is at most four. All claims are for every \(b\ge1\), every nonempty \(P\), and every positive power \(m\); no asymptotic or probabilistic condition is imposed.

## Proof
**1. The exact binary transport identity.** For equal-weight \(x,z\in\{0,1\}^n\), write
\[
\Delta_j(x,z)=\sum_{i=1}^{j}(x_i-z_i),\qquad
\Delta_0=\Delta_n=0.
\]
Then
\[
d(x,z)=\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}|\Delta_j(x,z)|. \tag{1}
\]
A swap at the cut after position \(j\) changes only \(\Delta_j\) by one, so at least \(|\Delta_j|\) swaps must cross each cut. To attain the lower bound, match the \(r\)-th one in \(x\) with the \(r\)-th one in \(z\) for each index \(r\), and move ones by adjacent interchanges without changing their order. The number of crossings at cut \(j\) is then exactly \(|\Delta_j|\), because the imbalance counts the net number of matched ones that must cross that cut. Thus (1) is exact. In particular, adjacent prefix imbalances differ by at most one:
\[
|\Delta_{j+1}-\Delta_j|\le1. \tag{2}
\]

**2. Reduce a shortest collision to distinct nonzero boundary imbalances.** Suppose, toward a contradiction, that \(P^2\) corrects two errors but some \(P^m\) does not. Choose the smallest such \(m\), and choose distinct \(x=x^{(1)}\cdots x^{(m)}\), \(z=z^{(1)}\cdots z^{(m)}\) in \(P^m\) with \(d(x,z)\le4\). Since a collision in \(P^1\) can be padded by an identical block to a collision in \(P^2\), we have \(m\ge3\). Define the boundary imbalances \(h_i=\Delta_{ib}(x,z)\), for \(0\le i\le m\), so \(h_0=h_m=0\).

Every internal \(h_i\) is nonzero. Otherwise split both words after block \(i\). The two corresponding prefixes have equal weights, as do the suffixes. At least one corresponding pair is unequal. Its distance is no greater than \(d(x,z)\), by (1) and the zero imbalance at the separating cut. This would be a collision at a smaller power.

Moreover, the internal boundary imbalances are pairwise distinct. If \(h_i=h_j\ne0\) for \(1\le i<j<m\), remove the complete blocks \(i+1,\ldots,j\) from both words. The removed segments have equal weights, so the remaining concatenations have equal weights; the retained initial \(i\) blocks still differ, since \(h_i\ne0\). On every retained cut the new prefix imbalance is exactly the old one, because \(h_i=h_j\). By (1), their distance is no greater than four. This again contradicts minimality of \(m\).

**3. No boundary imbalance can have magnitude at least two.** Assume \(b\ge2\). At a block boundary with \(|h_i|\ge2\), the immediately preceding and following cuts are not block boundaries, and each has imbalance of magnitude at least \(|h_i|-1\ge1\), by (2). As \(m\ge3\), there is at least one other internal block boundary, with nonzero imbalance. These four distinct cuts contribute at least \(2+1+1+1=5\) to (1), contradicting \(d(x,z)\le4\). Thus each internal \(h_i\) is \(+1\) or \(-1\).

Since these numbers are pairwise distinct, \(m-1\le2\). In view of \(m\ge3\), only \(m=3\) remains, with \(h_1=-h_2\in\{-1,1\}\).

**4. Cross-splice the remaining three-block collision.** Set
\[
u=x^{(1)}z^{(3)},\qquad v=z^{(1)}x^{(3)}.
\]
Both belong to \(P^2\), and they are distinct because \(h_1\ne0\). Their total weight difference is \(h_1+h_2=0\). The prefix imbalances in the first block coincide with those of \((x,z)\); at the splice cut the imbalance is \(h_1\); and at any cut within the second retained block the new imbalance is the **negative** of the old imbalance in the third block, since \(h_1=-h_2\). Consequently (1) yields
\[
d(u,v)=\sum_{j=1}^{b}|\Delta_j(x,z)|+
\sum_{j=2b+1}^{3b-1}|\Delta_j(x,z)|\le d(x,z)\le4.
\]
This gives a forbidden two-block collision. The contradiction establishes the theorem for all \(b\ge2\).

If \(b=1\), a singleton \(P\) has no collision, while the only nonsingleton binary block set is \(\{\mathtt{0},\mathtt{1}\}\), for which the second power already has a collision. The sharpness example therefore completes the proof.

## Verification
The proof is infinite and self-contained; finite computations serve as independent falsification checks rather than an extrapolation. Run, using Python 3 without third-party packages:

```text
python3 verify_distance.py
python3 verify_locality.py
```

The first program independently computes shortest paths in the adjacent-swap graph by breadth-first search for all ordered equal-weight binary word pairs through length nine and compares them with (1): 66,196 pairs pass. The second enumerates all nonempty binary block sets through block length four and checks that any set whose second power has no pair at distance at most four also has no such pair in powers three, four and five. At block lengths one, two, three and four, the numbers of safe sets are \(2,4,17,91\), respectively; every later-power check passes. It also checks the sharp horizon witness. The exact recorded outputs are supplied in the standalone output files. No finite test is used to assert the all-length conclusion.

## Relationship to prior work
Ataba and Zabokritskiy [1, Sections 2, 3 and 7] proved a \(2t+1\)-block locality test for arbitrary alphabets, characterized when this horizon is sharp, and bounded the binary two-error horizon by \(2\le D_2(2)\le4\). They explicitly leave the exact value open when \(\binom q2\le2t-2\); the present theorem resolves \((q,t)=(2,2)\) by a sharp two-block test. Their binary prefix-composition bounds and their first-collision-power framework are acknowledged. The new ingredient is the cancellation/cross-splicing proof for a **minimal** four-swap collision.

The studies [2–4] address either pairwise disjoint adjacent transpositions with a different channel budget or related block-coding constructions; they do not imply the two-error sequential two-block criterion. The result does not establish any locality formula for larger alphabets or larger error budgets.

## Limitations
The proof is specific to a binary alphabet and to at most two **successive** swaps on each predecessor path. It does not apply unchanged to three or more successive swaps, to nonbinary alphabets, or to the radius-one limited-permutation channel permitting many disjoint swaps at once. It supplies a decision horizon, not a polynomial-time bound in the listed size of \(P\), an optimal block-code size, or a decoder. Novelty is conditional on the inspected published material and indexed records; unindexed work could contain an equivalent statement. The finding has not been independently audited.
