# Sharp two-deletion capacity and equality census for balanced ternary six-words

## Finding

Let \(\mathcal W\) be the \(90\) linear words over the ordered labeled alphabet \(\{0,1,2\}\) with exactly two occurrences of each symbol. Let \(D_2(w)\) be the **set** of all length-four subsequences of \(w\), obtained by deleting any two positions, without rank standardization or forgetting the surviving order. A two-deletion-correcting code is a subset \(C\subseteq\mathcal W\) for which \(D_2(u)\cap D_2(v)=\varnothing\) whenever \(u\ne v\) belong to \(C\).

**Theorem.** The maximum size of such a code is **four**. Exactly **144** unordered, labeled four-word codebooks attain it. Under the action generated by all six global permutations of alphabet symbols and word reversal, they form exactly **14** orbits: four of size six and ten of size twelve. In addition, the standard fractional packing relaxation on the length-four deletion shadows has exact optimum **five**, exhibiting an integrality gap \(5/4\).

One optimal code is

```text
012201
102102
200211
211200
```

A representative of every optimal symmetry class is supplied in `data/equality_orbits.txt`.

## Assumptions and scope

Words are indexed **linear** words, not necklaces. Two different positions holding the same symbol are indistinguishable after deletion; duplicate outputs from different position choices are identified in \(D_2(w)\). The decoder sees the ordered sequence of the four remaining symbols and must identify the transmitted codeword for every choice of two deleted positions. The alphabet labels remain distinguished when counting 144 codebooks. Symmetry classes additionally identify global alphabet relabelings and reversal, but not cyclic rotations or arbitrary coordinate permutations. The statement is solely for length six, three symbols, and composition \((2,2,2)\); it makes no assertion for different parameters, unrestricted source words, or different error channels.

## Proof

There are exactly \(\binom62\binom42=90\) source words. For every source \(w\), generating all \(\binom64=15\) index quadruples gives exactly the distinct elements of \(D_2(w)\). Define the compatibility graph \(G\) on these 90 words by joining distinct \(u,v\) exactly when \(D_2(u)\cap D_2(v)=\varnothing\). Thus the required codebooks are exactly the cliques of \(G\). Equivalently, if \(\operatorname{LCS}(u,v)\) is the longest common subsequence length, an edge exists exactly when \(\operatorname{LCS}(u,v)\le3\): the two deletion shadows intersect if and only if they share a subsequence of length four.

The finite verification accompanying this theorem is an exhaustive proof, not extrapolation. Its core enumeration uses the lexicographically sorted list of all 90 words. For every vertex \(i\), it builds a bit mask \(N_i^+\) of compatible vertices with larger indices. Starting from the empty clique and all 90 candidate indices, it repeatedly selects each remaining least-index candidate \(j\), recursing only on \(N_j^+\) intersected with the remaining candidate mask. Each clique is visited **exactly once**, because it has a unique increasing index sequence. The exact counts at sizes zero through five are

\[
(1,90,1164,1788,144,0).
\]

The zero in the fifth positive position proves no five-word code exists. The explicitly displayed witness gives a four-clique, proving the sharp bound. All 144 four-cliques are then acted upon by the twelve transformations consisting of a global permutation of \(\{0,1,2\}\) and optional reversal. Every resulting transformed set remains a four-clique. Exact orbit partition gives \(4\) orbits of cardinality \(6\) and \(10\) of cardinality \(12\), with total \(4\cdot6+10\cdot12=144\). All orbit representatives are listed in the accompanying data file.

For the fractional packing claim, place a nonnegative variable \(p_w\) on each source word \(w\), subject to \(\sum_{w:\,s\in D_2(w)}p_w\le1\) for every four-letter read \(s\). An explicit feasible primal solution of total weight five assigns, to every word in the alphabet-permutation-and-reversal orbit of each representative, the weight shown below; other words have weight zero:

| Representative | Assigned weight |
|---|---|
| `001122` | \(1/15\) |
| `001212` | \(3/20\) |
| `010221` | \(1/60\) |
| `011220` | \(1/6\) |
| `012021` | \(2/15\) |

A feasible covering dual assigns weight \(1/6\) to each four-letter read in the union of the orbits of `0011`, `0012`, `0112`, `0120`, and zero otherwise; its total is five. The exact rational checker validates every constraint and both totals, so weak duality proves the relaxation optimum is exactly five. This certificate supports the structural distinction between fractional and integral two-deletion packing, not the integer upper bound by itself.

## Verification

Run from the directory containing this document with Python 3 (standard library only):

```text
python3 code/verify_shadow.py
python3 code/verify_lcs.py
python3 code/verify_fractional.py
```

The first independently regenerates all source words and two-deletion shadows, enumerates increasing-index cliques without repetition, tests the witness, and partitions optimal codebooks into symmetry orbits. The second independently generates all 90 source words by selecting the zero and one positions, derives compatibility by dynamic-programming LCS, and enumerates all compatible pairs, triples, quadruples and possible five-extensions. The third uses exact rational arithmetic to verify the fractional primal/dual. All three were executed successfully: \((90,1164,1788,144,0)\), orbit histogram \(\{6:4,12:10\}\), and matching primal/dual objective \(5\). The scripts are the finite certificates for the complete census; they are small enough to audit line-by-line and require no solver or external data.

## Relationship to prior work

Farnoud and Milenkovic (arXiv:1312.2163v1, 2013) study regular multipermutation codes in the Ulam metric and provide general bounds and constructions. Their setting has the same constant-composition source objects and closely related longest-common-subsequence distance, but the inspected definitions, bounds and construction sections do not provide the exact four-codeword optimum, the 144-codebook census, its symmetry classification, or this fractional shadow-packing gap. Their general bound \(A_{\circ}(6,2,3)\le S(4,3,2)=54\) is much weaker than four. The related study of Sala, Gabrys and Dolecek (2014) specifically concerns multipermutation deletions, but its complete paper was unavailable for comparison; its abstract discusses models and constructions rather than asserting this finite enumeration. Liu and Draper (arXiv:1507.07628, 2015) address LP-based multipermutation decoding in other channels, not this precise deletion-shadow packing computation.

A published related result, *Exact balanced ternary deletion codes and a chromatic obstruction*, concerns **one** deletion on the same 90 source words and establishes an eighteen-word optimum; it does not imply the two-deletion optimum or classify its extremizers. Other known results for unordered multiset-deletion channels and for different alphabets/lengths concern different confusability relations. These differences are substantive: their codewords and outputs cannot be substituted into the stated two-deletion shadow without changing the admissibility relation.

## Limitations

The result is a finite exact theorem, not a general all-length formula. The proof of the maximum and all equality cases relies on transparent exhaustive integer enumeration over exactly 90 sources; the separate LCS implementation mitigates, but does not eliminate, implementation risk. The exact fractional optimum does not independently certify the integer maximum. Although highly relevant available primary literature and semantic research records were compared, the full 2014 *Deletions in Multipermutations* conference paper could not be inspected, and an unindexed earlier equivalent enumeration cannot be ruled out. Independent expert audit has not occurred.
