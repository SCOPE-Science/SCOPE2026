# Mathematical review: sharp two-hyponormality boundary for quadratic Cauchy duals

## Correctness

**PASS.** The proof begins with the telescoping identity for the Cauchy dual moments and applies the standard all-shift finite Hankel characterization. Two exact rational determinant formulas reduce positivity to a cubic in \((a+n+2)^2\) and \(b^2\). Strict negativity of one derivative's discriminant gives a unique boundary; strict negativity of the cubic's discriminant in the shift variable proves that its initial block is decisive for every shift. The positive first principal minors at the cubic boundary are checked independently through an exact negative substitution at the larger one-hyponormal threshold. The sign-changing signed measure gives a genuine impossibility proof of subnormality, not an inconclusive numerical test. Exact symbolic verification was replayed.

## Originality

**PASS, with historical-priority qualification.** Chavan–Stochel (2023, Example 4.5) already treats exactly the same complex-root quadratic moment family, but proves weak concavity through a two-by-two log-concavity inequality, not full two-hyponormality or a three-by-three Hankel threshold. Reza–Zhang (2019) classifies complete Hausdorff positivity for reciprocal quadratics, so the non-subnormal side alone is known and not claimed original. McCullough–Paulsen's classical criterion is a tool rather than the finding. The examined cyclic two-isometry Cauchy dual, toral two-variable three-isometry, and atomic-charge results do not imply the new sharp all-parameter **finite two-hyponormality** boundary or its cubic and asymptotic constants. Closest accessible published mathematical records were compared by their claims, not by title similarity; the possibility of an unindexed equivalent cannot be excluded.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** This is a natural, exact finite-order positivity classification within the elementary polynomial-moment family underlying strict three-isometric unilateral shifts. It quantifies the substantive gap between one- and two-hyponormality, exhibits an entire non-subnormal two-hyponormal region, identifies a rank-defective boundary followed by positive-definite tail blocks, and establishes a universal large-parameter cutoff ratio. The classification is not a restatement of the known all-orders Hausdorff criterion.

## Limits and close literature

The theorem is for one-variable quadratic moments only. No higher \(k\)-hyponormality classification is asserted. Sources include McCullough–Paulsen (1992), Reza–Zhang (2019), Anand–Chavan–Jabłoński–Stochel (2020), Chavan–Stochel (2023, same polynomial family), Nailwal (2023), and Benhida–Curto–Exner (2024). Complete historical uniqueness and independent mathematical certification are not claimed.
