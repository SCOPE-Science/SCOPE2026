# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The proof reduces a nonlinear two-input bound to 144 explicitly listed row-stochastic averaging branch matrices. An exact-rational exhaustive step-response certificate identifies precisely one branch with coefficient greater than \(11/6\). A mean-comparison argument excludes that branch at all nonconstant inputs. Piecewise-affine integration proves the bound for every nonnegative pair; an explicit integer-valued pair attains \(11/6\). The standalone finite checker validates the complete 144-mask calculation and the witness, while the analytic steps handle the continuum of inputs.

**Originality — PASS, qualified.** Madrid (2015) studies centered and uncentered maximal-function variation for individual inputs on the integer lattice. González-Riquelme and Madrid (2020) examine single-input finite-graph variation, Hilbert norms, and qualitative normed-BV continuity. Liu and Zhang (2022) focus on directed-graph single-input variation. The closest publicly indexed exact two-input results concern centered maximal operators on stars or complete graphs and uncentered operators on different graphs, not the centered four-vertex path. Neither one-input variation estimates nor an exact Hilbert norm entail the present sharp two-input path-variation coefficient. Unindexed earlier equivalents remain a priority risk.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The four-vertex path is the first unweighted path not isomorphic to a star; the exact factor gives a smallest non-star benchmark for sensitivity of a natural graph maximal operator. The proof isolates a generalizable distinction between algebraically possible branch norms and branch patterns that are jointly active, rather than merely enumerating arbitrary sample cases.

**Scope and limitations.** Nonnegative real inputs, unit graph distances and masses, four vertices, and first adjacent variation only. The analysis does not prove constants for larger paths or arbitrary signed inputs under the absolute-value convention.
