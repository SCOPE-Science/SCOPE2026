# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The piecewise-affine active-radius derivative is exact on the nonnegative cone, and every row-selector preserves constants. The cyclic arc decomposition gives the operator seminorm through arc indicators. The five possible non-complementary arc lengths on cycles of order three, four, or five reduce to 104 exact rational box corners; `verify_exact.py` recomputes every value. All three lower witnesses are strict active-radius chambers and attain the claimed ratios without a limiting argument. The proof is restricted to the specified cycles and nonnegative inputs.

**Originality — PASS with residual historical-priority risk.** The directly closest published finite-cycle result treats the one-input ratio \(V(Mf)/V(f)\), not pairwise \(V(Mf-Mg)/V(f-g)\). The 2020 finite-graph paper includes a continuity result in a full BV norm, which does not yield a sharp pairwise seminorm constant. The 2010 discrete-line paper and 2026 finite-graph classification likewise concern one-input variation. The statements were compared by implication, not by titles. The resulting sharp constants and the first pairwise expansion at four vertices are not supplied by these results. Older unindexed literature remains a genuine risk.

**Value — PASS.** For a classical nonlinear regularity operator whose single-input variation constants are known and strictly below one, it is mathematically meaningful to ask whether perturbations are stable in the same seminorm. The theorem completely resolves this question for all finite cycles of diameter at most two and establishes the first dimension at which two-input expansion occurs. It separates nonlinear perturbation sensitivity from conventional variation smoothing, with exact attained constants and a small self-contained proof certificate.

**Limitations and closest work.** The proof concerns only nonnegative real vectors, unweighted graph balls and unweighted cyclic variation on three through five vertices. The published weighted-cycle result, González-Riquelme--Madrid (2020), González-Riquelme--Kovač--Madrid (2026), and Bober--Carneiro--Hughes--Pierce (2010) were inspected at the stated material portions. Further historical comparison may change priority assessment. No claim is made for longer cycles or the signed-input absolute-value operator.
