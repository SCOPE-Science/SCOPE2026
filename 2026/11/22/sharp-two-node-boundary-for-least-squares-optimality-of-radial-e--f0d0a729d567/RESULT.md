# Sharp two-node boundary for least-squares optimality of radial entropy limiting

## Finding

Fix an integer \(N\ge 2\), positive nodal weights \(w_1,\ldots,w_N\) with \(\sum_iw_i=1\), and a scalar entropy \(\eta\in C^3(\mathbb R)\) satisfying \(\eta''(s)>0\) for every real \(s\). For a prescribed weighted mean \(m\) and budget \(B>\eta(m)\), define the mean-conserving entropy set

\[
\mathcal C_{m,B}=\left\{z:\ \sum_iw_i z_i=m,\quad \sum_iw_i\eta(z_i)\le B\right\}.
\]

For a candidate \(x\) with this mean, let \(R_{m,B}(x)\) be the most distant feasible point from the constant vector \(m\mathbf1\) *on the segment joining it to \(x\)*: explicitly \(R_{m,B}(x)=m\mathbf1+\theta(x-m\mathbf1)\), where \(\theta\in[0,1]\) is maximal subject to the entropy inequality. Let \(P_{m,B}(x)\) be the nearest point in \(\mathcal C_{m,B}\) to \(x\) in the norm \(\|z\|_w^2=\sum_iw_i z_i^2\).

**Sharp classification.** If \(N=2\), the identity \(R_{m,B}(x)=P_{m,B}(x)\) holds for every admissible choice of \(\eta,m,B,x\). For every \(N\ge3\) and every fixed vector of strictly positive weights, the identity holds for **all** \(m\), all \(B>\eta(m)\), and all mean-matched \(x\) **if and only if** \(\eta\) is a strictly convex quadratic polynomial.

An explicit violation already occurs for the classical three-node Simpson quadrature: take \(w=(1/6,4/6,1/6)\), \(\eta(t)=t^2/2+t^4/4\), \(m=1\), \(x=(2/5,1,8/5)\), and \(B=59/64\). The radial limiter gives \(y=(1/2,1,3/2)\), but the feasible positive vector \(z=(191/400,2017/2000,2977/2000)\) has strictly smaller weighted squared correction:

\[
E(z)-E(y)=-\frac{254723823}{2560000000000}<0,\qquad
\|z-x\|_w^2-\|y-x\|_w^2=-\frac{509}{2400000}<0,
\]

where \(E(v)=\sum_iw_i\eta(v_i)\). Thus the radial correction is not generally the smallest entropy-feasible conservative \(L^2\) correction, even with positive nodal states.

## Assumptions and scope

All nodes here carry one scalar state; \(N\ge2\), all weights are strictly positive and normalized, and the entropy is defined on all of \(\mathbb R\), three times continuously differentiable, with everywhere strictly positive second derivative. The entropy budget is strictly above the Jensen minimum \(\eta(m)\). Only the entropy constraint and exact weighted mean are imposed on the general feasible set; further positivity or invariant-set constraints are not required for the classification. The three-node witness has strictly positive candidate, radial and comparison states.

The assertion concerns **global identity for every mean and budget**, not identity at one specially chosen candidate, nor the correctness of an entropy inequality. A ray limiter is uniquely defined by the largest feasible segment parameter; the metric projection exists and is unique by closed convexity of the feasible set and the strictly convex weighted squared distance.

## Proof

**1. The one-dimensional exception.** Let \(V=\{u:\sum_iw_iu_i=0\}\). For \(N=2\), \(V\) is one-dimensional. Strict convexity and Jensen's inequality show that \(F_m(u)=\sum_iw_i\eta(m+u_i)\) is strictly convex and uniquely minimized at \(u=0\). Consequently, \(\{u\in V:F_m(u)\le B\}\) is a closed interval containing \(0\) in its interior. On this line the nearest-point correction of any \(u\) is precisely the feasible point on the segment from \(0\) to \(u\) with largest segment parameter. Hence radial and weighted-metric projections agree.

**2. Quadratic sufficiency.** Suppose \(\eta(t)=at^2+bt+c\) with \(a>0\). Whenever \(u\in V\), weighted-mean cancellation gives \(F_m(u)=\eta(m)+a\|u\|_w^2\). Thus the feasible set in \(V\) is a centered weighted-Hilbert ball. Its metric projection is its radial retraction; again \(R=P\) for every budget and candidate.

**3. Radial projection forces radial entropy.** Assume \(N\ge3\) and \(R_{m,B}=P_{m,B}\) for every mean and admissible budget. Fix a mean \(m\) and a nonzero vector \(u\in V\). Set \(B=F_m(u)>\eta(m)\), and, for any \(\varepsilon>0\), let the candidate be \(x=m\mathbf1+(1+\varepsilon)u\). As \(t\mapsto F_m(tu)\) is strictly increasing for \(t>0\), the ray limiter of this candidate is \(m\mathbf1+u\). By hypothesis the same point is the nearest feasible correction. At the regular boundary \(F_m(u)=B\), the normal cone equals the nonnegative ray generated by \(\nabla_VF_m(u)\), the gradient in the weighted Hilbert geometry of \(V\). The metric-projection optimality condition therefore forces \(u\) to be collinear with \(\nabla_VF_m(u)\). Regularity follows from strict convexity, since a vanishing gradient can occur only at the unique minimizer \(0\).

Since \(\dim V=N-1\ge2\), every positive-radius weighted sphere in \(V\) is connected. Tangential derivatives of \(F_m\) on such a sphere vanish. Thus \(F_m\) depends only on \(\|u\|_w\), in particular \(F_m(u)=F_m(-u)\) for every \(u\in V\).

**4. A nonzero third-moment direction.** Taylor expansion at \(0\), for fixed \(u\in V\), gives

\[
F_m(tu)-F_m(-tu)=\frac{\eta'''(m)}{3}\,t^3\sum_iw_i u_i^3+o(t^3).
\]

For any vector of positive weights with \(N\ge3\), there is \(u\in V\) with \(\sum_iw_iu_i^3\ne0\). If two weights \(w_i\ne w_j\), use \(u_i=w_j\), \(u_j=-w_i\), and all remaining entries zero; the cubic sum is \(w_iw_j(w_j^2-w_i^2)\ne0\). If all weights are equal, choose three entries \(1,1,-2\) and the rest zero; their cubic sum is \(-6w_i\ne0\). Radiality of \(F_m\) now implies \(\eta'''(m)=0\). The mean \(m\) was arbitrary, so \(\eta'''\equiv0\) on \(\mathbb R\); with \(\eta''>0\), this yields exactly the strict quadratics.

**5. Three-node exact witness.** With the stated Simpson weights, \(\sum_iw_ix_i=\sum_iw_iy_i=\sum_iw_iz_i=1\) and \(y=m\mathbf1+(5/6)(x-m\mathbf1)\). Direct rational calculation yields \(E(y)=59/64\), whereas \(E(z)=2359745276177/2560000000000\). The corresponding squared distances are \(\|y-x\|_w^2=1/300\) and \(\|z-x\|_w^2=2497/800000\). Both strict inequalities in the finding follow. Jensen's inequality and strict convexity make the entropy profile strictly increasing after its constant-mean minimum, so the equality \(E(y)=B\) verifies that \(y\) is exactly the ray-limit output. This completes the proof.

## Relationship to prior work

Wu's EPO method defines a cell-average-anchored scaling ray and an entropy admissibility radius (arXiv:2604.00301, §§1, 5.1–5.2, and the quadratic example in §9.1). Liu, Guo, Jiang and Sun likewise use conservative scaling to enforce fully discrete entropy inequalities (arXiv:2603.27478). Those methods establish entropy control along a specified ray, not the nearest-correction optimization problem addressed here. The quadratic example in EPO gives the expected positive direction of the classification; the additional statement is its converse for every positive quadrature rule with at least three nodes, together with the sharp two-node exception and exact feasible three-node separation.

The general relation between radial geometry, quadratic functions, and additive separability is also known in other settings; see N'guessan and Kim's arXiv:2602.08419. That abstract describes a different characterization on an unconstrained coordinate-separable domain, whereas the present claim concerns a weighted zero-mean hyperplane, an entropy sublevel correction operator, and the minimal nodal count. No claim of originating radial-function rigidity in general is made.

## Limitations

The theorem applies to the scalar, strictly convex, globally \(C^3\) entropy-only limiter. Physical entropy functions restricted to a nontrivial admissible-state domain, multiple entropy pairs, systems of conservation laws, cell-average changes, or other norms require separate analysis. It does not challenge the validity of an EPO entropy inequality or imply any stability or convergence defect for a discretized PDE. No independent mathematical validation has been performed, and historical-priority assessment is limited to the cited inspectable primary material and available mathematical records; the full text of one general radial-separability manuscript was inaccessible during comparison.
