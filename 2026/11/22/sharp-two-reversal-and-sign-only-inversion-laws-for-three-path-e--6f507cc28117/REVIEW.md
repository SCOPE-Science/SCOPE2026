# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The claim reduces exactly to the sign of a strictly convex exponential curve minus a constant. The derivative's sign at zero and its unique interior minimum provide all positive-discharge and equality cases. Cramer's rule and a strictly positive determinant prove two-threshold identification and converse realizability. A Taylor expansion at the positive minimum proves the fold law; its third derivative gives the asymmetric displacement. A standalone Python checker passed 5,000 parameter classifications, 212 inverse checks, an exact algebraic witness and five fold tests. Numerical checks are corroborating only; the proof is analytical.

## Originality — PASS on inspected evidence

The environmental motivation and salinity-difference sign relationship belong to Biemond, de Swart and Dijkstra (2023). Their full text, including the overspill approximation and appendices, was inspected; its channel calculations are not the additive independent-path proxy, nor is a two-threshold sign-only inverse established. Full text of Cai et al. (2023) and Dijkstra et al. (2023) was also inspected for related salinity-profile inference and stochastic intrusion modeling. Jameson's classical generalized-Descartes framework covers a zero-count principle, which is explicitly credited, not claimed as new. Chauveau et al. (1996) address the inverse problem for signed exponential mixtures from full functions or noisy magnitude data, while the assessed result concerns only two threshold-zero positions; its full paper could not be inspected and is a concrete priority risk. Searches for discharge-driven dual salinity-sign crossings, exponential attenuation mixtures and sign-only recovery found no direct comparison establishing the complete positive-domain amplitude window, inverse and fold in the proposed salt-proxy class. Historical mathematical priority beyond these sources remains uncertain.

## Value — PASS

The specific mathematical gap is whether two different discharge levels where overspill changes sign can occur in the smallest heterogeneous salt-pathway proxy, and whether only their *locations* can identify independent contributions. A sharp feasibility window, a proof that one less pathway cannot suffice, explicit two-switch amplitude recovery and fold ill-conditioning answer these natural modeling questions together. This gives a falsifiable conceptual benchmark for low-information junction monitoring rather than a rephrasing of a generic zero-count estimate. No prediction for actual estuaries or field application is claimed.
