# Scientific review: cyclic two-site dilation

## Correctness

**PASS.** The proof includes the normalization and complete quantifiers (real data, \(n\ge3\), positive variation weights). On every strict chamber the filter is a local coordinate selector; every realizable selector sign pattern contains both signs, and the explicit cyclic increments construct the converse. Coarea and connected-component decomposition reduce an arbitrary perturbation to a single arc; its two boundary selectors give exactly four possible Boolean outputs. A strict-chamber construction realizes every boundary choice with actual input pairs. The singleton, co-singleton and separated-wall calculations then yield the stated finite formula and rigidity. Exact rational enumeration of selectors and explicit witnesses supplies supplementary checks, not the general proof. The dual min filter follows by sign reversal.

## Originality

**PASS, qualified.** Full scientific statements in the published path-window morphological sensitivity theorem, cyclic-median theorem, and tree max-dilation theorem were inspected. The path theorem excludes periodic boundaries, the cyclic median treats a different operator and uniform weights, and the tree theorem concerns one-input variation. Archival full-text studies by Anguelov--Rohwer and Anguelov--Plaskitt prove single-input variation behavior for LULU operators, not the claimed two-input cyclic weighted coefficient. Najman--Cousty's survey develops morphological graph operations without this coefficient. The plausible 2013 Cousty--Najman--Dias--Serra article was accessible only as an abstract; unindexed historical stack-filter results are a residual priority risk. None of the inspected statements is a dominating theorem or equivalent weighted two-wall result. Search absence alone is not used as proof of novelty.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** The problem measures sensitivity of a standard periodic morphological primitive in the natural first-variation seminorm under spatially inhomogeneous edge costs. The exact invariant is uniform in cycle size, classifies every positive edge-weight choice, and distinguishes cyclic two-wall behavior from truncated paths. The four-cycle alternating example proves that singleton tests miss the true sharp constant by a factor \(3/2\), so the separated-wall structure is essential, not a cosmetic restatement of one-input variation decrease. The full alternating-weight family gives a useful explicit benchmark, and nonexpansivity rigidity is genuinely global over every cycle size.
