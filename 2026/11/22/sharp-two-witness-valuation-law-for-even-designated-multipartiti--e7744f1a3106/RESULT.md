# Sharp two-witness valuation law for even designated multipartitions

## Finding
Let \(f_d(q)=\prod_{j\ge1}(1-q^{dj})\), set
\[
F(q)=\frac{f_6(q)}{f_1(q)f_2(q)f_3(q)},\qquad A_k(N)=[q^N]F(q)^k,
\]
and let \(k\) be any positive **even** integer. Then the exact two-adic order of the greatest common divisor of the infinite progression \(\{A_k(4n+3):n\ge0\}\) is
\[
\min_{n\ge0}\nu_2(A_k(4n+3))=
\begin{cases}
\nu_2(k),&4\mid k,\\
2,&k\equiv6\pmod8,\\
3,&k\equiv2\pmod8.
\end{cases}
\]
The minimum is attained at \(n=0\) (coefficient \(A_k(3)\)) except when \(k\equiv2\pmod{16}\), in which case \(A_k(7)\equiv8\pmod{16}\) attains it at \(n=1\). In particular, for **every even** \(k\), the first two progression coefficients determine the entire progression's exact common power of two:
\[
\nu_2\!\left(\gcd_{n\ge0}A_k(4n+3)\right)
=\nu_2\bigl(\gcd(A_k(3),A_k(7))\bigr).
\]
This supplies the even-parameter case of Conjecture 6.1 in Yaqubi–Mirzavaziri (2026). The previously established odd-parameter case is not asserted as a new finding.

## Assumptions and scope
All series are formal series in \(\mathbb Z[[q]]\). The integer \(k\) is even and positive, and \(n\ge0\). By convention \(\nu_2\) is the usual two-adic valuation of a nonzero integer; all minima below are attained at explicitly nonzero coefficients. The coefficient \(A_k(N)\) counts ordered \(k\)-tuples of partitions with one designated summand of each occurring part size and total weight \(N\). The infinite gcd is the positive generator of the ideal of \(\mathbb Z\) generated by these coefficients.

## Proof
**1. Base coefficients and two-dissection.** The standard designated-part series is
\[
F(q)=\prod_{j\ge1}\left(1+\sum_{m\ge1}m q^{jm}\right)
=1+q+3q^2+5q^3+10q^4+\cdots.
\]
The exact odd-index two-dissection of Yaqubi–Mirzavaziri, Lemma 3.11, gives
\[
\sum_{j\ge0} A_2(2j+1)x^j=2W(x),\qquad
W(x)=\frac{f_2(x)^4f_6(x)^4}{f_1(x)^8f_3(x)^4}.
\]
The elementary product congruences \(f_1^8\equiv f_2^4\pmod4\) and \(f_3^4\equiv f_6^2\pmod4\) imply
\[
W(x)\equiv f_6(x)^2\pmod4.
\]
Since this is a series in even powers of \(x\), all \(A_2(4n+3)\) are divisible by \(8\). Also \(F(q)^2\equiv F(q^2)\pmod2\), so **every odd-indexed coefficient of \(F^2\) is even**.

**2. The lifting lemma.** For any integral formal series \(H\) and any \(e\ge1\), the binomial theorem implies
\[
H(q)^{2^e}\equiv H(q^2)^{2^{e-1}}\pmod{2^e}.
\]
In particular, all odd-indexed coefficients of \(F^{2^e}\) vanish modulo \(2^e\). When \(e\ge2\), applying the congruence twice also gives
\[
F(q)^{2^e}\equiv F(q^4)^{2^{e-2}}\pmod{2^{e-1}}.
\]
For \(e=2\), the first congruence yields that every coefficient of \(F^4\) at an odd exponent is divisible by \(4\); the second yields divisibility by \(2\) at exponents \(2\pmod4\). For \(e=3\), the corresponding bounds for \(F^8\) are respectively \(8\) and \(4\).

**3. Divisibility for all even parameters.** If \(4\mid k\), put \(e=\nu_2(k)\ge2\). The first lifting congruence makes every odd-indexed coefficient of \(F^k\) divisible by \(2^e\).

If \(k\equiv2\pmod4\), write \(F^k=F^2(F^4)^m\) with \(m=(k-2)/4\). The power \((F^4)^m\) has its odd coefficients divisible by \(4\), and its coefficients at exponents \(2\pmod4\) divisible by \(2\). In a convolution producing exponent \(3\pmod4\), terms with a \(0\pmod4\) exponent from \((F^4)^m\) meet the \(8\)-divisible \(3\pmod4\) coefficients of \(F^2\); terms with a \(2\pmod4\) exponent meet even odd-index coefficients of \(F^2\). Hence every such coefficient is divisible by \(4\); odd-exponent contributions from \((F^4)^m\) are already divisible by \(4\).

If \(k\equiv2\pmod8\), repeat the argument with \(F^k=F^2(F^8)^m\), where \(m=(k-2)/8\). Now odd coefficients of \((F^8)^m\) are divisible by \(8\) and its \(2\pmod4\) coefficients by \(4\). The same convolution, using the even odd-index coefficients of \(F^2\) and its \(8\)-divisible \(3\pmod4\) coefficients, proves divisibility by \(8\) for the whole progression.

**4. Sharpness and the second witness.** Direct coefficient extraction gives
\[
A_k(3)=\frac{k(k+1)(k+14)}6.
\]
Its two-adic valuation is exactly \(\nu_2(k)\) for \(4\mid k\), exactly \(2\) for \(k\equiv6\pmod8\), and exactly \(3\) for \(k\equiv10\pmod{16}\). These cases meet the preceding lower bounds at \(n=0\).

The remaining case is \(k=16m+2\) with \(m\ge0\). All computations in the following finite formal-series congruence are modulo \((16,q^8)\):
\[
F(q)^2\equiv1+2q+7q^2+0q^3+7q^4+0q^5+11q^6+8q^7,
\]
\[
F(q)^{16}\equiv1+8q^2+4q^4+8q^6.
\]
Put \(T=8q^2+4q^4+8q^6\). As \(T^2\equiv0\pmod{(16,q^8)}\), it follows for every \(m\ge0\) that
\[
[q^7]F(q)^{16m+2}\equiv [q^7]F^2
+m\bigl(8[q^5]F^2+4[q^3]F^2+8[q]F^2\bigr)
\equiv8+m(8\cdot80+4\cdot16+8\cdot2)\equiv8\pmod{16}.
\]
This is an exact, nonzero valuation-three witness at \(A_k(7)\). All alternatives for even \(k\) have been exhausted, proving the theorem.

## Relationship to prior work
Yaqubi and Mirzavaziri (2026), Lemma 3.11, gives the exact two-dissection used here. Their Theorem 3.12 supplies lower bounds for the congruence classes of \(k\), Proposition 3.13 proves the sharp bound for \(k\equiv6\pmod8\), and Conjecture 6.1 explicitly predicts the complete pattern including the exceptional \(k\equiv2\pmod{16}\) case; they provide finite tests but not the uniform exact result. The new contribution is the all-even classification and a uniform two-coefficient sharpness certificate, particularly the second witness at \(7\). The previously established odd-parameter case is complementary and is not being claimed again. Earlier papers on single or odd-part designated partitions do not yield the stated arbitrary even tuple parameter result.

## Limitations
The result concerns only two-adic orders of common divisors, not complete gcds (odd prime factors), termwise valuations, or a distribution in \(n\). It does not re-prove or claim novelty for the odd-parameter theorem. Finite numerical tests do not prove general congruences; the proof instead uses formal-series congruences and the cited exact two-dissection. The interpretation as a complete resolution of Conjecture 6.1 depends on the separately established odd-parameter case.
