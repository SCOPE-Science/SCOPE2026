# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. Every real symmetric five-site kernel with unit sum has an exact quadratic Fourier symbol \(1-ay+by^2\) on \(0\le y\le1\). The multiplier norm therefore equals \(2^\kappa\max y^{\kappa/2}|1-ay+by^2|\). The scalar equation determining \(h\) has a unique root because one branch increases and the other strictly decreases. The equation for \(z>h\) likewise has one root because the quadratic is increasing beyond its last zero. Algebraic differentiation establishes the three alternating weighted contacts \(1/z,h/z,1\). A competing quadratic's difference divided by \(y\) is affine, so its signs at the three contacts force equality and uniqueness. All quantifiers cover every real \(\kappa>0\); positive integers yield the actual discrete derivative operator norms. `CHECK.py` independently evaluates root equations, contact identities, the Fourier coefficient normalization, sampled multiplier bounds and coefficient perturbations, including \(\kappa=5\). Finite checks are supplementary, not proofs of the universal statement.

## Originality
PASS relative to the inspected literature and records, not a claim of worldwide priority. The April 28, 2026 paper by Gaitán, Garzón and Madrid (arXiv:2604.25074v1, full HTML Introduction, Table 2 and relevant result sections) studies this same minimization for general radius, proving unrestricted orders one, two, three and Fourier-positive orders four and six; it identifies unrestricted higher orders as open in its general-radius table. The October 2026 published three-tap result is one-parameter radius one, not two-parameter radius two. The November 2026 fixed-radius high-order result gives asymptotic limiting constants and binomial convergence, but explicitly excludes finite-order exact solutions. Its stronger-in-radius asymptotic claim does not imply this finite-order formula; conversely this result does not imply its all-radius asymptotics. The current finite-order optimal symbol has a negative middle contact, excluding deduction from the positive Fourier-symbol cases. Independent discovery via earlier weighted-Chebyshev treatments remains possible; this risk is disclosed.

## Value
PASS. The support-radius-two class is the first genuinely two-parameter finite-support average, as opposed to the solved one-parameter three-tap model. It resolves exact unrestricted fourth, fifth, sixth and arbitrary higher difference orders at this natural support size with the unique optimizing kernel and exact contact geometry. This is not a cosmetic improvement in the radius-one result or an asymptotic estimate: the proof identifies global finite-order optimizers across the continuous frequency range. It does not settle larger support radii.
