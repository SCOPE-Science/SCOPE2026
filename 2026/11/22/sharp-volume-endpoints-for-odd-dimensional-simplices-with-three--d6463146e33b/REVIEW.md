# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

The proof reduces the centered simplex to a positive matrix with the exact constraints \(\operatorname{tr}A=n\) and \(\operatorname{tr}A^{-1}=1/(nh^2)\), and recovers simplex volume as \(V_{\mathrm{reg}}\sqrt{\det A}\). The constrained spectral problem has global extrema with exactly two eigenvalues; the Hessian test forces a unique exceptional largest eigenvalue at the minimum and a unique exceptional smallest eigenvalue at the maximum. Balanced Fourier sign vectors realize both equality patterns in every odd dimension. The equality characterization follows from equal vertex norms and the zero-sum condition. The analytic argument handles the continuous parameter range; finite rational Gram-determinant checks reproduce eighteen nontrivial instances. No statement about attainment of intermediate volumes is required or made.

## Originality — PASS (priority qualification)

Nour, arXiv:2611.14181v1, sections 2 and 3, derives a covering radius and a two-dimensional Fourier-subspace deformation, not the exact volume endpoints for centered isocentric simplices. Edmonds–Hajja–Martini (2005), especially section 4, establishes related center-coincidence constructions in dimension four without the fixed-radius volume minimax theorem. A published-finding corpus record from 11 October 2026 proves a three-dimensional concentric tetrahedral volume interval, so the new claim explicitly begins at dimension five; an extension is not inferred from wording alone. Searches over volume bounds, reciprocal trace and geometric center coincidences did not identify a dominating higher-dimensional statement. A full read of the three-dimensional published-finding corpus package was unavailable and historical priority remains provisional.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The geometric optimization fixes precisely the two shape radii that control Nour's ball-union obstruction and the centroid condition used by its explicit constructions. The exact two extremal volumes have different degeneracy exponents, \(n-1\) versus \(1\), showing an intrinsic volumetric tradeoff invisible to the covering radius alone. Both extremizers and their rigidity classes are determined at every nonregular radius; the odd-dimension parity condition is essential to sharp attainment. The result is a motivated high-dimensional theorem rather than a numerical example.

## Limits

This theorem requires all three centers to coincide, does not settle even-dimensional sharp endpoints and is not a resolution of a ball-covering conjecture. Independent mathematical priority or proof audit remains outstanding.
