# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The statement covers every integer \(n\ge1\), with the first-column and empty-rectangle initial conditions specified. The proof independently derives the signed domino recurrence, gauges it to a \(\{\pm1\}\)-coefficient continuant, proves that each sign string is represented by exactly \(2^{2n-1}\) Walsh frequencies, and checks the exact parity and six-period sign witnesses. The \(\ell^2\) convolution condition ratio follows from Walsh diagonalization and the stated unnormalized convention. A three-column continuation from a pair \((c,0)\), \(c=\pm1\), yields four distinct extensions and proves the nullity lower bound, with no finite-to-infinite extrapolation. Two executable and independent exact-integer checks confirm all-frequency statements through width six and the normalized state count through width fifteen. Limit: the full resonant nullity has only a recurrence characterization, not a claimed closed form.

## Originality — PASS, bounded comparison
The closest primary sources concern general weighted dimer partition functions (Cohn–Kenyon–Propp, arXiv:math/0008220, especially Sections 1, 6–8), unsigned matching parity and divisibility (Barkley–Liu, arXiv:1911.08102, Sections 1 and 3–5), tiling flips/toric ideals (Gross–Yamzon, arXiv:2008.02896, Sections 2–4), and vertical-domino-sign statistics for adjacency determinants (Aksenov–Kokhas, 2014, Theorems 3.5–3.6). Their one-parameter specialization does not supply arbitrary edge-sign Walsh coefficients. The Fibonacci parity implication for nonsingular widths is classical and is **not** claimed independently as original. What is not entailed by these sources is the combined necessity-and-sufficiency criterion for arbitrary edge-sign Fourier cancellation, exact sharp positive spectral gap in both residue classes, and explicit quantitative nullspace family. Related previous finite-grid work concerns spanning trees or different-height matching grids and does not imply the ladder perfect-matching spectral claim. Residual risk: a classical or unindexed signed-continuant theorem could subsume this elementary all-width spectral calculation. This is not an independent audit or an exhaustive global uniqueness claim.

## Value — PASS
Perfect matchings of an open ladder are a canonical one-dimensional dimer system. The theorem establishes exactly which widths permit full recovery under convolution and, even at singular widths, quantifies sharp \(\ell^2\) conditioning on the recoverable range. The infinite family of explicit null frequencies shows the information loss is not just one accidental cancellation. These are exact, naturally motivated recovery invariants, not a narrower arbitrary enumeration or a restatement of the unsigned Fibonacci count.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
