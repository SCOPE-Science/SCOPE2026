# Review of the weak-cutoff localized-pulse capacity law

## Correctness — PASS

Energy conservation produces the exact mass quadrature for the source's stationary pulse family. A turning-point rescaling gives the uniform-on-compacts limiting capacity curve \(F(r)=r^{3/2}(1-r)/2\). Two global mass lower bounds exclude maximizers escaping toward effective mortalities zero or one. The unique maximum of \(F\) at \(3/5\) gives the claimed coefficient; local ODE limits give the peak and limiting profile. The standalone numerical checker reproduces several scales; it is corroboration, not an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS (literature bounded)

The closest prior source, Kessler–Shnerb (arXiv:2611.16887v1), derives the pulse family, the finite-cutoff existence interval, the mass self-consistency curve, and a numerical fold at a particular cutoff. Its fixed-competition near-Maxwell asymptotics are in a different scaling regime. The new claim gives an all-pulse **maximal competition capacity** coefficient at weak cutoff, proves selection of the maximizing effective mortality, and identifies the sharp cosine/exponential profile. Maruvka–Shnerb (2007) studies finite-range front velocities, not this stationary global capacity. The same mathematical asymptotic might have appeared in less easily indexed cutoff-wave or singular-perturbation literature; full historical priority requires further independent examination.

## Value — PASS

A maximum admissible global interaction strength is a natural ecosystem-capacity invariant: above it a localized persistent trait distribution cannot be stationary. The exact leading coefficient and maximizing mortality distinguish the threshold-scale narrow pulse from the broad branch used to understand fixed weak competition and identify a nontrivial asymptotic bottleneck when resources grow scarcer. The result is not just a reformulation of the source's fixed-parameter height/width scalings.

## Limitations

One-dimensional, deterministic, sharp cutoff, positive even single-peaked pulses, small-cutoff asymptotic. No finite-cutoff maximizing uniqueness or nonlinear dynamic stability is proved. Numerical tests support but do not establish the continuous-parameter theorem. An independent literature and proof audit has not been performed.
