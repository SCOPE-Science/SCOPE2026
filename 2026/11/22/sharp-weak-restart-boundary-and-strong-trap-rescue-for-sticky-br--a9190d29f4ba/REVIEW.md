# Scientific review: sticky Brownian escape under full-state resetting

**Correctness — PASS.** The exact first-passage transform solves the Wentzell and absorption boundary-value equations, and Poisson renewal gives the mean. Two independent derivations of the weak-rate coefficient (Laplace expansion and backward moments) agree. A one-positive-root cubic argument proves the single local sign transition; a quadratic-in-stickiness argument and a scaled polynomial prove its movement and \(1/6\) limit. Direct power-series positivity gives strict all-rate harm for a reset site on the wall, while the strong-stickiness hyperbolic quotient gives fixed-rate rescue for each interior site. `verify.py` passed 118 checks including independent finite-volume convergence. Numerical checks are supplementary, not infinite proofs.

**Originality — PASS, qualified.** Bressloff (2023, arXiv:2302.13986v1) supplies the sticky generator. Scher, Reuveni and Grebenkov (2023, arXiv:2305.08701v1) already establish the relevant unreset slab escape transform; Pal and Prasad (2019) establish conventional resetting ideas. No novelty is claimed for these foundations or the reflected Brownian limit. The new assessed conclusion is the full stickiness-dependent weak-reset sign boundary and its migration, plus the singular strong-trap rescue and exceptional wall-start inequality. The closely matching published partially reactive-interval theorem has a Robin-absorbing endpoint, not this sticky-reflecting Wentzell endpoint; that difference prevents a direct implication. **Mathematical value — PASS.** Weak resetting can be favorable or unfavorable depending on the initial distance from a sticky trap; this boundary changes exactly and strictly with stickiness. At large stickiness the mean first-passage time can be rescued by any fixed positive reset rate from an arbitrarily close but strictly interior site, whereas resetting precisely on the wall always harms. The sharp \(1/6\) launch-position boundary layer makes the singular effect quantitative. The theorem does not assert a global finite-rate benefit threshold or uniqueness of positive optima.

**Closest literature and limitations.** *Escape of a Sticky Particle* (arXiv:2305.08701v1) determines the unreset escape transform and contains the primary overlap risk; this base formula, and the general Poisson renewal identity, were treated as known. The publisher abstract of Song and Wang (2020) was accessible but its full text was not, leaving a lower-confidence priority risk for nonlocal jump reductions. The result is independently unaudited and makes no assertion of publication-priority certification.
