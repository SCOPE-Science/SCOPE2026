# Scientific review: squared weighted \(\ell_1\) proximal stability

## Correctness — PASS
The exact KKT equations give the differentiable cell Jacobian \(J_S=\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf1_S)-\alpha vv^{\mathsf T}/(1+\alpha\|v\|_2^2)\), whose absolute row sums provide the exact global Lipschitz coefficient because every strict active-sign cell is realized. The sorted-prefix exchange calculation handles positive row excesses; the nonexpansive two-coordinate boundary is evaluated separately. The sharp normalized envelope follows from Cauchy–Schwarz, the top eigenvalue of a two-dimensional quadratic form, monotonicity in support size/weight energy, and its strictly positive full-support equality witness. The independent algebraic reproduction checker makes 464 finite checks; computation does not replace the proof. The theorem is restricted to positive weights, positive regularization and finite-dimensional Euclidean proximal maps.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
Lin–Sun–Toh–Yuan (arXiv:1902.00151, Sections 4.1–4.3, Proposition 7) already give the exclusive-lasso proximal map and rank-one generalized Jacobians; Lin–Yuan–Sun–Toh (arXiv:2306.14196, Section 3) extend the algorithmic treatment. Those results are not claimed as new. The novelty sought is the exact maximum-coordinate norm optimization over all supports, sorted-prefix rule, exact normalized-weight anisotropy envelope and equality/infimum classification. The closest checked published-finding corpus claim treats weighted cross-polytope **constraint projection** in an \(\ell_p\) metric, not the squared weighted \(\ell_1\) penalty's resolvent; its normalized Jacobian lacks the finite positive regularization denominator, so neither statement implies the new envelope. Literature and published-finding corpus query absences do not establish exhaustive novelty: a historical treatment of endpoint bounds for positive rank-one perturbations could contain an equivalent result.

## Mathematical value — PASS
For exclusive-lasso regularization, the ratio between Euclidean and coordinatewise perturbation stability is relevant to robustness. The sharp \(\sqrt n\) normalized worst-weight envelope, the unique exceptional-weight maximizers and the dimension-four uniform-weight crossover expose a structural weight-selection instability. The quadratic-time exact coefficient eliminates a priori exponential active-support enumeration, giving a directly evaluable parameter-specific benchmark, not a routine restatement of proximal firm nonexpansivity.
