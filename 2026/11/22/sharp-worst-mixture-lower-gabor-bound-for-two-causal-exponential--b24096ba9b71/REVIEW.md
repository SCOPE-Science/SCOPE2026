# Scientific self-review

## Correctness — PASS

The canonical Zak-frame normalization and the exact geometric sums reduce every fiber to a positive quadratic form in two sampled exponential vectors. Strict finite-dimensional Cauchy–Schwarz gives a positive lower bound; equality is realized by one boundary fiber and a uniquely determined complex coefficient, proving sharpness without relying on a numerical search. Finite Taylor expansion produces the exact coalescence coefficient; Riemann sums give the sharp high-redundancy limit. The standalone independent-series diagnostic passed 950 sampled fiber cases and five edge/coalescence parameter triples. All asserted quantifiers are fixed positive distinct rates, integer redundancy at least two, and arbitrary complex mixing coefficient.

## Originality — PASS, best-knowledge

Belov–Kulikov–Lyubarskii, *Gabor frames for rational functions*, arXiv:2103.08959v1 (Theorem 1.7 and Section 1.7.2), already provides qualitative frame existence for two-pole mixtures after Fourier duality. No novelty is claimed for this or the Zak criterion. Faulhuber–Shafkulovska (arXiv:2204.02917) studies sharp lattice bounds for single-rate one-sided and two-sided exponential windows. A published quantitative signed two-Cauchy note addresses a protected *critical-density* interval, whereas this result optimizes the lower frame bound across *all complex* mixtures for every integer oversampling factor at least two. A published two-sided asymmetric exponential result studies an unrelated piecewise window. Their assumptions and conclusions do not imply the claimed sharp uniform constant, unique worst mixture and weighted-variance collapse. Unlocated historical formulas remain a priority risk.

## Value — PASS

This is an exact, sharp reconstruction-stability guarantee in a natural two-pole signal family: a practitioner may choose any complex interference coefficient while retaining at least the explicit margin. It quantifies how oversampling changes the worst possible squared stability constant from zero at critical density to a positive value, and it identifies a sharp quadratic ill-conditioning law when poles coalesce. The unique destructive ratio and large-density limiting constant are design-relevant invariants, not an arbitrary finite enumeration or a generic renaming of frame existence.

## Limitations and priority risks

The work does not determine optimal upper bounds or individual-ratio lower bounds. It uses unit modulation spacing, integer translation oversampling and exactly two positive decay rates. The proof is self-reviewed, not independently audited; a fully equivalent formula in unlocated specialized literature could affect historical priority.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
