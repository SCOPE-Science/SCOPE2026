# Scientific review: minimax stability of order-sixteen Fourier sampling

**Correctness (PASS).** The prime-power full-spark theorem classifies every admissible sampling set. All 256 admissible sets split into three explicitly checked affine orbits of sizes 64, 128 and 64. The two competing orbit representatives each have an exact negative determinant certificate for the threshold-shifted Gram matrix, whereas the winning representative has exact positive signs for all four leading principal minors at every one of the 1820 possible frequency sets. Hermitian symmetry, exact cyclotomic arithmetic, rational cosine enclosures and Sylvester's criterion establish the claimed strict inequalities. The independent complex-matrix diagonalization is diagnostic and agrees but is not substituted for proof.

**Originality (PASS under bounded historical search).** The closest exact order-sixteen rank census determines whether submatrices are singular, not their worst positive singular values; even a complete rank table cannot imply the strict quantitative comparison. The full-spark criterion of Alexeev–Cahill–Mixon and the universal-sampling classification of Osgood–Siripuram–Wu decide feasibility, not which universally feasible design maximizes the least singular value. The latter's condition-number appendix addresses consecutive sampling rather than this optimization. Searches for cyclic four-frequency maximin stability, the explicit winning row pattern, and related Fourier-minor condition numbers found no stronger covering result. Unindexed independent calculations remain a priority risk.

**Value (PASS).** The problem asks a natural robust-sampling question once universal interpolation is known: which four sample sites protect best against an unknown four-frequency spectrum and measurement noise? The answer is a complete, nonconsecutive optimizer orbit, not another rank-only census. It quantitatively distinguishes the three otherwise equally valid universal sampling designs using a rigorous rational separator. The finite order-sixteen case is a canonical power-of-two test of universality and conditioning; no arbitrary frequency-support restriction is imposed.

**Scientific limits.** The result does not give a symbolic expression for the optimal lower singular value, a general-order theorem, or independent validation. It does not establish publication priority beyond the checked sources. The machine-independent integer checker is part of the evidence and must be run, not inferred from a saved output alone.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
