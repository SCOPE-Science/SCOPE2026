# Scientific review: maximal-clique shattering in Grassmann graphs

**Correctness (PASS).** The established Grassmann star/top maximal-clique theorem fixes the complete concept class. The proof tests all eight three-query labelings and uses the fact that adjacent vertices lie in exactly two maximal cliques, whose intersection is a projective pencil. A nonpencil triangle lies in exactly one maximal clique, excluding all shattered quadruples. Separate projective-point counts establish the coefficients, and a two-positive adaptive branch supplies the depth-four obstruction. The finite-field and rank boundaries and the empty-label witness inequality are stated explicitly. An independent graph maximal-clique enumerator reproduces both complete three-face counts on binary and ternary four-spaces and the binary four-support obstruction. The numerical tests are checks, not substituted for the all-parameter proof.

**Originality (PASS within accessible evidence).** The 2023 Kwiatkowski–Pankov paper supplies the underlying star/top structure but does not analyze shattering of their incidence sets. Shuldiner–Oldford (2022) treats Johnson subset graphs, where the extra projective pencils do not arise. Benediktsson–Macpherson–Adler (2020) concerns graph adjacency neighborhoods. Relevant indexed records on line-graph maximal cliques, flags and Grassmann neighborhoods likewise do not imply the present projective pencil criterion, support enumeration or all-rank theorem. Search coverage cannot rule out an unindexed equivalent.

**Mathematical value (PASS).** This is a complete parameter-uniform classification for a natural family of finite-field incidence set systems, with explicit all-degree counts and both static and adaptive dimensions. It detects the sharp difference from the set-theoretic Johnson analogue: collinear triples in finite-field pencils fail shattering although they are graph triangles. Purity, the exact excluded-face count and the closed Gaussian formula add structural information beyond a VC-dimension bound.

**Residual risks.** Historical priority is not independently certified. The standalone computational tests concern prime fields and selected parameters, while the mathematical proof explicitly treats all prime powers. The named structural star/top theorem is used from the literature rather than reproved from first principles.
