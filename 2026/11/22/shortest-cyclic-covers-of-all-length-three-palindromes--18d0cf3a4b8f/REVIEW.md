# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The lower bound comes from at least three uses of each unordered nonloop edge, two of each loop, and an additional perfect-matching parity expense when the alphabet has even size. The constructive upper bound expands each Euler-tour edge while preserving endpoints and explicitly includes both palindromes for each unordered pair, including all constant triples. These are general combinatorial arguments, not finite extrapolation. The Python witness and an independent integer-flow formulation corroborate the proof for small alphabets.

**Originality — PASS (qualified).** Anisiu, Anisiu and Kása (2006), Section 2 and Proposition 1, discuss storing length-specific palindromes and the exceptional alternating runs of overlapping palindromes, but the inspected full paper does not give the sharp cyclic covering formula here. The general multiplicity superwalk paper of Kapun and Tsarev (2013) covers a more general optimization model without a stated specialized solution. Simpson (2014) maximizes distinct palindromic factors rather than minimizing one cyclic word covering a prescribed target set. Indexed close results address ordinary five-letter palindromes over three letters, Watson–Crick four-letter palindromes, three-letter **nonpalindromes**, and tandem squares, so neither an exact table nor a stronger implication was found. The possible existence of an equivalent earlier specialized formulation remains a priority risk. Some indexed publication full texts were not accessible, so summary-level comparisons are not whole-document exclusions.

**Value — PASS.** Prescribed-motif cyclic shortest covers are natural compact sequence-storage invariants. This theorem determines the first nontrivial all-alphabet ordinary-palindrome case completely and identifies an unavoidable even-alphabet parity penalty with a constructive matching repair. It gives an exact quantitative boundary relevant to later longer-motif covering questions without claiming to solve them.
