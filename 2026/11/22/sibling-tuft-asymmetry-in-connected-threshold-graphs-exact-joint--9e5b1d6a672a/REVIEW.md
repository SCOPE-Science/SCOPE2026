# Scientific review of the threshold-graph sibling–tuft result

**Correctness (PASS).** The unique normalized threshold-graph creation string and its alternating runs yield exact closed-neighborhood twin-class and leaf-tuft identities. The first/internal/last run decomposition gives the rational bounded joint series without overlap; the one-run case excludes the two-vertex boundary explicitly. Fibonacci and dyadic marginals, exact moments, and the asymptotic total-variation separation follow from direct binary-word counts and longest-run estimates. `verify.py` reconstructs the underlying graphs and checks 4094 finite instances independently.

**Originality (PASS, with historical risk).** The central comparison is with Fürnsinn–Gangl–Rubey, arXiv:2505.03665, Theorems 1 and 3 and the chordal-class corollary: full-class and chordal-class symmetry does not imply symmetry conditioned on threshold structure, and in fact the latter is false. Earlier threshold-graph sources characterize creation strings, twin classes and other enumerators, but not this complete bounded sibling/tuft joint distribution and asymptotic singularity. Relevant mathematical-record searches of equivalent descriptions found no covering theorem. Residual risk: a separately indexed or older combinatorial run-enumeration argument could give some marginal cases or even an equivalent full law.

**Value (PASS).** The question is whether a recent striking joint equidistribution survives passage to one of the canonical hereditary chordal subclasses. The answer is quantitatively decisive, not a one-off counterexample: the rational joint law gives every finite coefficient and the marginal laws become asymptotically maximally different. This boundary makes clear that chordality by itself, not its simplest threshold subfamily, supports the published symmetry.

No full classification of hereditary classes preserving the symmetry is claimed. No independent audit was undertaken.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
