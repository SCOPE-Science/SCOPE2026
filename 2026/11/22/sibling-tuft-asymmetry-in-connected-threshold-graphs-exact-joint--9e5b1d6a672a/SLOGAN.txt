The sibling–tuft symmetry valid for all connected chordal graphs fails sharply already on connected threshold graphs: the numbers with no siblings and with no leaf tufts are, respectively, Fibonacci and powers of two. An exact rational generating function counts every bounded joint statistic, and in a uniform unlabeled threshold graph the tuft number stays tight with mean one while the sibling number grows like \(\log_2 n\), making their marginal total-variation distance tend to one.
