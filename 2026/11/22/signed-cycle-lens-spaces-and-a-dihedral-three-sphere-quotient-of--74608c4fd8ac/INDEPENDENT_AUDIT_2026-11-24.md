# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

The supporting-functional model is correct: each facet needs at least n−1 saturated coordinates; all n cannot be saturated because the signs would sum to a nonzero odd integer, and at n−1 the last coordinate constraint forces r of each sign and a zero. The displayed signed vertices are independent on any proper subset of labels up to changing their individual signs, so the facets are simplicial. The radial model is equivariant for label permutations and negation. An even power is a rotation: its invariant subspace in W_n has dimension gcd(n,k)−1 for rotation exponent k, giving the prime/composite distinction. Every odd power is minus a rotation and has zero fixed subspace. The Fourier planes give exactly the stated coprime weights, covering fundamental group and integral lens homology in the stated degrees. For p=5 the inverse of 7 mod 10 is 3, sending (7,9) to (1,7) by changing generator. Freeness rules out any simplex stabilizer (a setwise stabilizer would fix the barycenter); the 30 facets and ten vertices therefore give three tetrahedra and one vertex. Codimension-one faces have two incidences, which descend to the pairings of abstract tetrahedron sides, with self-identifications allowed. In the dihedral model s is complex conjugation on each Fourier plane; both s and gs fix one real line per plane and preserve orientation on W_5. They generate the group; the orbit-space fundamental group is trivial by Armstrong. Point stabilizers are finite rotation groups on the tangent 3-space with spherical link quotients, so the orbit space is a closed topological 3-manifold and Poincaré applies.

## originality

PASS

Pass for the particular signed-cycle classification (sharp primality threshold and the identified prime-family Fourier weights) and its concrete five-label cyclic/dihedral comparison. The Bier sphere and its very polytope realization are prior, and the generic implications from a free linear cyclic action to a lens space and from fixed-point generators in SO(4) to a sphere quotient are not novel. The inspected overlaps do not cover the action-specific calculation, which distinguishes odd and even powers uniformly and also checks the two reflection fixed planes, or the particular geometric three-tetrahedron quotient from this triangulation. This is a comparison of the strongest available results, not a historical priority certificate.

## value

PASS

A canonical rotation-plus-sign symmetry of a natural family has an exact arithmetic boundary for freeness. Identifying every free ordinary orbit space by its concrete weights is more useful than establishing merely that it is a cyclic quotient. The same five-label sphere gives a small geometric lens decomposition and a different, branched dihedral orbit space whose underlying space is S^3. The joint classification of this action is informative; the generic topological theorems are means, not claimed advances.
