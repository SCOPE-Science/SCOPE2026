# Signed-cycle lens spaces and a dihedral three-sphere quotient of balanced Bier spheres

## Finding
For every odd integer \(n=2r+1\ge5\), put
\[
K_n=\{A\subseteq\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z:|A|\le r\},\qquad B_n=K_n*_{\Delta}K_n^{\vee}.
\]
Here the deleted join uses vertices \(i^+,i^-\), forbids both signs of one label in a face, and \(K_n^\vee=K_n\). Let \(R:i^\pm\mapsto(i+1)^\pm\), let \(T:i^\pm\mapsto i^\mp\), and put \(g=TR\), of order \(2n\). **The \(\langle g\rangle\)-action on \(|B_n|\) is free if and only if \(n\) is prime.** For every odd prime \(p\ge5\), its ordinary geometric orbit space is canonically homeomorphic (via an explicitly specified radial and Fourier change of coordinates) to the weighted lens space
\[
|B_p|/\langle g\rangle\ \cong\ L\left(2p;p+2,p+4,\ldots,2p-1\right),
\]
where \(L(m;a_1,\ldots,a_s)\) means the unit sphere \(S^{2s-1}\subset\mathbb C^s\) modulo simultaneous multiplication in the \(j\)-th coordinate by \(\exp(2\pi i a_j/m)\). Thus \(\pi_1\cong\mathbb Z/(2p)\), and the integral homology groups are \(H_0=H_{p-2}=\mathbb Z\), \(H_{2j-1}=\mathbb Z/(2p)\) for \(1\le j\le(p-3)/2\), and zero in all other degrees.

For \(p=5\), this identifies a natural free tenfold quotient with \(L(10;7,9)\cong L(10;1,7)\). It has a **one-vertex, three-tetrahedron face-paired cell decomposition** induced by its original ten-vertex Bier triangulation. If the label reflection \(s:i^\pm\mapsto(-i)^\pm\) is also included, the order-twenty dihedral quotient instead satisfies
\[
|B_5|/\langle g,s\rangle\ \cong\ S^3.
\]
These statements concern the ordinary orbit spaces, not homotopy quotients.

## Assumptions and scope
All complexes are finite. The ground set is the cyclically labeled \(n\)-element set and the two colors are **signed vertices**, not two particles. The full signed-cycle action is generated by simultaneous rotation of the labels and swapping their signs. A dihedral extension is used only in the five-label conclusion; no formula is asserted for higher-dimensional dihedral quotients or for the topology of nonfree composite-order cyclic quotients. We use the standard orientation-independent definition of a weighted lens space given above, avoiding ambiguities of three-manifold lens-space parameter conventions.

## Proof
**1. The geometric sphere model.** Set \(W_n=\{(x_i)\in\mathbb R^n:\sum_i x_i=0\}\) with its Euclidean metric, and let \(v_i=e_i-\frac1n\mathbf1\). The centrally symmetric polytope
\[
P_n=\operatorname{conv}\{v_i,-v_i:i\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\}\subset W_n
\]
is full-dimensional, with the origin in its interior. Its facets are precisely the faces with \(2r\) vertices, in the vertex-set notation \(\{v_i:i\in A\}\cup\{-v_j:j\in B\}\) for disjoint \(A,B\) of size \(r\), leaving exactly one unused label. Indeed a supporting functional on \(W_n\), represented by \(y\in W_n\), achieves its maximum on a positive \(v_i\) when \(y_i=\max_j|y_j|\) and on a negative \(-v_i\) when \(y_i=-\max_j|y_j|\). A facet needs at least \(n-1=2r\) vertices in this supporting hyperplane. The zero-sum relation makes it impossible for all \(n\) entries to equal signs of one nonzero common magnitude (since \(n\) is odd). For \(n-1\) saturated coordinates, the remaining coordinate has magnitude at most the maximum; the zero-sum equation forces exactly \(r\) positive and \(r\) negative saturated coordinates, with the last coordinate zero. Conversely the vector with coordinates \(1\) on \(A\), \(-1\) on \(B\), and \(0\) on the last label supports the specified facet, and its \(2r\) vertices are affinely independent. Thus \(\partial P_n\) has exactly the facets of the balanced Bier sphere \(B_n\). This realizes the combinatorial sphere as a **linear-group-equivariant** polytope boundary. Radial projection \(\partial P_n\to S(W_n)\), \(x\mapsto x/\|x\|\), is an equivariant homeomorphism for every orthogonal symmetry used here.

**2. Freeness and Fourier weights.** Under this identification, the map \(g\) becomes \(-R\), where \(R\) cyclically permutes coordinate positions in \(W_n\). It has order \(2n\) because \(n\) is odd. For a prime \(p\), every nonidentity even power of \(g\) is a nontrivial rotation of prime order on \(W_p\), and has no fixed nonzero vector: its only invariant vectors in \(\mathbb R^p\) are constant, and constants intersect \(W_p\) trivially. An odd power is \(-R^k\); since all eigenvalues of \(R\) have odd order, none equals \(-1\), so it also has no fixed nonzero vector. Conversely, for an odd composite \(n\), take a proper divisor \(d\) with \(1<d<n\). The nonidentity even power \(g^{2d}=R^{2d}\) fixes nonzero vectors constant on the \(d\) rotation cycles with total sum zero, so the action is not free. This proves the sharp primality criterion.

For an odd prime \(p\), real discrete Fourier decomposition splits \(W_p\) orthogonally as \((p-1)/2\) oriented real planes with \(R\)-rotation angles \(2\pi k/p\), \(1\le k\le(p-1)/2\). Identifying each plane with \(\mathbb C\), the generator \(-R\) multiplies its complex coordinate by \(\exp(2\pi i(p+2k)/(2p))\). Each odd weight \(p+2k\) is coprime to \(2p\), making the free unitary action and its stated weighted lens-space quotient **literally conjugate** to the original action after radial projection. Standard free cyclic sphere covering theory yields the integral homology displayed in the finding; the top class is integral because every plane rotation preserves orientation.

**3. The special dihedral quotient.** For \(p=5\), the label reflection \(s\) on \(W_5\) is an involution conjugating \(g\) to \(g^{-1}\), so \(G=\langle g,s\rangle\) is dihedral of order \(20\). Both \(s\) and \(gs\) are orientation-preserving orthogonal involutions of \(W_5\), with two-dimensional fixed linear spaces. Their intersections with \(S(W_5)=S^3\) are nonempty circles. These two involutions generate \(G\), so Armstrong's orbit-space fundamental-group theorem gives \(\pi_1(S^3/G)=1\). Every element of \(G\) has determinant \(+1\) on the four-dimensional \(W_5\); consequently every point stabilizer acts by orientation-preserving linear transformations on the three-dimensional tangent space. The local quotient is homeomorphic to \(\mathbb R^3\): its link is \(S^2/H\), a topological two-sphere for each finite rotation subgroup \(H\le SO(3)\). Therefore \(S^3/G\) is a closed connected orientable topological three-manifold, with no boundary. Since it is simply connected, the three-dimensional Poincaré theorem identifies it homeomorphically with \(S^3\).

## Verification
The standalone `check_bier_lens.py` reconstructs all five-label Bier faces and their tenfold signed-cycle orbits without relying on external precomputed tables. It checks the ordinary quotient's face orbit counts \((1,4,6,3)\), the signed cellular boundary identities, and the exact Smith diagonal \((1,1,1,10)\) for its degree-two boundary. It also independently checks the integral four-dimensional action matrices, determinant \(+1\), the two reflection fixed-plane dimensions, and the dihedral relations. Execute `python check_bier_lens.py` with Python 3 and SymPy. The program confirms the finite five-label calculation; it does not purport to enumerate infinitely many primes or replace the uniform Fourier proof.

## Relationship to prior work
The classical Bier-sphere construction and sphere theorem are in Björner–Paffenholz–Sjöstrand–Ziegler (first arXiv posting 20 November 2003) and Murai (first arXiv posting 5 February 2010). Jevtić–Živaljević (first arXiv posting 2 August 2021, journal publication 2023) proves the balanced case and the convex realization \(P_n\); **this geometric model is prior work**, not claimed here as new. The contribution is the fixed-point/freeness dichotomy, explicit signed cyclic weights, the resulting prime-family lens orbit-space homeomorphisms, the three-tetrahedron quotient model, and the five-label dihedral sphere contrast. Nearby published results on cyclic associahedral and cyclohedral quotients and on cyclic nonmatching-complex sphere quotients concern distinct complexes, actions, and quotient dimensions; their claims do not logically determine this Bier-sphere result.

## Limitations
The proof classifies only the cyclic quotient for prime \(p\ge5\), the existence of fixed points for composite odd \(n\), and the full dihedral quotient for \(p=5\). It does not classify composite-index orbit spaces, higher prime dihedral quotients, equivariant smooth structures, Reidemeister torsion beyond the explicitly named lens weights, or possible independently obtained but unindexed equivalent results. The five-label chain computation is a finite reproducibility check; it is not the proof of the all-prime statement. The application of Poincaré uses a standard established three-manifold theorem.
