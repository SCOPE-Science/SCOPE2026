# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

For every \(n\ge1\) and ranks \(0\le r_i\le n\), the signed-permutation extension argument counts each rank-class element uniformly. Pulling admissible domains back through independent signed permutations proves an exact semigroup-valued intersection coupling, not only an assertion about ranks. The binomial moment formula follows by counting every admissible \(j\)-subset and is inverted by a finite triangular transform. Target-specific fibers follow from uniformity conditional on rank, and the top-class binomial law follows from independent signs of the \(n\) opposite pairs. The zero-time distribution, finite expectation, and lattice-phased asymptotics follow by elementary exact expressions. All boundary cases including empty maps, singleton signed pairs, length one, and heterogeneous ranks are covered. A stand-alone map-composition checker passed all stated finite cases; this supplements rather than replaces the symbolic proof.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

The 2008 original symplectic rook monoid representation paper and the 2014 scholarly survey give the signed-admissible partial-injection structure and order formula, but the inspected portions do not supply the complete rank-conditioned product distribution or the maximal singular-rank absorption law. The 2008 arXiv article develops general Renner monoid representations, not signed-rank convolution; the 2019 congruence classification concerns a different invariant. The closest full-result public comparison, a theorem on rank-uniform products in the *ordinary* symmetric inverse monoid, assumes unpaired arbitrary subsets and yields a different hypergeometric/coupon-collector process. Its statement is not strong enough to imply the signed-transversal formula. In particular, the exact \(2^{-(k-1)}\)-binomial law distinguishes the two structures. No matching published formula was found by the recorded searches, but search absence is not proof of uniqueness. An inaccessible 2008 publisher PDF and potentially older inverse-semigroup probability literature remain priority risks.

## Value — PASS

Classical symplectic Renner monoids are a natural and extensively studied family of finite inverse monoids. The rank-conditioned structure constants determine every prescribed product fiber for any finite sequence of proper singular ranks and form a commutative convolution algebra. The sharp binomial thinning and dyadic logarithmic zero absorption for the maximal proper rank class are structural consequences, not an arbitrary finite slice or repetition of the known order polynomial. They distinguish an involution-constrained monoid from the ordinary rook monoid and provide exact finite-length data usable in random-word and semigroup-product studies.

## Prior sources and scope risks

Li–Li–Cao (2008, 2014) establish the object; the 2006 enumeration paper establishes its unrefined cardinality. Feng–Li (2019) treat congruences, not multiplication probability. The ordinary symmetric inverse-monoid convolution theorem is mathematically closest but not covering by implication. Exact geometric extreme-value laws are standard; novelty is only claimed for the semigroup-valued reduction and its resultant formulas. All assertions exclude nonuniform factors, full-rank units, and parity-restricted even orthogonal rook monoids. Priority cannot be independently certified.
