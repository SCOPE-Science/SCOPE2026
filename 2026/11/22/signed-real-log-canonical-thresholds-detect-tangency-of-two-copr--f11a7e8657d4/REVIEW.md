# Review of the signed cusp-contact theorem

## Correctness
PASS. The local sign regions are explicit and exhaustive. On the negative side, \(y>0\), the substitution \(u=x^p-y^q\) has a two-valued real inverse with total Jacobian comparable to \(y^{-q(p-1)/p}\); the beta integral gives a necessary-and-sufficient exponent including logarithmic divergence at the endpoint. On the positive side, a \(y<0\) cusp wedge gives sharp divergence, while the remaining domain splits into a thin strip controlled by the negative-side exponent and an outside region bounded by the ordinary cusp integral. Newton--Puiseux parametrization verifies both irreducible branch characteristics, and an explicit local ideal computation gives intersection colength \(pr\). Exact rational test cases and low-degree resultants support the algebra without pretending that samples prove the infinite theorem.

## Originality
PASS on available evidence, with publication-priority qualification. Jaudon (arXiv:2611.17004) treats signed invariants but the explicit computational theorems are restricted to Newton-nondegenerate or analytically irreducible germs, whereas this cusp product is neither. Galindo--Hernando--Monserrat (arXiv:1211.6274) treat ordinary complex log canonical thresholds and contact values, not the signed pair. Sergeant-Perthuis--Tsigaridas--Tsukahara (arXiv:2608.20183) present a general algorithm for ordinary two-variable RLCTs. Kosta--Windisch (arXiv:2411.13392) concern hyperplane arrangements. Accessible indexed research comparisons did not identify the signed contact formula. General resolution methods apply abstractly, and uninspected or unindexed specialist literature remains a genuine priority risk.

## Value
PASS. This is a nontrivial, infinite, geometrically natural contact family of two cusps. The two branches each retain the fixed Puiseux pair \((p,q)\), while the signed negative threshold strictly records their variable intersection multiplicity \(pr\); the positive threshold does not change. The result demonstrates that the pair of signed integrability invariants detects genuinely different local real geometry, beyond the separate singularity types of the branches, and supplies an explicit closed formula outside the initiating paper's two computational classes.

## Limitations
The argument covers the specified parity and coprimality class only. Neither global thresholds nor all reducible plane germs are classified. Exact published priority outside the inspected sources remains unverified; no independent mathematical audit has been performed.
