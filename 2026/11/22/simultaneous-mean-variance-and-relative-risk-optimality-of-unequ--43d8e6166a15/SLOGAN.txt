For two independent equal-speed run-and-tumble walkers searching for their first encounter on a ring, fixing the sum of their reversal rates produces a strict three-way ordering: the mean, absolute variance and squared coefficient of variation of contact time all increase with the product of those rates. Concentrating every reversal in a single walker simultaneously minimizes all three statistics, whereas splitting reversals equally uniquely maximizes them; exact finite-parameter moment formulas establish both claims.
