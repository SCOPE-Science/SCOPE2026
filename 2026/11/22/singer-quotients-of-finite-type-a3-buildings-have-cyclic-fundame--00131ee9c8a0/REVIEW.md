# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The claim fixes the finite-field action, the geometric rather than homotopy-orbit quotient, and all quantifiers. The nontrivial simplex isotropy is exactly the projective quadratic-subfield subgroup \(H\) of order \(q+1\): invariance of a two-dimensional \(\mathbb F_q\)-subspace by a nonscalar multiplier forces the multiplier's minimal degree to be two. One spread orbit and \(q\) free line orbits give the claimed zero-cells. Edges and triangles have point or hyperplane vertices with trivial stabilizer, giving the two higher cell counts. The finite building is simply connected by Solomon–Tits; Armstrong's theorem gives \(\pi_1\cong G/H\), while cellular Euler characteristic and the freeness of the second cycle subgroup determine the complete integral homology. Crucially no coprimality of \(q+1\) and \(q^2+1\) is assumed. Independent exact chain reductions at \(q=2,3\) agree.

## Originality — PASS relative to inspected work

Drudge's full article determines Singer orbits and flat stabilizers but does not determine orbit-space fundamental groups or integral homology. Giuzzi–Sonnino likewise study line spreads for codes. Armstrong and Solomon–Tits supply broad prerequisite theorems but do not give the claim's flag-orbit enumeration. A previously documented special case at \(q=2\) is subsumed, so the new claimed contribution is the uniform all-prime-power calculation, including odd fields and growing torsion. Searches for geometric flag-complex quotients, cyclic Singer building quotients, and equivalent homological invariants found no full dominating statement. An unindexed equivalent synthesis or a less obviously named quotient theorem remains a publication-priority risk; absence from indexed searches is not proof of absolute novelty.

## Value — PASS

A canonical nonfree symmetry action on every finite type-A3 building yields a complete natural family of orbit-space fundamental groups and integral homology groups, with an explicit structural explanation in terms of the unique quadratic-subfield spread. The result treats every prime power and reveals order \(q^2+1\) torsion in degree one despite a simply connected, torsion-free covering building. This is a motivated full invariant classification, not an arbitrary numerical slice. No assertion is made about higher homotopy data.

## Limits

The integer checker independently verifies the two smallest prime-field instances, not the infinite family; the general proof is mathematical. Cohomology operations and the full homotopy type are undetermined. Possible equivalent unindexed results remain a research-priority risk.
