Deleting any single edge of the two-subset Kneser graph \(\operatorname{KG}(n,2)\), for every \(n\ge5\), creates exactly \(24(n-4)\) new dissociation-complex faces, but all disappear through \(12(n-4)\) elementary simplicial collapses. The original complex is therefore a simple-homotopy retract of its one-edge-fault version, and every integral homology class survives unchanged.
