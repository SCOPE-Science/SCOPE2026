# Single-rung faults create parity-selective spheres in ladder perfect-matching complexes

## Finding
Write \(L_n=P_n\square K_2\) for the straight two-row ladder on \(n\ge2\) columns, with vertical rung \(e_j\) at column \(j\), \(1\le j\le n\). Let \(\mathcal P(G)\) denote the simplicial **perfect-matching complex**: a face is a set of graph edges extendible to a perfect matching of \(G\). Then, for **every** \(n\ge2\) and **every** rung \(e_j\),
\[
\boxed{\quad
|\mathcal P(L_n-e_j)|\simeq
\begin{cases}
S^{(n-3)/2},&n\text{ odd and }2\le j\le n-1\text{ even},\\
\{*\},&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}\quad}
\]
For interior rungs \(2\le j\le n-1\), the more precise structural statement is a union of two simplicial cones \(A_j,B_j\) with the **exact** simplicial intersection
\[
A_j\cap B_j\cong \mathcal P(L_{j-2})*\mathcal P(L_{n-j-1}),
\]
where an empty ladder \(L_0\) has the augmented complex \(\{\varnothing\}\), which is the identity for simplicial joins. For \(n=3,j=2\), the intersection has empty geometric realization and the union has two connected components; all other interior cases have the usual suspension homotopy type of this intersection.

Deleting an interior rung of a square-ladder graph merges the two adjacent square faces into a hexagon. Thus a single hexagonal defect produces a sphere of arbitrarily large dimension precisely at even-numbered interior positions in odd-width ladders. In contrast, the intact ladder complex is contractible when \(n\) is odd, while it is \(S^{(n-2)/2}\) when \(n\) is even, as previously established in [1].

## Assumptions and scope
All graphs are finite, simple, undirected, with unit edges. The path \(P_n\) has exactly \(n\) vertices; its product \(P_n\square K_2\) has \(2n\) graph vertices. Columns are indexed \(1,\ldots,n\). A *rung* joins the two row vertices in one column, and a *horizontal pair* comprises both row edges between consecutive columns. Every face of \(\mathcal P(G)\) must extend to at least one perfect matching of the **modified graph**, not merely of the intact ladder. We use geometric realizations and ordinary homotopy equivalence, not simple homotopy equivalence or equivariant equivalence. The integer sphere dimensions and integral homology are consequences of homotopy equivalence, not finite-field extrapolation.

The empty ladder \(L_0\) is used only as an augmented combinatorial join identity. A graph with one column \(L_1\) has one rung and a one-vertex, contractible perfect-matching complex. The deleted-rung theorem starts at \(n=2\); the empty-ladder convention does not introduce any false geometric suspension at \(n=3\).

## Proof
**1. Perfect matchings are tilings.** For a cut between columns \(t\) and \(t+1\), the left side has \(2t\) graph vertices. Any perfect matching crosses this cut an even number of times. There are exactly two horizontal graph edges crossing it, so a perfect matching uses both or neither. Consequently every perfect matching of the intact or rung-deleted ladder consists exactly of disjoint *tiles*: a vertical rung covering one column or both horizontal edges covering two adjacent columns. Conversely, any tiling by such allowed tiles gives a perfect matching. This parity argument includes the missing-rung case.

**2. Endpoint fault.** If \(j=1\), column one cannot use a vertical rung; hence every perfect matching contains both horizontals between columns one and two. Therefore \(\mathcal P(L_n-e_1)\) is a simplicial cone (indeed, the simplex on the forced horizontal pair joined with \(\mathcal P(L_{n-2})\)). The same holds for \(j=n\) by reversal. This includes \(n=2\).

**3. Two-cone decomposition for interior faults.** Suppose \(2\le j\le n-1\). With the vertical rung at \(j\) absent, every tiling covers column \(j\) either with the paired horizontal edges between \(j-1,j\) (family \(A_j\)) or with those between \(j,j+1\) (family \(B_j\)). Let these denote the simplicial subcomplexes generated by the corresponding perfect matchings. Both are nonempty and each is a cone: every generating facet of \(A_j\), respectively \(B_j\), contains the same two fixed horizontal edges. All perfect matchings occur in exactly one of these families, so
\[
\mathcal P(L_n-e_j)=A_j\cup B_j.
\]
A face common to both families cannot contain any graph edge incident to columns \(j-1,j,j+1\). Indeed, every generating facet of \(A_j\) covers columns \(j-1,j\) by its forced horizontal pair, whereas every generating facet of \(B_j\) covers columns \(j,j+1\) by the other horizontal pair. Those two pairs are different and incompatible. Hence every common face lies entirely inside the disjoint subladders on columns \(1,\ldots,j-2\) and \(j+2,\ldots,n\). Its restrictions to these regions extend to perfect matchings of their subladders: for each horizontal edge present, its row-parallel partner occurs in any full tiling extending the face; the resulting disjoint tiles can be completed using vertical rungs on all remaining columns. Conversely, independently chosen extendible faces in these two subladders can be completed to a member of \(A_j\) by adding the horizontal pair \((j-1,j)\) and the rung at \(j+1\), and to a member of \(B_j\) by adding the rung at \(j-1\) and the horizontal pair \((j,j+1)\). Thus the asserted simplicial equality, with exactly the same face sets, holds:
\[
A_j\cap B_j=\mathcal P(L_{j-2})*\mathcal P(L_{n-j-1}).
\]
The geometric union of two nonempty contractible subcomplexes with nonempty intersection is a homotopy pushout, hence homotopy equivalent to the unreduced suspension of their intersection. If both shorter ladders have length zero (precisely \(n=3,j=2\)), \(A_j,B_j\) are disjoint simplices, and their union is homotopy equivalent to \(S^0\) directly.

**4. Apply the intact-ladder classification.** By [1, Theorem 3.3], \(\mathcal P(L_m)\) is contractible when \(m\ge1\) is odd and homotopy equivalent to \(S^{(m-2)/2}\) for positive even \(m\). The combinatorial factor for \(m=0\) is the augmented join identity, treated by omitting it from the join. For \(n\ge4\), if \(n\) is odd and \(j\) even, the two lengths \(j-2\) and \(n-j-1\) are even, possibly zero, and their join is a sphere of dimension \((n-5)/2\). Suspending gives \(S^{(n-3)/2}\). In every other interior case at least one of these lengths is positive odd; that shorter-ladder complex is contractible, so its join and the two-cone union are contractible. The direct \(n=3,j=2\) case has already been handled. Combined with step 2, this proves the classification for all allowable indices.

## Verification
The standalone standard-library script `verify_rung_fault.py` constructs perfect matchings by graph-vertex backtracking, independently of the tile argument. For every rung of every ladder with \(2\le n\le9\), it generates all extendible faces, checks the two-cone cover, compares the **entire face set** of the intersection with the join of the two shorter perfect-matching complexes, and calculates reduced mod-two Betti numbers by explicit binary boundary elimination. All 44 deletion cases pass. This finite exhaustive verification checks the structural lemma within the stated range; the unrestricted result rests on the cut-parity and exact-intersection proof above.

## Relationship to prior work
Chandrakar and Singh [1, Theorem 3.3, proof and appendix] classify the perfect-matching complexes of **intact** straight ladders, including the parity-dependent contractibility and sphere result. Their inspected treatment contains no single-rung-deleted ladder theorem. This intact classification is used, not claimed as new. The full inspected result [2] classifies a one-rung defect in **odd circular** ladders; its two-cone method is closely related, but its circular graph and its single shorter-ladder intersection do not include an interior fault of a straight ladder, whose intersection here is the **join of two independently variable shorter ladders**. The linear-ladder symmetry calculations in [3] concern the intact complex and do not imply the local-defect classification. The 2023 polygonal line-tiling work [4] concerns ordinary matching complexes, not the perfect-matching extendibility subcomplex. The new statement gives the complete position-and-width dependence for a single missing vertical rung in the linear ladder.

## Limitations
Only a single missing **vertical** rung is classified. Horizontal-edge deletion, simultaneous faults, modified graph metrics, natural homology-map normalizations, equivariance and simplicial collapsibility of the resulting spaces are not asserted. The full proof uses the prior intact-ladder homotopy classification; the independent finite tests are corroboration, not an all-order argument. The literature comparison does not establish global publication priority, and equivalent unindexed work remains possible.
