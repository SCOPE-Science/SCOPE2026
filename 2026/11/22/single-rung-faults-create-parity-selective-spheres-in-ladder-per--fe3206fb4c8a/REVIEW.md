# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The graph-cut parity argument proves that every perfect matching consists of disjoint one-column rung tiles and two-column horizontal-pair tiles. For an interior missing rung, the two possible tile coverings of that column generate contractible subcomplexes whose common faces are **exactly** the join of the perfect-matching complexes of the left and right subladders, not merely a subcomplex of that join. The CW homotopy-pushout lemma gives a suspension, with the unique empty-intersection case treated separately. Applying the previously established complete intact-ladder homotopy classification yields the claimed parity pattern for all widths and positions. Independently, finite graph-edge-backtracking enumeration and mod-two boundary calculations agree for every rung at widths two through nine (44 cases). The proof, rather than the finite tests, establishes the universal quantifiers.

## Originality — PASS against checked evidence
The closest directly pertinent published source is Chandrakar and Singh, arXiv:2407.05809, Theorem 3.3; its complete available result, proof and appendix were inspected. It classifies the intact two-row ladder and not the interior single-rung defect. An inspected published result on **odd circular** ladders with one missing rung uses a related two-cone method, but its exact intersection is one straight ladder, not the position-dependent join of two shorter straight ladders. Other inspected work gives symmetry characters of the intact straight ladder, not this fault response. General polygonal-line-tiling results on ordinary matching complexes involve a different simplicial complex. Database and web searches did not identify an equivalent or dominating defect theorem. Residual risk: unindexed or inaccessible equivalent work is possible, and bibliographic absence is not a proof of global originality.

## Value — PASS
A missing interior rung merges two square faces into a single hexagonal defect, a natural perturbation of a standard graph-family model. The complete classification establishes a sharp joint dependence on the parity of ladder width and the position of the defect. In the odd-width regime, deletion creates an integral sphere of unbounded dimension from a previously contractible complex; in the even-width regime, every rung deletion makes a previously spherical complex contractible. The exact join formula explains the phenomenon structurally and is useful for subsequent position-dependent boundary-fault questions.

## Limits
Single vertical-rung faults only; no claims about horizontal faults, multiple defects, equivariant homotopy or induced homology-map normalizations. The intact-ladder classification is credited. Finite computation is independent corroboration, not a substitute for the all-order proof.
