# Scientific review: single-start semi-Markov tomography

## Correctness — PASS
The full claim has a direct reconstruction proof under explicit no-self-jump, known-background, fresh-entry conditions. For every positive Laplace parameter, strict row discounting makes both renewal inverses nonsingular and known holding-survival diagonals strictly positive. Partitioning the renewal resolvent yields \(y=qB/(1-qh)\) and its algebraic inverse \(q=yB^{-1}/(1+yB^{-1}h)\); discounted hitting probabilities ensure positive denominators. The invertible homogeneous matrix proves preservation of every affine dimension, and uniqueness of finite-measure Laplace transforms proves identification of entire joint laws. Poisson age mixing proves the stationary reset-scan equivalence. A finite linear dependence among mass/Laplace constraints produces different positive holding laws indistinguishable at any finite prescribed set of rates. The independent numerical implementation passed 210 finite checks, which corroborate but do not replace the proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual publication-priority risk
The closest source, Voits and Schwarz (2026, arXiv:2611.06490v1), proves the sufficient local-affine condition for global occupation mutual linearity and contains the underlying forward memory-kernel machinery; its full main text and supplement were inspected. It does not state a complete inverse of the unknown outgoing joint-law row from one fresh-start occupation vector, the necessity of affine local variation for full-vector affine response, preservation of arbitrary affine dimension, or exact all-reset-rate-versus-finite-rate identification. Voits (2026, arXiv:2610.13505v1) and Bradley–Dingle–Knottenbelt (2003) give forward transient formulas and graph/aggregation techniques, not this inverse theorem. Related identified database items concern one-clock position statistics in a switching diffusion or curvature in a particular resetting semi-Markov network, not this complete kernel tomography. The forward resolvent is established classical mathematics, making an implicit equivalent in older inverse-renewal literature a genuine risk; no source checked provides a stronger theorem that directly covers the complete stated claim.

## Value — PASS
The complete full-vector converse answers a natural gap left by the sufficient-response theorem: what the complete observable response actually reveals about locally changed, nonexponential, destination-dependent waiting laws. Exact reconstruction from one starting state's marginal occupations, or experimentally from all Poisson reset rates without observing individual transitions, is a distinctive nonparametric information-completeness result. The impossibility of identification from any prescribed finite reset-rate scan supplies a sharp observational boundary. These are motivated structural facts, rather than an arbitrary parameter slice or a numerical recomputation. The theorem does not promise stable inference from a finite noisy experiment, simultaneous reconstruction of multiple unknown rows, or recovery in the presence of hidden self-jumps.

## Closest literature and limitations
Primary: Voits and Schwarz, arXiv:2611.06490v1 (original affine condition and resetting interpretation). Other: Voits, arXiv:2610.13505v1 (forward graph-theoretic representation); Bradley, Dingle and Knottenbelt (2003, forward aggregation); van der Meer, Ertel and Seifert (2022, inference from observed transitions and waiting times). A published published-finding corpus record on a semi-Markov harmonic trap reconstructs a residence-time clock from a position distribution, not a vector of network occupation probabilities. Full proof and checker are supplied, but not an external replication or an independent priority review.
