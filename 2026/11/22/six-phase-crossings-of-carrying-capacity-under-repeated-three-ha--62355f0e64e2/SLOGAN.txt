A fixed three-habitat Beverton–Holt dispersal network can switch between beneficial and detrimental effects **infinitely often** as the integer number of migration rounds increases, even while its equilibrium total converges to the sum of isolated carrying capacities. The sharp six-class expansion is \(T_k=3+2^{-k}h_{k\bmod6}/33+O(4^{-k})\) with \(h=(4,-1,-5,-4,1,5)\); two-habitat analytic models cannot have this six-phase asymptotic pattern.
