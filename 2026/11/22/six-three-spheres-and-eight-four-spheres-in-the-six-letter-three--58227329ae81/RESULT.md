# Six three-spheres and eight four-spheres in the six-letter three-cycle permutation complex

## Finding

Let \(S\subseteq \mathfrak S_6\) be the conjugacy class consisting of all \(40\) permutations with cycle type \((3,1,1,1)\). Regard each permutation as a full injective word on \(\{0,1,2,3,4,5\}\). If \(X(S)\) is the ordered-word permutation complex generated by the subsequences of these words, then

\[
|X(S)|\simeq \bigvee^{6}S^3\ \vee\ \bigvee^{8}S^4.
\]

Thus \(X(S)\) is two-connected, \(\widetilde H_3(X(S);\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^6\), and \(\widetilde H_4(X(S);\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^8\), with all other reduced integral homology zero. Although the complex is pure of dimension five, it is not shellable, because its reduced homology is nonzero below the top dimension.

## Assumptions and scope

The notation \(\mathfrak S_6\) means the permutations of the alphabet \(\{0,1,2,3,4,5\}\). A \(k\)-cell is a *distinct ordered word* \((a_0,\ldots,a_k)\) obtained by deleting zero or more positions from at least one one-line representation of an element of \(S\). Different orders of the same vertex set are different cells. An oriented word has boundary

\[
\partial(a_0,\ldots,a_k)=\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^j(a_0,\ldots,\widehat a_j,\ldots,a_k).
\]

This is the regular ordered semisimplicial realization used in the modern permutation-complex definition. The barycentric subdivision agrees with the injective-word order-complex definition. The theorem concerns the complete three-cycle conjugacy class in exactly six letters; it makes no claim for arbitrary subsets or other alphabet sizes.

## Proof

**Exhaustive face set.** Enumerate all \(720\) one-line words on the alphabet, retain precisely those whose cycle decomposition has block lengths \((1,1,1,3)\), and collect all distinct nonempty ordered subsequences. There are \(40\) facets. In degrees \(0\) through \(5\), the numbers of cells are respectively

\[
(f_0,f_1,f_2,f_3,f_4,f_5)=(6,30,120,247,194,40).
\]

Index each degree separately in lexicographic word order, concatenating the degree lists. The explicit file `morse_pairs.json` lists \(311\) ordered pairs \((u,v)\) of these zero-based global cell indices, each satisfying \(u\subset v\) with dimension difference one. The standalone `checker.py` independently reconstructs the generators, cells, incidences, and exact indexing: the certificate contains no precomputed boundary matrices.

**Acyclic integral matching.** Every pair in the certificate is an elementary unit-incidence match: its coefficient in the oriented boundary is \(+1\) or \(-1\). No cell participates in two pairs. Orient unmatched incidences from high to low and reverse each matched incidence; a complete topological sort visits all \(637\) cells. Hence the certificate is an acyclic discrete Morse matching in the sense of Forman. Its unmatched cells consist of exactly one vertex, six 3-cells and eight 4-cells, with no other critical cells.

**The residual integral differential vanishes.** For an unmatched 3-cell \(w\), define its reduction vector to be the corresponding coordinate basis vector if \(w\) is critical. For a matched 3-cell \(w\) paired with a 4-cell \(v\), recursively set

\[
R(w)=-\sum_{z\in\partial v,\ z\ne w}
\frac{\epsilon(v,z)}{\epsilon(v,w)}R(z),
\]

where \(\epsilon(v,z)\in\{+1,-1\}\) is the signed cellular incidence. The recursion terminates because the incidence matching has no directed cycles. The reduced boundary of each critical 4-cell \(c\) is \(\sum_{w\in\partial c}\epsilon(c,w)R(w)\). The checker evaluates all \(8\times6=48\) integer coefficients exactly and finds every coefficient to be zero. In particular, this is an *integral* calculation, not a field-rank estimate. The checker separately confirms every cellular boundary-square identity.

**Homotopy conclusion.** A finite acyclic discrete Morse matching yields a homotopy-equivalent CW complex with the listed critical cells. Its 3-skeleton is a wedge of six copies of \(S^3\). The degree-four cellular differential is zero, so every 4-cell attaching map has zero Hurewicz image in \(H_3\). Because this 3-skeleton is 2-connected, the Hurewicz map \(\pi_3\to H_3\) is an isomorphism; thus each attaching map is nullhomotopic. The resulting CW complex is precisely the stated wedge of six 3-spheres and eight 4-spheres, up to homotopy equivalence.

The checker regenerates all forty three-cycles, all \(637\) word cells, the complete signed cellular incidence relation, and the Morse Hasse graph. It verifies the \(311\) disjoint matching pairs, absence of directed cycles, critical-degree counts, and all forty-eight critical boundary coefficients. ## Relationship to prior work

Chachólski, Levi and Meshulam (2019 preprint; 2020 published work) introduced the general injective-word permutation complexes, proved general homotopy realization results and classified complexes generated by two permutations. Their two-generator classification does not evaluate the forty-generator conjugacy-class complex here. Govc's November 2026 theorem establishes that all permutation complexes have free fundamental group; it does not determine the higher-dimensional integral homotopy type of this three-cycle example. Other closely related indexed results concern full alternating-group or projective-special-linear permutation sets with different generating facets, and do not imply the present face-by-face conclusion. A broad priority search cannot exclude independently discovered or unindexed equivalent computations.

## Limitations

The theorem is an exact finite calculation for the complete 3-cycle conjugacy class in \(\mathfrak S_6\). It does not supply a uniform formula in the alphabet size or a symmetry representation. The finite matching and signed-coefficient computation are exhaustive only because the complete finite cell set is reconstructed and checked; they are not evidence for untested infinite families.
