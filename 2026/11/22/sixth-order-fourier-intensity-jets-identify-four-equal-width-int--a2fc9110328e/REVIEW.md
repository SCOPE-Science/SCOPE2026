# Review of the sixth-order four-interval intensity-jet theorem

**Correctness (PASS).** The proof uses the exact common-width Fourier factor and six pairwise distances. The formulas for their second, fourth and sixth even-power sums, the cubic elimination and its derivative have been symbolically verified by the attached checker. The geometric inequality \(W<(\sqrt U-\sqrt V)^2\) forces all admissible cubic roots onto the unique increasing branch; it applies at symmetric \(m=0\) as well. Exact rational configurations demonstrate that the fourth-order jet is not injective, completing sharpness. No finite numerical sampling is used as an infinite proof.

**Originality (PASS relative to evidence inspected).** Queries comparing finite Fourier-intensity jets, power sums of four point differences, homometry, four equal-width intervals, and sixth-order reconstruction found no equivalent four-interval claim. The full published-finding corpus papers on three equal-width intervals and equal-width homometry were read; the former has fewer geometric variables and the latter requires the complete intensity function, so neither logically yields the three-moment injection theorem. The recent Xia–Xu preprint was identified by its first submitted arXiv date and its abstract inspected, but its full text was inaccessible and could contain related material. Possible unindexed classical moment-reconstruction results are additional residual risks. Search noncoverage alone is not an assertion of priority.

**Mathematical value (PASS).** It proves a new sharp minimal finite-order recoverability boundary in a natural, infinite-parameter family of binary support objects. The key nontrivial step is showing that a cubic algebraic inverse has only one physically realizable branch, not merely identifying candidate solutions. The exact failure at lower derivative order and fully explicit reconstruction distinguish the result from a routine application of full-intensity turnpike uniqueness. Neither noise stability nor broader classes is claimed.
