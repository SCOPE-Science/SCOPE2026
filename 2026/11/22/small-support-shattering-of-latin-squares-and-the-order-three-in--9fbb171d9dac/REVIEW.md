# Scientific review

## Correctness

PASS. The result distinguishes pairwise partial-Latin compatibility from universal completion, includes both positive and negative trace constraints, and invokes the established Smetaniuk completion theorem only in its \(k\le n-1\) range. The order-three proof independently determines the 12 square concepts, exact pair/triple trace structure, complete face polynomial and maximal faces; its homology calculation is finite exact binary linear algebra with rank 133. Two separate reconstructors agree, and the order-four three-query census checks the general proof's first nontrivial extension. The full-\(n\) polynomial and integral homology are not proved.

## Originality

PASS within inspected evidence. Hatami–Qian's 2016 paper explicitly studies the identical Latin-square concept family and establishes asymptotic VC/teaching bounds, but its inspected theorem statements and proofs do not imply the exact order-three classification and homology. Luria–Simkin's 2017 Latin-box paper studies random containment thresholds, not Boolean traces. The Smetaniuk theorem supplies the completion input; it is established prior mathematics, not a new contribution. Indexed studies of partial-Latin-square complexes concern all partial placements, rather than the shattered-support complex generated by *complete* squares. Residual priority risk is an unindexed exact finite computation of the same 27-point incidence family or its homology.

## Value

PASS. This is a natural first nontrivial exact test case for the Latin-square concept class already used in learning-theoretic research: it records precisely where local Latin compatibility ceases to suffice for shattering, provides the sharp universal low-support threshold, and identifies nonpurity and two nontrivial reduced homology groups that neither the scalar VC dimension nor the number of squares reveals. The finite Betti calculation augments the structural obstruction instead of being offered as a disconnected numerical census.

## Closest literature and limitations

- Hatami–Qian (2016), arXiv:1606.00032: same hypothesis family, asymptotic VC bounds; no matched exact finite face/homology claim in inspected sections.
- Luria–Simkin (2017), arXiv:1711.09741: Latin-box geometry and random support thresholds; not exact Boolean shattering.
- Smetaniuk's completion theorem (1981): used for the sharp general small-support statement; prior result fully credited.
- Benediktsson–Macpherson–Adler (2020), arXiv:2007.16042: VC analysis of graph neighborhoods, not Latin squares.
- Indexed partial-Latin-square and three-sorted-incidence classifications concern different complexes or logical invariants; only their summaries were available for comparison.

The binary homology statement is a reproducible finite computation, not a homotopy classification. Historical novelty has not been independently certified.
