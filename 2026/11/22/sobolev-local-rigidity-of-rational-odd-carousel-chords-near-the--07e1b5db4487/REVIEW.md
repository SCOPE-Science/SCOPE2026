# Review of the odd-denominator local chord rigidity result

## Correctness: PASS

For a unit-speed closed curve, expressing the tangent as \(e^{i(t+\psi(t))}\) gives the exact analytic chord map. The differentiated squared-chord map has the explicit multiplier (1) in `RESULT.md`. Odd denominators prevent cosine zeros, while a cyclotomic-field norm rules out every remaining integer harmonic resonance at frequencies at least two; the result is a two-sided multiplier bound of order minus one. The closure map independently eliminates first harmonics by the implicit-function theorem. The resulting map between the high-mode Sobolev spaces is analytically invertible, proving both rigidity and quantitative stability. A family of harmonics indexed by multiples of the denominator proves the loss of one derivative is necessary. Standalone direct-integral finite differences test the explicit formula but do not stand in for the analytic steps.

## Originality: PASS, provisional

The motivating full paper of Asipchuk–Kosmakov proves global uniqueness only at \(1/5\) and \(2/5\); the 2004 Bracho–Montejano–Oliveros treatment proves other low-denominator global facts; these are excluded from the theorem's scope. The 2018 Bor–Levi–Perline–Tabachnikov Zindler family is governed by a tangent-resonance equation but does not address the Sobolev nonlinear inverse for odd rational separations. The 2026 \(1/6\) result is at even denominator; 2026 fractal Zindler construction is at half-perimeter. The nearest mathematical-record results concern other chord functionals (isoptic chords, tangent chords and tomography), without the present closure-constrained multiplier. This is a best-knowledge novelty comparison, not a conclusive historical priority certification; unindexed local rigidity studies are a remaining risk.

## Value: PASS

The question of whether constant perimetral chords force circularity arises directly in the planar floating-body literature, where global classification at denominator seven and higher remains difficult. A uniform result across all odd denominators \(q\ge7\), including every coprime numerator, establishes rigorous local rigidity without pretending to solve the open global problem. The quantified, sharp one-derivative stability identifies the exact regularity cost of recovering near-circle geometry from measured chord lengths and gives a natural baseline for perturbative questions.

## Limitations

Only sufficiently small mean-normalized tangent-angle perturbations in \(H^s\), \(s>3/2\), around positively oriented perimeter-\(2\pi\) circles are covered. The result has denominator-dependent constants; it is not a global theorem and says nothing about irrational or even-denominator separations.
