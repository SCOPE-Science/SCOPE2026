# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The matrix is a positive trace-class Gram operator with norm at most \(\pi\). Uniform Hilbert–Schmidt comparison to the continuously power-tapered Carleman operator makes all fixed trace-power errors \(o(1/\varepsilon)\). The convolution representation gives exact moment limits by a positive dominated-convergence argument. Compact moment determinacy and absence of positive limiting atoms then give fixed-threshold eigenvalue counting, including the exact profile and its separately taken edge limits. A finite checker supports elementary constants but is not used as an infinite-dimensional proof.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)

The closest spectral sources are Otte's finite Hilbert-section Szegő theorem (arXiv:2207.11029) and Fedele's general hard/scale regularization spectral-density theorem (DOI:10.1007/s00020-019-2556-9). Both use the same local Carleman symbol, but neither inspected result states the weak-power, continuously logarithmic taper \((mn)^{-\varepsilon/2}\) or its integrated three-halves edge law; Fedele's regularization rescales \(n/N\), not \(\varepsilon\log n\). A 2013 work whose title invokes generalized weighted Hilbert matrices studies a different antisymmetric denominator. published-finding corpus searches on power-weighted Hilbert matrices, soft-cutoff Carleman phase space and exact spectral profiles found adjacent Hilbert and Hankel claims, not the implication. A stronger abstract weighted Wiener–Hopf semiclassical theorem may still imply this special case; the latest Guo–Tang full text was inaccessible, so originality is conditional on the inspected primary material.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The first-order counting law solves a natural noncompact-to-trace-class spectral crossover rather than merely calculating an operator norm. It distinguishes *hard* section truncations from *soft logarithmic* power regularization, and gives the exact universal three-halves spectral-edge exponent and a quantitative counting profile useful as a benchmark for tapered Hilbert/Hankel models. This profile is not a trivial fixed-index or finite-table invariant.

## Limits

No fixed-\(\varepsilon\) complete eigenvalue asymptotic, uniform threshold-rate, or joint edge scaling is established. The Fourier diagonalization is classical; the new contribution is the specific slowly varying taper and its counting law.
