# Sparse vertex-erasure stability in scale-two toroidal grid Rips complexes

## Finding
Let \(m,n\ge7\) be integers, let \(V=\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\) have the shortest-path metric of \(C_m\square C_n\), and let \(K=\operatorname{VR}_{\le2}(V)\) be the inclusive Vietoris–Rips complex. Let \(S\subseteq V\) be any set of pairwise graph distance at least three. Then **the original complex \(K\) simplicially collapses onto the induced subcomplex \(K[V\setminus S]\)** using exactly \(36|S|\) codimension-one elementary collapse pairs. In particular the inclusion \(K[V\setminus S]\hookrightarrow K\) is a simple homotopy equivalence, and both spaces have the homotopy type of the two-torus.

More precisely, \(K\) has \(24mn\) nonempty simplices, and deleting \(S\) removes exactly \(72|S|\) nonempty simplices. For every \(m,n\ge10\) divisible by five, the periodic set \(S=\{(i,j):i+2j\equiv0\pmod5\}\) meets the distance condition, removes exactly one fifth of the vertices and three fifths of the nonempty simplices, and leaves a simple-homotopy equivalent complex. The distance hypothesis cannot simply be dropped: removing two adjacent vertices changes the Euler characteristic from zero to minus one.

## Assumptions and scope
Here \(d((i,j),(k,l))=\min(|i-k|,m-|i-k|)+\min(|j-l|,n-|j-l|)\), using residues represented by integers. A Vietoris–Rips face is a nonempty vertex set of pairwise original distance at most two. When vertices are removed, all remaining distances are **inherited from \(V\)**; shortest paths are not recomputed in the punctured graph. A simplicial elementary collapse removes a maximal simplex and its unique free codimension-one face. All equivalences are over the integers and preserve simple homotopy type, not merely homology.

Write \(E=(1,0), W=(-1,0), N=(0,1), S_0=(0,-1)\), with doubled axial labels \(E_2,W_2,N_2,S_2\) and four diagonal labels \(NE,NW,SE,SW\). In the local descriptions, \(S_0\) (south) is distinct from the deletion set \(S\). The companion exact certificate uses the label `S` for south.

## Proof
**1. Complete local link.** Translation symmetry makes every vertex link in \(K\) isomorphic. The twelve vertices of the link of the origin are exactly \(E,W,N,S_0,E_2,W_2,N_2,S_2,NE,NW,SE,SW\). Two are joined if their toroidal graph distance is at most two. Directly evaluating this distance gives precisely nine maximal simplices: five tetrahedra
\[
\{N,S_0,E,W\},\quad \{N,N_2,NE,NW\},\quad \{S_0,S_2,SE,SW\},\quad \{E,E_2,NE,SE\},\quad \{W,W_2,NW,SW\},
\]
and four triangles \(\{N,E,NE\},\{N,W,NW\},\{S_0,E,SE\},\{S_0,W,SW\}\). Thus its face counts, from empty face through four-vertex faces, are \((1,12,30,24,5)\). For circumferences at least seven there are no extra compatible pairs due to wrapping: the differences involved have absolute coordinate values at most four, and every difference incompatible in the infinite grid remains of cyclic distance above two in at least one coordinate or of total cyclic distance above two.

**2. Explicit 35-pair link collapse.** Delete the vertices \(N_2,S_2,E_2,W_2\) in that order, each by four elementary collapse pairs. At its turn, each has a two-simplex link: \(\{N,NE,NW\}\), \(\{S_0,SE,SW\}\), \(\{E,NE,SE\}\), or \(\{W,NW,SW\}\). Next delete \(NE,NW,SE,SW\) in that order. Their respective links in the remaining complex are trees with three, two, two and one edge: \(NW-N-E-SE\), \(N-W-SW\), \(E-S_0-SW\), and \(S_0-W\). Their cones can be collapsed away with four, three, three and two free-face pairs, respectively. The remaining complex is a filled tetrahedron on \(N,S_0,E,W\), which collapses to a vertex with seven pairs. Thus the link collapses to a single vertex in \(16+12+7=35\) pairs. The file `link_certificate.json` gives every free-face pair, and the standalone verifier reconstructs the entire link from distances and checks each step, including the final singleton.

**3. Vertex-deletion lifting.** If a vertex link \(L\) collapses to a single vertex via \(t\) free-face pairs, cone each pair with the deleted vertex \(v\). Every coned pair remains free in the ambient complex: a face containing \(v\) has all its cofaces containing \(v\), and those cofaces are precisely controlled by its link. The resulting star consists of the edge \(\{v,w\}\) and vertex \(\{v\}\), which form one final free-face pair. The original link faces remain untouched. Consequently \(K\searrow K-v\) in \(t+1=36\) pairs. Since any two vertices of \(S\) have distance at least three, no member of \(S\) lies in the link of another. Earlier deletions therefore never change the complete link of any remaining vertex of \(S\). Apply the 36-pair construction successively for every member of \(S\), proving the asserted **direct simplicial collapse**.

**4. Exact face savings.** All maximal simplices of \(K\), for \(m,n\ge7\), are five-vertex axial diamonds and four-vertex unit squares; this can be verified locally by classifying a clique according to whether it varies in one or both cyclic coordinates, as in the established rectangular-grid classification. Independently, the universal link face counts in Step 1 show that the number of faces with \(j\) vertices equals \(mn\,\ell_{j-1}/j\), where \((\ell_0,\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3,\ell_4)=(1,12,30,24,5)\). Hence the nonempty face vector is \((mn,6mn,10mn,6mn,mn)\), summing to \(24mn\). Each deleted vertex belongs to exactly \(1+12+30+24+5=72\) faces. A face cannot contain two selected vertices because its diameter is at most two, so the affected faces are disjoint and exactly \(72|S|\) are lost.

**5. Dense periodic erasure and a boundary.** If \(m,n\) are multiples of five, the linear congruence \(i+2j\equiv0\pmod5\) defines exactly \(mn/5\) vertices. Every nonzero lattice displacement of \(\ell^1\)-length one or two has nonzero value under \((a,b)\mapsto a+2b\pmod5\); the modulo-five condition is compatible with torus wrapping. Therefore the selected vertices have mutual graph distance at least three. The number of deleted nonempty faces is \(72mn/5\), or \(3/5\) of all faces. To test the boundary, first delete \(v=(0,0)\), whose link is contractible, preserving the original Euler characteristic zero. For its adjacent neighbor \(w=(0,1)\), the new link is the original twelve-vertex link with the axial vertex corresponding to \(v\) deleted. Its nonempty face vector is \((11,24,16,3)\), of Euler characteristic zero; the vertex-deletion identity \(\chi(K-v-w)=\chi(K-v)-1+\chi(\operatorname{lk}_{K-v}w)\) gives \(-1\). Thus adjacent erasures need not preserve the homotopy type.

The two-torus homotopy type of the unpunctured \(K\) follows from the cited 2025 torus-grid theorem for equal sufficiently large factors and, uniformly for all rectangular factors at least seven, from the cited 2026 complete phase diagram. The **new claim** is the local 36-pair direct collapse, valid for every prescribed distance-separated deletion set, and its optimal face-accounting and example; no new proof of the unpunctured torus classification is claimed.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify_sparse_torus.py` in the same directory as `link_certificate.json`, and compare standard output to `expected_output.txt`. This standard-library-only verifier independently rebuilds the twelve-vertex link from the distance formula, checks all nine maximal faces and all 35 certified link-collapse pairs, and lifts the certificate to actual large finite Rips face sets. It checks exact freeness, resulting induced faces, face-count identities and separations for the grids \((7,7),(7,8),(8,8),(10,10)\), including a 20-point periodic deletion in the last grid. It additionally computes the Euler characteristic of the neighbor link needed for the adjacent-pair counterexample. The general proof in Steps 1–5, not the finite cases, covers all parameters.

## Relationship to prior work
Adams, Adetowubo, Barriga-Acosta, Feng and Sterling (2025) establish torus-grid Rips homotopy types at small scales and study maximal simplices. The 2026 complete rectangular phase diagram establishes the full \(m,n\ge5\) unpunctured scale-two classification, including a collapse from the **full** Rips complex onto a torus triangulation using all vertices. Its full statement and proof do not state or supply an elementary collapse onto an **arbitrarily prescribed induced vertex-deleted subcomplex**; a collapse retaining all original vertices is not that assertion. The present result strengthens the stability question by providing an explicit local, uniform, prescribed-vertex simple-homotopy collapse and exact savings, without asserting any new unpunctured homotopy type. Searches under vertex erasure, punctured toroidal grids, induced-subcomplex retractions and simple-homotopy collapses did not identify a dominating published theorem. Unindexed equivalent arguments or more general domination results remain a priority risk.

## Limitations
The theorem uses radius exactly two, factor circumferences at least seven, inherited original distances and pairwise deletion distance at least three. It does not assert that arbitrary deletions preserve topology; indeed two adjacent deleted vertices provide a counterexample. The collapse need not respect symmetries, and no higher-scale persistence claim or higher-dimensional torus result is made. Literature searches cannot establish absolute novelty, and independent mathematical audit is not yet performed.
