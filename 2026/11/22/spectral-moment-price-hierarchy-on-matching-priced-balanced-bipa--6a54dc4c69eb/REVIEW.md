# Mathematical assessment

**Correctness — PASS.** For the exact connected weighted \(K_{m,m}\) domain, the proof establishes strict convexity for real \(p\ge0\) on the compressed Laplacian, injectivity of edge weights, automorphism-forced two-orbit structure, an exact three-class spectrum, a one-sided derivative support criterion, and unique interior critical points. Its continuous-order strict monotonicity follows from an explicit positive covariance expression, not from sample checks. Fixed-order and joint-order limits follow from the stable threshold identity and a stationary derivative expansion. A standalone script independently compares scalar spectra with explicit matrix eigenvalues and tests critical windows; it reported 852 passing assertions. Finite tests are not treated as proofs.

**Originality — PASS, on accessible evidence.** Patterson and Bamieh introduce the first and second coherence spectral moments without a marked-edge cost threshold on \(K_{m,m}\). Ghosh, Boyd and Saberi provide convex inverse-trace design and illustrative symmetry cases, not the real-order hierarchy. Closely related public records already give separate determinant, first-order resistance, and spectral-gap price thresholds on the same marked graph. These are expressly treated as **known endpoints**. Their available statements do not imply the exact all-real-order frontier, its strict global order monotonicity, or the logistic joint scaling. The full text of some closely related public records could not be inspected; that remains a publication-priority limitation, not a claim of exhaustive novelty.

**Value — PASS.** The marked perfect matching is a canonical decision about purchasing expensive redundant links. The result provides a continuous hierarchy of edge-support decisions rather than merely another isolated optimizer. It resolves a noncommuting high-order/large-network limit by an explicit logistic law, pinpointing order proportional to network size as the sharp crossover scale. It also quantifies the price window in which second-order coherence buys links that first-order coherence deletes. The advantage is structural support prediction, not a claimed constant-factor performance gap.
