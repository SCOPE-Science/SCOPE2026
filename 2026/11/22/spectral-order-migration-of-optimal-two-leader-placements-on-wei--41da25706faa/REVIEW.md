# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The final claim concerns exactly the continuous spectral-order family \(\Phi_r(S)=\operatorname{tr}(L_{FF}(S)^{-r})\) on positive-conductance double stars with \(p,q\ge2\), two perfect leaders, and all real \(r\ge1\). An explicit Schur-complement inverse gives the two nontrivial eigenvalues for cross-hub–leaf leader pairs. Strict two-variable weak-majorization inequalities exclude same-side hub–leaf pairs and select the smaller leaf side. Exact first-moment inverse calculations exclude every leaf–leaf pair by the convex power-mean inequality. A monotone spectral trace and a strict convexity argument give the unique threshold and its increase with order; explicit quadratic algebra supplies the first two thresholds, and a controlled large-conductance expansion supplies the fixed-branch large-order slope. This analytic chain checks all eight orbit classes, equality cases, normalization, positive-definiteness, and asymptotic quantifiers. Finite checker results are supplementary.

**Originality — PASS on inspected evidence, with residual priority risk.** The same-family first-order public result, *Exact two-leader coherence transition on weighted double stars*, was compared at statement level and its available `RESULT.md` read. It gives only \(\operatorname{tr}(L_{FF}^{-1})\), its single threshold in *resistance* variables, and corresponding first-order optimizers. The new theorem agrees at that endpoint after converting the bridge variable but establishes a genuinely different inverse-square boundary, all real orders \(r\ge1\), a strict order-monotone transition and linear large-order asymptotics. The Lin–Fardad–Jovanović full paper formulates first-moment leader selection with algorithms but provides no double-star inverse-power phase diagram. The Clark–Bushnell–Poovendran paper concerns link-noise leader selection, and Siami–Motee analyze ungrounded disturbance propagation. Other related double-star studies with variable edge allocations or finite-gain leaders solve distinct decision problems. No inspected general result was found to imply this exact continuum claim. This is not a proof that no specialized equivalent work exists.

**Value — PASS.** In leader placement on two-community trees, different spectral moments emphasize slow modes differently. This theorem resolves every leader pair and every positive bridge conductance in an infinite natural weighted family, shows a rigorously nonempty region of disagreement between two standard objectives, and supplies a sharp linear growth law for how the bridge strength required to move a leader changes with spectral order. It is an interpretable network-design classification, not merely a substitution into a known objective or a small numerical gain; the first-order endpoint is acknowledged as previously known rather than presented as new.

**Closest comparisons and limits.** The most decisive comparison is the published first-order double-star classification. General leader-selection relaxations and second-order ungrounded coherence bounds do not yield the new optimizer identity or transition law. Residual risks include unindexed specializations, alternate inverse-Schatten terminology, and inaccessible specialized material. The scope excludes \(r<1\), heterogeneous pendant conductances, finite leader gains, additional leaders and jointly optimized edge weights. The numerical checker supports but does not prove the continuous/infinite theorem.
