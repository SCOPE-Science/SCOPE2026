# Sphere wedges from point-fixing Singer blocks in rank-four buildings

## Finding
Let \(q\ge 2\) be any prime power, let \(L=\mathbb F_q\), let \(W=\mathbb F_{q^3}\), and regard \(V=L\oplus W\) as a four-dimensional vector space over \(\mathbb F_q\). Let \(B(V)\) be the order complex of the poset of all nonzero proper \(\mathbb F_q\)-subspaces of \(V\). The multiplicative group \(G=W^\times\), cyclic of order \(q^3-1\), acts on \(V\) by
\[
 a\cdot (\ell,w)=(\ell,aw),\qquad a\in\mathbb F_{q^3}^{\times},\ \ell\in L,\ w\in W.
\]
For the **ordinary geometric orbit space** \(Y_q=|B(V)|/G\), rather than the Borel construction, there is an integral homotopy equivalence
\[
 \boxed{Y_q\simeq\bigvee^{q^3+q}S^2.}
\]
In particular \(Y_q\) is simply connected, \(H_2(Y_q;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{q^3+q}\), and all other reduced integral homology groups vanish. This is a quotient by a *reducible, point-fixing Singer block*, not by an irreducible projective Singer group on \(V\).

## Assumptions and scope
The field order \(q\) is any prime power. The action is the identity on the distinguished one-space \(L\) and multiplicative on the independent three-space \(W\), and it induces a faithful linear action on \(V\). Each vertex of \(B(V)\) is a proper nonzero subspace, with a simplex for each strictly increasing flag. Maximal flags have three vertices of dimensions \(1<2<3\), so the complex and its orbit space have dimension two. The stated object is a finite CW orbit space (or simplicial orbit space with distinct rank-labelled vertices), not the classifying space of the group action. We do not assert any equivariant splitting of the covering building or homeomorphism type for the quotient.

## Proof
**1. The building is simply connected.** By the Solomon–Tits theorem, \(|B(V)|\simeq\bigvee^{q^6}S^2\): it is a two-dimensional, connected, simply connected complex. The number of spheres follows from the Möbius function of the lattice of subspaces of a four-space, \(\mu(0,V)=(-1)^4q^{\binom 42}=q^6\). Thus \(\chi(|B(V)|)=1+q^6\).

**2. All elements have fixed points, and the quotient is simply connected.** Every \(a\in G\) fixes the rank-one vertex \(L\). Because flags contain at most one vertex of each subspace dimension, the action has no simplex inversions; geometric fixed points are exactly fixed-point flags. Armstrong's orbit-space theorem applied to the simply connected realization identifies \(\pi_1(Y_q)\) with \(G\) modulo the normal subgroup generated by point-fixing elements. This normal subgroup is all of \(G\), so \(\pi_1(Y_q)=1\). This step uses ordinary orbit-space topology and does not follow merely from the group action having a fixed vertex without the cited theorem.

**3. Fixed-subspace classification.** For \(a\in W^\times\), write \(g_a=\operatorname{diag}(1,m_a)\), where \(m_a\) is multiplication by \(a\) on \(W\). Three disjoint cases exhaust the group:

- For \(a=1\), the entire building is fixed and \(\chi(|B(V)|^{g_a})=1+q^6\).
- If \(a\in\mathbb F_q^\times\setminus\{1\}\), the two eigenvalues \(1,a\) are distinct. Each invariant subspace is uniquely \(A\oplus U\), with \(A\in\{0,L\}\) and \(U\le W\) any \(\mathbb F_q\)-subspace. The fixed-subspace lattice, including its bottom and top, is the product \(\mathcal L(L)\times\mathcal L(W)\). Multiplicativity of the Möbius function gives \(\mu=(-1)(-q^3)=q^3\), hence the fixed proper-part order complex has Euler characteristic \(1+q^3\).
- If \(a\notin\mathbb F_q^\times\), the minimal polynomial of \(a\) over \(\mathbb F_q\) has degree three, as the extension degree is prime. Hence \(W\) is an irreducible \(\mathbb F_q[a]\)-module, nonisomorphic to the trivial module \(L\). The only nonzero proper \(g_a\)-invariant subspaces of \(V\) are \(L\) and \(W\), which are incomparable. The fixed space is two isolated vertices, of Euler characteristic \(2\).

There are respectively \(1\), \(q-2\), and \(q^3-q\) elements in these three cases. The preceding classification is about **all invariant subspaces**, not just fixed vectors.

**4. Euler characteristic of the quotient.** The rank-preserving simplicial action fixes each stabilized simplex pointwise. Therefore fixed-point Burnside averaging applies to its cellular quotient:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\chi(Y_q)
 &=\frac{(1+q^6)+(q-2)(1+q^3)+2(q^3-q)}{q^3-1}\\
 &=\frac{q^6+q^4-q-1}{q^3-1}
 =q^3+q+1.
\end{aligned}
\]
There is no orientation-sign correction because each simplex's ordered vertex dimensions are preserved.

**5. Integral homotopy type.** The space \(Y_q\) is a connected simply connected finite two-dimensional CW complex. Its first homology vanishes; its top homology is a subgroup of the free cellular group \(C_2(Y_q;\mathbb Z)\), hence free. Euler characteristic then gives \(\operatorname{rank}H_2(Y_q;\mathbb Z)=\chi(Y_q)-1=q^3+q\). Hurewicz realizes a basis by maps of two-spheres. Their wedge is a homology equivalence between simply connected CW complexes, hence a homotopy equivalence by the homology form of Whitehead's theorem. This proves the asserted full (nonequivariant) homotopy classification.

## Verification
The included `verify.py` reconstructs the binary example directly, independently of the general counting formula. It enumerates all nonzero proper subspaces of \(\mathbb F_2^4\), applies the order-seven block multiplication using \(t^3+t+1=0\), forms flag orbits, checks the quotient cellular boundary composition and computes the Smith diagonal after integral tree reduction. Running `python verify.py` with Python 3 and SymPy produces:

```
BINARY_PARABOLIC_SINGER_PASS original=(65,315,315) quotient=(11,45,45)
d1rank=10 d2smith_unit_invariants=35 H1=0 H2=Z^10 fixed_nonidentity_vertices=2
BURNSIDE_POLYNOMIAL_PASS q=2,3,4,5,7,8,9
```

For \(q=2\) the rank counts are \((15,35,15)\) before quotient at vertex level, \((11,45,45)\) after quotient, giving \(\chi=11\) and ten spheres. For \(q=3\) the symbolic fixed-point formula gives \(\chi=31\) and thirty spheres, but the script does **not** enumerate the ternary orbit-chain complex. Finite tests do not certify the all-prime-powers statement, which rests on the field-module and topological arguments above.

## Relationship to prior work
The Solomon–Tits theorem gives the covering building's sphere-wedge homotopy type and is used, not reclaimed. Armstrong's theorem supplies the orbit fundamental-group calculation in general; the application to the present point-fixing cyclic action is explicit. K. Drudge's Singer-orbit paper counts flat orbits for groups acting irreducibly and transitively on projective points; the group here is reducible and fixes a point, so those flat-orbit formulas do not determine this ordinary quotient's homotopy type. Arone–Dwyer relate partition complexes and Tits buildings through equivariant homological approximations and Borel constructions, not the displayed point-fixing ordinary orbit space.

Previously published studies of the **irreducible** rank-four Singer quotient and its subgroups identify torsion and rational homology for a different action; an earlier normalizer-quotient result likewise has no fixed line shared by every group element. None of these claims implies the precise \(q^3+q\)-sphere classification by substitution, because \(G\) is not a subgroup of the irreducible projective Singer action on \(\mathbb F_{q^4}\). General character formulae for Steinberg representations can in principle recover rational invariant dimensions here; the claim is the ordinary **integral homotopy type** under this specified reducible action, not novelty of the character formula or the tools used to prove it.

## Limitations
The result determines a nonequivariant homotopy type only. It does not give an equivariant homotopy decomposition, explicit homology module under a commuting normalizer, a homeomorphism classification, or a general formula for higher-dimensional parabolic Singer blocks. It depends on standard Solomon–Tits, Armstrong, Möbius, Hurewicz and Whitehead results. Searching indexed sources does not exclude unpublished or inaccessible equivalent results; no independent mathematical audit has been performed.
