# Scientific review

## Correctness
The rank-four spherical building is simply connected by Solomon–Tits. The group action fixes the line \(L\) pointwise and is flag-rank preserving, so Armstrong's formula makes the orbit simply connected. The three exhaustive element classes (identity, nonidentity scalar in \(\mathbb F_q^\times\), and degree-three field element) have fixed-space Euler characteristics \(1+q^6\), \(1+q^3\), and \(2\), respectively. Burnside yields \(\chi=q^3+q+1\). Any connected simply connected two-dimensional CW complex is a wedge of two-spheres, with their number fixed by Euler characteristic. Exact binary orbit-cell and Smith computations independently confirm the formula at \(q=2\). The proof does not rely on an extrapolation from a finite test.

## Originality
The closest mathematical literature gives irreducible Singer cycle orbit counts (Drudge), a classic fundamental-group mechanism (Armstrong), the covering sphere-wedge theorem (Solomon–Tits, as treated by Kahn–Sun), and homological comparisons (Arone–Dwyer). Published building-quotient notes determine full/subgroup irreducible projective Singer quotients or the full normalizer; their groups are not the point-fixing reducible block acting on \(L\oplus W\). The present ordinary integral homotopy classification is neither a direct parameter specialization of those results nor a restatement of their rational characters. One residual risk is that a general Steinberg-character restriction or unindexed source may already present this exact orbit-space consequence; novelty is not asserted as absolute priority.

## Value
This is a complete natural infinite-family homotopy classification for the first spherical building dimension in which simply connected orbit spaces can have substantive second homotopy. It identifies the exact change from nontrivial torsion for an irreducible full Singer quotient to \(q^3+q\) integral sphere classes under the point-fixing maximal Singer block, valid uniformly for prime powers rather than isolated enumeration. The precise sphere rank is a natural invariant of the flag space for the distinguished Levi factor and can be used in comparing spherical links under different finite-field cyclic stabilizers. The claim is stronger than simply recording Euler characteristic and distinct from the underlying unquotiented Solomon–Tits statement.

## Limits and nearest prior statements
The result is nonequivariant homotopy equivalence only; no homeomorphism or higher-rank generalization is claimed. Classic underlying theorems are used without a novelty claim. The binary checker confirms the finite example but not universal scope. Primary literature inspection and indexed searches reduce, but cannot remove, priority risk.
