# Spherical strong Morse complexes of once-subdivided stars

## Finding
**Theorem.** Let \(n\ge3\), and let \(T_n\) be the tree with vertices \(c,u_1,w_1,\ldots,u_n,w_n\) and edges \(cu_i,u_iw_i\), for \(1\le i\le n\). Let \(\mathcal{SM}(T_n)\) denote the full complex of strong discrete-Morse matchings arising from vertex filtrations, in the sense of Knudson and Owens-White. There is a sequence of dominated-vertex deletions (strong simplicial collapses)
\[
\mathcal{SM}(T_n)\searrow_{\!s}\bigast_{i=1}^n\{(w_i,u_i),(u_i,w_i)\}\cong\partial\mathcal O_n,
\]
where the two oriented incidences in each brace are distinct isolated vertices, and \(\partial\mathcal O_n\) is the boundary of the \(n\)-dimensional cross-polytope. Consequently \(\mathcal{SM}(T_n)\simeq S^{n-1}\); its only nonzero reduced integral homology group is \(\widetilde H_{n-1}\cong\mathbb Z\). Under the natural action of the arm permutation group \(S_n\), this group is the integral sign representation. Although its strong core has dimension \(n-1\), \(\mathcal{SM}(T_n)\) has maximal simplices of dimension \(2n-1\), and its exact number of inclusion-maximal facets is \(2^n+n\). In particular, these complexes are nonpure.

## Assumptions and scope
The tree is a one-dimensional simplicial complex (no filled triangles). An oriented incidence \((v,u)\) represents the matching pair between vertex \(v\) and the edge \(\{v,u\}\), **not** a directed edge in a multipath complex. A vertex filtration is any strict total order of tree vertices. In its strong matching, a vertex \(v\) supplies \((v,u)\) precisely if \(u\) is its unique earlier neighbor. If there are zero or at least two earlier neighbors, \(v\) supplies no incidence. This follows because the descending link is a discrete set and is a simplicial cone exactly when it is a singleton. The full complex \(\mathcal{SM}\) is the downward-closed collection of all subsets of matchings generated by these filtrations. We use geometric-realization homotopy and integral simplicial homology. The theorem's genuinely branching range is \(n\ge3\); the \(n=2\) four-edge path is a previously solved boundary case and is not claimed as new.

Abbreviate four incidences per arm by
\[
 A_i=(w_i,u_i),\quad B_i=(u_i,w_i),\quad C_i=(u_i,c),\quad D_i=(c,u_i).
\]
Here a simplex \(\Delta\{A_i,C_i\}\) has both of these vertices as an edge, while \(\{A_i,B_i\}\) is a discrete two-point complex. Joins and subcomplexes include the empty face in their combinatorial definitions.

## Proof
**1. Exact filtration-generated decomposition.** Let
\[
 J_n=\bigast_{i=1}^n\left(\Delta\{A_i,C_i\}\sqcup\{B_i\}\right),
 \qquad
 L_i=\{A_i,B_i\}*\bigast_{j\ne i}\Delta\{A_j,C_j\}.
\]
We claim the following equality of simplicial complexes:
\[
 \mathcal{SM}(T_n)=J_n\cup\bigcup_{i=1}^n(D_i*L_i).
 \tag{1}
\]
In a filtration without a pair \(D_i\), the permitted matched incidences on each arm are either \(A_i,C_i\) (both simultaneously possible, in the order \(c,u_i,w_i\)), or \(B_i\) (in the order \(w_i,u_i\)); the two possibilities cannot coexist because the source \(u_i\) or outer edge would be repeated. Hence the matching lies in \(J_n\). All facets of \(J_n\) do occur as subsets of some filtration matching: if the selected \(B\)-arms are empty, put \(c\) before all \(u_i\) and \(w_i\); if they are nonempty, order \(w_i\prec u_i\) for those arms, then place \(c\), and then order \(u_j\prec w_j\) on the other arms. When exactly one \(B\)-arm is selected, this filtration adds one extra \(D_i\), which does not matter for downward closure.

If a filtration supplies \(D_i\), then \(u_i\) is the **only** earlier neighbor of \(c\). Thus all \(u_j\), \(j\ne i\), occur after \(c\), cannot supply \(B_j\), and may supply both \(C_j,A_j\). On arm \(i\), the possible outer incidences are \(A_i\) or \(B_i\), never both; \(C_i\) is excluded since \(D_i\) already occupies the central edge. Moreover no second \(D_j\) can occur because \(c\) can be matched only once. The two maximal simplices
\[
\{D_i,A_i\}\cup\bigcup_{j\ne i}\{C_j,A_j\},\qquad
\{D_i,B_i\}\cup\bigcup_{j\ne i}\{C_j,A_j\}
\]
are realized respectively by \(u_i\prec c\prec w_i\) and by \(w_i\prec u_i\prec c\), always placing all remaining \(u_j\prec w_j\) after \(c\). This proves both inclusions in (1).

**2. Strong collapse of center-matched vertices.** For each \(i\), the link of \(D_i\) is \(L_i\). Since \(n\ge2\), choose \(j\ne i\). Every facet of \(L_i\) contains \(A_j\), as the \(j\)-th factor is the full edge \(\Delta\{A_j,C_j\}\). Thus \(L_i\) is a simplicial cone and \(D_i\) is dominated by \(A_j\). No simplex contains two \(D\)-vertices, so the dominated-vertex deletions can be performed successively and leave precisely \(J_n\). Within \(J_n\), every vertex \(C_i\) is dominated by \(A_i\): its link has a cone apex \(A_i\), and this remains true after any deletions of other \(C\)-vertices. Deleting all \(C_i\) therefore gives the embedded join of \(n\) discrete two-point complexes, the boundary of an \(n\)-cross-polytope. Every deletion is a strong simplicial collapse, hence an ordinary homotopy equivalence.

**3. Integral representation.** The final subcomplex is invariant under permutations of the arms. Choose its fundamental cycle as the ordered simplicial join
\[
 z=(A_1-B_1)*(A_2-B_2)*\cdots*(A_n-B_n),
\]
where each parenthesized difference is a reduced integral zero-cycle. Interchanging two join factors of degree zero changes orientation by \((-1)^{(0+1)(0+1)}=-1\). Therefore an arm permutation \(\sigma\) sends \([z]\) to \(\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)[z]\). Inclusion of this invariant subcomplex into \(\mathcal{SM}(T_n)\) is an integral homology isomorphism by Step 2 and is itself equivariant, even though the **chosen collapse sequence** need not be equivariant. The claimed integral representation follows.

**4. Complete facet count and nonpurity.** The maximal facets of \(J_n\) correspond to one choice \(\{A_i,C_i\}\) or \(\{B_i\}\) per arm. If no \(B\)-arm occurs, the resulting \(2n\)-element face is maximal in the full complex. If exactly one \(B_i\) occurs, its face extends by \(D_i\), so is not maximal. If at least two distinct \(B\)-arms occur, no \(D\)-vertex can be added, and the face is maximal. There are \(2^n-n-1\) such faces. Additionally, for each \(i\), there are precisely two maximal faces involving \(D_i\), displayed in Step 1, and one all-\(A,C\) maximal face. Thus the total number is
\[
 (2^n-n-1)+2n+1=2^n+n.
\]
The \(2n+1\) top faces have \(2n\) vertices and dimension \(2n-1\); the all-\(B\) face has only \(n\) vertices and is maximal for \(n\ge2\). The complex is nonpure. This completes the proof.

## Verification
The standalone Python 3 standard-library checker `verify_spider.py` independently enumerates **every vertex order** for \(1\le n\le4\), computes actual strong-gradient incidence sets and their inclusion-maximal members, and compares them with the structural facets from the proof. For \(n=2,3,4\) it then independently tests each dominated-vertex removal and verifies that the surviving maximal faces are exactly the \(2^n\) facets of a cross-polytope boundary. The script prints `VERIFY_OK: exhaustive vertex orders and dominated-vertex reductions, n=1..4` on success. Run `python3 verify_spider.py` next to the result; no third-party packages or external data are needed. This finite enumeration corroborates the proof but is not the basis for the unbounded statement.

## Relationship to prior work
Knudson and Owens-White [1, Definition 4.2 and Theorem 6.6] define the strong matching complex and compute it for paths, stars, cycles and simplex families. The nonbranching \(n=2\) instance is their previously known \(\mathcal{SM}(P_4)\simeq S^1\); their results do not imply the collapse for the branching trees \(n\ge3\). A related published diameter-three purity criterion for strong matching complexes of trees [3] explicitly does not classify the full complex for trees of diameter four, which includes the present family.

Donovan and Scoville [2, Corollary 3.14] determine the **ordinary**, not strong, Morse complex of the *same* tree: \(\mathfrak M(T_n)\simeq\bigvee^{n-1}S^n\). This sharply differs both in homology degree and multiplicity from the result here. A separately published computation [4] shows that the *bidirected multipath complex* of this tree is also homotopy equivalent to \(S^{n-1}\), with the same arm-permutation sign character. The latter is a genuinely different simplicial complex: it forbids multiple arcs entering the center, whereas \(\mathcal{SM}(T_n)\) includes the face \(\{C_1,A_1,\ldots,C_n,A_n\}\) with \(n\) center-pointing incidences. Neither previously known classification implies (1) or the dominated-vertex collapse of the strong matching complex.

## Limitations
The theorem is for identical two-edge arms, not arbitrary trees or spiders of unequal or greater arm lengths. It determines a full integral homotopy type and one integral symmetry representation, not a complete equivariant homotopy type or the representation of arbitrary graph groups. The new assertion is the branching range \(n\ge3\); nonbranching instances have existing coverage. The collapse is not claimed to be equivariant. The finite checker covers only \(n\le4\), while the all-rank result rests on the exact decomposition. Mathematically equivalent unindexed work cannot be excluded. Independent validation has not been performed.
