# Split and nonsplit regular-semisimple witness fibers for trace-zero commutators over finite chain rings

## Finding
Let \(R\) be an arbitrary finite commutative chain ring of length \(k\ge1\), with maximal ideal generated by \(\pi\), residue field \(\mathbb F_q\) of odd order, and \(\pi^k=0\). Put \(\mathfrak g=\mathfrak{sl}_2(R)\). For \(X=\begin{psmallmatrix}c&a\\b&-c\end{psmallmatrix}\), write \(Q(X)=c^2+ab=-\det X\). Let \(\chi:\mathbb F_q\to\{0,\pm1\}\) be the quadratic character with \(\chi(0)=0\). For \(s=\pm1\), define the ordered, labeled fiber
\[
F_s(Z)=\#\{(X,Y)\in\mathfrak g^2:[X,Y]=Z,\ \chi(\overline{Q(X)})=s\}.
\]
The first-operand condition is equivalent to requiring that \(\overline X\) be split regular semisimple when \(s=+1\), or nonsplit regular semisimple when \(s=-1\).

For every nonzero \(Z\in\mathfrak g\), write \(Z=\pi^jZ_0\) with \(0\le j<k\) its coordinatewise valuation, and let \(d=\chi(Q(\overline{Z_0}))\in\{0,\pm1\}\). The reduced leading coefficient is defined up to a nonzero scalar, which does not change \(d\). Then
\[
\boxed{F_s(Z)=q^{3k+j-2}H_s(d),\qquad
H_s(d)=\begin{cases}
q(q-1),&d=0,\ s=+1,\\
0,&d=0,\ s=-1,\\
\dfrac{(q-1)(q-d)}2,&d\in\{-1,+1\},\ s\in\{-1,+1\}.
\end{cases}}
\]
At the zero target, instead,
\[
\boxed{F_s(0)=\frac{q-1}{2}(q^2+sq)q^{4k-3}.}
\]
Thus **every** target admits a bracket representation with split regular-semisimple first operand, whereas the targets admitting no nonsplit regular-semisimple first operand are exactly the nonzero \(Z\) with \(Q(\overline{Z_0})=0\). Their number is
\[
\boxed{(q^2-1)\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}q^{3i}
=\frac{(q^2-1)(q^{3k}-1)}{q^3-1}.}
\]
Both nonzero-fiber classes coincide when \(Q(\overline{Z_0})\ne0\), despite their different zero-target populations and the nonsplit obstruction.

## Assumptions and scope
A finite commutative chain ring has a chain \(R\supset(\pi)\supset\cdots\supset(\pi^{k-1})\supset(\pi^k)=0\); every successive quotient has size \(q\), so \(|R|=q^k\). No equicharacteristic or unramified assumption is used. Because \(q\) is odd, \(2\in R^\times\). The valuation of nonzero \(Z\) is the minimum valuation of its three trace-zero coordinates. Its leading coefficient is well-defined over the residue field after choosing a generator \(\pi\); a change of generator only rescales \(\overline{Z_0}\), leaving \(\chi(Q(\overline{Z_0}))\) unchanged. All pairs are ordered and counted as actual elements, not as conjugacy orbits. The result does not concern group commutators or characteristic two.

## Proof
**1. Regular first factors and trace orthogonality.** Write \(X=\begin{psmallmatrix}c&a\\b&-c\end{psmallmatrix}\) and \(Z=\begin{psmallmatrix}h&u\\v&-h\end{psmallmatrix}\). The trace pairing is
\[
\operatorname{tr}(XZ)=2ch+av+bu.
\]
Its Gram matrix is invertible because \(2\) is a unit. If \(\chi(\overline{Q(X)})=\pm1\), then \(\overline X\ne0\) is regular and has one-dimensional Lie centralizer over the residue field. Indeed, the full matrix centralizer is spanned by the identity and \(\overline X\); the trace-zero part is \(\mathbb F_q\overline X\). The adjoint \(Y\mapsto[X,Y]\) therefore has rank two after residue reduction and admits a unit \(2\times2\) minor over \(R\). Since \(X\) is a primitive vector in \(R^3\) and \([X,X]=0\), this map has kernel precisely \(RX\), a free rank-one summand with \(q^k\) elements. Invariance of trace gives \(\operatorname{im}(\operatorname{ad}_X)\subseteq X^\perp\); both modules are free rank two, and the unit minor makes them equal. Consequently, for each permitted \(X\), the equation \([X,Y]=Z\) has \(q^k\) solutions \(Y\) if \(\operatorname{tr}(XZ)=0\), and no solutions otherwise.

**2. Reduction of a nonzero target.** Take \(Z=\pi^jZ_0\) with \(j<k\), where \(\overline{Z_0}\ne0\). The orthogonality equation \(\operatorname{tr}(XZ)=0\) means \(\operatorname{tr}(XZ_0)=0\) modulo \(\pi^{k-j}\). For any specified \(\overline X\) on the corresponding residual orthogonal plane, the primitive linear equation has exactly \(q^{2k+j-2}\) lifts \(X\in R^3\): there are \(3(k-1)\) free lifting digits, with \(k-j-1\) additional independent linear equations. Multiplying by \(q^k\) choices of \(Y\) shows
\[
F_s(Z)=q^{3k+j-2}\#\{x\in\overline{Z_0}^{\perp}:\chi(Q(x))=s\}.
\]
The remaining factor is a finite-field quadratic-form count.

**3. The two residual plane types.** The quadratic form \(Q(a,b,c)=c^2+ab\) is a split, nondegenerate ternary form. If \(d=\chi(Q(\overline{Z_0}))\in\{\pm1\}\), its orthogonal complement is a nondegenerate binary plane. Its discriminant is split exactly when \(d=+1\). An elementary hyperbolic-plane count gives \(q-1\) vectors representing each nonzero norm for a split plane; the norm form of a nonsplit plane represents each nonzero norm \(q+1\) times. Equivalently, each nonzero norm has \(q-d\) representatives. Summing over the \((q-1)/2\) nonzero norm values of either quadratic character yields \(H_{+1}(d)=H_{-1}(d)=(q-1)(q-d)/2\).

If \(Q(\overline{Z_0})=0\), the nonzero isotropic vector can be carried by a ternary orthogonal isometry to a standard isotropic vector. Its orthogonal plane has one-dimensional radical and quadratic form \(c^2\) in suitable coordinates; thus exactly \(q(q-1)\) vectors have nonzero square norm, and none has nonsquare norm. This gives \(H_{+1}(0)=q(q-1)\), \(H_{-1}(0)=0\).

**4. The zero fiber.** There are \((q-1)/2\) nonzero residue norm values of each character. For the split ternary \(Q\), each nonzero value \(t\) has \(q^2+q\chi(t)\) representations: this is the standard rank-three quadratic-form count, also obtained by directly summing \(c^2+ab=t\). Thus the number of first operands of type \(s\) is
\[
q^{3(k-1)}\frac{q-1}{2}(q^2+sq).
\]
Every permitted first operand has exactly \(q^k\) commuting second operands. Multiplication proves the displayed zero formula.

**5. Exact image obstruction.** In the nonsplit case, the above formula is positive except when \(Z\ne0\) and the leading residue vector is isotropic. In the split case every factor is positive. The number of nonzero isotropic vectors of the split ternary form over \(\mathbb F_q\) is \(q^2-1\). For each valuation \(j\), exactly \((q^2-1)q^{3(k-j-1)}\) targets have an isotropic nonzero leading coefficient. Summing gives the geometric-series formula. No other targets are missing. All claims hold for every finite commutative chain ring with the stated residue data.

## Verification
The standalone C++17 checker `verify_semisimple_witnesses.cpp` independently constructs finite rings using modular integer arithmetic or truncated polynomial digit arithmetic. It explicitly enumerates every ordered pair \((X,Y)\) in \(\mathfrak{sl}_2(R)^2\), filters \(X\) by the square/nonsquare type of \(-\det\overline X\), multiplies matrices to determine \([X,Y]\), and compares **every prescribed target** against both formulas, including zero, ramified isotropic and nonisotropic cases. It also checks total pair counts. Run:

```sh
c++ -O3 -std=c++17 verify_semisimple_witnesses.cpp -o verify_semisimple_witnesses
./verify_semisimple_witnesses
```

Captured successful output is in `verify_semisimple_witnesses_output.txt`. Checks cover \(\mathbb F_3\), \(\mathbb F_5\), \(\mathbb Z/9\mathbb Z\), \(\mathbb F_3[t]/(t^2)\), \(\mathbb Z/27\mathbb Z\), and \(\mathbb F_3[t]/(t^3)\), totaling 6 complete ring configurations with all 27, 125, 729, 729, 19683 and 19683 targets, respectively. The length-three tests independently evaluate all 387420489 ordered pairs each. Finite checks support the derivation; the unrestricted ring-length statement follows from the proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Stasinski's 2012 study establishes qualitative commutator representation over principal ideal rings in the full matrix algebra, and his 2016 follow-up strengthens trace-zero factor results and treats regularity. These provide the direct commutator-theoretic context, but neither supplies counts with the first trace-zero factor required to be specifically split or nonsplit regular semisimple. Choosuwan, Jitman and Udomkavanich compute determinant fibers of full matrices over finite principal ideal rings; general determinant counts do not directly give this constrained trace-orthogonal Lie-bracket distribution. Existing results on primitive square-zero first-factor witnesses concern a disjoint first-operand locus; a finite first-Takiff \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) result counts unrestricted first operands. In particular, neither implies the sharp nonsplit obstruction without additional discriminant-plane analysis. The classification uses standard classical quadratic-form arithmetic and does not claim new proofs of those ingredients.

## Limitations
Only the two split/nonsplit **unit-determinant character types** for the first operand are counted. Nilpotent, scalar, or other imprimitive first factors are excluded, and this theorem does not give a distribution by individual fixed determinant values or conjugacy orbit of the first operand. Characteristic two is excluded. Finite enumeration does not establish infinite ring-length validity. Although the specific constrained fiber theorem was not identified in the inspected sources, historical priority is not independently certified: a general previously published orbit-incidence or quadratic-module counting theorem could subsume it.
