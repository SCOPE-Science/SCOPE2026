# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

For the rim fault, the hub-free simplex and the cone on I(P_n) meet in I(P_n), giving its suspension. For the spoke fault, the hub-free subcomplex is the rim boundary sphere S^{n-2}. Its hub link L is I(P_{n-1}) with a cone on the subcomplex A excluding simultaneous selection of both endpoints: with rim vertex 0 present those endpoints would form a four-cycle through the hub, and no other cycles survive the path independence constraint. For n>=6, L is up to homotopy the quotient of the join of the endpoint edge with I(P_{n-5}) by the join of its boundary with I(P_{n-5}); this is the double suspension of I(P_{n-5}). At n=5 the quotient is an interval with endpoints identified and L is a circle. Since dim L<=n-3, its inclusion into S^{n-2} is null. The mapping cone splits off the original rim sphere (and hence its primitive class) and adds the suspension of L. The usual path recursion with bases m=1,2,3 gives exactly the two period-three tables integrally, not just Betti ranks. Read the recurrence from m>=4 when not choosing a P_0 convention. Dihedral symmetry gives the two edge-deletion cases.

## value

PASS

The exact non-equivariant integral homotopy type on an unbounded natural graph family distinguishes the only two single-edge fault orbits. In particular it explains how the rim top class survives a spoke fault but disappears after a rim fault, and identifies the complete lower-degree response rather than just an Euler characteristic or a few numerical examples. The rim computation is a useful controlled baseline, not a separate contribution.
