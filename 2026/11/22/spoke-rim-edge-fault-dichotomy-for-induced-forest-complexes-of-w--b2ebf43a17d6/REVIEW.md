# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

The graph model and fault locations are explicit. For a rim fault, the full rim simplex and the cone on the path independence complex form a homotopy pushout. For a spoke fault, the rim boundary sphere and cone on the hub link are a second pushout; the link is the homotopy cofiber of the independently defined endpoint-exclusion subcomplex. Its quotient is a double suspension of an explicitly counted middle-path independence complex. A face-dimension bound makes the remaining attachment null-homotopic. The classical path suspension recurrence is also proved from a deletion-cone decomposition. The five-rim-vertex boundary case is handled separately. These steps establish integral *homotopy types* for all rim lengths in scope, not merely the finite mod-two checks. Executable enumeration confirmed all 16 graph cases with 5–12 rim vertices.

## Originality — PASS against inspected evidence

The closest paper is Carnero Bravo, arXiv:2307.13271, specifically its full wheel proposition and join-of-graphs discussion. Its general join lemma **does imply the rim-edge formula**, because the rim-fault graph is the join of one universal vertex and a path; that branch is a known corollary used only as a comparison. The paper does not furnish the endpoint-exclusion link for the nonjoin missing-spoke graph, which is the original mathematical contribution. A face-counting acyclic polynomial cannot supply that missing-spoke homotopy classification without an additional argument. Another published indexed result on a missing spoke uses a three-cut complex, not vertex-induced forest faces. The own-claim comparison found no matching defect-wheel induced-forest theorem. Search-index gaps remain a risk and the absence of a found result is not a proof of global uniqueness.

## Value — PASS

The two edge types form the complete nontrivial fault-orbit classification of wheels: spoke loss *always* preserves the rim's integral top sphere, while rim-edge loss eliminates it, with a mod-three contractibility/sphere transition at lower dimensions. The result is an infinite-family structural classification, not an arbitrary isolated rank or a numerical restatement of the intact-wheel result. It motivates fault sensitivity of induced-acyclic-set topology and supplies a concrete cofiber technique for other nearly coned graphs.

## Limitations and closest literature

The theorem concerns unrestricted vertex-induced forests for one edge deletion, rim length at least five. It does not compute the bounded-degree filtration or equivariant action. Carnero Bravo (2023) is the source for the intact-wheel context; Barton–Brown–Pike (2022) is relevant to face enumeration, not homotopy. Independent validation is outstanding.
