# Stable Kneser orbit spaces: subgroup fundamental groups and two Moore-type classifications

## Finding

For each integer \(m\ge 8\), let \(G_m=\operatorname{SG}_{2,m-4}\) be the stable Kneser graph: its vertices are nonconsecutive two-element subsets of \(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z\), and two vertices are adjacent when the subsets are disjoint. Let \(X_m=|\operatorname{Ind}(G_m)|\). The dihedral group \(D_m=\langle r,s\mid r^m=s^2=1,\ srs=r^{-1}\rangle\) (of order \(2m\)) acts by \(r(i)=i+1\) and \(s(i)=-i\).

**Theorem.** For every subgroup \(H\le D_m\), the ordinary geometric orbit space \(Q_{m,H}=X_m/H\) has the homotopy type of a finite CW complex of dimension at most two. Its fundamental group is
\[
\pi_1(Q_{m,H})\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z/(d/\gcd(d,6))\mathbb Z,&H=C_d\le\langle r\rangle,\quad d\mid m,\\
1,&H\text{ contains a reflection}.
\end{cases}
\]
The expression with \(d=1\) denotes the trivial group. Its complete integral homology is \(H_0\cong\mathbb Z\), \(H_1\cong\pi_1(Q_{m,H})_{\mathrm{ab}}\), \(H_2\cong\mathbb Z^{B_{m,H}}\), and \(H_j=0\) for \(j\ge3\), where
\[
B_{m,H}=\frac{1}{|H|}\sum_{h\in H}\chi_m(h).
\]
Here \(\chi_m\) is the previously computed rational character of the second homology, recorded explicitly below. In particular, for a rotation subgroup \(C_d\),
\[
 B_{m,C_d}=\frac{b_m-d+1+(m/2)\mathbf1_{2\mid d}+(2m/3)\mathbf1_{3\mid d}}{d},
\qquad b_m=\frac{m^3-12m^2+35m-6}{6}.
\]
For \(H=\langle r^{m/d},r^ts\rangle\) with \(|H|=2d\), the same formula uses the reflection terms \(\chi_m(r^{t+qm/d}s)\) for \(0\le q<d\).

The first two parameters in the simply connected range have the **stronger exact homotopy classification**
\[
\begin{aligned}
Q_{8,C_8}&\simeq M(\mathbb Z/4,1),& Q_{8,D_8}&\simeq *,\\
Q_{9,C_9}&\simeq M(\mathbb Z/3,1)\vee S^2,& Q_{9,D_9}&\simeq S^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here \(M(\mathbb Z/q,1)=S^1\cup_{q}e^2\) is the two-dimensional degree-\(q\) Moore space. These statements concern geometric orbit spaces, not homotopy-orbit or Borel constructions.

## Assumptions and scope

All complexes are finite, all actions are the geometric actions on their realizations, and all integral homology has coefficients \(\mathbb Z\). The integer \(m\) is arbitrary at least eight, and the subgroup \(H\) may be any dihedral subgroup. For an order-\(d\) rotation subgroup, its generator is \(r^{m/d}\). A reflection-containing subgroup has order \(2d\), generated by \(r^{m/d}\) and some \(r^ts\). No assertion about smaller parameters or higher homotopy groups is made. The previous nonequivariant wedge-of-spheres theorem and rational character theorem are inputs, not claimed here as new.

## Proof

**1. Simply connected covering complex.** Braun's Theorem 1.4, first publicly available on 2009-12-03, proves that \(X_m\) is a wedge of \(b_m\) two-spheres for every \(m\ge8\). Hence it is connected and simply connected. The graph and actions agree with Braun's definitions under \(k=m-4\).

**2. The complete list of rotational elements having fixed points.** Regard vertices of \(\operatorname{Ind}(G_m)\) as stable chords of the cyclic \(m\)-gon. A face is a pairwise-intersecting family of such two-element sets. Every such family is either a *star* with a common polygon vertex or, in the sole nonstar case, the three edges of a triangle on three pairwise nonconsecutive polygon labels: given two chords \(\{a,b\},\{a,c\}\), every further chord meeting both either contains \(a\) or is \(\{b,c\}\), and no fourth distinct chord can meet all three triangle chords.

If a nonidentity rotation fixes a point of the realization, some nonempty face is invariant as a set, by passing to a barycentric simplex containing the point. A star containing at least two distinct chords has a unique common polygon vertex, which any preserving rotation would have to fix; no nonidentity rotation fixes a polygon label. A single chord can be invariant precisely under the half-turn, of order two; this occurs when \(2\mid m\), with antipodal stable chords as witnesses. An invariant triangle's three labels must form orbits under the rotation. Since every nonidentity rotation acts freely on labels, it must have order three; conversely when \(3\mid m\) the equally spaced triple gives an invariant face, whose barycenter is fixed. Thus the nonidentity fixed-point rotations are **exactly** the elements of order two or order three.

**3. Every reflection has a fixed point.** A reflection acts on polygon labels by \(i\mapsto t-i\). The chord \(\{i,t-i\}\) is invariant; it is stable whenever \(t-2i\not\equiv0,1,-1\pmod m\). Each forbidden congruence has at most two solutions, and \(m\ge8\), so a suitable \(i\) always exists. Accordingly every reflection fixes an actual vertex of the independence complex.

**4. Fundamental groups via the orbit-space theorem.** Armstrong's orbit-space theorem says that, for a finite simplicial group action on a simply connected polyhedron, the fundamental group of the quotient is the acting group modulo the normal subgroup generated by its point-fixing elements. The action here is finite, and barycentric subdivision removes inversions. In a rotation subgroup \(C_d\), the fixed-point elements generate the unique subgroup of order \(\gcd(d,6)\), by Step 2, leaving the cyclic quotient of order \(d/\gcd(d,6)\). A reflection-containing subgroup is generated by two reflections (or by one if it has order two), and Step 3 makes its quotient fundamental group trivial. This proves the group formula for every \(m\) and \(H\) in scope.

**5. A two-dimensional model and integral homology.** Let \(U_m\) be the union of simplices consisting of chords incident to each polygon label. The remaining faces of \(X_m\) are exactly the maximal triangle two-faces described in Step 2, whose entire boundaries lie in \(U_m\). There is a dihedral-equivariant face-poset reduction \(U_m\simeq H_m\), where \(H_m\) is the graph of stable chords on the original polygon labels: send a singleton chord face to the corresponding graph edge and every larger star face to its common label; the inverse poset maps a graph vertex to its full star and a graph edge to its singleton chord face. Their compositions are the identity and an extensive closure operator. The order-homotopy lemma gives an equivariant homotopy equivalence. Attaching the remaining triangle disks via equivariant homotopy pushout yields a two-dimensional finite equivariant CW model of \(X_m\), hence a two-dimensional model for every quotient. This reduction is also present, in equivalent form, in the published dihedral-character calculation listed in the References; it is used, not claimed anew.

It follows that \(H_j(Q_{m,H};\mathbb Z)=0\) for \(j\ge3\) and \(H_2\) is a subgroup of a free abelian cellular two-chain group, hence free. Hurewicz in degree one identifies \(H_1\) with the abelianization of the fundamental group from Step 4. After barycentric subdivision, rational chains of a geometric orbit space are the coinvariant chains of the covering complex; averaging over a finite group identifies their homology with invariant rational homology. Consequently the rank of \(H_2\) is the group-average \(B_{m,H}\) of the previously established character. That character, with \(b_m\) as above, has
\[
\chi_m(r^a)=\begin{cases}
b_m,&a=0,\\
m/2-1,&2\mid m,\ a=m/2,\\
m/3-1,&3\mid m,\ a\in\{m/3,2m/3\},\\
-1,&\text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\]
and \(\chi_m(r^as)=1\) for odd \(m\), while for even \(m\) it equals \(3-m/2\) for even \(a\) and \(m/2-3\) for odd \(a\). Summing the rotation terms gives the displayed \(C_d\) formula; summing the indicated reflections gives the other case. This completes the integral-homology classification.

**6. Exact homotopy types in the first two cases.** The independent standalone programs `orbit_complex.py`, `orbit_presentation.py` and `verify_orbit_cells.py` enumerate all nonempty stable-chord faces and every barycentric flag, then form orbits under the literal cyclic or dihedral permutations. A barycentric flag has strictly increasing face cardinalities, so the group acts without inversions and deleting distinct flag positions gives distinct rank signatures. Thus an orbit cell occurs at most once in the boundary of a given higher cell, and the recorded unique-coface, incidence-one cancellations are genuine elementary collapses.

For \(m=8\), the cyclic quotient has 1105 orbit cells. Exactly 517 verifiable free-face collapses leave 12 vertices, 35 edges and 24 triangles. Contracting a spanning tree gives 24 circle generators and 24 triangular relators. A sequence of 23 reversible generator-relator eliminations leaves a single generator \(u\) and a single relator \(u^4\), proving \(Q_{8,C_8}\simeq M(\mathbb Z/4,1)\). The full dihedral quotient has 569 cells and 284 free-face collapses leave one vertex.

For \(m=9\), the cyclic quotient has 9406 orbit cells; 4646 free-face collapses leave 18 vertices, 56 edges and 40 triangles. A spanning tree leaves 39 circle generators. After 38 reversible generator-relator eliminations, the two remaining attaching words are both \(u^{-3}\). The second attachment is nullhomotopic after the first, giving \(M(\mathbb Z/3,1)\vee S^2\). The full dihedral quotient has 4720 orbit cells; 2341 collapses leave 8 vertices, 18 edges and 12 triangles. Spanning-tree contraction and eleven reversible eliminations leave one trivial two-cell attached to a point, giving \(S^2\). The scripts reconstruct all actual cells and cancellations rather than using archived counts. The general group calculation in Steps 2–4 independently agrees with these finite presentations.

## Verification

From the directory containing the four standalone scripts, run `python3 verify_stabilizers.py` and `python3 verify_orbit_cells.py` with a standard Python 3 interpreter. The first recomputes 100 group-element stabilizer and signed Lefschetz-character cases for \(8\le m\le12\), checks the identity coefficient and the cyclic subgroup arithmetic, and terminates with `STABLE_KNESER_ORBIT_CHECK_OK`. The second constructs exact quotient cells, collapse sequences and relator substitutions in the four cases at \(m=8,9\); it terminates with `STABLE_KNESER_HOMOTOPY_CHECK_OK`. The finite checks confirm, but do not replace, the all-\(m\) stabilizer and fundamental-group arguments.

## Relationship to prior work

Braun's arXiv:0912.0720v1, Theorem 1.4 and Section 3, proves the nonequivariant sphere-wedge classification and identifies star and triangle facets. The published 2026 result *The dihedral character of stable Kneser independence-complex homology* computes all rational dihedral traces and an equivariant star-union reduction. Neither result states the quotient fundamental groups, integral torsion or first two exact quotient attaching-map types; those are the additions here. The rational character alone does not determine the fundamental group or Moore-space attaching maps. Armstrong's orbit-space formula is a classical input, not claimed anew. Schultz's arXiv:1003.5688 concerns the **graph Hom/box complex**, which is not this independence complex. No claim is made about independent validation or exhaustive publication priority.

## Limitations

The all-parameter statement determines \(\pi_1\), every integral homology group, and a homotopy dimension bound, **not** the full homotopy type for every \(m\). The exact Moore-space identifications are proved only for \(m=8,9\). The homotopy computations use finite exhaustive algorithms; their correctness depends on the explicit enumeration and reversible-cancellation code, not merely a stored log. Results under other graph actions or for \(m<8\) are outside scope. A mathematically equivalent but unindexed earlier account remains possible.
