# The last-spoke jump in ordinary three-cut persistence

## Finding

Let \(a,b\ge4\), let \(x\) be a vertex of the \(a\)-vertex shore of \(K_{a,b}\), and let \(G_t\) be obtained by deleting the edges from \(x\) to the first \(t\) vertices \(p_1,\ldots,p_t\) of the other shore. Let \(\Delta_t=\Delta_3(G_t)\) be the ordinary three-cut complex and \(L_t=\Delta_t^\vee\) its combinatorial Alexander dual on the fixed set of \(N=a+b\) vertices. Set \(C=\binom{a-2}{2}\binom{b-1}{2}\).

On deleting the final edge \(xp_b\), the dual changes from
\[
|L_{b-1}|\simeq \bigvee^{b-1}S^1\vee\bigvee^C S^3
\quad\text{to}\quad
|L_b|\simeq \bigvee^{b+a-2} S^1\vee\bigvee^C S^3.
\]
The inclusion \(L_b\hookrightarrow L_{b-1}\) is an isomorphism on \(H_3(-;\mathbb Z)\) and a split surjection on \(H_1(-;\mathbb Z)\), with a free kernel of rank \(a-1\). Thus \(\Delta_{b-1}\hookrightarrow\Delta_b\) is an isomorphism on \(\widetilde H_{N-6}(-;\mathbb Z)\), of rank \(C\), and a primitive split injection on \(\widetilde H_{N-4}(-;\mathbb Z)\) with free cokernel of rank \(a-1\). These are the only possible nonzero reduced homology degrees of the two original complexes. The contribution is the isolated-hub terminal step and its integral map, not the nonterminal persistence preceding it.

## Definitions and scope

The graphs are finite, undirected and simple. The ordinary three-cut complex is generated by the complements of all disconnected induced triples, not just independent triples. Consequently its Alexander dual consists of sets all of whose induced triples are connected. In particular, all two-element sets are allowed in the dual whether or not they are edges of the graph. The wedge statements refer to the dual realizations, not to the homotopy types of the original cut complexes.

## Proof

Write the shores as \(\{x\}\sqcup U\) and \(B=\{p_1,\ldots,p_b\}\), with \(|U|=a-1\). For a set \(D\), let \(E(D)\) be the simplicial complex of subsets of \(D\) of size at most two. Let \(D_0(U)\) consist of the empty face and singletons in \(U\), and put \(Z=E(U)*E(B)\). At both stages, deleting \(x\) from the dual leaves exactly \(Z\): a triple with two vertices from one shore and one from the other is connected in the intact biclique on \(U\sqcup B\), whereas any same-shore triple is disconnected. Both factors of \(Z\) are connected graphs. For \(|D|=s\ge2\), \(E(D)\) has the homotopy type of a wedge of \(\binom{s-1}{2}\) circles by collapsing a spanning tree. The join formula gives \(|Z|\simeq\bigvee^{C}S^3\).

At stage \(b-1\), the only graph neighbor of \(x\) is \(p_b\). The triangles containing \(x\) in the dual are precisely \(\{x,u,p_b\}\) for \(u\in U\), with no higher-dimensional faces containing \(x\). Accordingly, apart from the \(b-1\) edges \(xp_j\) for \(j<b\), it attaches a cone with apex \(x\) over \(D_0(U)*\{p_b\}\) to \(Z\). This base is a contractible star. The attachment does not change the homotopy type of \(Z\) because the base inclusion is a simplicial cofibration and the base and attaching cone are contractible. Each exceptional edge has its endpoints in the resulting connected core and appears in no triangle, so adds a wedge circle.

At stage \(b\) there are no allowed triples through \(x\), and its cone now attaches along \(D_0(U)\) alone, with the \(b\) edges \(xp_j\) attached separately. The inclusion \(|D_0(U)|\hookrightarrow |Z|\) is nullhomotopic because its domain is a discrete set and \(|Z|\) is path connected. The cone attachment is the homotopy cofiber of that inclusion, and therefore wedges onto \(Z\) the unreduced suspension of \(a-1\) points, a wedge of \(a-2\) circles. The \(b\) exceptional edges add a further \(b\) circles. This gives the stated terminal homotopy type.

The last inclusion leaves all 3-simplices unchanged: in either stage they lie in \(Z\). The only simplices removed from \(L_{b-1}\) to form \(L_b\) are the fan triangles \(\{x,u,p_b\}\), which do not occur in the boundary of any 3-simplex. Both complexes are at most 3-dimensional, so identifying the 3-chains identifies the kernels of \(\partial_3\) and gives the claimed \(H_3\) isomorphism. For \(H_1\), fix \(u_0\in U\). At stage \(b-1\) the loops \(x,p_j,u_0,x\) for \(j<b\) are an integral basis, one per exceptional edge. At stage \(b\), use all \(b\) such loops together with \(x,u_0,u,x\) for \(u\in U\setminus\{u_0\}\). To see the basis claim directly, the terminal complex attaches a new vertex via an edge to every vertex of \(U\cup B\); choose \(xu_0\) as its first attachment, then use the indicated paths inside the simply connected \(Z\) to close each remaining edge into a loop. Under inclusion the first \(b-1\) loops go to the old basis. The \(j=b\) loop bounds the triangle \(\{x,p_b,u_0\}\). The additional \(U\)-loops bound the three faces \(\{x,u_0,p_b\},\{x,u,p_b\},\{u_0,u,p_b\}\), with consistent orientations. This gives the kernel of rank \(1+(a-2)=a-1\) and a splitting.

Combinatorial Alexander duality on the fixed ground set gives natural isomorphisms \(\widetilde H_i(\Delta_t;\mathbb Z)\cong\widetilde H^{N-i-3}(L_t;\mathbb Z)\). Because the dual homology is free, the universal coefficient theorem identifies the cohomology maps with the duals of the computed homology maps. This gives the asserted isomorphism and primitive split injection for the original complexes.

## Verification and limitations

The proof is integral and not limited to a finite range. `verify_starcut.py` and `verification_output.txt` document checks of face sets and mod-two Betti numbers at all stages for \(4\le a,b\le6\), including both stages used here. The finite computation does not certify integral maps. The theorem addresses only the terminal inclusion in this specified deletion process and asserts neither the homotopy type nor equivariant invariants of the original cut complex.

## Comparison with available results

- *Integral cut-homology persistence under a bipartite star defect* (Resultary record [f726abd4eca3](https://github.com/Resultary/2026/tree/main/2026/11/22/SCOPE-integral-cut-homology-persistence-under-a-bipartite-star-defect--f726abd4eca3)) is described in its indexed statement as treating the same defect up to \(b-1\) edges removed, with the same two integral homology degrees and a primitive higher-degree birth at each of those deletions. No priority for the nonterminal range is asserted here. The catalog summary stops before the terminal deletion; it does not describe a map to the isolated-hub stage. This comparison is at the level of the available indexed statement, not a claim about priority from dates or about all the contents of the source.
- Bayer et al., *Topology of Cut Complexes of Graphs* ([arXiv:2304.13675](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13675), Thm. 7.4), treat intact complete bipartite graphs. Bayer et al., *Topology of Cut Complexes II* ([arXiv:2407.08158](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.08158)), describe face and \(h\)-polynomials for disjoint unions and homology representations for unions of two complete graphs. The terminal graph factors as \(K_{a-1,b}\sqcup K_1\), so general disjoint-union facts may apply to endpoint face data. The last-step comparison here also specifies the Alexander dual's homotopy type and the map from the preceding, connected graph, not just data on the endpoint by itself.
- The one-edge deletion record (Resultary 897c03454bbf) and the partial-matching deletion record (ab19f54685e2) address the one-edge endpoint and faults with disjoint endpoints, respectively. A final-spoke transition for a robust clique complex (e126d0127f67) is for a different simplicial predicate. These statements do not specify the ordinary three-cut terminal map proved here.
