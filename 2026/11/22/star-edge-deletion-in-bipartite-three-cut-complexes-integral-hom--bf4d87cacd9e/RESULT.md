# Star-edge deletion in bipartite three-cut complexes: integral homotopy and two-degree persistence

## Finding
Let \(a,b\ge4\). Fix a hub \(x\) in the \(a\)-vertex shore of \(K_{a,b}\), order the vertices \(p_1,\ldots,p_b\) of its other shore, and write \(G_t=K_{a,b}\setminus\{xp_j:1\le j\le t\}\), for \(0\le t\le b\). Let \(\Delta_t=\Delta_3(G_t)\) be the **ordinary** three-cut complex, and let \(L_t=\Delta_t^{\vee}\) denote its combinatorial Alexander dual on the common vertex set of size \(N=a+b\). Define
\[
C=\binom{a-2}{2}\binom{b-1}{2},\qquad q=b-t,
\]
with \(\binom{m}{2}=0\) for \(m<2\). Then
\[
\boxed{|L_t|\simeq
\begin{cases}
\bigvee^{t}S^1\vee\bigvee^{C+(a-2)\binom{q-1}{2}}S^3,&0\le t<b,\\
\bigvee^{b+a-2}S^1\vee\bigvee^{C}S^3,&t=b.
\end{cases}}
\]
A zero-fold wedge is omitted, and the unsubscripted wedge point is allowed to vary up to homotopy. In particular, \(\widetilde H_{N-6}(\Delta_t;\mathbb Z)\) and \(\widetilde H_{N-4}(\Delta_t;\mathbb Z)\) are free of the respective three-sphere and circle multiplicities, and all other reduced integral homology groups vanish.

The inclusions \(\Delta_{t-1}\hookrightarrow\Delta_t\) induce **primitive split surjections** on \(\widetilde H_{N-6}(-;\mathbb Z)\). Their ranks lost are \((a-2)(b-t-1)\) for \(1\le t\le b-2\), and zero for the final two steps. In degree \(N-4\), these maps are **primitive split injections**, adding one class per deletion for \(1\le t<b\) and an exceptional \(a-1\) new classes at \(t=b\). The endpoints of zero and one missing edge recover known results; the new conclusion is the complete multi-edge star-fault filtration and its terminal jump.

For example, with \(a=5\) and \(b=6\), the consecutive lower-degree ranks are \(60,48,39,33,30,30,30\), while the higher-degree ranks are \(0,1,2,3,4,5,9\).

## Assumptions and scope
All graphs are finite, simple and undirected. The deleted edges have one common endpoint \(x\); no other edges are removed. The ordinary three-cut complex is generated by complements of all three-vertex subsets inducing *disconnected* subgraphs, rather than only independent triples (the total cut convention). Its combinatorial Alexander dual comprises subsets containing no disconnected triple. All groups are integral unless a finite-field test is explicitly indicated. Homotopy types refer to realizations of the Alexander dual; only homology is claimed for the original cut complex. The parameter bounds \(a,b\ge4\) avoid exceptional small skeletons and ensure all stated degree separations.

## Proof
**1. Exhaustive classification of dual faces.** Write the two shores \(A=\{x\}\sqcup U\), \(|U|=a-1\), and \(B=P_t\sqcup Q_t\) with \(|P_t|=t\), \(|Q_t|=q\). Three vertices in a bipartite graph induce a connected graph precisely when they consist of one vertex in one shore and two adjacent-to-it vertices in the other shore. Thus a subset in \(L_t\) has at most two vertices in each shore. If it does not contain \(x\), it has no further restriction; if it contains \(x\), it may include at most one element of \(U\) and at most two elements of \(Q_t\). The additional pairs \(\{x,p\}\), \(p\in P_t\), are faces of the dual but belong to no triangles, because every triple containing the missing edge \(xp\) is disconnected. Put \(E(W)=\operatorname{sk}_1\Delta^{|W|-1}\), \(D(U)=\operatorname{sk}_0\Delta^{|U|-1}\), with the standard empty-face convention. The following is therefore **an exact identity of simplicial complexes**:
\[
L_t=\bigl(E(U)*E(B)\bigr)\ \cup_{D(U)*E(Q_t)}\ \bigl(x*(D(U)*E(Q_t))\bigr)
\ \cup\bigcup_{p\in P_t}\{x,p\}.
\]
When \(q=0\), interpret \(E(Q_t)=\{\varnothing\}\), so the attaching subcomplex is \(D(U)\), not a nonexistent geometric graph. This identity uses only the definition of connected induced triples.

**2. Contractible-cone attachment and nullhomotopy.** The base \(Z=E(U)*E(B)\) is path connected and simply connected, and the cone on \(x\) attaches along \(W_t=D(U)*E(Q_t)\). The inclusion \(D(U)\hookrightarrow E(U)\) is nullhomotopic because \(D(U)\) is a discrete set and \(E(U)\) is path connected. Taking joins with \(E(Q_t)\hookrightarrow E(B)\) shows \(W_t\hookrightarrow Z\) is nullhomotopic when \(q\ge1\): one joins the homotopy to a constant map, after which the join factors through the contractible cone \(\{\mathrm{pt}\}*E(B)\) inside \(Z\). At \(q=0\) the map is the same nullhomotopic inclusion of the discrete set \(D(U)\) into the connected space \(Z\). The homotopy-cofiber description of a cone attachment therefore yields
\[
\bigl|Z\cup_{W_t}(x*W_t)\bigr|\simeq |Z|\vee\Sigma|W_t|.
\]
Here \(\Sigma\) is the unreduced suspension; for nonempty connected \(W_t\) it has the usual reduced homotopy type. The attaching maps are actual simplicial cofibrations, so there is no hidden homotopy-pushout assumption.

**3. Compute the two pieces integrally.** For any finite \(|V|=s\ge2\), the complete graph as a one-dimensional complex \(E(V)\) has type \(\bigvee^{\binom{s-1}{2}}S^1\). Also \(D(U)\simeq\bigvee^{a-2}S^0\). The join formula consequently gives
\[
|Z|\simeq\bigvee^C S^3.
\]
If \(q\ge1\), the join \(D(U)*E(Q_t)\) is contractible for \(q=1,2\), and is a wedge of \((a-2)\binom{q-1}{2}\) two-spheres for \(q\ge3\). Suspending produces that many three-spheres. If \(q=0\), the attaching set is the discrete space \(D(U)\) with \(a-1\) points, and its suspension is a wedge of \(a-2\) circles. These are integral homotopy descriptions, not merely field-dependent Betti numbers.

**4. Add the exceptional edges.** For every deleted edge \(xp\), both endpoints already lie in the path-connected cone-attached core, but the one-simplex \(xp\) is absent from that core and from all its triangles. Attaching each of the \(t\) intervals between endpoints therefore wedges on one further circle. Combining this fact with steps 2–3 proves both claimed homotopy formulas for \(L_t\), including the isolated-hub case \(q=0\).

**5. Exact homology and primitive persistence.** Combinatorial Alexander duality on the fixed set of \(N\) vertices gives natural isomorphisms
\[
\widetilde H_i(\Delta_t;\mathbb Z)\cong\widetilde H^{N-i-3}(L_t;\mathbb Z).
\]
The wedge decompositions show that the groups are free and concentrated in the two stated degrees. To determine the *actual maps*, note that \(L_t\subseteq L_{t-1}\), and each \(L_t\) has simplicial dimension at most three. Hence its degree-three homology is the sublattice \(\ker(\partial_3)\subset C_3(L_t;\mathbb Z)\); inclusion into \(H_3(L_{t-1};\mathbb Z)\) is injective. Moreover its cokernel injects into the coordinate quotient \(C_3(L_{t-1};\mathbb Z)/C_3(L_t;\mathbb Z)\), which is free, so the degree-three inclusion is **primitive** and split. Dualizing gives the asserted split-surjectivity for degree \(N-6\) of the original cut complexes. Subtracting the displayed ranks yields \((a-2)(b-t-1)\) lost when \(1\le t\le b-2\) and zero at \(t=b-1,b\).

For degree one, fix any \(u\in U\). The old circle generators at stage \(t\) are represented by the distinct loops \(x-p_j-u-x\) for \(1\le j\le t\). For \(t=b\), add \(a-2\) difference loops \(x-u-v-x\) as \(v\) varies through \(U\setminus\{u\}\). These loops form integral bases by the free-circle decomposition and the distinct exceptional-edge coordinates; the latter family is a basis for the suspension of \(D(U)\). Under \(L_t\hookrightarrow L_{t-1}\), loops involving earlier missing edges retain their basis classes. The newly filled edge \(xp_t\) kills its own exceptional loop, and at the last step a remaining \(Q_{b-1}\) vertex fills each extra difference loop by triangles. Thus the reverse map on \(H_1\) is always a split surjection, with kernel rank one at stages \(1\le t<b\), and \(a-1\) at \(t=b\). The universal coefficient theorem and natural Alexander duality turn these into the claimed split injections for \(\widetilde H_{N-4}(\Delta_{t-1};\mathbb Z)\to\widetilde H_{N-4}(\Delta_t;\mathbb Z)\). This proves the complete integral persistence statement.

## Verification
The accompanying standard-library Python file `verify_starcut.py` computes disconnected triples by an independent breadth-first connectivity test, enumerates *every* face of their Alexander dual, and separately constructs the proof's proposed union-of-joins model. It compares the full face sets and performs complete mod-two simplicial boundary elimination for each pair \(4\le a,b\le6\) at every \(0\le t\le b\): **54 exhaustive finite cases passed**. It also prints all seven states for \(a=5,b=6\), including the final circle-rank jump. The recorded exact standard output is in `verification_output.txt`. The general integral, infinite-family theorem and primitive maps follow from the topological argument above; the finite-field computation alone would not prove them.

## Relationship to prior work
Bayer and collaborators [1, Theorem 7.4] classify the intact \(K_{a,b}\) cut complexes but do not treat a noncomplete bipartite graph with several incident edge deletions. Their sequel [2] addresses disjoint unions, squared paths, grids and group actions, not star-deficient bicliques. A published one-missing-edge result [3] already computes precisely the case \(t=1\); that case is background, not new. The published partial-*matching* deletion theorem [4] allows several deleted edges with **disjoint endpoints**. For \(t\ge2\), our defect has a common endpoint and does not meet its hypotheses; its rank polynomial also differs. A square-filling theorem [5] assumes no pair of vertices has more than two common neighbors; \(a,b\ge4\) here violates that hypothesis. The known robust-clique star-defect theorem [6] treats a different simplicial condition, bounding independence numbers in every selected vertex subset rather than requiring all triples to be connected. Neither that theorem nor the one-edge result implies the present repeated-deletion and final-phase statements. The novelty claim is restricted to the latter claims, subject to the possibility of unindexed equivalent literature.

## Limitations
Only incident-edge star defects of complete bipartite graphs with \(a,b\ge4\) and the ordinary three-cut convention are covered. The full homotopy type of the original cut complex, equivariant group actions, and non-star deletion patterns are not determined. The homotopy statements apply to the **Alexander dual**; the original complex is classified only in integral homology and by its induced maps. Finite computer checks are corroborative, not infinite or integral certificates. The historical literature search cannot rule out all unpublished or unindexed equivalent formulations.
