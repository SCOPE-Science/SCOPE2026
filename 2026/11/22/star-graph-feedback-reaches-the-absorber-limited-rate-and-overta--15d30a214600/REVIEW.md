# Scientific review

**Correctness (PASS).** The graph-specific pair generator is reconstructed from the published, exact Kraus-operator closure. Its bipartite incidence block has fully explicit singular values, proving all eigenvalues and the unique positive lowest mode. Rationalization proves the asymptotic absorber-bound saturation; an exact sign polynomial, four algebraic boundary cases and a uniform cosine estimate prove the ring comparison. Direct finite Kraus-operator calculations independently corroborate the normalization at small sizes. No numerical experiment is used to prove the all-size result.

**Originality (PASS, qualified).** The closest November 2026 paper already proves the arbitrary-graph pair closure and the periodic-ring gap, so neither is claimed as new. Its graph-specific exact solutions concern rings, hypercubic geometries and all-to-all monitoring, not the star incidence spectrum or the exact change of ordering at six to seven sites. The October 2025 precursor likewise establishes a generic random-walk transport mapping and one-dimensional scaling rather than this star theorem. Searches for star-graph Dicke preparation, singlet-projector star feedback, star pair-graph decay spectra and synonyms did not locate a prior statement covering the exact spectrum and comparison. These are evidence-based coverage comparisons, not a claim that unindexed equivalent work is impossible.

**Value (PASS).** This is a natural comparison of two connected low-edge-count measurement geometries for the same fully symmetric Dicke target. It shows that a hub topology with only \(n-1\) measured bonds achieves the maximal leading-order absorber throughput, replacing the ring's quadratic pair-relaxation time by linear scaling under equal edge clock rates. The exact finite-size crossover and complete spectral decomposition quantify when this advantage begins. The high-degree hub's growing aggregate clock rate is a material physical limitation and is explicitly stated.
