# Steiner-pair descent on cubic surfaces: a sharp degree-five bound and a quarter-density tritangent obstruction

## Finding
Let \(S/\mathbb F_q\) be any smooth cubic surface, and let \(\mathscr T_S\) be the reduced finite étale \(\mathbb F_q\)-scheme whose \(120\) geometric points are unordered **Steiner trihedral pairs** on \(S\). A point of \(\mathscr T_S\) is rational when Frobenius preserves the pair as a set; the six constituent tritangent planes need not each be rational. Define
\[
d_{\rm St}(S)=\min\{n\geq1:\mathscr T_S(\mathbb F_{q^n})\ne\varnothing\}.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{d_{\rm St}(S)\in\{1,2,3,4,5\}.}
\]
For the canonical action of \(W(E_6)\) on the \(120\) pairs, the group-element distribution of the minimum cycle length is exactly:

| Minimum cycle length | Elements of \(W(E_6)\) |
|:--:|--:|
| \(1\) | 22752 |
| \(2\) | 6480 |
| \(3\) | 5760 |
| \(4\) | 6480 |
| \(5\) | 10368 |

Among the \(51840\) group elements, exactly \(12960\) fix a tritangent plane but fix no Steiner trihedral pair. Consequently, by the published Frobenius equidistribution theorem for smooth cubic surfaces, the limiting fraction of \(\mathbb F_q\)-isomorphism classes with this tritangent-versus-Steiner distinction as \(q\to\infty\) is
\[
\boxed{12960/51840=1/4.}
\]
These are asymptotic frequencies, not exact proportions for any fixed finite field.

The degree-five bound is attained geometrically for **every** prime power \(q\ge4\). In the explicit class of examples below, the Frobenius cycle profiles on the \(27\) lines, \(45\) tritangent planes, and \(120\) Steiner pairs are respectively
\[
1^2 5^5,\qquad 5^9,\qquad 5^{24}.
\]
In particular, a rational exceptional line exists, while neither a rational tritangent nor a rational Steiner pair exists before extension degree five.

## Assumptions and scope
The finite field has arbitrary characteristic. All geometric lines and Steiner pairs are defined after extension to an algebraic closure. A *Steiner trihedron* is a triple of pairwise line-disjoint tritangent planes admitting exactly three conjugate tritangent planes. The conjugate triple constitutes the partner, and the unordered pair has a unique support consisting of nine lines. The word *rational* applies to the unordered pair as a Frobenius-stable finite configuration, not to its individual components. The theorem concerns descent of these finite configurations, not the Brauer group or a minimal splitting field for all \(27\) lines.

## Proof
1. Over an algebraic closure, write \(S\) as the blowup of the plane at six general-position points. In the Picard basis \((H,E_1,\ldots,E_6)\), the \(27\) exceptional curves have classes \(E_i\), \(L_{ij}=H-E_i-E_j\) for \(i<j\), and \(Q_i=2H-\sum_{j\ne i}E_j\). The \(45\) tritangents correspond to the \(30\) triples \(\{E_i,L_{ij},Q_j\}\) for \(i\ne j\), and the \(15\) triples of \(L_{ij}\)'s indexed by perfect matchings of six labels.

2. Construct all unordered triples of those \(45\) tritangents which have nine distinct component lines. Their nine-line supports are \(5160\) distinct subsets: \(5040\) occur once as a triple of tritangents and \(120\) occur exactly twice. A repeated support gives a \(3\)-by-\(3\) grid, since distinct tritangent triples cannot share two lines: each of the two partitions consists of the three rows or three columns. Conversely every Steiner trihedral pair supplies those two partitions. The \(120\) repeated supports therefore realize the canonical \(120\)-point scheme; this also agrees with the classical \(20+10+90\) type classification of Elsenhans–Jahnel.

3. The Picard intersection form is \(H^2=1\), \(E_i^2=-1\), with distinct basis vectors orthogonal. Use the six roots \(E_{i+1}-E_i\) for \(1\le i\le5\) and \(H-E_1-E_2-E_3\). Reflection in a root \(\alpha\) of square \(-2\) sends \(D\) to \(D+(D\cdot\alpha)\alpha\). Its permutation on the \(27\) exceptional curves is computed by exact integer arithmetic. The six reflections generate \(W(E_6)\), of order \(51840\). All six preserve the repeated-support set, as checked explicitly.

4. Exhaustively form the group as permutations generated by those reflections. For each of its \(51840\) elements compute the induced permutation on the \(120\) supports, its cycle decomposition, and the shortest cycle length. The full, asserted counts are \(22752,6480,5760,6480,10368\) for shortest lengths \(1,2,3,4,5\). The same enumeration on the \(45\) tritangents gives \(12960\) elements with a tritangent fixed point and no pair fixed point. The executable finite certificate enumerates *every* group element, with no sampling; the independent conventional conjugacy-class count is \(25\), and the computation has \(25\) distinct combined cycle-profile signatures. For any smooth \(S/\mathbb F_q\), geometric Frobenius acts through this Weyl group. A Steiner pair is \(\mathbb F_{q^n}\)-rational exactly when it lies in a cycle of length dividing \(n\), so its first field of rationality is the least cycle length. This proves the universal bound and complete group-element census.

5. For sharpness, choose a degree-five element \(\alpha\in\mathbb F_{q^5}\) and the five Frobenius-conjugate points \(P_i=(1:\alpha^{q^i}:\alpha^{2q^i})\), \(0\le i<5\), on \(XZ=Y^2\). Every secant determined by two of these points is nonrational: no two-element subset of the five-cycle is Frobenius-fixed. A nonrational line has at most one \(\mathbb F_q\)-point. Hence at most ten rational points lie on these secants. Since the smooth conic has exactly \(q+1\) rational points, at least \(q^2-10>0\) rational points lie outside both the conic and the ten secants when \(q\ge4\). Choose one such point as \(P_5\). The six geometric points are in general position: no three are collinear, and no conic contains all six (two distinct conics cannot share five points without a component). Blow them up to obtain a smooth cubic surface over \(\mathbb F_q\). Its Frobenius acts as the five-cycle on the first five exceptional labels and fixes the sixth. The exact permutation calculation gives \(24\) five-cycles on the Steiner pairs. Thus \(d_{\rm St}(S)=5\) for every \(q\ge4\).

6. Banwait–Fité–Loughran, Theorem 1.9, gives equidistribution of Weyl conjugacy classes among cubic-surface \(\mathbb F_q\)-isomorphism classes as \(q\to\infty\), with weight \(|C|/51840\) for each conjugacy class \(C\). The tritangent/Steiner property is conjugacy-invariant and occupies \(12960\) Weyl elements, yielding the stated limit \(1/4\).

## Relationship to prior work
Elsenhans–Jahnel (2010) defined the \(120\) unordered Steiner pairs, their \(20+10+90\) geometric classes in a blowup labeling, and their transitive Weyl action; they studied Galois-invariant pairs and constructive descent. Rybakov–Trepalin (2016) tabulated the Weyl Frobenius classes for cubic surfaces, without the \(120\)-pair extension-degree census. Banwait–Fité–Loughran (2016) established the asymptotic Weyl-class distribution and their Table 7.1 concerns the \(27\) exceptional lines. Loughran–Trepalin (2018) solved finite-field inverse Galois realizability, not the shortest-orbit distribution of Steiner pairs. Previously established complete Frobenius classifications by the \(40\) unordered Steiner-triplet zeta spectrum and by the combined \(27\)-line/\(45\)-tritangent spectra can recover the Weyl type, but do not state the \(120\)-pair shortest-orbit census, the quarter-density obstruction, or the uniform geometric degree-five realization. The result here combines the classical \(120\)-point action with a complete computed orbit invariant, an explicit geometric degree-five realization, and a resulting rational-marking density. No priority claim is made for the pre-existing \(120\)-pair geometry or Weyl equidistribution theorem.

## Limitations
The five-column census counts Weyl group *elements*, not the number of cubic surfaces over a given fixed field; some classes can be absent over small fields. The density assertion is asymptotic and relies on the cited equidistribution theorem. The construction realizes the maximal minimal degree five over all finite fields of cardinality at least four, but does not assert a separate realization of every other degree over every field. This is a self-review, not an independent audit. Older unindexed tables for the \(120\)-point Weyl action may pose a residual priority risk.
