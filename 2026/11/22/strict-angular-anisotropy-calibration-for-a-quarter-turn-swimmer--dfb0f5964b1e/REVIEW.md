# Scientific assessment: stationary fourfold angular calibration

## Correctness — PASS
The stationary two-coordinate beta product is established in Smith et al. (2022), and its normalization is reproduced. The ratio identity reduces the observable to an expectation of a bounded even strictly distance-decreasing function of a log-beta difference. Symmetric unimodality is proved separately by log-concavity for shape at least one and by a positive mixture-of-exponentials expansion for shape at most one. Beta multiplication adds independent nondegenerate noise to the log ratio, so strict concentration loss proves the all-parameter ordering. Both sharp endpoints, the exactly integrable unit-ratio case, and the large-ratio Gaussian expansion are independently derived. The bundled script ran 38 numerical consistency assertions. Numerical evaluation is supplementary and never used to justify the continuum theorem.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)
The 2022 source states the exact stationary joint density and angular marginal and describes anisotropy qualitatively, but does not prove strict parameter monotonicity for the single fourth angular harmonic or its global inverse identification. A directly inspected public radial-shell result treats a different pushforward of the same source law; a directly inspected fourth radial-moment envelope treats isotropically oriented general-turn swimmers, not the square-supported angular harmonic. Targeted comparisons for Fourier anisotropy, beta multiplicative coupling, equivalent log-ratio formulations and dominating angular-order statements returned no directly covering theorem. This does not establish publication priority against unindexed papers, missing source appendices, or a stronger theorem whose consequence is this strict harmonic ordering. The identified primary source's discussion of high- and low-rate anisotropy is qualitative and does not imply all-parameter strictness without further argument.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The observable is a natural fourfold symmetry diagnostic for the explicitly four-direction swimmer. The theorem makes it globally injective for every positive turn/trap ratio even though planar position-density mode types change at a different parameter value. Unlike numerical illustrations at selected rates, strict parameter-ordering eliminates ambiguous calibrations from the stationary angular distribution; invariance under propulsion speed removes an otherwise unknown scale. The proof uses a nontrivial probabilistic structure of beta factors and symmetric unimodality, not a renamed forward moment formula. The result is scoped to an exactly solvable model and does not claim finite-sample efficiency or broader swimmer classes.
