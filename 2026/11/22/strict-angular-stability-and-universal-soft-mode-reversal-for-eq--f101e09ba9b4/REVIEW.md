# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The boundary covering-speed identity gives \\(|B'(e^{it})|\\) as the sum of \\(n\\) Poisson kernels. Parseval produces the exact moment variance \\(2\\sum_{m\\ge1}r^{2m}|\\sum_j e^{im\\theta_j}|^2\\); all angular derivatives converge uniformly for each fixed \\(r<1\\). A second-variation calculation with unitary Fourier normalization and real conjugate pairs gives the \\(n-1\\) positive half-Hessian eigenvalues specified in `RESULT.md`. Positivity follows for every radius and degree by representing the residue difference as a negative second derivative of a strictly concave hyperbolic parallel sum. Direct endpoint expansions and finite-dimensional spectral continuity establish an actual reversal of least-curved modes for every \\(n\\ge5\\). The standalone `check_hessian.py` passed 1,399 supplementary tests, including independent pair-kernel finite differences. The proof does not depend on extrapolating these tests.

## Originality — PASS, best knowledge
Kovalev–Yang (arXiv:2007.09760, 2020) examine the extreme maximum and minimum values of the boundary derivative, not this \\(L^2\\) variance Hessian. Gluchoff–Hartmann (2013), Section 4, analyze angular positioning for degree two and three in the interior Hardy mean of \\(|B|^2\\), while their derivative estimates concern separate Hardy means; the all-degree boundary-speed spectral statement is not implied by these results. The earlier degree-four boundary-speed stiffness analysis does not cover \\(n\\ge5\\). Two independently published equispaced-pole Hessian calculations are for different **weighted Bergman-pole energies**, with different moments and do not imply the Poisson-speed eigenvalues or their reversed ordering. Comparisons were at the level of objects, hypotheses, derived formulas, and conclusions, rather than only titles. Older unindexed circular-energy work and inaccurate mathematical transcription in a secondary full-text host remain historical-priority risks. Novelty is provisional pending independent literature review.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The constant-radius placement of zeros governs distortion of the circle covering induced by a finite Blaschke product. The theorem proves strict angular stability for **every degree and radius in the new range** and computes its full Hessian, including a structurally forced switch in the least-resistant deformation from high to low angular frequency as the zeros move outward. This supplies a sharp local conditioning invariant for phase-distortion problems rather than a numerical example or routine restatement of positivity. No claim is made that the regular configuration is the unique global minimizer or that the crossing is unique.
