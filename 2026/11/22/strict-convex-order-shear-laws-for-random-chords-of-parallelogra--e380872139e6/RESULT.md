# Strict convex-order shear laws for random chords of parallelograms

## Finding

Fix two positive **half-side lengths** \(a,b>0\), and let \(P_c\) be a Euclidean parallelogram generated by vectors \(u_c,v_c\) with \(|u_c|=a\), \(|v_c|=b\), and \(u_c\cdot v_c=ab c\), where \(0\le c<1\). Its perimeter is \(4(a+b)\). Choose two points independently, either uniformly in area (superscript \(\circ\)) or uniformly in boundary arclength (superscript \(\partial\)); denote their squared Euclidean distance by \(Z_c^\circ,Z_c^\partial\).

**Theorem (strict angular convex ordering, with optimal quadratic modulus).** For either sampling convention and any \(0\le c_1<c_2<1\),
\[
 Z_{c_1}^{\star}\ <_{\rm cx}\ Z_{c_2}^{\star},\qquad \star\in\{\circ,\partial\}.
\]
Here \(<_{\rm cx}\) means equal expectations, inequality for *all* continuous convex test functions on \([0,4(a+b)^2]\), and a strict inequality for every strictly convex such function. The common means are
\[
 \mathbb E Z_c^\partial=\frac{2(a+b)^2}{3},\qquad
 \mathbb E Z_c^\circ=\frac{2(a^2+b^2)}3.
\]
More precisely, if \(\Phi\in C^2([0,4(a+b)^2])\) satisfies \(\Phi''\ge m\ge0\), then
\[
\begin{split}
\mathbb E\Phi(Z_{c_2}^\partial)-\mathbb E\Phi(Z_{c_1}^\partial)
&\ge 2m a^2b^2\left(\frac43+\frac{8ab}{9(a+b)^2}\right)(c_2^2-c_1^2),\\
\mathbb E\Phi(Z_{c_2}^\circ)-\mathbb E\Phi(Z_{c_1}^\circ)
&\ge \frac{8m}{9}a^2b^2(c_2^2-c_1^2).
\end{split}
\]
**Both constants are best possible for the stated curvature class**, with equality whenever \(\Phi(z)=mz^2/2+\alpha z+\beta\).

Consequently the mean \(p\)-th Euclidean distance between two points, for every fixed \(p>0\), is strictly **decreasing** as \(c\) increases when \(0<p<2\), strictly **increasing** for \(p>2\), and independent of \(c\) for \(p=2\). Thus, at fixed side lengths and either sampling convention, the rectangle is the unique maximizing shape for subquadratic moments and the unique minimizing shape for superquadratic moments. The infimum/supremum in the opposite direction is approached as the parallelogram collapses to a segment.

For boundary sampling the collinear endpoint is independent even of the individual side lengths: writing \(s=a+b\), for every \(p>0\),
\[
\lim_{c\uparrow1}\mathbb E\bigl[(Z_c^\partial)^{p/2}\bigr]
=\frac{2^{p+1}s^p}{(p+1)(p+2)}.
\]
In particular the sharp limiting mean distance divided by perimeter is \(1/6\), approached from above by every fixed-side-length shear family.

## Assumptions and scope

There are two different probability laws: normalized two-dimensional area on the interior of \(P_c\), and normalized one-dimensional Euclidean arclength on \(\partial P_c\). The endpoints are independent and sampled with replacement. The vectors generating the parallelogram have lengths \(a,b\), so the geometric sides have lengths \(2a,2b\). Replacing a negative inner-product cosine by its absolute value gives a congruent parallelogram; hence \(c\in[0,1)\) covers all included angles up to congruence. The limiting collinear configuration \(c=1\) is **not** a nondegenerate member of the class. Convex order compares the entire distributions of *squared distances*, not stochastic domination of distances and not radial distance to the center.

## Proof

**1. Angle-independent coordinate law.** Center the parallelogram at zero and choose unit vectors \(e_1,e_2\) with \(e_1\cdot e_2=c\), so \(u_c=ae_1\), \(v_c=be_2\). Every point has coefficients \(X=aWe_1+bVe_2\). For area sampling, \(W,V\) are independent uniform variables on \([-1,1]\). For boundary sampling, put \(s=a+b\): with probability \(a/s\), take \((W,V)=(T,\varepsilon)\), and otherwise take \((W,V)=(\varepsilon,T)\), where \(T\) is uniform on \([-1,1]\) and \(\varepsilon\) is an independent symmetric sign. This is exactly normalized arclength: the two parallel sides of length \(2a\) have total weight \(a/s\), and the other two have total weight \(b/s\).

In **either** model the joint law of \((W,V)\) is invariant under reversing either coordinate sign separately. For independent copies, write \(U=W-W'\), \(V_*=V-V'\). The joint law of \((U,V_*)\) is invariant under \((U,V_*)\mapsto(U,-V_*)\). Consequently
\[
 Z_c=a^2U^2+b^2V_*^2+2ab cUV_*=A+cJ,
 \quad A=a^2U^2+b^2V_*^2,\quad J=2abUV_*;
\]
and the joint law of \((A,J)\) is invariant under \((A,J)\mapsto(A,-J)\). Neither \(A\) nor \(|J|\) depends on \(c\).

**2. Full convex order and strictness.** For a convex \(\Phi\), fix \(A\) and \(|J|\) and set
\[
 h(t)=\frac{\Phi(A+t|J|)+\Phi(A-t|J|)}2.
\]
By convexity, \(h(t)\) is nondecreasing for \(t\ge0\) as long as both arguments lie in the domain. This follows directly from convexity of symmetric secants, or by writing the smaller two-point symmetric spread as a mixture of the larger spread and its reflection. The symmetry of \((A,J)\) proves \(\mathbb E\Phi(Z_{c_1})\le\mathbb E\Phi(Z_{c_2})\). If \(\Phi\) is strictly convex, the inequality is strict whenever \(J\ne0\). That event has positive probability because \(a,b>0\) and the coefficient law contains nontrivial continuous edge or area configurations with both coordinate differences nonzero. The mean is constant because \(\mathbb EJ=0\). Directly, for area sampling \(\mathbb EW^2=\mathbb EV^2=1/3\), and for boundary sampling
\[
\mathbb EW^2=\frac{a/3+b}{s},\qquad
\mathbb EV^2=\frac{a+b/3}{s}.
\]
Since \(\mathbb EU^2=2\mathbb EW^2\) and similarly for \(V_*\), simplification gives exactly the two stated means.

**3. Sharp strong-convexity constants.** If \(\Phi''\ge m\), then \(h''(t)\ge m J^2\) and \(h'(0)=0\). Therefore
\[
 h(c_2)-h(c_1)\ge\frac m2J^2(c_2^2-c_1^2).
\]
For the area law, \(W,V\) are independent, \(\mathbb EU^2V_*^2=4/9\), and thus \(\mathbb EJ^2=16a^2b^2/9\). In the boundary law put \(\mu_{20}=\mathbb EW^2\), \(\mu_{02}=\mathbb EV^2\), \(\mu_{22}=\mathbb EW^2V^2\). The coordinate-sign symmetry gives \(\mathbb EWV=0\); moreover \(\mu_{22}=1/3\) and
\[
\mu_{20}\mu_{02}=\frac13+\frac{4ab}{9s^2}.
\]
Expanding the two independent differences gives
\[
\mathbb EU^2V_*^2=2\mu_{22}+2\mu_{20}\mu_{02}
=\frac43+\frac{8ab}{9s^2},
\qquad
\mathbb EJ^2=4a^2b^2\left(\frac43+\frac{8ab}{9s^2}\right).
\]
Substituting these identities proves the asserted constants. When \(\Phi(z)=mz^2/2+\alpha z+\beta\), the symmetric-secant inequality is an identity, simultaneously for every \(a,b,c_1,c_2\). Thus no larger universal coefficients can hold for the indicated curvature hypothesis.

**4. Moment reversal.** For \(p>0\), \(\Phi_p(z)=z^{p/2}\) is strictly concave when \(0<p<2\), affine when \(p=2\), and strictly convex when \(p>2\). Applying step 2 (or the negative of its convex inequality) gives the complete strict angular phase diagram, with no restriction to integer \(p\). The case \(p=1\) is the ordinary Euclidean chord mean.

**5. Common boundary needle law.** As \(c\uparrow1\), the two generating directions become collinear. The boundary-coordinate law then becomes \(aW+bV\), with two intervals \([-a+b,a+b]\), \([-a-b,a-b]\) of length \(2a\) and two intervals \([a-b,a+b]\), \([-a-b,-a+b]\) of length \(2b\), each contributing constant density \(1/(4s)\). Almost every point of \([-s,s]\) is covered by exactly two of these intervals, so the limit law is uniform on \([-s,s]\) **for every** \(a,b>0\). Two independent such uniforms have absolute difference density \((2s-r)/(2s^2)\) on \(0\le r\le2s\), hence
\[
 \mathbb E r^p=\int_0^{2s}\frac{r^p(2s-r)}{2s^2}\,dr
 =\frac{2^{p+1}s^p}{(p+1)(p+2)}.
\]
Convergence of moments is justified by bounded support and continuity for \(p>0\). Strict angular ordering shows these bounds are not attained by any nondegenerate parallelogram.

## Verification

A standalone standard-library-only Python program, `verify.py`, independently integrates the complete boundary side-pair polynomial and the four-variable area-pair polynomial with exact rational arithmetic for six combinations of side lengths and angle cosines in each model (twelve exact comparisons). It checks the exact second and fourth moments and angular curvature coefficients. A separate deterministic direct side-pair quadrature tests the strict power-moment reversal for three shear angles. Run `python verify.py`; the recorded result was `SHEAR_CONVEX_ORDER_CHECK_OK exact_pair_moments=12 sample_power_tests=3 strong_curvature_coefficients=exact`. The infinite family and convex-order quantifiers are proved analytically above, not inferred from finite tests.

## Relationship to prior work

Tokmachev's 2026 theorem [1] compares area-uniform and boundary-uniform samples **inside the same fixed convex body**, using separately convex test functions of the points; it does not compare the squared chord laws of two differently sheared parallelograms. The published fourth-chord computation [2] gives exact fourth-moment formulas and a shape-dependent **boundary/interior fourth-moment ratio**; our fourth-moment identities are compatible with [2] and **are not** claimed independently novel. A fourth moment alone cannot establish domination for every convex function, and the angular behavior of a quotient of two increasing functions does not determine that of each function. The published radial convex-order theorem [3] compares \(|X|^2\), where \(X\) is a **single** boundary sample, and not \(|X-X'|^2\). No convex-order implication from the first random variable to the second follows without an additional coupling argument. Prior explicit rectangle mean-distance results [4] vary the side ratio at a **right angle**, rather than the angle at fixed sides, and concern just the first power. General domain-interior mean-distance formulas [5] likewise do not provide the all-convex-function angular result.

## Limitations

The strict ordering requires fixed nonzero side lengths and Euclidean area-uniform or boundary-arclength-uniform independent endpoint sampling. It does not assert stochastic ordering of unsquared lengths, transfer to arbitrary quadrilaterals, or higher-dimensional surface sampling. The strong-convexity constants are optimal for functions with a prescribed lower second-derivative bound, not the individually optimal deficit for every particular nonquadratic test. The collinear endpoint is excluded as a nondegenerate convex body. Historical priority remains conditional on indexed and accessible primary sources; no independent external audit has been performed.
