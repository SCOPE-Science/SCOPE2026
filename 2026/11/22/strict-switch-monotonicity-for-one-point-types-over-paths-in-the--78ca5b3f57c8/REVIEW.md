# Scientific assessment

**Correctness — PASS.** The three-state finite-extension encoding explicitly lists all and only old-edge/new-point transitive triangles; no nonadjacent pair of old path vertices can create a triangle. The two exact transfer matrices follow by enumerating possible ending states. The cut-and-suffix-inversion bijection proves a strictly positive factorized difference every time one switch indicator changes from equal to unequal, independently of the other indicators. The strict extrema and cubic recurrences follow by finite-dimensional matrix algebra. Direct three-state enumeration was compared with transfer results for every orientation through eight vertices, and further independent exhaustive coordinate checks reproduce the claimed local strictness. This is an infinite-parameter proof, not an extrapolation from enumeration.

**Originality — PASS, subject to independent historical priority.** Cherlin constructs/classifies the appropriate homogeneous forbidden-tournament oriented graph, rather than these named-base type extrema. Kühn, Osthus, Townsend and Zhao determine typical global structure and counts of finite oriented graphs forbidding tournaments, not the type enumerator conditional on a fixed *oriented path* or a coordinatewise strict change under reversing a suffix. Related numerical path-type calculations for the distinct class forbidding *cyclic* rather than *transitive* triangles cannot imply this theorem because their local forbidden status pairs are different. Searches for exact local-switch formulas, all-orientation extremal results and recurrence fingerprints found no covering statement. The remaining risk is a less-visible transfer-matrix or hard-constraint spin-system formulation that implies the result without using model-theoretic language; no absence-of-prior-work claim is made.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** This is a complete structural extremal theorem for the canonical family of finite connected path bases in a homogeneous forbidden-tournament structure, not an arbitrary numerical slice. It proves a *strict* partial order on all path orientation patterns under addition of switches, identifies every extremizer for all orders, and gives exact recurrences and exponentially separated growth rates. The bridge-product identity explains why direction alternation increases definable local complexity even when other directions are unrestricted. The result is precise enough for further comparisons with other tournament-free Fraïssé classes.

## Limits and literature
The result does not concern arbitrary oriented trees, ordered tuples of arity greater than one, or the separate cyclic-triangle-free homogeneous class. Primary sources inspected: Cherlin, *Infinite homogeneous directed graphs*, §1 and §2.1, https://ow3.math.rutgers.edu/~cherlin/Paper/1992Digraph.pdf; and Kühn–Osthus–Townsend–Zhao, *On the structure of oriented graphs and digraphs with forbidden tournaments or cycles*, arXiv:1404.6178, particularly pp. 1–3 and the stated scope of §§4–5, https://arxiv.org/pdf/1404.6178.
