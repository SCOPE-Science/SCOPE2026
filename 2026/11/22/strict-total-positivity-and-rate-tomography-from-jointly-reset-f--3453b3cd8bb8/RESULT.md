# Strict total positivity and rate tomography from jointly reset Fourier-mode energies

## Finding

For a finite-cutoff, one-dimensional periodic Edwards–Wilkinson interface whose *entire profile* is reset to flat at one rate-\(r\) Poisson clock, the fourth-order covariances between squared real Fourier modes form a **strictly totally positive Cauchy matrix**, despite the vanishing cross-covariance of distinct unsquared Fourier modes. Every minor indexed by strictly increasing distinct wavenumbers is positive, for every \(r>0\), every diffusivity \(\nu>0\), every noise strength \(D>0\), and every finite cutoff containing the chosen modes. The energy-covariance matrix has full rank at every finite size, notwithstanding that its dependence is generated by only one shared scalar reset age.

The diagonal entries require an important distinction: they are measured by the covariance of **two different** sine and cosine energies at the same wavenumber, not by the variance of one squared mode. Write \(q_n=2\pi n/L\), \(b_n=2\nu q_n^2\), and choose positive energy weights \(w_n\). For the cosine and sine coefficients \(C_n,S_n\), define their energies \(E_n^c=w_n C_n^2\), \(E_n^s=w_n S_n^2\). The physical elastic-mode energy corresponds to \(w_n=\nu q_n^2/2\). Define the observable matrix
\[
K_{ij}=\begin{cases}
\operatorname{Cov}(E_i^c,E_j^c),&i\ne j,\\
\operatorname{Cov}(E_i^c,E_i^s),&i=j.
\end{cases}
\]
Then, exactly,
\[
\boxed{K_{ij}=\frac{4rD^2w_iw_j}{(r+b_i)(r+b_j)(r+b_i+b_j)}.}
\]
Its dimensionless normalized off-diagonal coefficient, using the sine–cosine estimates for the diagonal, is
\[
\boxed{\rho_{ij}=\frac{K_{ij}}{\sqrt{K_{ii}K_{jj}}}
=\frac{\sqrt{(r+2b_i)(r+2b_j)}}{r+b_i+b_j}}
\qquad(i\ne j).
\]
This is **not** the Pearson correlation of the raw energies, because the true variance of one energy contains an additional conditional Gaussian fluctuation term. For distinct wavenumbers, \(\rho_{ij}\) increases strictly with \(r\), from \(2\sqrt{b_i b_j}/(b_i+b_j)\) to \(1\), and gives an exact noise-amplitude-independent inverse:
\[
\boxed{r=\frac{|b_i-b_j|}{\sqrt{1-\rho_{ij}^{2}}}-b_i-b_j.}
\]
Thus a single pair of distinct wavenumbers, together with the corresponding sine–cosine energy covariances, determines \(r\) when \(\nu,L\) are known. If \(\nu\) is unknown, the same statistic determines \(r/(2\nu)\) but not the absolute reset rate.

## Assumptions and scope

Let \(L,\nu,D,r>0\). On the circle \(\mathbb R/L\mathbb Z\), retain finitely many nonzero real orthonormal Fourier modes \(\phi_n^c(x)=\sqrt{2/L}\cos(q_nx)\), \(\phi_n^s(x)=\sqrt{2/L}\sin(q_nx)\) for \(n=1,\ldots,N\). The zero Fourier mode is fixed at zero. Between global resets the linear field solves
\[
\partial_t h=\nu\partial_{xx}h+\sqrt{2D}\,\xi,
\]
with independent real white-noise Fourier coordinates. At independent Poisson times of rate \(r\), *every* retained mode is reset to zero simultaneously, and subsequent noise increments are fresh. Stationary moments are taken under the unique regenerative stationary law. Let \(C_n=\langle h,\phi_n^c\rangle\), \(S_n=\langle h,\phi_n^s\rangle\). Positive weights \(w_n\) may be any known deterministic positive constants, including elastic-energy weights. All finite-matrix assertions hold for every \(N\ge2\); the full-rank statement concerns arbitrarily chosen finite cutoffs and makes no unregularized infinite-dimensional operator claim.

For ordered distinct index sets \(I=(i_1<\cdots<i_m)\) and \(J=(j_1<\cdots<j_m)\), let \(K_{I,J}\) be their matrix minor. Then the **exact positive minor formula** is
\[
\boxed{\det K_{I,J}=(4rD^2)^m
\left(\prod_{i\in I}\frac{w_i}{r+b_i}\right)
\left(\prod_{j\in J}\frac{w_j}{r+b_j}\right)
\frac{\prod_{a<b}(b_{i_b}-b_{i_a})\prod_{a<b}(b_{j_b}-b_{j_a})}
{\prod_{i\in I,j\in J}(r+b_i+b_j)}>0.}
\]
For \(I=J\), the numerator contains the squared Vandermonde product. The matrix is strictly totally positive in mode-index order. If repeated decay rates were inserted as separate indices, strictness would fail; the claim explicitly selects **distinct wavenumbers**.

## Proof

**1. Renewal age and conditional Gaussian factorization.** The stationary backward recurrence time of a homogeneous Poisson clock is \(A\sim\operatorname{Exp}(r)\). Conditional on \(A=a\), each sine and cosine coordinate evolves from zero as an independent centered OU coordinate with drift \(-\lambda_n x\), diffusion \(\sqrt{2D}\), and \(\lambda_n=\nu q_n^2\). Its conditional variance is
\[
v_n(a)=\frac{D}{\lambda_n}(1-e^{-2\lambda_na})
=\frac{2D}{b_n}(1-e^{-b_na}).
\]
Distinct real-mode squared energies are conditionally independent, including the sine and cosine components at a fixed wavenumber. Hence every entry of the defined observable matrix equals
\[
K_{ij}=w_iw_j\operatorname{Cov}(v_i(A),v_j(A)).
\]
It is essential here that the diagonal \(K_{ii}\) uses different real coordinates; replacing it with \(\operatorname{Var}(E_i^c)\) would add \(2w_i^2\mathbb E[v_i(A)^2]\) and invalidate the subsequent Cauchy determinant.

**2. Exact covariance.** The exponential-age Laplace transform is \(\mathbb E e^{-sA}=r/(r+s)\) for \(s\ge0\). Since \(\operatorname{Cov}(1-X,1-Y)=\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Cov}(v_i(A),v_j(A))
&=\frac{4D^2}{b_i b_j}\left[
\frac{r}{r+b_i+b_j}-\frac{r^2}{(r+b_i)(r+b_j)}\right]\\
&=\frac{4rD^2}{(r+b_i)(r+b_j)(r+b_i+b_j)}.
\end{aligned}
\]
This proves the entrywise formula with all normalization factors.

**3. Strict total positivity.** Factor each entry as
\[
K_{ij}=\frac{u_i u_j}{r+b_i+b_j},\qquad
u_i=\frac{2D\sqrt r\,w_i}{r+b_i}>0.
\]
For increasing distinct decay rates, the Cauchy double-alternant determinant identity gives
\[
\det\left[\frac1{r+b_i+b_j}\right]_{i\in I,j\in J}
=\frac{\prod_{a<b}(b_{i_b}-b_{i_a})\prod_{a<b}(b_{j_b}-b_{j_a})}
{\prod_{i\in I,j\in J}(r+b_i+b_j)}.
\]
The identity follows, for example, by multiplying by the denominator, observing alternating dependence on each ordered node set, then comparing the coefficient at the highest distinct-degree monomial. Every factor is strictly positive. Multiplying by the positive row and column prefactors yields the stated determinant formula. This proves all minors simultaneously, including positive principal determinants of arbitrary finite order, hence full rank for each set of distinct modes.

**4. Sharp inverse.** The positive weights and noise strength cancel on normalizing the off-diagonal by the *cross-component* diagonal. Direct algebra yields
\[
\rho_{ij}^2=\frac{(r+2b_i)(r+2b_j)}{(r+b_i+b_j)^2}
=1-\frac{(b_i-b_j)^2}{(r+b_i+b_j)^2}.
\]
For unequal decay rates, differentiation of the last expression gives a strictly positive derivative in \(r>0\), so \(\rho_{ij}\) is strictly increasing. Solving this identity with the positive square root gives the inverse, its boundary limits, and the strict realizability interval. Substituting \(b_i=2\nu q_i^2\) proves the weaker identification of the ratio when \(\nu\) is unknown. No temporal sampling or independent duplicate field is assumed.

## Verification

Run `python3 verify.py` in a Python 3 standard-library environment. The checker computes conditional-age moments a second way, independently compares every Cauchy minor on a five-mode rational example using exact fractions, tests the normalized inverse for each distinct pair, and checks several covariances by numerical integration under the independent variable \(z=e^{-rA}\), which is uniform on \((0,1)\). It reports 608 assertions. The code tests a finite collection of parameters; the theorem for all positive parameters and all finite cutoffs follows solely from the analytic Laplace-transform and Cauchy-determinant argument above.

For a concrete nondimensional illustration with \(r=\nu=D=1\), \(q_1=1\), \(q_2=2\), and equal unit weights, the two decay rates are \(b_1=2\) and \(b_2=8\). Then \(\rho_{12}^2=85/121\) and the inverse recovers \(r=1\). Actual Fourier wavenumbers need not equal these numbers on a given ring; selecting \(L=2\pi\) realizes this illustration exactly.

## Relationship to prior work

Gupta, Majumdar and Schehr, *Fluctuating interfaces subject to stochastic resetting* (arXiv:1312.5954, first posted 20 December 2013), establish whole-interface renewal resetting and non-Gaussian stationary height fluctuations; their inspected manuscript focuses on one-point height laws and interface width, not the sine–cosine calibrated fourth-order Fourier-energy kernel. Wu and collaborators, *Heat statistics in the relaxation process of the Edwards-Wilkinson elastic manifold* (arXiv:2304.02843, first posted 6 April 2023), give the independent real Fourier-mode evolution and heat statistics for a reservoir quench **without** global stochastic resetting. Nagar and Gupta, *Stochastic resetting in interacting particle systems: A review* (arXiv:2305.16955, first posted 26 May 2023), present the general renewal representation and review interface-height statistics but do not in the inspected interface section state the all-minors result or two-mode spectral-energy inverse.

A published study, *Joint recovery of resetting, pinning and noise strength in a fluctuating interface* (public scientific record dated 2026), also uses global resetting but concerns infinite-line, harmonically pinned point-height variance, separated-point spatial covariance, and one-point kurtosis. Its inspected full result has neither the finite periodic Fourier-energy cross-covariance matrix nor the sine–cosine inverse. Jolakoski (arXiv:2605.30059, 2026) gives a structurally similar Cauchy rational covariance factor for *first-order stochastic-gradient state fluctuations around a nonzero deterministic mean*, not squared-energy covariance of centered reset Fourier modes. A separate published causal-exponential-contrast Gram result applies the **classical** Cauchy determinant to scale-channel designs, not the common-clock fourth-order interface statistic. Cauchy determinant algebra is classical and is not claimed as a new identity.

The new result is specifically the observable fourth-order interface-energy matrix, its all-orders positive-minor hierarchy, and direct single-pair calibration of the reset rate without knowing thermal strength. The ingredients are simple enough that a broader, differently described spectral-statistics theorem might implicitly subsume these conclusions. Priority beyond the explicitly inspected sources remains subject to independent literature review.

## Limitations

This is an exact ensemble-level theorem for a periodic, flat-reset **linear** Edwards–Wilkinson field with independent Fourier-noise coordinates and one shared homogeneous Poisson clock. No assertion is made for local or partial resets, colored noise, nonlinear KPZ dynamics, an uncontrolled zero mode, sampled finite-data stability, or unregularized continuum determinant operators. The inferred parameter is \(r/(2\nu)\) unless the diffusivity is calibrated independently. At very rapid resetting \(\rho_{ij}\to1\), so inverse estimation could be ill-conditioned despite uniqueness. Positive mode weights are required for literal *positive* minors; negative mode weights would change minor signs. The matrix diagonal is **cross-sine/cosine covariance**, not an energy variance; conflating these would invalidate the theorem. The classical Cauchy formula, prior renewal representation and independent Gaussian modal dynamics are acknowledged inputs.
