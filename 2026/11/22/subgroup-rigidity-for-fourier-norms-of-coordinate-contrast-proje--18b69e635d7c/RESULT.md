# Subgroup rigidity for Fourier norms of coordinate-contrast projections

## Finding

Let \(1\le m<n\) be integers, put \(N=m+n\), and let \(A\) be **any** finite abelian group of order \(N\). For any subset \(C\subset A\) of size \(n\), let \(P_C\) be orthogonal projection onto the zero-sum vectors supported on \(C\), namely \(P_C=\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf1_C)-n^{-1}\mathbf1_C\mathbf1_C^*\). With counting-measure matrix norm and the unitary two-dimensional Fourier transform on \(A\times A\), write

\[
\operatorname{FR}(P_C)=\frac{\|\widehat{P_C}\|_{\ell^1(\widehat A\times\widehat A)}}{\|P_C\|_{\mathrm F}}.
\]

Then **every** such subset satisfies

\[
\|\widehat{P_C}\|_1\ge 2(n-1),\qquad \operatorname{FR}(P_C)\ge 2\sqrt{n-1}.
\]

Equality holds **if and only if** the complement \(A\setminus C\) is a coset of a subgroup of \(A\) of order \(m\). This projection is exactly the eigenvalue-\(m\) Laplacian spectral projector \(\Pi_m\) when \(C\) labels the \(n\)-vertex shore of the graph \(K_{m,n}\). Consequently, for each fixed finite abelian group \(A\) of order \(m+n\),

\[
\min_{C\subset A,\ |C|=n}\operatorname{FR}(P_C)=2\sqrt{n-1}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad m\mid n.
\]

When \(m\mid n\), the minimum is exactly \(2\sqrt{n-1}\), and all minimizing coordinate subsets and graph labelings are described by the coset criterion. When \(m\nmid n\), the minimum is strictly larger; its exact value is not determined here.

## Assumptions and scope

The graph is unweighted, complete bipartite, and strictly unbalanced: \(1\le m<n\). Its Laplacian is \(L=D-A_G\), where \(A_G\) is the adjacency matrix. The eigenvalue-\(m\) eigenspace consists exactly of vectors supported on the \(n\)-vertex shore whose coordinate sum is zero, and has dimension \(n-1\). For the finite abelian labeling group \(A\), set \(U_{\chi,x}=N^{-1/2}\overline{\chi(x)}\), where \(\chi\in\widehat A\). The two-dimensional Fourier transform of a kernel is \(UPU^{\mathsf T}\); its entrywise absolute values are a column permutation of those of \(UPU^*\). The Frobenius norm is invariant under these unitary transforms. A shore's internal vertex-ordering is immaterial; a labeling is determined for this question by the subset assigned to the \(m\)-vertex shore.

## Proof

**1. The projector and a universal factor-two bound.** Write \(B\subset A\) for the labels of the \(m\)-vertex shore and \(C=A\setminus B\), of size \(n\). The projector is

\[
P(x,y)=\mathbf1_C(x)\mathbf1_C(y)\left(\mathbf1_{x=y}-\frac1n\right),
\qquad \operatorname{rank}P=n-1,
\qquad \|P\|_{\mathrm F}=\sqrt{n-1}.
\]

Translate the labels so the group identity \(0\) belongs to \(B\); this translation preserves the Fourier absolute values. Put \(M=UPU^*\). It is a Hermitian positive-semidefinite orthogonal projector of trace \(n-1\). Because \(Pe_0=0\) and \(Ue_0=N^{-1/2}\mathbf1\), one has \(M\mathbf1=0\). Thus each row satisfies

\[
\sum_{\psi\ne\chi}M_{\chi\psi}=-M_{\chi\chi},
\qquad M_{\chi\chi}\ge0,
\]

and the triangle inequality gives

\[
\sum_{\chi,\psi}|M_{\chi\psi}|
\ge 2\sum_\chi M_{\chi\chi}=2(n-1).
\]

Dividing by \(\sqrt{n-1}\) proves the asserted lower bound. Equality in the rowwise triangle inequalities holds precisely when every off-diagonal entry of \(M\) is **real and nonpositive**. In that case \(M\) is a weighted graph Laplacian on the character set: with \(w_{\chi\psi}=-M_{\chi\psi}\ge0\) for distinct characters,

\[
v^*Mv=\sum_{\{\chi,\psi\}}w_{\chi\psi}|v_\chi-v_\psi|^2.
\]

**2. Equality forces subgroup labels.** Suppose equality holds, and let \(H\le A\) be the subgroup generated by \(B\) (which contains the identity), of order \(h\ge m\). For every \(b\in B\), the vector \(Ue_b\) lies in \(\ker M\). The preceding nonnegative Laplacian energy therefore implies that \(M_{\chi\psi}\ne0\) for distinct \(\chi,\psi\) only if \(\chi(b)=\psi(b)\) for every \(b\in B\). Equivalently, only if the restrictions of \(\chi\) and \(\psi\) to \(H\) coincide. Hence \(M\) has no entries between different restriction classes of characters.

Let \(\varphi\ne1\) be a character of \(H\). There are exactly \(r=N/h\) characters of \(A\) restricting to \(\varphi\). Define \(S_B(\varphi)=\sum_{b\in B}\overline{\varphi(b)}\). Every character \(\chi\) in this class is nontrivial on \(A\), and direct Fourier expansion of \(P\) gives, for characters \(\chi,\psi\) in this same class,

\[
M_{\chi\chi}=\frac1N\left(n-\frac{|S_B(\varphi)|^2}{n}\right),
\qquad
M_{\chi\psi}=-\frac1N\left(m+\frac{|S_B(\varphi)|^2}{n}\right)
\quad(\chi\ne\psi).
\]

Indeed, the sum of a nontrivial character over \(A\) vanishes, while the character \(\chi\psi^{-1}\) is trivial on \(H\) and hence sums to \(m\) over \(B\). The zero row-sum identity, together with the vanishing of entries into other restriction classes, now yields

\[
|S_B(\varphi)|^2=n(h-m)\qquad(\varphi\ne1).
\]

Parseval on \(H\), applied to \(\mathbf1_B\), gives independently

\[
\sum_{\varphi\ne1}|S_B(\varphi)|^2=hm-m^2=m(h-m).
\]

If \(h>m\), comparison of the last two identities forces \((h-1)n=m\), impossible because \(n>m\) and \(h\ge m+1\). Thus \(h=m\); as \(B\subseteq H\) and both have size \(m\), the translated set \(B\) is exactly \(H\). Undoing the translation, the original shore labels are a subgroup coset.

**3. Subgroup cosets attain equality.** Conversely, suppose \(B\) is a coset of a subgroup of order \(m\). Translate it to the subgroup itself. For nontrivial \(\chi\), its character sum over \(B\) equals \(m\) if \(\chi|_B=1\), and zero otherwise. Using

\[
M_{\chi\psi}=\frac1N\left(S_C(\chi\psi^{-1})-\frac{S_C(\chi)\overline{S_C(\psi)}}n\right),
\qquad S_C(\chi)=\sum_{x\in C}\overline{\chi(x)},
\]

we see that \(M_{1,\psi}=M_{\chi,1}=0\), every off-diagonal entry within a nontrivial common restriction class is negative, and every entry between distinct restriction classes is zero. The same conclusion applies to distinct nonidentity characters trivial on \(B\). All off-diagonal entries are therefore real nonpositive, so equality holds in Step 1.

Finally, every finite abelian group of order \(N\) has a subgroup of every divisor order \(m\mid N\). Since \(N=m+n\), this divisibility is exactly \(m\mid n\). This proves existence, necessity, and the full equality classification.

## Verification

The standalone file `check.py` constructs the spectral projector and both Fourier normalizations independently, verifies \(\operatorname{FR}\ge2\sqrt{n-1}\), and checks the equality criterion exhaustively on 3,910 subsets of cyclic and noncyclic finite abelian groups of small order, plus three independent normalization comparisons. It is supplementary finite evidence only; the preceding proof establishes the result for every admissible finite abelian group and both shore sizes.

Run with `python check.py` (requires Python, NumPy). The expected terminal output is `PASS: 3910 exhaustive subset cases, abelian product examples, and normalization checks`.

## Relationship to prior work

Ferguson, Gupta, Iosevich, Iosevich, Kelly, Smith and Yang (arXiv:2610.36276, first posted October 26, 2026) determine the corresponding top-eigenvalue projector ratio and formulate the exact intermediate-projector minimization and equality classification as Question 9.7, as well as a finite-abelian labeling-group question (Question 9.8). Their Theorem 7.2 gives the **multiplicity** lower bound for intermediate projectors, but not the factor-two bound and equality criterion here. The present result settles the divisible part of the eigenvalue-\(m\) question for every finite abelian labeling group, but does **not** settle the nondivisible minimum or the opposite intermediate projector.

Gupta and Iosevich (arXiv:2606.15475, first posted June 13, 2026) prove the general Fourier-projector multiplicity bound. Gupta et al. (arXiv:2610.31807, first posted October 2026) refine it by Fourier collision dimension. These estimates do not supply the claimed factor-two identity or subgroup-coset equality classification for \(\Pi_m\). The precise relation to older unindexed matrix and Fourier-algebra inequalities remains a literature risk.

## Limitations

For \(m\nmid n\), strict inequality follows by finiteness of the labeling set, but this theorem does not determine the exact larger minimum or its minimizers. The theorem excludes balanced \(m=n\) graphs because the two shore-contrast eigenspaces coalesce into one Laplacian eigenvalue. No stability estimate or complexity guarantee for finding the optimal labeling from data is claimed. Independent mathematical validation is not asserted.
