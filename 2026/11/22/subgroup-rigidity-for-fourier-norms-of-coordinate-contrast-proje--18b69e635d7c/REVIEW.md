# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The stated universal inequality and equality classification are obtained analytically from the zero-row Fourier triangle bound, equality as a weighted Laplacian, character restrictions to the generated subgroup, and an exact Parseval contradiction unless the excluded set is itself a subgroup coset. The group-order divisibility implication is exact. The supplementary checker covers 3,910 small-group subset cases; the proof, not these samples, covers the infinite family.

## Originality — PASS relative to inspected evidence

Ferguson et al. (arXiv:2610.36276) explicitly leave intermediate-projector minima open (Question 9.7) and ask about finite abelian labeling groups (Question 9.8). Their top-projector and adjacency classification is not the new lower bound for the rank-\(n-1\) contrast projector. Gupta and Iosevich (arXiv:2606.15475) and Gupta et al. (arXiv:2610.31807) give weaker rank or collision bounds and different equality conditions. Actual targeted public-record searches found no equivalent statement. An unindexed older matrix/Fourier-algebra result is a remaining risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The result gives a nontrivial sharp structural theorem for an entire family of two-dimensional finite Fourier transforms, and classifies the label sets realizing the exact optimum. It answers the natural divisible regime of a newly published harmonic-projector minimization problem, uniformly over finite abelian vertex-labeling groups. The nondivisible minimum and opposite intermediate eigenprojector remain unresolved.

## Relationship and limits

The general projector multiplicity lower bound is \(\sqrt{n-1}\), versus the new sharp factor-two value \(2\sqrt{n-1}\) when \(m\mid n\). Equality classifies subgroup-coset labels and no others. No general exact value is asserted when \(m\nmid n\); the balanced graph case is excluded; no separate stability estimate is proved.
