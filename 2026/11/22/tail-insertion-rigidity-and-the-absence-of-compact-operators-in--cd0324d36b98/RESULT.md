# Tail-insertion rigidity and the absence of compact operators in infinite-variable quasi-radial Toeplitz algebras

## Finding

Let \(H=\ell^2(\mathbb N)\), partitioned into infinitely many consecutive nonempty **finite** blocks \(I_j\) of dimensions \(k_j\ge1\). Suppose that \(B\) is the positive injective diagonal covariance operator with entry \(u_j>0\) on block \(I_j\) and \(\sum_{j\ge1}k_j u_j<\infty\). Let \(\mathcal F_B\) be the Gaussian analytic Fock space with orthonormal monomials \(e_\alpha\), indexed by finite-support multiindices. A bounded symbol is block-quasi-radial if it depends only on \((\|z_{I_j}\|)_{j\ge1}\). Let \(\mathcal T\) be the unital \(C^*\)-algebra generated by all corresponding Toeplitz compressions.

Let \(\kappa=(\kappa_j)\) be any finite-support vector of nonnegative block degrees, and let \(P_\kappa\) project onto the span of all \(e_\alpha\) with \(\sum_{i\in I_j}\alpha_i=\kappa_j\) for all \(j\). This is a nonzero finite-rank projection. Every \(S\in\mathcal T\) is diagonal by block degree: \(Se_\alpha=\gamma_S(\kappa)e_\alpha\). We prove the tail-insertion identity

\[
\lim_{j\to\infty}\gamma_S(\kappa+\mathbf e_j)=\gamma_S(\kappa)
\qquad(\kappa\text{ fixed}).
\]

In particular,

\[
\boxed{\mathcal T\cap\mathcal K(\mathcal F_B)=\{0\},\qquad
\operatorname{dist}(P_\kappa,\mathcal T)=\tfrac12.}
\]

The constant is attained by \(\tfrac12 I\in\mathcal T\). If \(\mathcal A\) denotes the bounded diagonal sequences whose restriction to the first \(L\) block degrees belongs to the \(L\)-block quasi-radial Toeplitz \(C^*\)-algebra for every finite \(L\), then \(P_\kappa\in\mathcal A\) for all \(\kappa\), and thus \(\mathcal T\subsetneq\mathcal A\). This resolves the projection nonmembership conjecture explicitly posed in Section 5.1 and supplies a negative answer to the equality part of Problem 1 of Bauer–Rodriguez Rodriguez (2022).

## Assumptions and scope

Fix the complex product Gaussian probability measure \(\mu_B\) on \(H\), with independent coordinates \(Z_i\) satisfying \(\mathbb E|Z_i|^2=u_j\) when \(i\in I_j\). The trace-class hypothesis ensures \(Z\in H\) almost surely. For finite-support \(\alpha\), set

\[
e_\alpha(z)=\prod_i\frac{z_i^{\alpha_i}}{\sqrt{\alpha_i!\lambda_i^{\alpha_i}}},
\qquad \lambda_i=u_j\text{ if }i\in I_j.
\]

The vectors \(e_\alpha\) form the standard orthonormal basis of \(\mathcal F_B\subset L^2(\mu_B)\). Let \(\Pi\) denote orthogonal projection onto \(\mathcal F_B\). For any bounded measurable block-quasi-radial symbol \(a\), the bounded Toeplitz operator is \(T_a=\Pi M_a|_{\mathcal F_B}\). Block-unitary invariance makes \(T_a\) a scalar on each block-degree sector, with coefficient

\[
\gamma_a(\kappa)=\int_H a(z)|e_\alpha(z)|^2\,d\mu_B(z),
\quad \kappa_j=\sum_{i\in I_j}\alpha_i.
\]

No continuity or cylindrical assumption is imposed on \(a\). All conclusions concern the **commutative, block-quasi-radial** Toeplitz \(C^*\)-algebra; they are not claims about all Toeplitz compressions, the noncommutative algebra generated by them, or the inductive-limit Fock model.

## Proof

**1. Orthogonal Gaussian perturbations.** Fix a finite-support block-degree vector \(\kappa\) and choose any monomial index \(\alpha\) with block degrees \(\kappa\). Define the probability measure \(d\nu_\alpha=|e_\alpha|^2d\mu_B\); its normalization follows from \(\|e_\alpha\|_2=1\). Because the density depends on only finitely many coordinates, outside their support the normalized Gaussian coordinates remain independent and standard. Let \(q_j\in I_j\) be the first coordinate of each block, and take \(j\) outside the block support of \(\alpha\). Define

\[
Y_j(z)=\frac{|z_{q_j}|^2}{u_j}-1.
\]

Under \(\nu_\alpha\), the variables \((Y_j)\) for these blocks are independent, centered, and satisfy \(\mathbb E_{\nu_\alpha}|Y_j|^2=1\), since the squared modulus of a standard complex Gaussian is exponential with mean and variance one. Thus \((Y_j)\) is orthonormal in \(L^2(\nu_\alpha)\).

For \(j\) beyond the support of \(\alpha\), the monomial normalization gives the exact identity

\[
|e_{\alpha+\mathbf e_{q_j}}|^2=|e_\alpha|^2(1+Y_j).
\]

Therefore for any bounded block-quasi-radial symbol \(a\),

\[
\gamma_a(\kappa+\mathbf e_j)-\gamma_a(\kappa)
=\int_H aY_j\,d\nu_\alpha.
\]

Because \(a\in L^\infty(\nu_\alpha)\subset L^2(\nu_\alpha)\), Bessel's inequality for the orthonormal sequence \((Y_j)\) makes the right-hand side tend to zero. This is an infinite-dimensional proof; no finite truncation or numerical experiment is used.

**2. Closed-algebra inheritance.** Define

\[
\mathcal C=\left\{\gamma\in\ell^\infty(\mathcal Z_0):
\lim_{j\to\infty}\big(\gamma(\kappa+\mathbf e_j)-\gamma(\kappa)\big)=0
\ \text{for every finite-support }\kappa\right\}.
\]

This is a unital, self-adjoint algebra, closed in the supremum norm: sums, products and adjoints preserve each limit, and uniform limits preserve each limit. The eigenvalue sequences of the generating \(T_a\) belong to \(\mathcal C\) by Step 1, so \(\gamma_S\in\mathcal C\) for every \(S\in\mathcal T\). This also proves the tail-insertion identity for all members of the norm-closed algebra, not merely for individual Toeplitz generators.

**3. Compactness obstruction.** Suppose \(S\in\mathcal T\) is compact. For any fixed \(\kappa\), choose \(\alpha\) of its block degrees. The vectors \(e_{\alpha+\mathbf e_{q_j}}\) are orthonormal as \(j\to\infty\); compactness implies \(\|Se_{\alpha+\mathbf e_{q_j}}\|\to0\). Since they are eigenvectors, \(\gamma_S(\kappa+\mathbf e_j)\to0\). Tail-insertion rigidity forces \(\gamma_S(\kappa)=0\). This holds for every \(\kappa\), so \(S=0\).

**4. Sharp distance.** For any \(S\in\mathcal T\), compare its eigenvalue \(c=\gamma_S(\kappa)\) at one unit vector in \(P_\kappa\mathcal F_B\) with the orthogonal sequence of vectors in the sectors \(\kappa+\mathbf e_j\). Because \(P_\kappa\) vanishes on the latter sectors,

\[
\|P_\kappa-S\|\ge\max\{ |1-c|,\limsup_{j\to\infty}|\gamma_S(\kappa+\mathbf e_j)|\}
=\max\{|1-c|,|c|\}\ge\tfrac12.
\]

The constant-symbol Toeplitz operator \(\tfrac12I\) belongs to \(\mathcal T\) and has \(\|P_\kappa-\tfrac12I\|=\tfrac12\), giving equality.

**5. Strictness of the finite-coordinate envelope.** For any positive integer \(L\), restrict the diagonal characteristic function \(\mathbf1_{\{\kappa\}}\) to multiindices supported in the first \(L\) blocks. If \(\kappa\) has a nonzero block outside that prefix, the restriction is identically zero. Otherwise it is the characteristic function of a singleton in \(\mathbb Z_+^L\). The finite-block quasi-radial Toeplitz \(C^*\)-algebra contains such singleton functions by its square-root-metric characterization (Dewage–Ólafsson, 2022, also recalled as Theorem 5.5 by Bauer–Rodriguez Rodriguez). Hence \(P_\kappa\in\mathcal A\). Step 4 shows \(P_\kappa\notin\mathcal T\), so the inclusion is proper.

## Verification

The pivotal identities are exact: \(\mathbb E|Z_{q_j}|^2=u_j\), \(\operatorname{Var}(|Z_{q_j}|^2/u_j)=1\), and \(\int|e_\alpha|^2d\mu_B=1\). Their independence after a finite monomial tilt establishes the orthonormal sequence needed for Bessel's inequality. The compactness step uses only the established fact that a compact operator maps an orthonormal sequence to a norm-null sequence. The distance lower bound compares two orthogonal sectors and is attained by a literal constant-symbol compression. Finite-L singleton membership is established by the cited finite-dimensional algebra theorem. None of these steps requires a computational certificate.

## Relationship to prior work

Bauer and Rodriguez Rodriguez, *Commutative Toeplitz Algebras and Their Gelfand Theory: Old and New Results* (2022), Section 5.1, explicitly remark that the infinite product of sector projections \(P_\kappa\) might not belong to the infinite-variable quasi-radial Toeplitz algebra and state that a proof is missing. Equation (5.9) gives \(\mathcal T\subset\mathcal A\), while their Problem 1 asks whether equality holds. Their Example 5.9 rules out a **single bounded symbol** for a particular diagonal sequence in \(\mathcal A\), but this does not exclude approximation by sums and products of Toeplitz operators. The uniform tail-insertion identity supplies the additional closed-algebra obstruction.

Dewage and Ólafsson (2022) identify the finite-variable quasi-radial Fock Toeplitz algebra with the bounded functions uniformly continuous in the square-root metric; that theorem is used here, not claimed anew. Janas and Rudol (1995) discuss the absence of compact **individual Toeplitz operators** in the Gaussian infinite-variable model, which does not by itself imply absence of compact elements in the generated \(C^*\)-algebra. Guo and Yan (2022) prove an absence-of-compacts statement for a different algebra on the infinite-dimensional Hardy polydisc, not the present Gaussian infinite-variable quasi-radial setting. Dewage and Mitkovski (2025) study norm density of quasi-radial Toeplitz operators in **finite-dimensional** Fock/Bergman models, and do not imply the present tail-insertion law.

## Limitations

The statement requires infinitely many independent nontrivial finite blocks and a diagonal trace-class covariance compatible with them; finite-dimensional Toeplitz algebras can contain sector projections. It concerns bounded symbols, the specified Gaussian measure realization, and the block-quasi-radial \(C^*\)-algebra only. The result gives a necessary tail regularity property, not a complete characterization of all its eigenvalue sequences. The 1995 Janas–Rudol full text was not retrievable from an advertised public PDF link, so possible stronger historical results there remain a qualified priority risk. The originality comparison is limited to examined public records and cannot establish exhaustive historical uniqueness.
