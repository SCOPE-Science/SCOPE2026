# Tangent rigidity and octagonal matching components for binary filling curves

## Finding
Let \(k=\mathbb F_2\) and fix the smooth symplectic cubic surface
\[
S=V(G)\subset\mathbb P^3_k,\qquad G=X^2Y+XY^2+Z^2W+ZW^2.
\]
Let \(\mathcal V=J_4/(G\cdot k[X,Y,Z,W]_1)\), with \(J\) the homogeneous ideal of all fifteen points of \(\mathbb P^3(k)\). Let \(\mathcal C\subset\mathcal V\setminus\{0\}\) consist of those nonzero quartic classes \([F]\) for which the curve \(C_F=S\cap V(F)\) is geometrically smooth. For distinct \([F],[H]\in\mathcal C\), let \(a(F,H)\) count rational points \(P\) at which the two embedded curves have the same tangent line \(T_PC_F=T_PC_H\).

**Theorem.** The set \(\mathcal C\) has \(144\) elements, and every pair of distinct members satisfies \(a(F,H)\geq1\). Across its \(\binom{144}{2}=10296\) unordered pairs, the exact distribution is

| \(a(F,H)\) | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 10 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Pairs | 360 | 1440 | 2160 | 720 | 1152 | 1440 | 1440 | 1080 | 504 |

Consequently **no pencil** of quartic sections of \(S\) through all its rational points has all three nonzero \(k\)-rational members geometrically smooth. In fact, the third member generated by any two distinct smooth sections is singular at a rational point.

There are \(288\) perfect matchings of the point–line incidence graph of the symplectic generalized quadrangle \(W(3,2)\). Each of its \(288\) quartic classes nonsingular at all fifteen rational points defines a distinct such matching by assigning each point its curve tangent line; every matching occurs. The **edge-disjointness graph** on these \(288\) matchings (adjacent if they share no point–line incidence flag) has exactly **\(36\) connected components of eight vertices each**. Each component is a cycle of length eight alternating between the geometrically smooth and geometrically singular matching orbits, together with four additional edges making a cycle on the four singular-orbit vertices. The \(144\) geometrically smooth sections give an independent set containing exactly four vertices in each component, and hence an independent set of maximal possible size. This octagonal decomposition explains the pointwise tangent obstruction.

## Assumptions and scope
All sections, rational points, pencil parameters, and tangent incidences are defined over exactly \(k=\mathbb F_2\). Two quartic forms are regarded as equivalent if they differ by \(G\) times a linear form; thus a pair of distinct nonzero classes determines a pencil with nonzero members \([F],[H],[F+H]\). When a class is called smooth or nonsingular, it is required to be nonzero: the zero class cuts out all of \(S\), not a quartic section curve. Smooth means geometrically smooth, not only nonsingular at rational points. The 36 cycles belong to the graph defined by *edge-disjointness of entire perfect matchings*, not to the different eight-cycle-exchange graph. A rational tangent flag is an incidence of a specific rational point with a specific rational isotropic line.

## Proof
**1. Exhaustion of the quartic section space.** The six cubic forms \(X_i^2X_j+X_iX_j^2\), indexed by \(0\leq i<j\leq3\), generate the homogeneous ideal of \(\mathbb P^3(\mathbb F_2)\) in degree three. Multiplying by all four variables yields \(24\) explicit quartic vectors in the \(35\)-dimensional coefficient space. Binary echelon reduction yields rank \(20\). The four multiples \(GX_i\) have rank four. An explicit deterministic basis for \(\mathcal V\) is obtained by taking these four multiples first and greedily adjoining independent cubic-generator multiples. Consequently \(\dim\mathcal V=16\), and every section is represented exactly once by a \(16\)-bit mask.

At every nonzero binary vector \(P\), the cubic gradient is \(dG(P)=(Y,X,W,Z)\). A quartic class is nonsingular on its intersection with \(S\) at \(P\) exactly when \(dF(P)\notin\{0,dG(P)\}\). Enumerating the \(65536\) class masks by Gray code, updating gradients in fifteen-bit-point order, finds exactly \(288\) classes satisfying this test simultaneously. The executable enumerator specifies each matrix entry and tests every class; the \(288\) is an exhaustive finite result.

**2. Determine geometric smoothness without inferring it from rational checks.** The fifteen symplectic transvections \(T_v(u)=u+B(u,v)v\) preserve \(G\), with \(B(u,v)=u_0v_1+u_1v_0+u_2v_3+u_3v_2\). Their actions on \(\mathcal V\), calculated by exact binary polynomial substitution, split the \(288\) surviving masks into precisely two orbits of \(144\). For one orbit, mask `27773` defines the quartic
\[
\begin{aligned}
F={}&XW^3+X^2W^2+Y^2W^2+X^2YW+XY^2W+Y^3W+Y^2ZW+YZW^2\\
&+X^3Y+X^3Z+X^2Y^2+X^2YZ+XYZ^2+XZ^3+Y^3Z+Y^2Z^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
For this representative, the ideal generated by \(G,F\) and all six two-by-two Jacobian minors is the unit ideal on each of the four affine charts \(X_i=1\), by exact characteristic-two Gröbner calculation. For mask `27760` in the other orbit, the same ideal is nonunit on the chart \(X=1\). The Jacobian criterion therefore proves the first orbit geometrically smooth and the other geometrically singular. The group action transports these results to every mask in the respective orbit, proving that precisely the first orbit gives \(\mathcal C\).

**3. The tangent flags and their pencil criterion.** Every rational tangent is one of the fifteen totally isotropic rational lines on \(S\). A line is the three-point set \(\{a,b,a+b\}\) with \(B(a,b)=0\). For each surviving mask \(F\) and each of the fifteen rational points, enumerate the three isotropic incident lines and select the unique one annihilated by both \(dG(P)\) and \(dF(P)\). The resulting fifteen-entry vector is a perfect matching: all fifteen lines occur exactly once. Direct enumeration gives \(288\) different vectors. Separately, a subset-mask dynamic-programming count of all point-to-line matchings of \(W(3,2)\) returns exactly \(288\), proving surjectivity rather than merely injectivity.

For two smooth sections in characteristic two, \(F+H\) fails the Jacobian rank test at \(P\) exactly when \(dF(P)+dH(P)\in\langle dG(P)\rangle\), which is equivalent to equality of their one-dimensional tangent spaces there. (There is only one nonzero scalar in \(\mathbb F_2\), so no unaccounted projective scaling.) Comparing all \(10296\) distinct unordered smooth-mask pairs gives the table in the theorem, with no zero-agreement pair. Therefore every third pencil member is rationally singular.

**4. The full octagonal obstruction.** Put an edge between two of the \(288\) perfect-matching vectors iff they differ in every one of their fifteen coordinates. Exhaustive comparison of all unordered pairs yields the degree partition \(144\) degree-two vertices (the smooth orbit) and \(144\) degree-four vertices (the other orbit). Breadth-first search gives exactly \(36\) connected components of eight vertices each, every component containing four vertices from each orbit. Its cross-orbit edges form a connected two-regular spanning subgraph, hence an eight-cycle; the four vertices of the singular orbit also form a four-cycle by their two additional neighbors, while the four smooth vertices have no mutual edges. Thus every component contains a spanning eight-cycle and has independence number at most four; its four smooth vertices attain the bound. Summing over the \(36\) components gives maximum independent-set size \(144\).

## Relationship to prior work
Campbell–Dedvukaj–McCormick–Moon–Morales established constructions and the minimum degree for complete-intersection space-filling curves. Published finite classifications already give the \(144\) geometrically smooth quartic sections, their projective orbit, ordinarity and the fact that each rational tangent assignment is a perfect matching; **none of those counts is claimed here as an original scalar count**. The independent contribution is the *pairwise tangent-flag intersection theorem*, its exact spectrum, the resulting no-smooth-pencil obstruction on the minimal cubic, and the distinct 36-component octagonal disjointness relation on complete matchings. Prior work on bidegree-\((4,3)\) curves on a split quadric proves a similar tangent-overlap obstruction in a **different ambient surface and genus**, and cannot determine this theorem. Existing work on the Tutte–Coxeter graph treats a graph in which matching adjacency means an alternating eight-cycle *switch*, not edge-disjointness; it does not provide these 36 octagons.

## Limitations
This is a finite-field theorem over \(\mathbb F_2\) for quartic sections of the fixed symplectic cubic. It does not preclude smooth pencils of higher-degree curves, smooth pencils on a different ambient surface, or all families over field extensions. The exact matching configuration was computed by exhaustive integer and characteristic-two operations and independently justified by the stated finite enumeration; the result is not a uniform theorem for larger finite fields. Classification data previously published allow independent reconstruction of some ingredients, so the historical-priority assessment has the residual risk of an unindexed equivalent incidence theorem. Independent validation is not asserted.
