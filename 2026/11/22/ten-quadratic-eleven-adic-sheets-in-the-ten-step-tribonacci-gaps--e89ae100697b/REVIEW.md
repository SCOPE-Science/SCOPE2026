# Scientific assessment: ten-step Tribonacci gaps at eleven

## Correctness — PASS

The single claim is the complete \(11\)-adic image and fiber theorem for \(D_n=T_{n+10}-T_n\), including least periods and Haar measure. The companion-matrix computation gives \(X^2\equiv0\pmod{11}\) and \(X^3/11\equiv7(X\bmod11)\pmod{11}\), yielding a nondegenerate quadratic reduction on each of ten distinct index branches. The proof supplies a convergent integral analytic interpolation, a unique critical point, and a globally isometric quadratic coordinate; it then counts squares modulo every \(11^m\). The standalone integer checker confirms matrix identities and complete images at the five initial prime-power depths. Computation is corroboration, not the justification for the infinite statements. Critical analytic requirements are addressed: denominator valuations in the Mahler series, integrality of the analytic tail, invertibility of the derivative slope, and global surjectivity of the coordinate map.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

Klaška's 2008 full text is the closest source, with exact generalized-Tribonacci periods and foundational matrix congruences. These establish that the ten-step gap sequence has an unusually short period, but neither identify the complete \(11\)-adic image nor its ten critical-value sheets, exact square-root fibers, or Haar measure. The new conclusion requires a quadratic Mahler expansion and global analytic normal form, not only a period argument. Baker–Luca–Porrill (2026) concerns \(11\)-adic zeros of the original Tribonacci values, not this difference sequence. A separately published \(2\)-adic Tribonacci image concerns a distinct prime and object. Meaning-based scientific-record searches and primary literature searches did not locate the same difference-image theorem. Non-discovery is not proof of priority: a general distribution theorem, or a result implicit in the close matrix literature, may still exist. Full-text access to all potentially related later literature was not complete.

## Value — PASS

Ten-step gaps form a natural, recurrence-preserving arithmetic statistic at the exceptional ramified prime \(11\). A period alone provides no criterion for which residues actually occur. The theorem gives all residue images, all represented-fiber multiplicities, a sharp period, and a nontrivial \(5/12\) limiting Haar measure. The ten separated translated-square components expose a genuine critical-point obstruction, explaining why almost half the candidate \(11\)-adic values are omitted despite all nonzero classes appearing modulo eleven. The result is reusable for membership and counting at arbitrary prime-power depth. Its restriction to this natural lag is substantive rather than an arbitrary finite slice.

## Literature and limitations

Primary comparison: J. Klaška, *Tribonacci modulo \(2^t\) and \(11^t\)*, Mathematica Bohemica 133 (2008), 377–387, https://doi.org/10.21136/MB.2008.140627; especially §3, Lemma 3.1 and Theorem 3.10. Further comparisons: M. Catalani, arXiv:math/0209265 (2002), and L. Baker, F. Luca, K. Porrill, arXiv:2609.22364 (2026). The claim is confined to the specified sequence, lag and prime; the critical values are defined by Hensel equations rather than elementary closed forms. Independent mathematical validation has not been performed.
