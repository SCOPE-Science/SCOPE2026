# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

The endpoint rank reduction is exact for iid nonatomic endpoints. Triangle-freeness is equivalent to having at most two open intervals at each sweep position (interval Helly property). The claimed ternary encoding is a bijection: every later opening either begins a disconnected component, lets the old carrier survive an overlap, or lets the new carrier survive. Its inverse reconstructs the exact endpoint matching. This establishes iid equiprobable letters under the conditioning. The forest/caterpillar description, binomial components, and fixed-size component means follow directly from edge attachments and renewal boundaries. The degree mixture is derived from a geometric carrier run and independent terminal choice. Fixed-degree weak laws use finite-dependence truncation, while logarithmic limits follow from elementary longest-run bounds. Exhaustive pairings through seven intervals test the finite statements, but not the infinite claims. All hypotheses, exceptional boundary cases, normalization and unproved limitations are stated in RESULT.md.

## Originality — PASS, with historical qualifications

A comparison of statements, not merely title similarities, was carried out against the complete HTML manuscript of Diaconis–Holmes–Janson (2011), the full accessible text of Korbel–Mörters (2020), the relevant chapters/abstract of Iliopoulos's thesis, the publicly available abstracts/records of Scheinerman (1988) and Justicz–Scheinerman–Winkler (1990), and topical research-index searches. The 2020 sock-path theorem covers the weighted Dyck-path probabilities and thereby gives an alternate route to the height-two enumeration and component count. Those enumerative quantities are not presented as independently original. It does not explicitly establish the conditional *graph* carrier degree mixture, full limiting degree spectrum, or the two distinct maximum-size/maximum-degree scales. The 2011 graph-limit results concern unconditional dense-graph behavior; they do not imply the rare-conditioned law via the cited theorems alone. Full historical priority remains uncertain because the 1988 and 1990 articles could not be read in full; available sources did not provide the 1990 complete text. A previously published but unlocated equivalent conditional graph result remains a residual risk.

## Value — PASS

A triangle-free interval graph is a natural rare structural constraint on a typically dense random interval graph; it enforces a forest, but *does not by itself specify* the resulting distribution. The iid ternary encoding supplies an exact conditional sampler and a complete description of its component-size and degree spectra. The two different logarithmic scales separate rare long connected components from high-degree carrier vertices, offering a concrete structural benchmark for rare-event sampling and conditioned random intersection networks. This is a complete motivated conditional-ensemble description, rather than an arbitrary parameter slice or merely a new numerical constant.

## Scope and residual limitations

The graph is constructed from independent endpoint pairs with any common nonatomic law. It is not uniform over unlabeled interval graphs. The stated logarithmic limits are first-order laws, not centered extreme-value distributions. The baseline sock-path formula is credited; inaccessible original interval-graph papers could contain an implicit or explicit special case. These uncertainties do not affect the internal proof of the new conditional graph characterization.
