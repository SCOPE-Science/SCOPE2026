# Review: Ternary residue-fiber spectrum of the fourth binomial column

## Correctness — PASS

The proof uses exact polynomial identities, not interpolation or a finite sample: for inputs \(3t+1\) and \(3t+2\), divided differences are three-adic units and each branch is bijective. On inputs \(3t\), the normalized value equals \(1/128+w^2\) after a rigorously established global three-adic isometry. Therefore the integer residue counts are the sum of two unconditional preimages and three lifts of a square-root congruence. The elementary square-root classification establishes all multiplicities for every integer \(k\ge1\), including the zero-square boundary. The least period and Haar-density claims follow from the same identities. The verifier performs 45,544 finite assertions, including \(1\le k\le8\) complete residue counts and explicit modular square conjugacy; this corroborates but does not replace the infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS (qualified by historical-priority risk)

Sun and Zhang, *Binomial coefficients and the ring of p-adic integers* (arXiv:0812.3089, first public version 2008-12-16), Theorem 1.1, proves that the fourth fixed column attains all classes at every power of three. This is a known corollary of the present theorem and is not claimed anew. Their theorem and inspected proofs do not give exact target-by-target multiplicities, a three-adic three-versus-four inverse-fiber law, or an unbounded Haar pushforward density.

An indexed result titled *Exact good-prime-power images and p-adic density of pentatope numbers* treats the same fourth column but explicitly restricts to primes at least seven, excluding the prime three where the denominator has a factor of three. The indexed *Exact ternary residue images of the sixth binomial polynomial* treats a different column and its partial image; it does not give the fourth-column surjective fiber histogram. Earlier two-adic fifth- and third-column findings likewise do not imply this ternary computation by substitution. The accessible author PDF of Barat and Grabner (2001), including its introduction and Sections 3–4, treats digit-function statistics and binomial valuation distributions over varying pairs of indices rather than fixed fourth-column output fibers. Its Theorems 6–7 do not imply this target-by-target law. Other, potentially stronger general specializations remain a historical-priority risk. The full texts of the two closest indexed findings were not obtained; only their indexed statements were compared, so these are priority limitations rather than certified absences.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The fourth binomial column is the first nontrivial quartic fixed column, and the prime three divides its denominator. Beyond the known question whether every three-adic value is reached, the natural unresolved invariant is how often each target is reached. The result gives a complete, all-prime-power, target-by-target answer rather than isolated residues: every fiber has a baseline of two; a uniquely centered square branch creates arbitrarily high multiplicities and an integrable unbounded Haar density. A precise one-eighth enhancement-set measure and the exceptional inverse-fiber size three make the ramified local geometry explicit. This sharp distinction between surjectivity and distribution is substantive, and the exact law is reusable for binomial-value sampling and local polynomial counting.
