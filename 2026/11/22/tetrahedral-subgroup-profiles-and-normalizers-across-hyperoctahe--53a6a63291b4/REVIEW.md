# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The seven-type stabilizer-character table is obtained by an exhaustive classification of the subgroups and sign characters of \(A_4\). The transitive signed-action centralizer lemma is proved from equivariant bijections, the faithfulness criterion from the core of \(\ker(\chi)\), and the whole-group normalizer from the fact that all seven types are invariant under \(\operatorname{Aut}(A_4)\). The class series, embedded-subgroup series and pole calculation follow with stated factors. Exact coefficient and direct finite-group checks reproduce all predicted counts through the specified ranges; the infinite proof is independent of enumeration. A possible transcription or coding error at larger degrees is limited by the algebraic proof; computational tests alone do not certify all degrees.

**Originality — PASS relative to inspected material.** The complete profile-dependent normalizer and seven-family embedded \(A_4\) classification is not asserted by inspected Coxeter involution papers (Zibrowius; Marberg–Zhang) or the general signed-wreath element-centralizer work (Bernhardt et al.). The directly relevant open-access Schlage-Puchta paper was examined in full: its Lemma 4 and displayed homomorphism count recover general *homomorphism* enumerators and thus cover the method behind the exponential coefficient identity, but do not classify embedded subgroup conjugacy orbits and their individual normalizer sizes. The earlier Müller (2000) classification of wreath representations is important possible dominating background; no inspected statement has been found giving this specific \(A_4\) normalizer census. The rational conjugacy-class series is a stronger object than a homomorphism-count EGF. Search absence is not a priority proof; residual risk of an equivalent older specialization remains.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** An exact profile-by-profile subgroup normalizer classification is a natural structure theorem for nonabelian subgroup embeddings in an infinite Coxeter family. It distinguishes the two inequivalent signed three-coordinate actions, accounts for the outer automorphisms of \(A_4\), and recovers both subgroup quantities and a genuine period-twelve growth law. This is more than a single small-dimensional or arbitrary-parameter computation; however it is explicitly an application of established group-action and wreath enumeration principles, not a new general principle for all source groups.
