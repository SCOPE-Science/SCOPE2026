# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The claim is precisely about the finite Galois set of geometric negative-support Zariski chambers of a smooth degree-four del Pezzo surface, for every finite field. The geometric support theorem [1] and classification of sixteen negative curves reduce the problem to independent subsets in the explicitly calculated intersection graph, giving cardinality \(393\). The group model is the faithful natural \(W(D_5)\)-action; signed-cycle conjugation exhaustively confirms all \(18\) element classes and \(1920\) group members. Two nonidentical computations—the direct 393-set action and fixed subsets as unions of curve-orbits—agree, and the full spectra differ for all eighteen. The deterministic standalone verifier reproduces the CSV. Trepalin [2] separately confirms arithmetic realization for \(q\ge4\). Residual correctness risks are limited to the published negative-support correspondence and finite algorithm implementation, both independently cross-checked at their key interfaces.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
Bauer--Funke--Neumann [1] establish the total \(393\) and the support theorem but not a finite-field Frobenius-spectrum inversion. Trepalin [2] supplies the \(18\) Frobenius conjugacy types and existence, not the 393-chamber spectra. Brodie--Constantin [3] compute complex geometric cohomology chambers, not their arithmetic Galois permutation types. Published work on the Clebsch independence-complex homology describes a nine-dimensional homology character rather than the full 393-face permutation cycle structure. Semantic research-record and literature searches found no equivalent full-spectrum injection theorem. It remains possible an unindexed graph cycle-index table implicitly entails the result; this cannot be excluded by searches alone.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The Galois action on Zariski chambers is a natural arithmetic refinement of geometric stable-base-locus decomposition. A single 393-point finite-etale configuration zeta function reconstructs all 18 possible arithmetic Weyl types, whereas the smaller 16-curve splitting data realizes only 12 spectra. Explicit same-line-zeta but different-chamber-count examples show genuinely new distinguishing power, and Trepalin ensures these are realized over all \(q\ge4\). This is not a routine restatement of the geometric 393 count or merely a code reproducibility result.
