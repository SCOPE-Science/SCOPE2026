# Review of the Möbius–Kantor majority basin classification

**Correctness — PASS.** The graph is specified by the 24 edges of \(\operatorname{GP}(8,3)\), and its shores each have eight sites. Explicit maps \(g,h\) induce exact 256-input tables, including \(P^3=P^2\), the sharper identities needed for \(F^5=F^3\), and a 72-symbol terminal phase set whose projection-fiber weights are 1 (22 targets), 2 (48) and 69 (2). Multiplicative fiber enumeration derives every one of the nine individual basin classes. An independently coded direct C++ functional-graph census agrees on all 65,536 configurations. Boundaries, quantifiers and temporal-orbit conventions are explicit. Risk: this is a finite exact computation, not an infinitely parametrized graph theorem.

**Originality — PASS, with residual historical uncertainty.** Goles–Olivos, Adam–Dahleh–Ozdaglar and Benjamini et al. establish general eventual period-two and bipartite counting behavior, but do not specify the Möbius–Kantor phase fibers or individual attraction basins. The closely related published Heawood and Desargues censuses concern different incidence graphs; the Heawood projection's 36/1 fiber weights are different and neither gives these new 69/2/1 weights. Exact phrase, object, graph-alias, basin and numerical searches did not find an equivalent published tabulation. Risk: unindexed earlier enumerations cannot be excluded, and the general orbit-count relation itself is prior work.

**Value — PASS.** The Möbius–Kantor graph is the canonical unique symmetric cubic graph on sixteen vertices and the Levi graph of the unique eight-point triple-incidence configuration. The phase retraction produces a naturally interpretable three-level distribution of preimage multiplicities, not merely a total fixed-point count. It resolves the complete exact nine-class attraction structure and a sharp temporal cutoff, providing a meaningful contrast with Fano/Heawood incidence dynamics without implying any unjustified family theorem.
