In the radical-multiplier/unit-translation case, \(f^p(x)=x+pa\) for odd \(p\), proving the sharp distinction between orders \(p\) and \(p^2\). The closed coefficient sum is \(q^3(q-1)\). A separate concrete enumerator computes actual cycles for the six test rings at \(q=3,5,9\), including two non-prime residue-field examples; all pass. The general theorem rests on its proof, not these tests. Limit: characteristic two and higher length are excluded.

The new scope is the uniform comparison over nonprime residue fields, including \(\mathrm{GR}(p^2,f)\) and \(\mathbb F_{p^f}[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)\), and the exact inverse interpretation.
