# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** A commutative bilinear product on the three-dimensional binary vector space has precisely six independent vector outputs, hence 18 binary parameters. All 262,144 codes were enumerated; the defining identity was checked on every pair of base-field vectors; the 168 changes of basis were enumerated and their orbits partitioned exactly. The formal degree-three identity was independently evaluated after extension to \(\mathbb F_4\), and Burnside's fixed-point sums independently reproduced the orbit counts. The complete canonical representative table and the standalone C++17 checker are supplied. The proof explains why formal coefficient vanishing is equivalent to validity over every scalar extension.

**Originality — PASS (with residual priority risk).** The motivating Hernández–Martin–Rodrigues–Silva classification explicitly requires fields of at least three elements. Earlier complete classifications concern nilpotent, zeropotent or isopotent *subclasses*, not all commutative bilinear products on \(\mathbb F_2^3\) meeting the Jordan identity. Indexed published-finding and literature searches did not identify the exact 47/32/15 orbit statement or a theorem implying it. A complete search of all historical specialist tables is impossible, so priority remains provisional. The related published nilpotent classification gives five classes and does not imply the present whole-space census.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The exact smallest-field endpoint was omitted from a new natural low-dimensional classification, and the omission matters: 15 additional base-field identity classes disappear when polynomial identities are required after extension. This is a natural complete isomorphism classification and a mathematically motivated small-field obstruction, not a selected numerical slice. Full representatives, stabilizer orders and independent reproduction support future comparisons of field-dependent classification conventions.

**Closest literature and limitations.** Sources: arXiv:2611.00315v1 (Sections 2, 4–6); arXiv:1301.6349v4 (Theorem 34); Cedilnik–Jerman (2020), doi:10.24330/ieja.662996; Cedilnik (2023), doi:10.24330/ieja.1217445. The classification concerns commutative bilinear products on \(\mathbb F_2^3\) and scalar-extension stability of the ordinary Jordan identity, not quadratic Jordan algebra structures.
