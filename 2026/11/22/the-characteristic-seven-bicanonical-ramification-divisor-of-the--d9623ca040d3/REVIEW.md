# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** For the complete \(|2K_C|\) on the Fermat quartic in characteristic seven, an exact Hasse-series computation establishes generic orders \((0,1,2,3,4,7)\) and the rational-function Wronskian identity. This gives eighty simple interior points and twelve coordinate hyperflexes of weight three. The geometric count exhausts the ramification degree \(116\). Independent finite-field arithmetic verifies eight rational support points over \(\mathbb F_7\) and complete splitting of all ninety-two support points over \(\mathbb F_{49}\); the Hasse–Weil upper bound then identifies this support with all \(C(\mathbb F_{49})\). The standalone scripts were run on the stated proof data; sample counts are not substituted for the divisor proof.

**Originality — PASS, provisional.** Garcia–Voloch's Fermat nonclassicality criterion already predicts that the degree-four curve is nonclassical for the conic series in characteristic seven; this is explicitly prior and not the claimed novelty. Stöhr–Voloch/Torres provide the general ramification framework. Alwaleed–Sakai classify the complex Kuribayashi quartics, including the characteristic-zero Fermat bicanonical points, but not the characteristic-seven eighty-plus-twelve weighted support or its Frobenius zeta. An indexed characteristic-seven genus-three hyperelliptic bicanonical computation concerns a different curve and an eighty-four-plus-eight divisor. The original 1988 full text could not be read; unindexed earlier explicit computations remain a residual priority risk.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** Exceptional characteristic seven changes the generic bicanonical order sequence and the entire geometric ramification scheme, from the characteristic-zero 108-degree distribution to a degree-116 distribution. The exact intrinsic divisor, local order types and arithmetic splitting, and exact coincidence with the full maximal-curve rational point set give a complete and motivated boundary classification, useful for osculation theory and characteristic-dependent Weierstrass geometry rather than an arbitrary finite subcase.
