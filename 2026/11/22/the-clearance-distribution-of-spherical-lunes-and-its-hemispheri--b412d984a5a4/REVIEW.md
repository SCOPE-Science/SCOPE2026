# Scientific review: spherical-lune clearance distribution

## Correctness (PASS)

The object is the geodesic distance of one surface-uniform point to the two bounding great hemispheres of a spherical lune. The nearest-face projection is valid because its relative longitude is no more than half the opening and therefore no more than \(\pi/2\). Conditioning a spherical Gaussian direction on its azimuthal wedge gives an independent uniform half-angle and \(\operatorname{Beta}(1,(d-2)/2)\) squared transverse radius. The survival integral, moments, strict ordering, and both endpoint constants follow explicitly. In the critical aperture, the key ratio is quadratic rather than linear in the rare-event scale; after normalization its curved support yields the indicated law. The conditional marks follow by an \(L^1\) density limit, not merely weak convergence. The analytic argument works for every integer \(d\ge3\); the boundary \(\alpha=\pi\) is treated separately. The included executable diagnostic passed 5,230 numerical assertions. The numerical tests do not certify the all-dimensional assertions.

## Originality (PASS, provisional)

The nearest geometric foundations are Lassak's *Width of spherical convex bodies* (first online December 6, 2013; DOI 10.1007/s00010-013-0237-3) and Lassak–Musielak's *Reduced spherical convex bodies* (arXiv:1607.00132, July 1, 2016). The latter full text studies thickness, reduced bodies, support hemispheres, and spherical convexity, but not the law of one random point's distance to a lune boundary. The earlier paper's accessible converted text was inspected around its lune definitions and thickness claims, but a full authoritative equation-level check of the entire PDF was not completed; that is a residual priority risk, not affirmative evidence of novelty.

Besau et al., *Spherical convex hull of random points on a wedge* (2023, DOI 10.1007/s00208-023-02704-9) was inspected in full accessible HTML, including its random-hull problem, wedge-coordinate measure and arguments. It studies expected facets of random spherical polytopes in the orthogonal wedge, not the one-point boundary-clearance distribution or its marked near-incenter law. A published result concerning **antipodal distances between two random points in a spherical lune** has a hemisphere crossover but is about a different statistic: it does not give the clearance of one point to a fixed boundary. Likewise, the published orthogonal-wedge convex-hull perimeter deficit does not imply the new distribution, its aperture inversion, or the rare-event conditional density. No covering implication was identified in the searched descriptions and inspected source texts. A negative search does not establish historical priority, and more specialized spherical inner-parallel-volume literature may exist.

## Mathematical value (PASS)

Boundary clearance equals the survival volume fraction of the inner geodesic parallel sets of a canonical spherical cell, a natural quantity in stochastic spherical geometry and sampling near faces. The work supplies the full law for every dimension, determines lune opening from one low-order moment, and distinguishes two geometric mechanisms at the incenter: an aperture-corner boundary layer below a hemisphere and a smooth cap layer at the hemisphere. Their sharp exponents differ (\(d/2\) versus \(d-1\)), and the complete crossover and marked Dirichlet-to-curved-support transition identify the configurations responsible. This is more than a normalization check or a single isolated elementary integral. These facts are limited to spherical lunes and do not yield analogous formulas for arbitrary spherical polytopes.
