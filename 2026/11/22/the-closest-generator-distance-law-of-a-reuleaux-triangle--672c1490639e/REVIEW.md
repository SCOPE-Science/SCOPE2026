# Scientific review: closest-generator distance law of a Reuleaux triangle

## Correctness — PASS
The three generating disks and the closest-site inequalities are translated without approximation into angular constraints in the polar coordinates of each vertex. The outer angular component disappears uniquely at \(r_0=\sqrt{2-\sqrt3}\), and all three angular-length branches are nonnegative on their stated intervals. Their polar integral gives the exact density and elementary CDF, with total mass one. Explicit differentiation of the primitive functions gives the \(3/2\)-power onset, the second-derivative jump \(24/A\), and the quadratic final tail. The attached checker independently samples the original planar region and evaluates both analytic and numerical integrals. Computation corroborates but does not substitute for the continuous proof. Risk: none identified in domain handling or normalization; the claim is limited to the stated three sites and sampling measure.

## Originality — PASS (provisional historical priority)
Harrell (arXiv:math/0009137v1) and Bezdek (arXiv:0903.5361v1) are direct full-text sources on Reuleaux and disk-polygon geometry but contain area-extremality and radius formulas, not the nearest-corner distribution. The publicly available centered-disk profile treats distance from the centroid and therefore has a different event and thresholds. The published regular-polytope nearest-vertex squared-distance margin is for polytopes and a different statistic. The 2016 triangle point-picking article treats polygonal triangle interiors and thus does not give the area law on curved three-disk intersections. published-finding corpus and public-literature searches for nearest vertex, Voronoi, disk union, angular transitions and exact thresholds found no claim whose hypotheses and conclusion dominate this one. Risk: historical literature not indexed or inaccessible may have an equivalent disk-union calculation; a failed search does not certify priority.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The nearest-corner distance is the natural quantization error for the three sites generating the classical constant-width Reuleaux domain. Its full law distinguishes a curved-boundary regime, an overlap/interlacing regime, and a central Voronoi regime, including two provably different transition regularities, and supplies exact coverage probabilities for three finite disks clipped by the fixed Reuleaux region. This is a complete geometric distribution rather than a chosen numerical slice or routine area recomputation. No extension to other disk-polygons is asserted.
