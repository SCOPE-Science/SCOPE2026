# The complete adjoint-rank spectrum and triangular quotient of the Sweedler Hopf algebra

## Finding

For any odd prime power \(q\), let \(H_4\) denote the four-dimensional Sweedler Hopf algebra over \(\mathbb F_q\). The left Hopf adjoint representation
\[
\operatorname{ad}_h(b)=\sum h_{(1)}bS(h_{(2)})
\]
has an exact all-element rank polynomial
\[
\boxed{\sum_{h\in H_4}T^{\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_q}(\operatorname{ad}_h)}
=q+q(q-1)T+2q^2(q-1)T^2+q^2(q-1)^2T^4.}
\]
In particular, ranks \(0,1,2,4\) occur and rank \(3\) does not. Additionally, the image of the adjoint representation is the three-dimensional lower triangular matrix algebra \(\operatorname{LT}_2(\mathbb F_q)\), and its kernel is the one-dimensional ideal \(\mathbb F_q(v+cv)\). As an \(H_4\)-module under this action, \(H_4\) is a direct sum of two one-dimensional character modules and an indecomposable two-dimensional triangular module.

## Assumptions and scope

The field \(\mathbb F_q\) has odd characteristic and \(q\) is an arbitrary prime power. The algebra is generated by \(c,v\) with relations \(c^2=1\), \(v^2=0\), \(vc=-cv\), and ordered basis \((1,c,v,cv)\). Its coproduct and antipode are
\[
\Delta(c)=c\otimes c,\quad
\Delta(v)=c\otimes v+v\otimes1,\quad
S(c)=c,\quad S(v)=-cv.
\]
The **left** Hopf-adjoint action is intended, not the Lie commutator map nor the right Hopf-adjoint action. Each \(h\) contributes once, including zero and nonunits. Rank means the ordinary rank of the induced \(4\times4\) linear map over \(\mathbb F_q\); the result is not a count of isomorphism classes.

## Proof

For basis monomials the multiplication is
\[
(c^iv^j)(c^kv^\ell)=
\begin{cases}(-1)^{jk}c^{i+k\bmod2}v^{j+\ell},&j+\ell\le1,\\0,&j+\ell=2,\end{cases}
\]
where the exponents of \(c\) are interpreted modulo two. Since the antipode is antihomomorphic, \(S(cv)=S(v)S(c)=v\); also \(\Delta(cv)=1\otimes cv+cv\otimes c\). Direct substitution into the Hopf-adjoint definition gives
\[
\operatorname{ad}_1=I_4,\quad
\operatorname{ad}_c=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,-1,-1),\quad
\operatorname{ad}_v=-2E_{4,2},\quad
\operatorname{ad}_{cv}=2E_{4,2}.
\]
Here \(E_{4,2}\) sends the second basis vector \(c\) to the fourth basis vector \(cv\), annihilating the others. For an arbitrary element \(h=a1+bc+dv+e(cv)\), set
\[
\lambda=a+b,\qquad \mu=a-b,\qquad \theta=2(e-d).
\]
Then the matrix is
\[
[\operatorname{ad}_h]_{(1,c,v,cv)}=
\begin{pmatrix}
\lambda&0&0&0\\
0&\lambda&0&0\\
0&0&\mu&0\\
0&\theta&0&\mu
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
For odd characteristic, the linear map \((a,b,d,e)\mapsto(\lambda,\mu,\theta)\) is onto \(\mathbb F_q^3\), and each triple has exactly \(q\) preimages. The representation kernel is thus exactly \(\lambda=\mu=\theta=0\), equivalently \(h\in\mathbb F_q(v+cv)\). On the invariant plane \(\langle c,cv\rangle\) the action is \(\begin{psmallmatrix}\lambda&0\\\theta&\mu\end{psmallmatrix}\), and on the complementary invariant lines \(\langle1\rangle\) and \(\langle v\rangle\) it is multiplication by \(\lambda\) and \(\mu\) respectively. The three-dimensional image is therefore a copy of the lower triangular \(2\times2\) matrix algebra, as multiplication of adjoint operators agrees with multiplication in the Hopf algebra. The plane is indecomposable because the radical generator acts nontrivially from the \(\lambda\)-weight vector to the \(\mu\)-weight vector, while any invariant complement to its stable \(\mu\)-line would require a second eigenline stable under that radical generator.

The displayed matrix has rank
\[
\mathbf1_{\lambda\ne0}+\mathbf1_{\mu\ne0}+
\operatorname{rank}\begin{pmatrix}\lambda&0\\\theta&\mu\end{pmatrix}.
\]
If both \(\lambda\) and \(\mu\) are nonzero, this is \(4\), occurring for \(q(q-1)^2q=q^2(q-1)^2\) elements. If exactly one is nonzero, it is \(2\), occurring for \(2(q-1)q\cdot q=2q^2(q-1)\) elements. When both vanish, it is \(1\) for \(\theta\ne0\), giving \(q(q-1)\) elements, and \(0\) for \(\theta=0\), giving \(q\) elements. These four cases exhaust all \(q^4\) elements, proving the polynomial and the forbidden rank \(3\).

## Verification

The standalone script `verify_adjoint.py` builds the four basis-action matrices directly from the multiplication table, coproduct and antipode, rather than relying on the theorem's closed matrix formula. It enumerates all \(q^4\) elements and computes their matrix ranks by modular Gaussian elimination for \(q=3,5,7,11\). The verified rank arrays are \((3,6,36,0,36)\), \((5,20,200,0,400)\), \((7,42,588,0,1764)\), and \((11,110,2420,0,12100)\). Run with `python3 verify_adjoint.py`; it prints `SWEEDLER_HOPF_ADJOINT_RANKS_OK`. The proof above, not finite testing, establishes all odd prime powers.

## Relationship to prior work

Gordienko and Morozova study ordinary and Hopf-algebra polynomial identities and codimensions of \(H_4\), including equality of its ordinary polynomial identities with those of the upper triangular matrix algebra in characteristic zero. That identity equivalence is different from the explicit quotient representation, whose kernel and rank fibers are computed here. Nguyen uses the left Hopf adjoint module to relate Tate-Hochschild and Tate cohomology, and treats \(H_4\) in detail; those results motivate examining the action, without giving this rank-count polynomial in the inspected sections. Kolb, Lorenz, Nguyen and Yammine study the general adjoint representation and its locally finite part, rather than this finite-field fiber census. The matrix-unit computations are elementary consequences of the defining Hopf operations; the additional claim is their exact all-field rank stratification and its explicit module-theoretic interpretation.

## Limitations

The statement requires odd characteristic; in characteristic two these Hopf relations and the factor \(2\) behave differently. This is a small, fully described Hopf algebra, not an extension to arbitrary Taft algebras or all Hopf algebras. Finite-field enumeration is corroborative, not an infinite proof. Historical novelty is provisional, particularly against older representation-theoretic treatments that might encode the same decomposition implicitly; no independent review is claimed. The image quotient itself may follow readily from known structural descriptions, so the exact rank census should not be interpreted as a broad new representation theorem.
