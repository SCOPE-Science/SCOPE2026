# The complete edge-general-position and maximal-set polynomials of the four-cube \(Q_4\)

## Finding

Let \(Q_4\) be the graph on binary four-vectors in which two vectors are adjacent precisely when they differ in one coordinate. A set of edges is **edge-general-position** if no shortest path contains three or more of its members. Its edge-general-position polynomial \(P_{Q_4}(z)=\sum_S z^{|S|}\) sums over all such sets \(S\). Its facet polynomial \(F_{Q_4}(z)=\sum_{S\text{ inclusion-maximal}}z^{|S|}\) sums over those that cannot be enlarged by adding an edge.

The complete polynomials are

\[
P_{Q_4}(z)=1+32z+496z^{2}+4384z^{3}+22520z^{4}+66688z^{5}+114992z^{6}+127360z^{7}+106612z^{8}+76960z^{9}+49392z^{10}+26400z^{11}+10936z^{12}+3360z^{13}+720z^{14}+96z^{15}+6z^{16}.
\]

\[
F_{Q_4}(z)=480z^{6}+1376z^{7}+600z^{8}+1920z^{9}+288z^{10}+16z^{12}+6z^{16}.
\]

Thus there are exactly \(610955\) legal edge sets and exactly \(4686\) inclusion-maximal legal edge sets. The latter have precisely the cardinalities \(6,7,8,9,10,12,16\); in particular, the smallest inclusion-maximal set has size \(6\) while the maximum size is \(16\). The \(6\) maximum sets are exactly the unions of two of the four coordinate edge classes. In particular, the simplicial complex of edge-general-position sets is not pure, with missing maximal cardinalities \(11,13,14,15\).

## Assumptions and scope

All graph vertices are distinguished binary vectors \(0,\ldots,15\); coordinates are indexed \(0,1,2,3\). This is an exact theorem for the single finite graph \(Q_4\), with its \(16\) vertices and \(32\) edges, not a claim about all hypercube dimensions. The polynomial counts unlabelled *subsets of edges* of that fixed, labelled graph (not graph-isomorphism classes). The word *maximal* means inclusion-maximal, not maximum cardinality.

## Proof

**Step 1: an exact finite forbidden-triple formulation.** Every shortest path between two binary vertices flips precisely the coordinates on which the endpoints differ, in any order. Hence every geodesic of \(Q_4\) has length at most \(4\), and every one is generated by choosing its endpoints and a permutation of their differing coordinates. Form the family \(\mathcal T\) of unordered triples of distinct edges occurring together on at least one of these paths, including nonconsecutive triples on four-edge paths. Direct deduplication gives \(|\mathcal T|=576\), as certified independently by the two algorithms below. By the definition, an edge set \(S\) is legal if and only if no triple in \(\mathcal T\) is contained in \(S\). Both directions of this equivalence are immediate: any violation supplies a forbidden triple, and any forbidden triple has a witness geodesic.

**Step 2: exhaustive enumeration without omissions or duplicates.** Order all \(32\) cube edges by increasing first endpoint and then coordinate. For each pair of distinct edge indices \(i,j\), let \(B(i,j)\) be the bitmask of all indices \(h\) with \(\{i,j,h\}\in\mathcal T\). Consider a search state \((i,S,B)\), where the first \(i\) edges have been decided, \(S\) is the set selected so far, and \(B\) is the set of edges that would create a forbidden triple if added to \(S\). Initially \(S=B=\varnothing\). At each index \(i\), first recurse on the branch excluding it. Include it only if \(i\notin B\), and then update the forbidden set to

\[
B'=B\cup\bigcup_{j\in S}B(i,j).
\]

In that branch the selected set becomes \(S\cup\{i\}\). This update is an invariant: a newly illegal third edge must complete a triple whose first two members are either both old (already in \(B\)) or one is \(i\) and the other is an old selected edge. Hence every branch selected is legal, and every legal set has one and only one include/exclude path. At a terminal state, inclusion-maximality is equivalent to \(S\cup B=E(Q_4)\). If an edge is missing and outside \(B\), it can be added legally, while a missing edge in \(B\) cannot. Tallying terminal states by \(|S|\) produces the complete coefficient table below; no limit, sampling, heuristic, or timeout truncation is involved.

**Step 3: independent complete construction and extremizers.** The second verifier generates all shortest paths through breadth-first distance layers from each source, rather than endpoint-coordinate permutations. It constructs the legal family iteratively by adding each edge to every previously legal subset if doing so completes no forbidden triple. It independently tests maximality through the existence of a valid immediate superset in its full generated family. Its resulting coefficients agree term by term. Moreover, it constructs the four coordinate edge classes directly, verifies that the exactly six size-\(16\) maximal sets are the \(\binom42=6\) unions of two classes, and finds no other size-\(16\) sets. All asserted polynomial and extremal conclusions follow.

## Verification

The included standalone `verify_q4.cpp` (C++17) implements the bitmask branching proof from Step 2. Run `g++ -std=c++17 -O2 verify_q4.cpp -o verify_q4 && ./verify_q4`; its output is the entire table and the 576-constraint count. The standalone `verify_q4_independent.py` (Python 3.8 or later) implements the distinct breadth-first-path/iterative-family proof from Step 3; run `python3 verify_q4_independent.py`. Their recorded, actual outputs are in `verification_output.txt`. Both sources are self-contained and reconstruct all input data from the labelled four-cube, with no external datasets. The second enumerator stores all \(610955\) distinct legal subsets, tests every missing-edge addition for maximality, and checks the six extremizers against the coordinate classes.

## Relationship to prior work

Manuel, Prabha, and Klavžar (2021), Theorem 3.2, already established \(\operatorname{gp}_e(Q_d)=2^d\) and Lemma 3.1 gave the two-coordinate-class construction; the known *numerical maximum* \(16\) is **not** asserted as new here. Their result does not determine the full number of legal sets, the smallest inclusion-maximal cardinality, the facet spectrum, or the number of maximum sets. Manuel, Prabha, and Klavžar (2022 preprint; 2025 journal), Theorem 4.2, extends the maximum-value computation to edge \(k\)-general-position sets but does not enumerate the present \(k=3\) feasible family or facets. Klavžar and Tan (2023) give maximal coordinate-class unions for Fibonacci and Lucas cubes, distinct induced partial cubes; these claims do not imply the exact full-four-cube census. Tian, Klavžar, and Tan (2024) establish extremal values for other graph classes rather than the present polynomials. Those published claims and the actual research-record searches in the associated originality assessment were compared at the level of implication, not title alone.

## Limitations

This is a finite, exhaustive computational proof for \(Q_4\); it is not an infinite-dimensional formula or a claim of independent external validation. The classification of every maximal set is given by cardinality enumeration, not by orbit representatives. Existing references already give the largest-size formula. Historical priority remains provisional because literature indexing can be incomplete.
