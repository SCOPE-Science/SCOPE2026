# The complete generalized-weight hierarchy of the anticanonical torus code on the degree-eight del Pezzo surface

## Finding

Let \(q\geq5\) be a prime power and put \(t=q-1\). For the split degree-eight toric del Pezzo surface \(X\cong\mathbb F_1\), the code obtained by evaluating its nine anticanonical monomials on the dense rational torus has generalized Hamming-weight hierarchy

\[
(d_1,d_2,d_3,d_4,d_5,d_6,d_7,d_8,d_9)
=(t^2-3t,\ t^2-2t-1,\ t^2-2t,\ t^2-t-2,\ t^2-t-1,\ t^2-t,\ t^2-2,\ t^2-1,\ t^2).
\]

The first entry, the ordinary minimum distance, is not claimed as new. The new contribution is the uniform determination of the higher generalized weights for this specific nine-monomial toric surface code. Geometrically, the optimal common-zero counts of systems of \(r\) independent anticanonical sections are

\[
(M_1,M_2,M_3,M_4,M_5,M_6,M_7,M_8,M_9)
=(3t,\ 2t+1,\ 2t,\ t+2,\ t+1,\ t,\ 2,\ 1,\ 0).
\]

## Assumptions and scope

Work over an arbitrary finite field \(k=\mathbb F_q\) with \(q\geq5\), including nonprime fields and fields of even characteristic. Blow up the torus-fixed point \([1:0:0]\) of \(\mathbb P^2_k\). The anticanonical divisor is \(3H-E\), so its global sections are precisely the cubics vanishing at this point. On the dense torus chart \([1:x:y]\), they form the vector space

\[
V=\operatorname{Span}_k\{x^a y^b:a,b\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0},\ 1\leq a+b\leq3\},\qquad \dim_kV=9.
\]

The convex hull of these exponent vectors is the anticanonical polygon of \(\mathbb F_1\). Evaluate \(V\) on \(T=(k^\times)^2\), a set of \(t^2\) rational points. Since all individual exponents are below \(t\), evaluation is injective. For \(1\leq r\leq9\), write \(M_r\) for the largest possible number of points where all members of an \(r\)-dimensional subspace \(W\subset V\) vanish. Then \(d_r=t^2-M_r\). This refers only to evaluation on the *dense torus*, not to the entire projective surface.

## Proof

**Upper bounds via a finite footprint certificate.** Fix any monomial order and any \(r\) linearly independent members of \(V\). Row reduction by leading terms produces a basis with \(r\) distinct leading monomials, all belonging to the nine-element exponent set

\[
A=\{(a,b)\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^2:1\leq a+b\leq3\}.
\]

Adjoin \(x^t-1\) and \(y^t-1\) to the ideal generated by the chosen basis. Because the grid consists of \(t^2\) distinct simple points, the dimension of the quotient is the number of common grid zeros. The initial ideal contains the selected leading monomials and the monomials \(x^t,y^t\), so the standard-monomial footprint bounds the common-zero count by

\[
F_B(t)=\#\{(i,j)\in\{0,\ldots,t-1\}^2:\text{there is no }(a,b)\in B\text{ with }i\geq a,\ j\geq b\},
\]

where \(B\subset A\) consists of the \(r\) distinct leading-exponent pairs. For every nonempty \(B\), since the exponents are at most three, this number is affine in \(t\) on \(t\geq4\). Explicitly put

\[
\begin{aligned}
c_B&=\sum_{0\leq i,j<3}\prod_{(a,b)\in B}\mathbf1_{\{i<a\text{ or }j<b\}},\\
h_B&=\sum_{0\leq j<3}\prod_{(a,b)\in B}\mathbf1_{\{j<b\}},\\
v_B&=\sum_{0\leq i<3}\prod_{(a,b)\in B}\mathbf1_{\{i<a\}},
\end{aligned}
\qquad F_B(t)=(h_B+v_B)t+c_B-3(h_B+v_B).
\]

The formula partitions the grid into its \(3\times3\) corner, horizontal and vertical strips, and an interior block; the interior contributes nothing because \(B\ne\varnothing\) and each leading monomial has both exponents below four. An exhaustive exact enumeration of all \(2^9-1=511\) nonempty subsets \(B\) gives the following table. The column \(F_B(4)\) is the *largest footprint value* at \(t=4\), while the final column is the largest slope of any such affine footprint.

| \(r\) | Number of subsets | \(\max_B F_B(4)\) | \(\max_B(h_B+v_B)\) | Upper bound for \(M_r\) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 1 | 9 | 12 | 3 | \(3t\) |
| 2 | 36 | 9 | 2 | \(2t+1\) |
| 3 | 84 | 8 | 2 | \(2t\) |
| 4 | 126 | 6 | 1 | \(t+2\) |
| 5 | 126 | 5 | 1 | \(t+1\) |
| 6 | 84 | 4 | 1 | \(t\) |
| 7 | 36 | 3 | 0 | \(3\) |
| 8 | 9 | 2 | 0 | \(2\) |
| 9 | 1 | 1 | 0 | \(1\) |

For each row, *every* candidate footprint has both \(F_B(4)\) no larger than the displayed bound at \(4\) and slope no larger than the displayed slope; thus the stated affine bound holds simultaneously for every \(t\geq4\). The source file `verify_anticanonical_f1.py` reconstructs all 511 records and verifies both inequalities without relying on precomputed output.

**Sharper bounds at ranks seven, eight, nine.** Any three distinct torus points impose independent evaluation conditions on \(V\). To see this, for any target among those three, take one affine linear form vanishing at each of the other two points and one at the origin, with none vanishing at the target. Their product is a cubic in \(V\) that vanishes at the other two points but not at the target. Therefore evaluation onto any three torus points is surjective. Consequently, a subspace of dimension \(7\), \(8\), or \(9\) can have at most \(2\), \(1\), or \(0\) common torus zeros, respectively, improving the last three footprint bounds.

**Attainment in all fields.** Because \(t\geq4\), choose three different nonzero scalars and form the product of their distinct lines through the origin \(x=cy\). This one cubic has precisely \(3t\) zeros on the torus. Next take the product \(F=(x-c_1y)(x-c_2y)\) of two such lines. These give two disjoint ruling fibers of total size \(2t\). The subspace \(F\cdot\{\ell:\ell\text{ is affine linear and }\ell(P)=0\}\), for any torus point \(P\) off both fibers, has dimension two and common zero set exactly those fibers together with \(P\); it attains \(2t+1\). The three-dimensional space \(F\cdot\operatorname{Span}\{1,x,y\}\) attains \(2t\).

For ranks four, five and six choose a fixed line \(L:x=cy\) through the origin. Multiply \(L\) by all affine polynomials of degree at most two that vanish at, respectively, two, one or zero chosen torus points off \(L\). This gives subspaces of respective dimensions four, five and six. Their common torus-zero sets consist of the \(t\) torus points of \(L\) plus the prescribed off-line points; the residual quadratic systems have no further base points, since products of affine linear forms can vanish at the prescribed points while avoiding any additional specified point. These attain \(t+2,t+1,t\). Finally, kernels of evaluation at two, one, or zero torus points yield dimensions seven, eight, and nine; the proven three-point separation property shows their common zeros are exactly those specified. Hence all nine bounds are sharp.

## Verification

The enclosed standard-library Python 3 program `verify_anticanonical_f1.py` enumerates the 511 candidate leading-monomial sets and checks their exact affine footprints, exhaustively computes evaluation ranks for all \(2^{16}=65\,536\) subsets of the torus over \(\mathbb F_5\), and independently tests the low-rank geometric constructions over \(\mathbb F_5\), \(\mathbb F_7\), and \(\mathbb F_{11}\). It terminates with the line `ANTICANONICAL_F1_HIERARCHY_VERIFIED` when the checks pass. The symbolic proof above, not finite-field testing, establishes the assertion for all prime powers \(q\geq5\). A separate file `footprint_certificate.json` records the exact affine coefficients and leading-monomial subsets of every footprint used in the bound.

## Relationship to prior work

Little–Schenck and earlier work of Hansen establish toric-code geometry and ordinary minimum-distance techniques; they do not state the full nine-weight hierarchy for this polygon. Boguslavskii classifies generalized weights of codes from *all rational points* on split del Pezzo surfaces, including the degree-eight surface, but that code has length \(q^2+2q+1\), not \((q-1)^2\). Its generalized-weight table does not directly give the higher weights of the dense-torus puncturing. González Sarabia–Camps–Sarmiento–Villarreal study homogeneous-degree evaluation on a projective torus, principally second weights. Beelen–Datta and Datta develop complete hierarchies and relative hierarchies for the full affine Cartesian degree-filtered code family; the nine-monomial space here excludes the constant term and is a distinct toric polygon space. Patanker–Singh address squarefree/hypersimplex exponent families, unlike this cubic polygon. The proof uses the standard footprint technique but also gives an exact polygon-specific certificate and interpolation sharpness not quoted from those works.

## Limitations

The result concerns rational dense-torus evaluation of \(\mathcal O_X(-K_X)\) on the split surface. It does not determine higher weights for the code evaluating at every rational point of the projective surface, for nonsplit forms, or for \(q<5\). The classification gives numerical generalized weights, not a complete classification of *all* minimizing subcodes. An unindexed earlier toric-code computation or a stronger general theorem not identified in the literature search could affect the priority assessment; independent mathematical verification remains outstanding.
