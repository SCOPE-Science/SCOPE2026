# Review of the genus-four binary-field gonality–Weil spectrum

## Correctness

**PASS.** Over the base field, a hyperelliptic genus-four curve has gonality two; a smooth nonhyperelliptic canonical curve on a split quadric or cone has an arithmetic ruling and hence gonality three. A nonsplit canonical quadric has no rational ruling, and the published degree-four-map/effective-degree-two-divisor lemma makes its gonality four exactly when it has a base-field or quadratic point, and five otherwise. All 620 source-indexed entries were checked using the compact 620-row table, and a separate direct finite-field enumerator verified one explicit hyperelliptic/cone/nonsplit triple through degree four. The published source counts and sum constraints reconcile exactly.

## Originality

**PASS, qualified.** Xarles (2020) already provides the entire 1,044-curve enumeration, 620 Weil groups, hyperelliptic versus nonhyperelliptic counts and examples with isogenous curves on different quadrics, including two hyperelliptic plus three-quadric-type cases. Faber–Grantham–Howe (2022) already pose the zeta/gonality question, provide counterexamples and prove the genus-four exceptional boundary. The new assertion is specifically the *complete exact eight-category gonality-support spectrum*, with 178 mixed groups and 21 triple-gonality groups. The closest primary texts, supplementary raw database and thematic research results were compared by implication. A previous unpublished or unindexed incidence calculation remains possible; no blanket historical-priority guarantee is made.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** Rather than recompute the original table of points per curve, the result resolves the natural arithmetic question of how frequently Jacobian isogeny fails to preserve gonality across every binary genus-four curve, distinguishes eight possible support profiles and exhibits a fully checked three-gonality witness with one zeta function. It is a complete, natural joint invariant with a clear use in comparing curve gonality and Jacobian arithmetic.
