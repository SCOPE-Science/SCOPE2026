# Self-review of the deformation-hull Hilbert–Samuel theorem

## Correctness — PASS
The domain is the specific qG deformation hull whose two octagonal-cone factors and six formal directions are explicitly given by Petracci–Villa [1, Proposition 3.1 and Theorem 4.1]. The ten homogeneous relations of each factor generate a graded ideal. An exact-rational Gröbner calculation yields twelve stated leading monomials; all 66 S-pairs reduce to zero. LCM inclusion–exclusion proves the full, not merely truncated, rational series. The radical and its two linear components are established in [1], making subtraction of Hilbert series an exact determination of the nilradical. Tensor-product factorization yields the full hull's series. The bundled checker was run successfully. No invariant-theoretic quotient is tacitly equated with the hull.

## Originality — PASS, with residual historical risk
The primary source [1] establishes the full defining ideal and a seven-piece primary decomposition but does not state the graded Hilbert–Samuel series, its all-order coefficients, or the exact nilradical Hilbert series. These evaluated invariants are therefore not a verbatim result and require a substantive Gröbner/monomial calculation. The nearest accessible research records on graded deformations of dilated del Pezzo hexagons and on a separate Igusa Kuranishi germ concern different presentations and do not imply the result. The earlier related toric-Fano studies [2, 3] concern different models; abstract-level checks do not fully exclude an unindexed computation. A ring presentation determines its Hilbert series in principle, and that dependency is fully credited rather than described as a new deformation-hull construction.

## Value — PASS
For a genuinely nonreduced qG deformation base, complete Hilbert–Samuel coefficients record dimensions of all infinitesimal orders, unlike component dimensions and generic multiplicities. The exact nilradical series isolates the persistent eleven-dimensional high-order contribution of the embedded and nonreduced scheme structure. These are natural invariants of the local deformation problem, not arbitrary slices or finite sampling, and permit exact comparison of higher-order obstruction geometry with the reduced support.
