# The complete short-cycle window of the ordered length-three nerve

## Finding

Let \(C_n\) be the simple cycle on \(n\) vertices, with the unweighted shortest-path metric. For each integer \(L\ge0\), let \(N_L(C_n)\) be the ordered length-filtered enriched nerve, defined by the simplicial set of vertex tuples of consecutive total distance at most \(L\). Its geometric realization is denoted \(X_{n,L}\). For **each** \(n\in\{3,4,5,6\}\), the complete integral homotopy type at the first nontrivial cubic cutoff \(L=3\) is:

\[
\begin{aligned}
X_{3,3}&\simeq\bigvee^{16}S^3,\\
X_{4,3}&\simeq\bigvee^{9}S^3,\\
X_{5,3}&\simeq\bigvee^{9}S^2\vee\bigvee^{5}S^3,\\
X_{6,3}&\simeq\bigvee^{5}S^2\vee\bigvee^{6}S^3.
\end{aligned}
\]

Moreover, for every \(n\in\{3,4,5,6\}\), the **inclusion** \(X_{n,2}\hookrightarrow X_{n,3}\) induces the zero homomorphism on *every positive-degree reduced integral homology group*. This includes the nontrivial degree-two classes of the earlier stage: for \(n=3,4,5,6\), the ranks of \(H_2(X_{n,2};\mathbb Z)\) are respectively \(8,7,5,6\), but none survives to cutoff three. Thus the two-spheres for \(n=5,6\) are all newly born at that stage.

The range \(3\le n\le6\) is precisely the complete exceptional short-cycle range relative to the strict high-girth hypothesis \(2L<g(C_n)\) at \(L=3\). The stable \(n\ge7\) case is not claimed as new.

## Assumptions and scope

The graph is **undirected**, **simple**, and has its intrinsic integer shortest-path metric \(d(i,j)=\min\{(i-j)\bmod n,(j-i)\bmod n\}\), with vertices \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\). The length nerve is the *ordered simplicial set*, not the ordinary unordered diameter-based Vietoris–Rips complex. Its \(k\)-simplices are all tuples \((v_0,\ldots,v_k)\) satisfying \(\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}d(v_i,v_{i+1})\le L\); faces delete vertices and degeneracies duplicate a vertex. A nondegenerate tuple has no equal adjacent entries, but its nonadjacent entries may coincide (backtracking is allowed). Every nondegenerate step costs at least one, so \(X_{n,3}\) is a finite, connected CW complex of dimension at most three. All assertions concern integral, not merely rational, homology and genuine geometric homotopy type.

## Proof

**Step 1 — exact normalized chain model.** The normalized simplicial chain groups of \(N_3(C_n)\) are free abelian on the nondegenerate ordered tuples just defined. Deletion of vertex \(i\) contributes sign \((-1)^i\); a face with equal adjacent vertices is degenerate and contributes zero. The triangle inequality ensures deletion stays within cutoff three. Therefore the full cellular boundary is

\[
\partial_k(v_0,\ldots,v_k)=\sum_{i=0}^{k}(-1)^i\,\overline{(v_0,\ldots,\widehat v_i,\ldots,v_k)},
\]

where a bar means zero for a degenerate tuple. There are no cells beyond dimension three. A complete integer enumeration of these finite bases and maps gives:

| \(n\) | \((\operatorname{rank} C_0,\ldots,\operatorname{rank} C_3)\) | \((\operatorname{rank}\partial_1,\operatorname{rank}\partial_2,\operatorname{rank}\partial_3)\) | \((\beta_2,\beta_3)\) |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 3 | \((3,6,12,24)\) | \((2,4,8)\) | \((0,16)\) |
| 4 | \((4,12,32,32)\) | \((3,9,23)\) | \((0,9)\) |
| 5 | \((5,20,60,40)\) | \((4,16,35)\) | \((9,5)\) |
| 6 | \((6,30,72,48)\) | \((5,25,42)\) | \((5,6)\) |

Every nonzero Smith invariant of all three boundary matrices is \(1\). Thus their images are primitive sublattices, and the resulting integral homology is torsion-free. The ranks also yield \(\widetilde H_0=H_1=0\) for all four complexes. These are complete finite integer calculations, not statistical experiments; the source checker reproduces the matrices and assertions from the definition.

**Step 2 — fundamental group, separately from the homology check.** Fix a cycle edge orientation for bookkeeping. At cutoff three, every ordered one-cell \((u,w)\) may be homotoped relative to endpoints to a walk of original unit graph edges: choose a shortest graph path \(u=v_0,\ldots,v_r=w\), \(r\le3\), and use the two-cell for each ordered triple \((v_0,v_1,v_r)\), then induct. The backtrack two-cell \((u,v,u)\) of total length two identifies the unit edges \((u,v)\) and \((v,u)\) as mutually inverse in the fundamental groupoid. Consequently \(\pi_1(X_{n,3})\) is generated by the single original cycle loop. For \(3\le n\le6\), choose two vertices admitting complementary cycle walks of lengths at most three: lengths \((1,2)\), \((2,2)\), \((2,3)\), and \((3,3)\) for \(n=3,4,5,6\) respectively. Each walk is homotopic relative endpoints to the *same* directed one-cell between its endpoints, using two-cells of total length at most three, so their difference is the original cycle loop. This loop vanishes. Therefore \(\pi_1(X_{n,3})=1\), not merely \(H_1=0\).

**Step 3 — upgrade to actual homotopy equivalence.** A connected simply connected CW complex of dimension at most three with free abelian homology is a wedge of two- and three-spheres. Indeed the degree-two Hurewicz map is an isomorphism; the Whitehead exact sequence gives surjectivity \(\pi_3\twoheadrightarrow H_3\) for a simply connected three-complex. Choose sphere maps representing integral bases in degrees two and three. Their wedge induces an integral homology isomorphism, hence is a homotopy equivalence by the homology form of Whitehead's theorem for simply connected CW complexes. Applying this to the integral ranks from Step 1 proves all four homotopy types.

**Step 4 — the cutoff-two inclusion is zero.** Repeat the same integral chain construction at cutoff two. For \(n=3,4,5,6\), its \(H_2\) ranks are \((8,7,5,6)\); its \(H_1\) ranks are \((0,0,1,1)\), and all its positive homology lies in degrees one and two. The checker embeds the entire rational two-cycle space for cutoff two into the normalized chain group at cutoff three and verifies exactly that these cycles lie in the *image* of \(\partial_3\) at cutoff three; the rational rank increase of \([\partial_3\mid Z_2(X_{n,2})]\) is zero for each \(n\). This implies the integral map on \(H_2\) vanishes because the target \(H_2\) is torsion-free; the degree-one map vanishes because the target has \(H_1=0\). For the pentagon and hexagon, the vanishing also has a structural explanation: the basic edge-sphere cycle \((u,v,u)-(v,u,v)\) is (up to sign) the boundary of the new three-cell \((u,v,u,v)\). This concludes the inclusion assertion.

It generates every nondegenerate ordered tuple from scratch at both cutoffs, constructs the integral boundary matrices, verifies \(\partial^2=0\), computes **exact integer Smith forms**, and checks the rank of the induced cutoff-two-to-three map on homology. A recorded successful run is embedded as `cycle_cutoff3_output.txt`. The finite matrices are small (the largest has 72 rows), and all ranks are exact rational arithmetic; no numerical tolerances are used. The fundamental-group and three-dimensional homotopy arguments of Steps 2–3 are independently necessary; matrix ranks alone do not certify a homotopy type.

## Relationship to prior work

Otter introduced the length-filtered enriched-nerve framework. Ivanov and Xu's open-access analysis of \(\ell_p\)-Vietoris–Rips simplicial sets (especially §2.3) explicitly identifies the \(\ell_1\) simplicial set with the enriched length nerve and develops general stability and continuous-circle results; these do not provide the four finite-cycle homotopy classifications and integral inclusion maps stated here. Livernet–Roff–Whitehouse's 2026 magnitude-path spectral-sequence work provides recent motivation for the topology of these length-filtered objects but does not state this exact exceptional-window classification. The ordinary unordered graph-metric Vietoris–Rips complex differs fundamentally from the ordered construction, which retains backtracking cells and has a separate cellular chain model. The strict high-girth theorem for cutoff three concerns \(n\ge7\) and does not imply the present \(3\le n\le6\) cases. The initial complete-graph case is included for completeness of the natural boundary window, not presented as an independent general complete-graph theorem.

## Limitations

Only the inclusive ordered length threshold three and the four cycle lengths \(3\le n\le6\) are classified here. The claim does not give homotopy types at later lengths, nor does it classify arbitrary graphs with short cycles or equivariant symmetry actions. The inclusion vanishing is established for this precise family; no general universal vanishing statement is asserted. The original framework sources are not certificates of novelty. The finite exact checker is reproducible, but an independent scientific audit and exhaustive global publication-priority assessment have not been performed.
