# The complete square-symmetry character of the four-by-four perfect-matching complex

## Finding
Let \(G=P_4\square P_4\) be the ordinary nonperiodic square grid with vertex set \(\{0,1,2,3\}^2\). Let \(X=\mathcal P(G)\) be the simplicial complex of graph-edge sets that are subsets of perfect matchings (not the ordinary matching complex). The eight square symmetries act simplicially. Set \(r(i,j)=(j,3-i)\), \(s(i,j)=(i,3-j)\). Write \(E\) for the standard rational two-dimensional plane representation of \(D_4=\langle r,s\mid r^4=s^2=1,\ srs=r^{-1}\rangle\), and \(\eta\) for the one-dimensional rational character with \(\eta(r)=-1,\eta(s)=1\). The complete reduced rational homology representations are
\[
\boxed{\widetilde H_2(X;\mathbb Q)\cong E,\qquad \widetilde H_3(X;\mathbb Q)\cong\eta,\qquad \widetilde H_j(X;\mathbb Q)=0\quad(j\notin\{2,3\}).}
\]
In particular, the full square-symmetry quotient has **zero reduced rational homology in every degree**:
\[
\boxed{\widetilde H_j(|X|/D_4;\mathbb Q)=0\quad\text{for all }j.}
\]
The previously established nonequivariant homotopy type \(X\simeq S^2\vee S^2\vee S^3\) is **not** claimed as new; the new assertion is the action of every square symmetry on these homology groups and its quotient consequence.

## Assumptions and scope
Vertices are \((i,j)\), where \(0\le i,j\le3\); grid edges join vertices at Manhattan distance one. Index the 24 graph edges by scanning grid vertices row-major, recording the downward neighbor (if present) and then the rightward neighbor. A simplex is the bitmask of its indexed graph edges. Its orientation is increasing edge-index order. The full group of geometric square symmetries acts on the 24 indexed edges and on oriented chains with the usual permutation sign. All homology groups are reduced singular homology with rational coefficients unless explicitly stated otherwise. There are exactly 36 perfect matchings.

The character table, with conjugacy columns identity, quarter-turns, half-turn, reflections in horizontal/vertical axes, and diagonal reflections, is
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrr}
&1&r,r^3&r^2&\text{axis}&\text{diagonal}\\ \hline
\chi_2&2&0&-2&0&0\\
\chi_3&1&-1&1&1&-1
\end{array}.
\]

## Proof
**Exact finite face model.** Recursively enumerate perfect matchings by matching the smallest currently uncovered grid vertex to each currently uncovered neighbor. There are 36 matchings. List all subsets of their eight edges; their union has 5,407 faces counting the empty face, with face counts by dimensions \(-1\) through \(7\):
\[
(1,24,212,796,1516,1596,938,288,36).
\]
This is a finite exhaustive enumeration, not sampling.

**An exact chain reduction.** At each step select an active nonempty simplex having exactly one active immediate coface, remove it together with that coface, and repeat deterministically. The attached reproducible checker performs 2,603 elementary simplicial-collapse pairs, leaving a \(3\)-dimensional complex with \((16,64,85,35)\) faces in dimensions \(0,1,2,3\). Its matrices \(\partial_1,\partial_2,\partial_3\) with increasing face-index orientations have *exact rational ranks* \((15,49,34)\). Matrix multiplication checks \(\partial_1\partial_2=0\) and \(\partial_2\partial_3=0\). Thus the only nonzero reduced rational homology ranks are \(2\) in degree two and \(1\) in degree three. This agrees with the independently published nonequivariant homotopy classification.

**Top-dimensional character from a primitive cycle.** The integer kernel of the remaining \(85\times35\) matrix \(\partial_3\) is generated by a single primitive vector having 35 nonzero coefficients, all \(+1\) or \(-1\); the checker obtains it by exact rational nullspace computation and integer normalization. For each of the eight square symmetries, first permute each oriented tetrahedron in this integral cycle with its exact sign. Then apply the standard chain projection for every elementary collapse \((a,b)\), with \(\partial b=\varepsilon a+\cdots\), by replacing the coefficient \(c_a a\) by \(-c_a\varepsilon(\partial b-\varepsilon a)\) and discarding \(b\). Every resulting chain is *exactly* the initial primitive cycle times a sign. These signs are \((1,-1,1,-1,1,1,-1,-1)\) for \((1,r,r^2,r^3,s,r^2s,rs,r^3s)\), taking the latter four as axis, axis, diagonal and diagonal reflections respectively. This proves the full \(\widetilde H_3\) representation is \(\eta\). Since the group and cycle action are computed at integral chain level, no floating-point or modular inference enters this step.

**Degree-two character by the signed fixed-simplex formula.** For a symmetry \(g\), its reduced Lefschetz number is
\[
\widetilde L(g)=-1+\sum_{\substack{\varnothing\ne\sigma\in X\\g\sigma=\sigma}}(-1)^{|\sigma|-1}\operatorname{sgn}(g|_\sigma).
\]
The checker enumerates every nonempty face and computes the orientation sign from the 24-edge permutation. For the same eight symmetries the respective numbers of setwise fixed nonempty simplices are \((5406,6,86,6,214,214,6,6)\); the reduced Lefschetz values are \((1,1,-3,1,-1,-1,1,1)\). There is homology only in degrees two and three, so \(\widetilde L(g)=\chi_2(g)-\chi_3(g)\). Substituting the exact degree-three character gives \(\chi_2=(2,0,-2,0,0)\) by dihedral conjugacy classes. This is exactly the character of \(E\). Since rational representations of finite groups are semisimple and the character determines the module here, the representation assertions follow.

**Quotient.** For finite simplicial group actions, barycentric subdivision gives an admissible action; averaging in characteristic zero identifies rational homology of the orbit space with invariant rational homology. Neither \(E\) nor \(\eta\) contains a trivial \(D_4\)-summand. Therefore every reduced rational homology group of \(|X|/D_4\) vanishes. This does **not** assert that the quotient is contractible or integrally acyclic.

Run `python3 CHECKER.py` in an environment with Python 3 and SymPy. The checker independently reconstructs all matchings and faces from graph adjacency, executes and verifies each simplicial collapse, computes all three *exact rational* boundary-matrix ranks and both chain-complex products, obtains a primitive integral top cycle, checks its exact \(\pm1\) action under all eight symmetries through chain projection, and independently calculates every signed fixed-face Lefschetz sum. Its completion marker is `DIHEDRAL_PERFECT_MATCHING_4X4_OK`. Exact counts and every expected character value are asserted rather than merely logged. No external files or precomputed tables are required.

## Relationship to prior work
Chandrakar, Chau, Goyal, Karki and Subhash (arXiv:2611.18058, submitted 13 November 2026) classify the homotopy type of the **three-row even-column** perfect-matching complex. They do not calculate four-by-four square-symmetry characters. A previously published result, *The four-by-four grid perfect-matching complex is a mixed-dimensional sphere wedge*, establishes \(X\simeq S^2\vee S^2\vee S^3\) and is fully acknowledged here: that nonequivariant result does **not** specify the action on homology. Another published result, *The \(D_4\)-homology character of the four-by-four grid matching complex*, determines characters for the **ordinary** matching complex \(\mathcal M(G)\), whose face set and homology differ from \(\mathcal P(G)\). Neither result implies the equivariant character calculated above. No independent publication-priority guarantee is asserted.

## Limitations
The theorem concerns precisely the nonperiodic Cartesian \(4\times4\) grid and rational homology. It does not identify the integral equivariant module, the homotopy type of the quotient, or characters for other dimensions. The checker is a complete finite calculation for this complex but not an infinite-family proof. Equivalent unindexed prior calculations may exist. Independent mathematical review is not represented by this package.
