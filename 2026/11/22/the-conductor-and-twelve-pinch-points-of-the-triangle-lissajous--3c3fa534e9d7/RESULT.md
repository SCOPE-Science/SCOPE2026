# The conductor and twelve pinch points of the triangle Lissajous sextic

## Finding
Let \(S\subset\mathbb A^3_{\mathbb C}\) be the phase-shifted triangle Lissajous hypersurface
\[
F=x^4+y^4+z^4-2(x^2y^2+y^2z^2+z^2x^2)+4x^2y^2z^2=0.
\]
It is integral, and its normalization is the algebraic two-torus. More precisely, put
\[
R=\mathbb C[y,z,s,t]/(s^2+y^2-1,\ t^2+z^2-1),\qquad x=zs+yt.
\]
Then \(\operatorname{Spec}R\to S\) is its finite birational normalization. Its conductor, viewed as an ideal of \(R\), is exactly \((xyz)R\). As an ideal of \(A=\mathbb C[x,y,z]/(F)\), the conductor is generated by
\[
2xyz,\quad y(x^2-y^2+z^2),\quad z(x^2+y^2-z^2),\quad
x(x^2-y^2-z^2+2y^2z^2).
\]
The reduced singular locus consists of exactly the six affine lines
\[
\begin{aligned}
&y=0,\quad x=\pm z;\\
&z=0,\quad x=\pm y;\\
&x=0,\quad y=\pm z.
\end{aligned}
\]
At every nonzero point of these lines except the twelve points with exactly one coordinate zero and the other coordinates in \(\{1,-1\}\), the analytic singularity is a transverse double crossing. At those twelve points it is a Whitney pinch point, analytically equivalent to \(a^2=b^2c\). At the origin the normalization has four distinct smooth branches, with tangent planes \(x\pm y\pm z=0\) (the four choices with coefficient of \(x\) positive).

## Assumptions and scope
All schemes, normalizations, conductors and local analytic germs are over \(\mathbb C\). The claim concerns the whole affine sextic, not its projective closure or singularities at infinity. A conductor is the common ideal \(\{r\in R:rR\subseteq A\}\subseteq A\subseteq R\). The phrase transverse double crossing means the completed local analytic equation \(ab=0\), possibly times a smooth parameter.

## Proof
**Integral normalization.** The ring \(R\) is the product coordinate ring of two smooth affine conics, and is isomorphic to \(\mathbb C[u^{\pm1},v^{\pm1}]\) under \(u=y+is\) and \(v=z+it\). As a module over \(B=\mathbb C[y,z]\), it is free with basis \(1,s,t,st\). The element \(x=zs+yt\) satisfies \(F=0\) because
\[
x^2=y^2+z^2-2y^2z^2+2yzst.
\]
With columns the coordinates of \(1,x,x^2,x^3\) in that basis, their determinant is \(4y^2z^2(y^2-z^2)\), which is nonzero in \(\mathbb C(y,z)\). Hence the polynomial \(F\), monic of degree four in \(x\), is irreducible over \(\mathbb C(y,z)\); the induced inclusion \(A\hookrightarrow R\) is birational. The elements \(s,t\) satisfy monic equations over \(A\), so \(R\) is finite over \(A\). Since \(R\) is smooth and normal, this is the normalization.

**Exact conductor.** Direct algebra in \(R\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
2xyz\,s&=y(x^2-y^2+z^2),\\
2xyz\,t&=z(x^2+y^2-z^2),\\
2xyz\,st&=x(x^2-y^2-z^2+2y^2z^2).
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus \(2xyz\,R\subseteq A\), equivalently \((xyz)R\subseteq\mathfrak c\). In \(R\), the reduced divisor of \(xyz\) comprises exactly six smooth-at-the-generic-point irreducible curves: the two choices \(s=\pm1\) above \(y=0\), the two choices \(t=\pm1\) above \(z=0\), and the two choices \(s=\pm t\) above \(x=0\) (corresponding to \(y=\mp z\)). The map is not an isomorphism at the generic points of any of these curves: the generic target point has two distinct preimages. A conductor cannot be a unit at such a point. Hence the conductor vanishes along each of these six prime divisors and lies in their intersection, which is the radical principal ideal \((xyz)R\). Equality follows, and the four displayed generators follow from the free \(B\)-basis of \(R\).

**Singular locus.** Away from the image of \(xyz=0\), the conductor is a unit, so \(S\) is isomorphic to the smooth torus there. The image of \(y=0\) is \(x=\pm z\), that of \(z=0\) is \(x=\pm y\), and \(x=0\) forces \(y=\pm z\). Along every one of these lines the normalization map is generically not an isomorphism, so each line lies in the closed singular locus. There can be no other singular points.

**Local analytic types.** Above a general point of each of the six lines there are two distinct normalization points, and each branch immerses with a distinct tangent plane, giving an ordinary transverse double crossing. Over \((x,y,z)=(1,0,1)\), use the analytic branch \(s=\sqrt{1-y^2}\) and the local coordinates \(h=x-zs\), \(b=y\), \(c=1-z^2\). Since \(h=yt\) and \(t^2=c\), the local equation is \(h^2=b^2c\). Sign changes and coordinate permutations cover all twelve points of the stated form. At the origin there are four normalization preimages \((s,t)=(\pm1,\pm1)\); their differentials give tangent equations \(x=\pm z\pm y\). Equivalently, the degree-four tangent cone factors as
\[
(x-y-z)(x-y+z)(x+y-z)(x+y+z).
\]
These are four distinct smooth local branches. This exhausts the singular strata.

## Verification
The accompanying standalone \(\texttt{verify.py}\) performs exact polynomial and module calculations over \(\mathbb Q\): reduction of \(F\) in the normalization ring, the basis determinant, all four conductor-generator identities, vanishing of \(F\) and its derivatives on the six lines and all twelve pinch points, and the four linear tangent-cone factors. The script runs with Python 3 and SymPy using \(\texttt{python verify.py}\). Its assertions passed. These finite exact checks support, but do not replace, the generic-fiber, divisor and analytic-local proofs above.

## Relationship to prior work
Mascarin and Telen introduced the Lissajous-variety framework and the three-cycle examples [2]. Mascarin [1, Example 3.10] writes precisely this phase-shifted triangle sextic and investigates its intersection with the Cayley cubic; [1, Section 5] gives general singular-locus inclusion formulas, and its exact hypersurface characterization assumes at least four ambient coordinates. Those established facts are not claimed anew. The result here identifies the explicit normalization conductor and complete local singularity stratification of the three-coordinate sextic, which neither inspected text reports. The comparison concerns mathematical implication, not merely titles.

## Limitations
This theorem does not classify the projective closure at infinity, the Jacobian scheme's embedded components, or resolutions of the four-branch origin. The originality comparison includes the accessible full texts [1] and [2] and mathematical-record searches, but cannot exclude unindexed or uninspected literature. Mathematical review is not an independent audit.
