# The Dürer four-cut complex: 105 seven-spheres and its dihedral homology character

## Finding

For the twelve-vertex Dürer graph \(G=\operatorname{GP}(6,2)\), the ordinary four-cut complex \(K=\Delta_4(G)\) and its combinatorial Alexander dual \(L=K^\vee\) have integral homotopy types
\[
K\simeq\bigvee^{105}S^7,\qquad L\simeq\bigvee^{105}S^2.
\]
Writing \(r\) for simultaneous rotation of the two six-vertex layers by one step and \(s\) for simultaneous reversal, the entire automorphism group is \(D_6=\langle r,s\mid r^6=s^2=1,\,srs=r^{-1}\rangle\). Its rational action on \(\widetilde H_7(K;\mathbb Q)\) has the following character, with entries indexed by \(k=0,1,2,3,4,5\):
\[
\bigl(\operatorname{tr}(r^k)\bigr)_k=(105,-1,3,5,3,-1),\qquad
\bigl(\operatorname{tr}(sr^k)\bigr)_k=(-7,1,-7,1,-7,1).
\]
In particular \(\dim_{\mathbb Q}\widetilde H_7(K;\mathbb Q)^{D_6}=8\). All reduced integral homology outside dimension seven vanishes, and \(\widetilde H_7(K;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{105}\). The group action is part of the finding, not a claim about an equivariant sphere-wedge decomposition.

## Assumptions and scope

All complexes are abstract finite simplicial complexes and all homotopy types are ordinary geometric realizations. Number the vertices \(u_i=i\), \(v_i=6+i\) for \(0\le i<6\), with subscripts taken modulo six. The simple undirected graph \(G\) has exactly the eighteen edges
\[
\{u_i,u_{i+1}\},\quad \{u_i,v_i\},\quad \{v_i,v_{i+2}\},\qquad 0\le i<6.
\]
For a graph on vertex set \(V\), the ordinary four-cut complex is generated by the faces \(V\setminus T\) where \(T\subset V\), \(|T|=4\), and the induced subgraph \(G[T]\) is disconnected. Its Alexander dual consists precisely of the subsets \(S\subset V\) containing **no** disconnected induced four-set. Thus, a four-set is a face of \(L\) precisely when its induced subgraph is connected; a five-set belongs to \(L\) precisely when each of its four-subsets induces a connected subgraph. These definitions exclude the distinct *total* cut complex.

## Proof

**Exact finite combinatorics and elementary collapses.** Directly from the specified eighteen edges, there are \(429\) disconnected induced four-subsets out of \(\binom{12}{4}=495\). The Alexander dual \(L\) consequently has \(66\) four-vertex simplices, and exhaustive testing of all subsets gives the complete numbers of nonempty faces by cardinality:
\[
(f_0,f_1,f_2,f_3,f_4)=(12,66,220,66,6).
\]
The six five-vertex faces are the cyclic translates of \(\{u_0,u_1,u_2,v_0,v_2\}\); there is no face with six or more vertices. The companion file `collapse_pairs.json` contains a **specific ordered list of 66 pairs** \((\sigma,\tau)\), each with \(\sigma\subset\tau\) and \(|\tau|=|\sigma|+1\). The self-contained `verify_durer.py`, using only the Python standard library, reconstructs all faces from the eighteen edges and for each pair verifies that \(\tau\) is the *only* proper coface of \(\sigma\) in the remaining complex before removing both. In particular every move is an elementary simplicial collapse, not merely a discrete-Morse pairing. The first six moves remove a four-simplex with a tetrahedral free face; the other sixty remove a tetrahedron with a triangular free face. After all moves there remain exactly twelve vertices, sixty-six edges, and 160 triangles, with no higher-dimensional faces.

The original complex \(L\) has the full two-skeleton of an eleven-simplex, hence is simply connected. Elementary collapses preserve its homotopy type. Its final two-dimensional complex is therefore simply connected, with reduced Euler characteristic
\[
\widetilde\chi(L)=-1+12-66+160=105.
\]
Its second integral homology is free of rank 105, since it is the kernel of the integral boundary on a two-dimensional simplicial complex; all other reduced homology vanishes. By Hurewicz and the homology form of Whitehead's theorem, a simply connected two-dimensional CW complex with this homology has the homotopy type \(\bigvee^{105}S^2\). (Choose maps representing an integral basis of \(\pi_2\cong H_2\) and apply Whitehead.)

**Return from the dual.** Combinatorial Alexander duality on twelve vertices yields
\[
\widetilde H_j(K;\mathbb Z)\cong\widetilde H^{9-j}(L;\mathbb Z).
\]
Thus the only nonzero group is \(\widetilde H_7(K;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z^{105}\). As \(L\) has no face of cardinality six, every six-vertex subset lies in \(K\), so \(K\) contains the complete five-skeleton of the eleven-simplex and is at least four-connected. Integral Hurewicz successively upgrades this to six-connectedness because \(H_5(K)=H_6(K)=0\). Every facet of \(K\) is the complement of a disconnected induced four-set, so \(K\) is seven-dimensional. Choose 105 maps of seven-spheres representing a basis of \(H_7(K)\cong\pi_7(K)\); their wedge induces an integral homology isomorphism, hence a homotopy equivalence by Whitehead. This proves the first displayed homotopy type without assuming anything from finite-field ranks.

**Symmetry and the character.** The two triangles of \(G\) are precisely those on inner vertices of even and odd index. Therefore any graph automorphism preserves the six inner vertices; it must send the outer six-cycle to itself, so is one of the twelve transformations \(u_i\mapsto u_{i+k},v_i\mapsto v_{i+k}\) or \(u_i\mapsto u_{k-i},v_i\mapsto v_{k-i}\). Each is an automorphism, giving the full dihedral group. All twelve act as *even* permutations of the twelve vertices.

Because \(\widetilde H_*(L;\mathbb Q)\) is supported only in degree two, the augmented simplicial Lefschetz trace formula gives for every automorphism \(g\):
\[
\operatorname{tr}\bigl(g\mid\widetilde H_2(L;\mathbb Q)\bigr)
=-1+\sum_{\substack{\varnothing\ne\sigma\in L\\g\sigma=\sigma}}
(-1)^{|\sigma|-1}\operatorname{sgn}\bigl(g\mid_{\sigma}\bigr).
\]
Here the sign is calculated by comparing the increasing vertex order of each fixed simplex with its image order. Exhaustively enumerating the the 370 nonempty simplices using the standalone verifier gives the two rows of six traces stated above. Equivariant Alexander duality twists the corresponding action on \(\widetilde H_7(K;\mathbb Q)\) by the sign of the ambient twelve-vertex permutation; this sign is \(+1\) for every group element, so the character rows are identical. Finally averaging the twelve traces gives \((105-1+3+5+3-1-7+1-7+1-7+1)/12=8\).

## Verification

Run `python3 verify_durer.py` next to `collapse_pairs.json`. The verifier depends only on the Python standard library, exhaustively reconstructs the four-cut dual, checks all 66 concrete free-face moves, validates the group-preserving permutations and oriented-chain traces, and checks the absence of six-vertex dual faces. `verification_output.txt` is the output of an actual execution. This finite computation is an exhaustive certificate for a specified twelve-vertex complex; the deductions from collapses to homotopy type use the mathematical results proved above. No claim about arbitrary generalized Petersen graphs or larger parameters is made.

## Relationship to prior work

Bayer, Denker, Jelić Milutinović, Rowlands, Sundaram and Xue (arXiv:2304.13675, first submitted 26 April 2023) introduced and studied ordinary cut complexes; their Sections 6–7 provide general face-counting and topology results for trees, cycles, multipartite graphs, squared cycles, and clique prisms. Their Theorem 6.1 requires a complete six-skeleton in this case; that condition fails because the dual has six five-vertex faces, corresponding to missing six-dimensional faces of the cut complex. Thus even the theorem's specialized face-lattice and Euler conclusions cannot simply be invoked here; in particular it does not establish the Dürer dual collapse, integral homotopy type, or dihedral character. Their sequel (arXiv:2407.08158, submitted 11 July 2024) treats disjoint unions, squared paths, grids, and selected homology representations, not this generalized Petersen example.

The published result titled *Homotopy classification of four-cut complexes at the pentagonal girth boundary* assumes girth at least five; the Dürer graph has two triangles, so the hypothesis fails. Published records for the ordinary four-cut complex of the Petersen graph treat the different graph \(\operatorname{GP}(5,2)\), with a different automorphism group and homology rank. Another same-graph published record classifies the first three higher-independence complexes, a different vertex-subset condition that does not imply the ordinary four-cut topology or twelve-element character table. Relevant search results do not imply the new dual collapse or dihedral character. Bibliographic completeness is not certified; a separately indexed or unpublished equivalent remains possible.

## Limitations

This is a classification for the explicitly defined twelve-vertex graph, not a formula for all generalized Petersen graphs. The complex has a nonequivariant wedge-of-spheres homotopy type; the character computation does not construct an equivariant wedge decomposition. The group-fixed subspace of rational homology has dimension eight, but no claim is made about fixed-point spaces or quotient homotopy type. Finite certificates are checked exhaustively for this particular graph; independent validation has not been performed.
