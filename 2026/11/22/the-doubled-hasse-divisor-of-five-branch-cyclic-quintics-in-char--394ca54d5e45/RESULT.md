# The doubled Hasse divisor of five-branch cyclic quintics in characteristic eleven

## Finding
Let \(k\) be a field of characteristic \(11\), and let \(a,b\in k\setminus\{0,1\}\) with \(a\ne b\). Define the smooth projective plane quintic
\[
C_{a,b}:\quad Y^5=X(X-Z)(X-aZ)(X-bZ)Z
\]
and set \(s=a+b\), \(t=ab\), and \(\Delta=t(t-s+1)(s^2-4t)\). On the smooth parameter space \(\Delta\ne0\), put
\[
\begin{aligned}
H(s,t)={}&-5s^4+5s^3t+5s^3-5s^2t^2-5s^2t-5s^2\\
&+4st^2+4st+4t^3+3t^2+4t\in\mathbb F_{11}[s,t].
\end{aligned}
\]
Then \(C_{a,b}\) has genus \(6\), and the determinant of the **unrooted Cartier coefficient matrix** (equivalently, the associated Hasse–Witt Frobenius matrix, up to transpose) in the explicit basis below is
\[
\det B=-t^8(t-s+1)^8(s^2-4t)^4H(s,t)^2
\]
(the actual Cartier matrix has the eleventh roots of these coefficients). Consequently its **geometric nonordinary divisor is twice the reduced hypersurface** \(H=0\) on \(\Delta\ne0\). Every smooth point on this nonordinary support has Cartier rank \(4\) and \(a\)-number \(2\); all other smooth members are ordinary.

Over the prime field \(\mathbb F_{11}\), **all \(72\) ordered admissible parameter pairs are ordinary**. Over \(\mathbb F_{121}\), there are precisely \(14,042\) admissible ordered pairs: \(13,832\) ordinary and \(210\) nonordinary. All \(210\) exceptional curves have Jacobian \(11\)-rank \(4\) and \(a\)-number \(2\), and the \(210\) pairs form exactly three orbits of sizes \(30,60,120\) under reordering the five rational branch positions and rational projective changes of their coordinate. These are branch-configuration orbits, not asserted to be distinct abstract curve isomorphism classes over \(\mathbb F_{121}\).

## Assumptions and scope
The field characteristic is exactly \(11\). The notation \(\Delta\ne0\) means \(a,b,0,1\) are pairwise distinct, equivalently all five branch points \(0,1,a,b,\infty\) of the degree-five Kummer cover are distinct. No statement is made for colliding branch points, other Kummer exponent vectors, or the complete genus-six moduli space. Arithmetic counts refer to ordered pairs in the displayed normalized family, not twists or isomorphism classes. The two rational-branch field counts refer respectively to \(\mathbb F_{11}\) and \(\mathbb F_{121}\); the geometric divisor statement is valid over every characteristic-eleven extension field and its algebraic closure.

## Proof
At each finite branch point \(f(x)=x(x-1)(x-a)(x-b)\) has a simple zero, so the partial derivative in \(x\) is nonzero when \(y=0\); at other finite points the \(y\)-derivative of \(y^5-f(x)\) is nonzero. The only projective point at infinity is \([1:0:0]\), where the derivative in \(Z\) is nonzero. Thus the plane quintic is smooth and has genus \((5-1)(5-2)/2=6\).

At infinity a local parameter has \(x\) of pole order \(5\), \(y\) of pole order \(4\), and \(dx\) of pole order \(6\). Hence a basis of holomorphic differentials is \(x^i\,dx/y^j\), with \(j=2,3,4\) and \(0\le i\le j-2\); the three blocks have dimensions \(1,2,3\).

Since \(p=11\) and \(y^5=f(x)\), the Cartier identity
\[
\frac{x^i\,dx}{y^j}=\frac{x^if(x)^{2j}\,dx}{y^{11j}}
\]
gives an independent Cartier block for each \(j\). Writing \(f(x)^{2j}=\sum_m c_{j,m}x^m\), the matrix of the \(j\)-block, up to entrywise eleventh roots, has entry \(c_{j,11k+10-i}\) in row \(k\), column \(i\), for \(0\le i,k\le j-2\). This follows from the Cartier extraction rule \(\mathcal C(x^{11k+10}dx)=x^kdx\).

Coefficient extraction and exact polynomial arithmetic in \(\mathbb F_{11}[s,t]\) give the three block determinants
\[
\begin{aligned}
\det B_2&=H(s,t),\\
\det B_3&=-t^2(t-s+1)^2(s^2-4t)H(s,t),\\
\det B_4&=t^6(t-s+1)^6(s^2-4t)^3.
\end{aligned}
\]
Their product is the asserted determinant of the six-dimensional unrooted Hasse–Witt matrix. The polynomial greatest common divisor \(\gcd(H,\partial H/\partial s)=1\) proves that its support is reduced, so the divisor multiplicity is precisely two. Moreover the ideal generated by the four entries of \(B_3\) and \(u\Delta-1\) has reduced Gröbner basis \(\{1\}\) in \(\mathbb F_{11}[u,s,t]\). Thus \(B_3\) never has rank zero on the smooth locus. On \(H=0\), the \(B_2\) block has rank zero, \(B_3\) rank one, and \(B_4\) rank three, proving the geometric Cartier rank and \(a\)-number assertion.

For the arithmetic counts, represent \(\mathbb F_{121}=\mathbb F_{11}[i]/(i^2+1)\); its polynomial is irreducible because \(-1\) is a nonsquare modulo \(11\). Enumerate all ordered \((a,b)\) with \(a,b\notin\{0,1\}\), \(a\ne b\), evaluating both \(H\) and the three exact Cartier blocks. There are \((121-2)(121-3)=14,042\) pairs, with exactly \(210\) zeros of \(H\) and none with both parameters in \(\mathbb F_{11}\). At every exceptional pair the Frobenius-twisted product of the rank-one two-dimensional block with its eleventh-power conjugate has rank one and nonzero trace. Therefore its iterates have stable rank one; the six-dimensional Cartier operator has stable rank \(0+1+3=4\), proving the asserted \(11\)-rank. An independent \(\mathbb F_{121}\)-presentation using \(u^2=2\) reproduces the count \(210\).

Finally, a projective coordinate change taking any ordered three of the five branch points to \(\infty,0,1\), followed by the two orders of the remaining points, produces at most \(5!=120\) normalized pairs. The cross-ratio formula computes these pairs exactly. It partitions the \(210\) exceptional pairs into three distinct orbits of sizes \(30,60,120\), corresponding to branch-configuration stabilizer orders \(4,2,1\).

Its exact terminal assertion is:

## Relationship to prior work
Badr and Lorenzo García (2017, arXiv:1701.06065, Lemma 3.3) give the five-branch cyclic-quintic model in their automorphism-stratification study, under their own characteristic assumptions \(p=0\) or \(p>13\); the characteristic-eleven smoothness and Cartier assertions here are proved independently and do not invoke their classification in characteristic eleven. Their study does not compute the characteristic-eleven Hasse divisor or the two split-branch arithmetic censuses. The 2026 plane-quintic superspeciality study of Ohashi (arXiv:2605.29624) explicitly focuses on larger automorphism groups and one-parameter order-eight and order-ten families, not the two-parameter, five-simple-branch characteristic-eleven divisor established here. Related previously recorded cyclic-quintic genus-four and cyclic-order-ten genus-six results concern different branching patterns or characteristic ranges and do not imply this statement.

## Limitations
This is a same-model verified mathematical result, not an independent audit or a literature-wide priority certificate. The orbit counts classify **branch presentations** under rational projective coordinate changes, not complete arithmetic or geometric isomorphism classes of curves. The \(11\)-rank assertion for exceptional curves is proved for parameters over \(\mathbb F_{121}\); for geometric parameters over larger extensions only Cartier rank and \(a\)-number are asserted. No Newton polygon or full Hasse–Weil polynomial is determined. The 2017 source's automorphism-stratum classification is not extended wholesale into characteristic eleven. Unindexed results could affect historical priority.
