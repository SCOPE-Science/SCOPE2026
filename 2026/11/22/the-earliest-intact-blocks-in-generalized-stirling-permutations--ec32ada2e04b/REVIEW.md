# Review of the early-intact-label Poisson theorem

## Correctness — PASS

The theorem fixes an integer multiplicity \(r\ge2\). Every valid word is uniquely generated by inserting the next maximal run into a uniformly selected gap, so the finite joint survival law follows by counting the disjoint internal gaps of intact marked runs. The one-label product has the stated exact Gamma form. Its exponent \(\alpha=(r-1)/r\) forces the scale \(n^{\alpha/(1+\alpha)}\), and the independent-counterpart intensity converges to \(\int_0^t x^\alpha\,dx\). The joint survival product differs by a multiplicative \(1+o(1)\) from independent one-label probabilities for any fixed number of labels in a compact positive scaled window. Bernoulli's inequality gives a domination bound for *all* subset sizes, permitting a globally summable Laplace-functional expansion. An explicit boundary estimate controls labels whose scaled coordinates approach zero. The resulting Laplace functional uniquely identifies the Poisson point process and all fixed-rank extreme laws. Exact small-instance enumeration independently checked all selected-subset events. The finite checks supplement, not replace, the asymptotic argument.

## Originality — PASS, with historical-priority qualification

The established literature provides more than basic Stirling-permutation definitions: Janson–Kuba–Panholzer (2008) supply the uniform insertion model; Kuba–Panholzer (2010) treat block-size counts and specified-label distances; Haglund–Visontai (2012) give label-indexed multivariate plateau generating polynomials. A closer publicly available age-resolved covariance-kernel result states the **same exact joint inclusion formula** used here and a macroscopic covariance limit. Neither that finite formula nor its covariance consequences are claimed as new. The distinct final result is a full rare-birth-label point-process limit on the smaller \(n^{(r-1)/(2r-1)}\) scale, with an explicit intensity, a sharp Weibull minimum, and all fixed ordered-label laws. A previous oldest-leaf Poisson theorem treats a different recursive attachment process with a square-root scale. Exact finite encodings can in principle be asymptotically analyzed, so an unnoticed equivalent earlier extreme-value statement remains a historical-priority risk. No assertion of independently certified priority is made.

## Value — PASS

The first intact birth label is a natural statistic of generalized Stirling permutations, not a freely chosen exceptional parameter. It reveals where intactness first survives despite continual random insertions and gives a phase of the label-resolved plateau process that global plateau counts and bulk covariance limits do not describe. The result is universal in the fixed integer multiplicity, has a nontrivial multiplicity-dependent exponent, and determines an entire ordered family of extremes through a Poisson point process. Its finite exact inclusion law provides a reproducible bridge from the classical insertion model to the new limit.
