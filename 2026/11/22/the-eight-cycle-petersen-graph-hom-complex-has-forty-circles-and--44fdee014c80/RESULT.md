# The eight-cycle Petersen graph-Hom complex has forty circles and 240 rigid maps

## Finding
Let \(P=\operatorname{KG}(5,2)\) be the Petersen graph, and let \(C_8\) be the simple eight-cycle. The ordinary polyhedral graph-homomorphism complex has the complete integral homotopy classification
\[
\operatorname{Hom}(C_8,P)\simeq |P\times K_2|\;\sqcup\;\coprod_{j=1}^{40}S^1\;\sqcup\;\coprod_{j=1}^{240}\{\mathrm{pt}\}.
\]
Here \(P\times K_2\) is the connected bipartite (Kronecker) double cover of \(P\), a graph with twenty vertices, thirty edges, and first Betti number eleven. Consequently there are exactly 281 connected components and
\[
H_0(\operatorname{Hom}(C_8,P);\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{281},\qquad H_1\cong\mathbb Z^{51},\qquad H_q=0\quad(q\ge2).
\]
The four-cycle and six-cycle comparison cases have one connected component with respectively 150 and 870 graph-homomorphism vertices, with the six-cycle also having 120 isolated components; hence no circle component occurs at a smaller simple even cycle length. The 240 isolated components are precisely the directed, rooted parametrizations of the fifteen simple eight-cycles of \(P\). Each of the forty circle components has precisely 48 graph homomorphisms as vertices, 72 edges, and 24 triangular two-cells, with no higher cells. The triangle boundaries in each such component are pairwise edge-disjoint, so each circle component simplicially collapses (as a triangle complex) to a connected graph with one cycle.

## Assumptions and scope
The Petersen graph has vertices the ten two-element subsets of \(\{0,1,2,3,4\}\), with two vertices adjacent if and only if they are disjoint. A graph homomorphism \(f:C_8\to P\) is an eight-tuple \((f_0,\ldots,f_7)\) with \(f_i\sim f_{i+1}\) for every index modulo eight. A polyhedral cell of \(\operatorname{Hom}(C_8,P)\) is a tuple \((S_0,\ldots,S_7)\) of nonempty subsets of Petersen vertices such that every pair in \(S_i\times S_{i+1}\) is adjacent, for each index modulo eight. This cell is a product \(\prod_{i=0}^{7}\Delta^{|S_i|-1}\), of dimension \(\sum_i(|S_i|-1)\). This is the ordinary graph-Hom construction, not a directed Hom complex and not a clique complex.

## Proof
1. **Exhaustive finite graph-Hom census.** The standalone `verify.py` generates all closed eight-step Petersen walks by extending adjacent vertices and retaining only closing edges, obtaining exactly 7,590 distinct graph homomorphisms. Two such vertices are adjacent in the one-skeleton precisely when they differ in one coordinate \(i\) and the new color is in \(N(f_{i-1})\cap N(f_{i+1})\). Exhaustive traversal gives one component with 5,430 vertices, forty components with 48 vertices each, and 240 isolated vertices. The stated counts exhaust \(5,430+40\cdot48+240=7,590\).
2. **All polyhedral cells, not merely the one-skeleton.** For each graph-homomorphism vertex \(f\), every possible local cell-set \(S_i\) containing \(f_i\) lies in \(N(f_{i-1})\cap N(f_{i+1})\). The verifier enumerates every nonempty such subset and retains a product cell only if every adjacent pair of sets is completely joined in \(P\). Every Hom cell contains at least one vertex \(f\), so this procedure is exhaustive. Cells are canonicalized by their tuple of vertex sets, eliminating multiplicities. Each cell belongs to the connected component containing any of its vertices. The forty 48-vertex components all have full dimension profiles \((48,72,24)\); the isolated components have profile \((1)\); the large component has profile \((5430,20880,33960,30960,17580,6480,1560,240,20)\), in dimensions zero through eight. Hence the Euler characteristics are respectively \(0\), \(1\), and \(-10\).
3. **Circle and point components.** In every 48-vertex component the 24 triangular faces have 72 distinct boundary edges (checked by explicit edge-key construction). Since no cells of dimension above two occur, every triangle has a free edge; removing a free edge together with its triangle, successively, collapses the complex onto a connected graph with 48 vertices and 48 edges, necessarily homotopy equivalent to \(S^1\). An isolated homomorphism yields a point component. Since \(P\) has girth five, any cyclically nonbacktracking closed eight-walk is a simple eight-cycle; the verifier directly checks that all 240 isolated vertices are such simple cycles, and there are fifteen unoriented eight-cycles, each with sixteen cyclic parametrizations.
4. **Remaining component.** The Petersen graph is connected, nonbipartite and square-free: each pair of distinct vertices has at most one common neighbor (verified directly). The published square-free-codomain classification of Fujii–Kimura–Nozaki, Theorem 1.2, says that when the source is connected bipartite and target is connected nonbipartite square-free, each component is homotopy equivalent to a point, a circle, or the connected double cover \(|P\times K_2|\), and exactly one such double-cover summand occurs in the overall Hom complex. The component with Euler characteristic \(-10\) is neither a point nor a circle; its type must therefore be the double cover. For \(P\), the double cover has 20 vertices and 30 edges, Euler characteristic \(-10\) and first Betti rank \(30-20+1=11\). This proves the stated homotopy and integral homology claims.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py` with Python 3.10+ and compare its output with `expected.txt`. Both files are included. No third-party package is required. The verifier checks square-freeness, component profiles for the four- and six-cycle sources, all 7,590 eight-cycle Hom vertices, all one-skeleton components, every cell through dimension eight, cell-by-component profiles, disjoint triangle boundaries, and counts of simple Petersen six- and eight-cycles. The exhaustive proof relies on explicit finite loops in the included source; a previously recorded log or sampling would not suffice. Homotopy of the large component uses the published theorem, rather than an unsupported inference from an Euler characteristic alone.

## Relationship to prior work
The square-free-target general theorem of Fujii, Kimura and Nozaki (first public December 26, 2024) supplies the restricted list of possible component homotopy types and the existence of one double-cover component for the even source, but it does not tabulate the forty 48-vertex circles or the 240 isolated maps for \(C_8\) into the Petersen graph. Matsushita's independently proved theorem and Proposition 5.10 give the same general structural coverage. The specific component counts and the explicit two-dimensional triangular-circle subcomplexes are the classification here. The Petersen graph's fifteen octagons were already known; their count alone does not determine the forty circle components or the topology of the remaining complex.

## Limitations
This is a complete finite classification for the eight-cycle source and Petersen target, not a formula for all even lengths, arbitrary square-free targets, or an equivariant classification. The large component is identified up to homotopy by an established theorem; an explicit collapse of the large polyhedral component to a double-cover graph is not constructed here. The numerical classification is machine-assisted but exhaustive and independently reproducible from the included finite definitions. An independent mathematical audit has not been performed, and the available literature search cannot establish absolute publication priority.
