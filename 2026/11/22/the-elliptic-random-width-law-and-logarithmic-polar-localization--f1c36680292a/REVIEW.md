## Correctness — PASS
For the square slice at fixed second coordinate, the support optimization is exactly the Euclidean norm in the two effective variables, yielding the claimed caliper width without regularity assumptions. The independent spherical-coordinate factorization, product-density convolution and elliptic substitution are explicit. Endpoint coefficients follow from the classical complete elliptic integral expansion with the parameter/modulus distinction respected. For the conditional limit, the joint spherical-coordinate Jacobian is integrated over all four sign sectors, and normalization by the logarithmic lower tail proves uniform convergence on interior intervals. The companion checker separately compares quartic and elliptic densities, normalization, endpoint limits, and direct support optimization.

## Originality — PASS, provisional
The known classical formulae concern Steinmetz volume and surface area, not a directional width law. Finch's fully inspected width paper concerns the convex hull of perpendicular disks, not the bicylinder intersection, and computes a mean rather than an entire distribution. Two closest published records concern bicylinder section areas and John–Löwner ellipsoids; neither abstract claims the present random-width law. A full-text comparison with those records was unavailable, so they are recorded as residual priority risks rather than called definitively absent. The exact mathematical shape of the claim was also checked against published record searches for products of coordinates of spherical directions and elliptic-density aliases.

## Value — PASS
The bicylinder is a canonical nonpolyhedral convex solid, and its complete caliper-width law is a natural integral-geometric invariant distinct from familiar volume and area formulae. A logarithmic density singularity creates a new conditional geometry: near-minimizing normals are not uniformly spread over the two circles of minimizing directions but concentrate on their crossing poles, with a scale-free uniform log-splitting limit. The explicit moment and two different endpoint laws are internally motivated consequences of this complete characterization.

## Uncertainty and limitations
Historical priority for general laws of products of spherical coordinates and older stereological identities remains provisional. The theorem is exact only for the equal-radius orthogonal bicylinder; numerical tests do not replace proof. The primary argument supplies both infinite-law and conditional-asymptotic statements.
