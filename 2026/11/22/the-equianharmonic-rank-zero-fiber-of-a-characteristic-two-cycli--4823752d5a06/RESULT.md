# The equianharmonic rank-zero fiber of a characteristic-two cyclic-septic genus-six family

## Finding

Let \(k\) be an algebraically closed field of characteristic \(2\), and let \(t\in k\setminus\{0,1\}\). Define \(C_t\) as the smooth projective curve
\[
y^7=x(x-1)(x-t)=x^3+(1+t)x^2+tx.
\]
It is a genus-six tame cyclic cover of \(\mathbf P^1\), branched at \(0,1,t,\infty\) with local Kummer exponents \((1,1,1,4)\). Its Cartier operator \(\mathcal C\) on regular differentials has the following **complete power-rank classification**:
\[
(\operatorname{rank}\mathcal C^m)_{m\ge0}
=\begin{cases}
(6,4,3,3,3,\ldots),&t^2+t+1\ne0,\\
(6,4,3,2,1,0,0,\ldots),&t^2+t+1=0.
\end{cases}
\]
Consequently the Jacobian has \(a\)-number \(2\) for all smooth parameters, and geometric \(2\)-rank \(3\) generically but \(0\) precisely at the two primitive cube roots of unity. These two marked exceptional parameters represent a **single geometric cyclic-cover isomorphism class** under permutation of the three equally ramified finite branch points. The jump occurs at the equianharmonic branch configuration, not at a singular fiber.

## Assumptions and scope

The field is algebraically closed and perfect of characteristic two. For \(t\notin\{0,1\}\) the cubic \(f=x(x-1)(x-t)\) is squarefree, so the degree-seven Kummer covering is tame and has four branch points of full ramification index seven. Riemann--Hurwitz gives \(2g-2=7(-2)+4(7-1)=10\), hence \(g=6\). The displayed coefficient condition uses the normal form with the unique exponent-four branch point at infinity. The geometric zero-rank condition and its sole exceptional cover class are intrinsic to this ramification-labeled family.

## Proof

**Regular differentials.** At each finite branch point, \(y\) is a uniformizer and \(\operatorname{ord}(dx)=6\). At infinity, \(\operatorname{ord}(x)=-7\), \(\operatorname{ord}(y)=-3\), and \(\operatorname{ord}(dx)=-8\). For \(x^i dx/y^j\) the valuations are \(6-j\) at a finite branch and \(3j-8-7i\) at infinity. Thus the following six differentials are everywhere regular and linearly independent (the distinct \(y\)-characters are independent):
\[
\begin{aligned}
e_0&=dx/y^3,&e_1&=dx/y^4,\\
e_2&=dx/y^5,&e_3&=x\,dx/y^5,\\
e_4&=dx/y^6,&e_5&=x\,dx/y^6.
\end{aligned}
\]
Their number equals the genus, so they form a basis.

**Cartier action.** In characteristic two, with \(x\) a separating variable, \(\mathcal C(h^2\eta)=h\mathcal C(\eta)\), \(\mathcal C(x^{2r}dx)=0\), and \(\mathcal C(x^{2r+1}dx)=x^r dx\). For odd \(j\), write \(y^{-j}=f(x)(y^{-(j+7)/2})^2\); for even \(j\), write \(y^{-j}=(y^{-j/2})^2\). Direct coefficient extraction gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal C(e_0)&=t^{1/2}e_2+e_3, &\mathcal C(e_1)&=0,\\
\mathcal C(e_2)&=t^{1/2}e_4+e_5, &\mathcal C(e_3)&=(1+t)^{1/2}e_5,\\
\mathcal C(e_4)&=0, &\mathcal C(e_5)&=e_0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Since \(t(1+t)\ne0\), the two images \(\mathcal C(e_2),\mathcal C(e_3)\) are independent in \(\langle e_4,e_5\rangle\), while \(\mathcal C(e_0)\) spans a line in \(\langle e_2,e_3\rangle\) and \(\mathcal C(e_5)=e_0\) spans a third character space. Hence \(\operatorname{rank}\mathcal C=4\). A direct composition, respecting inverse-Frobenius semilinearity, gives
\[
\mathcal C^2(e_0)=t^{3/4}e_4+\bigl(t^{1/4}+(1+t)^{1/2}\bigr)e_5,
\quad \mathcal C^2(e_2)=e_0,
\quad \mathcal C^2(e_5)=t^{1/2}e_2+e_3.
\]
The remaining nonzero second image is \(\mathcal C^2(e_3)=(1+t)^{1/4}e_0\); since \(t\ne0\), these images span three independent character lines and \(\operatorname{rank}\mathcal C^2=3\).

**Stable rank and exceptional nilpotence.** The \(y\)-character spaces of denominators \(y^3,y^5,y^6\) form a three-step cycle of dimensions \(1,2,2\), while \(e_1\) belongs to an acyclic character and is killed immediately. The first return map on the one-dimensional \(e_0\)-space is
\[
\mathcal C^3(e_0)=\eta(t)e_0,\qquad
\eta(t)=t^{1/8}+(1+t)^{1/4}.
\]
Because \(k\) is perfect, \(\eta(t)=0\) precisely when \(t=(1+t)^2\), equivalently \(t^2+t+1=0\). If \(\eta(t)\ne0\), the return map is invertible on this one-dimensional space and each leg of the cycle has a one-dimensional stable image. Thus \(\operatorname{rank}\mathcal C^m=3\) for every \(m\ge2\). If \(\eta(t)=0\), the cycle is nilpotent. The explicit matrix gives \(\mathcal C^3(e_2)=t^{1/2}e_2+e_3\), \(\mathcal C^3(e_3)=(1+t)^{1/8}(t^{1/2}e_2+e_3)\), and at the exceptional parameters \(\mathcal C^3(e_5)=t^{3/4}e_4\), while the other third images vanish. Thus \(\operatorname{im}\mathcal C^3\) is the direct sum of the lines generated by \(t^{1/2}e_2+e_3\) and \(e_4\), so has rank two. Its Cartier image is precisely the nonzero line \(k e_4\); a further Cartier application kills that line. Hence the successive exceptional ranks are \(4,3,2,1,0\). The stable Cartier rank equals the Jacobian \(2\)-rank, while \(a=g-\operatorname{rank}\mathcal C=2\).

**Geometric exceptional class.** The equation \(t^2+t+1=0\) has exactly two distinct roots in \(\mathbf F_4\). They are interchanged by \(t\mapsto1/t\), induced by an exchange of two of the three finite equally ramified branch points (and a rescaling of \(y\)). Thus these two coefficient-marked curves are geometrically isomorphic as cyclic covers, and there is one exceptional geometric class. No assertion of a complete Newton-polygon or automorphism-group classification is made.

## Relationship to prior work

Moonen's 2010 classification of cyclic covers of the projective line provides a broad modular framework for degree-seven, four-point covers; this theorem concerns a concrete rank stratification of one full one-dimensional ramification-labeled family. Bouw and Wewers (2012) study superelliptic covers and their arithmetic reduction, rather than this coefficientwise Cartier-iterate jump. General results on Newton polygons of cyclic covers, including Li--Mantovan--Pries--Tang (2018), concern broader stratification of special families; no explicit equal-inertia cyclic-septic characteristic-two equation \(t^2+t+1\) with the present full Cartier iterates was identified in the inspected sources. Published published-finding corpus records involving genus-six cyclic septic covers concern characteristic five and different inertia labels, and therefore do not imply the stated characteristic-two result. The familiar equianharmonic cross-ratio itself is classical: novelty claimed is only the resulting exact Jacobian Cartier-power and \(2\)-rank classification.

## Limitations

The theorem classifies Cartier powers and \(2\)-rank within the stated one-parameter family, not all order-seven cyclic covers, all genus-six Jacobians, or their entire Newton polygons and Ekedahl--Oort types. The broader family might have been treated under a different normal form in earlier research; historical priority is provisional. The verification programs examine finite fields, whereas the proof covers every algebraically closed characteristic-two field. There has been no independent mathematical audit.
