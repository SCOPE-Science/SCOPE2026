# Scientific assessment — four-dimensional centroid displacement

## Correctness — PASS
The proof uses the established variational characterization of the sharp four-dimensional centroid constant. Three polynomial moments, the rational objective and its exact derivative are reconstructed. The derivative's numerator has exactly one positive root by Descartes' rule and endpoint signs, with positive denominator, proving global optimality over the full compactified affine-parameter domain. Exact elimination and a finite-field irreducibility certificate give the degree-six minimal polynomial. The explicit thin-polytope construction realizes the sharp value as a limit; no finite sample is used to assert an infinite inequality.

The November 2026 work proves the boundary-centroid inequality using that same variational constant and lists dimension two, dimension three and the limiting value, not the explicit fourth-dimensional sextic or its unique affine optimizer. The new contribution is the exact degree-six algebraic determination and sharp unique profile, not the already-published general inequality or general sharpness construction. Searches covering equivalent centroid displacement, scalar variational profiles, algebraic constants, and exact four-dimensional claims did not disclose an equivalent prior result. This is not a proof of worldwide novelty: historical explicit computations in older articles or notes remain possible.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The coefficient \(D_4\) is a canonical dimension-specific optimal constant in two proven geometric inequalities; evaluating it exactly was not provided by the motivating results, which explain the algebraic obstacles to dimensional evaluation. The computation determines the uniquely optimal aspect-ratio parameter and demonstrates that the constant has algebraic degree six, so it cannot be represented by a lower-degree algebraic relation. Its concrete sharpness sequence makes the value independently usable in four-dimensional centroid comparisons. This natural exact invariant is not an arbitrary numerical slice, though it is a specialization rather than a new general theory.
