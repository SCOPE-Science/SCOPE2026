# The exact cokernel exponent of prime-power cyclic GCD matrices

## Finding
Let \(p\) be any prime, let \(k\ge1\), put \(N=p^k\), and form the integer circulant
\[
G_{p,k}=\bigl(\gcd(a-b,p^k)\bigr)_{0\le a,b<p^k}.
\]
Here \(\gcd(0,p^k)=p^k\). This matrix is nonsingular. Let \(E(p,k)\) be the exponent of the finite abelian group \(\mathbb Z^{p^k}/G_{p,k}\mathbb Z^{p^k}\), equivalently the largest positive integer Smith invariant factor of \(G_{p,k}\). Then
\[
\boxed{E(p,k)=\operatorname{lcm}\!\left(
 p^{k-1}(p-1),\;k p^{2k-2}(p-1)\bigl(p+k(p-1)\bigr),\;
 \left\{p^{k+m-1}(p-1)m(m+1):1\le m<k\right\}\right).}
\]
The set in braces is empty when \(k=1\). Moreover, the rank of \(G_{p,k}\) over \(\mathbb F_p\) equals \(p\) for all \(k\ge1\), so exactly \(p^k-p\) of its Smith factors are divisible by \(p\).

The exponent is not in general the least common multiple of the ordinary rational eigenvalues. For example, the orders \(p^k=4,8,9,16\) give exponents \(32,480,252,1536\), respectively. This distinguishes integral torsion from spectral information at the ramified prime.

## Assumptions and scope
All matrices have rows and columns indexed by the complete residue ring \(\mathbb Z/p^k\mathbb Z\). The group exponent is the least positive integer \(M\) such that \(M x=0\) in the cokernel for every class \(x\). Smith factors are positive and ordered by divisibility. The result covers every prime and every positive depth, including \(p=2\) and \(k=1\). It does not claim to determine all intermediate Smith factors.

## Proof
For \(0\le j\le k\), define \(H_j(a,b)={\bf1}_{a\equiv b\pmod{p^j}}\) (in particular, \(H_0\) is the all-ones matrix and \(H_k=I\)). The identity \(\sum_{d\mid m}\varphi(d)=m\) gives
\[
G_{p,k}=\sum_{j=0}^k \varphi(p^j)H_j.
\]
Set \(P_j=p^{-(k-j)}H_j\), and \(P_{-1}=0\). These are pairwise commuting orthogonal projections onto the subspaces of vectors constant on each residue class modulo \(p^j\). They satisfy \(P_iP_j=P_{\min(i,j)}\), and the differences \(P_j-P_{j-1}\) are mutually orthogonal projections summing to \(I\). On \(\operatorname{im}(P_j-P_{j-1})\), the matrix \(G_{p,k}\) acts by the positive scalar
\[
L_0=p^{k-1}\bigl(p+k(p-1)\bigr),\qquad
L_j=(k-j+1)p^{k-1}(p-1)\quad(1\le j\le k).
\]
Indeed, \(H_i(P_j-P_{j-1})=0\) for \(i<j\) and equals \(p^{k-i}(P_j-P_{j-1})\) for \(i\ge j\). In particular \(G_{p,k}\) is invertible over \(\mathbb Q\). Rearranging its spectral inverse gives the **exact rational matrix identity**
\[
G_{p,k}^{-1}=\frac{I}{L_k}+\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\left(\frac1{L_j}-\frac1{L_{j+1}}\right)\frac{H_j}{p^{k-j}}
=\sum_{j=0}^k b_j H_j,
\]
where \(H_k=I\) and
\[
\begin{aligned}
b_k&=\frac1{p^{k-1}(p-1)},\\
b_0&=-\frac1{k p^{2k-2}(p-1)(p+k(p-1))},\\
b_j&=-\frac1{p^{2k-j-1}(p-1)(k-j)(k-j+1)}\qquad(1\le j<k).
\end{aligned}
\]
For the first coefficient, use \(L_0-L_1=p^k\); the remaining coefficients follow by subtraction of adjacent reciprocals.

For each \(s\in\{0,\ldots,k-1\}\), there exist two distinct residues \(a,b\) with \(\nu_p(a-b)=s\). The corresponding inverse entry is exactly \(b_0+\cdots+b_s\). A diagonal inverse entry is \(b_0+\cdots+b_k\). Therefore the additive subgroup of \(\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z\) generated by all entries of the inverse equals that generated by \(b_0,\ldots,b_k\), by taking successive differences. A positive integer \(M\) annihilates the cokernel precisely when \(M G_{p,k}^{-1}\) is integral: this follows directly from \(M\mathbb Z^{p^k}\subseteq G_{p,k}\mathbb Z^{p^k}\). Hence its least possible value is the least common multiple of the denominators of the \(b_j\). Every displayed \(b_j\) has numerator \(\pm1\) and the claimed least-common-multiple formula follows after the substitution \(m=k-j\).

Finally, reduce the totient decomposition modulo \(p\). For \(k\ge1\),
\[
G_{p,k}\equiv H_0-H_1\pmod p.
\]
The space of input vectors factors through their sums on the \(p\) residue classes modulo \(p\). On this \(p\)-dimensional quotient, the induced matrix is \(J_p-I_p\), which is invertible over \(\mathbb F_p\), since \((J_p-I_p)^{-1}=-I_p-J_p\) in characteristic \(p\) (because \(J_p^2=pJ_p=0\)). The block-sum map has rank \(p\), so the original matrix has rank \(p\). For \(k=1\), this is the whole matrix. The Smith rank assertion follows.

## Verification
The accompanying `checker.py` constructs the matrices directly, constructs the rational inverse independently from the coefficient formula, verifies the exact matrix product, computes independent integer Smith forms with SymPy, and checks ranks over finite fields. It passed 1,259 exact assertions for \((p,k)=(2,1),(2,2),(2,3),(2,4),(3,1),(3,2),(5,1),(7,1),(3,3)\). At size \(27\), the checker verifies the inverse and modular rank, but does not attempt an expensive full Smith computation. Example largest factors from direct full Smith calculations are \(E(2,2)=32\), \(E(2,3)=480\), \(E(2,4)=1536\) and \(E(3,2)=252\).

Run `python checker.py` with Python 3 and SymPy installed. The finite checks corroborate algebraic identities; the preceding proof, not finite enumeration, establishes the universal claim.

## Relationship to prior work
Classical arithmetic GCD matrices generally have entries \(\gcd(i,j)\), rather than a cyclic difference kernel. Bege's 2010 study treats such index-GCD generalizations. Telloni and Williams (2014) study Smith forms over polynomial rings of special circulant \(\lambda\)-matrices; their results do not directly state this numerical exponent. Noferini and Williams (2025) provide more general structural methods for companion-ring Smith forms, but the material inspected does not supply the explicit prime-power cyclic GCD inverse and least denominator. These broader techniques could in principle provide alternative derivations; independent historical-priority verification remains desirable.

## Limitations
The theorem gives the **largest** invariant factor and the exact characteristic-prime corank, not every intermediate invariant factor, nor a closed formula for arbitrary composite moduli. Its mathematical originality is assessed against the inspected literature and accessible indexed records, not certified by an exhaustive search of all historical results. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
