# Mathematical and computational verification

The result is established by the exact integer Möbius factorization, the strict increase of a positive-coefficient polynomial on positive arguments, the equality case of the complex triangle inequality, and a quantified logarithmic asymptotic; it does not rely on finite sample coverage. All nonzero roots on the predicted smallest circle are simple by a strictly positive derivative after phase alignment. The zero at the origin has multiplicity \(p^{a-1}q^{b-1}\).

To reproduce auxiliary checks, run `python3 verify.py` using Python 3 and only its standard library. It tests 36 sparse integer polynomial identities for selected prime powers, the angular congruences, the positive radius equation and derivative, and nine large-prime comparisons against the Lambert-\(W\) scale. All tests passed for the included cases. The numerical bisection does not independently certify the theorem for all primes, and floating-point error at extreme parameters is outside the checker scope.

Proof limits: exactly two distinct primes; the asymptotic fixes the smaller prime; all non-innermost roots are unclassified. Publication-priority screening was performed against identified full texts and related records but is not an independent historical certificate.
