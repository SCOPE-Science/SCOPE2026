# Verification of the quantitative index obstruction

The exact spectral step uses \(\pi(S)^*\pi(S)=\pi(S)\pi(S)^*=1\) and \(\sigma(\pi(S))=\{z:|z|=1\}\). For \(a>b>0\), the two spectral circles are separated by the mid-radius \(r=(a+b)/2\). Each mid-circle inverse in the Calkin algebra has norm exactly \(2/(a-b)\). The Fredholm index on that circle is \(-p\), computed with \(\operatorname{ind}(S-z)=-1\) for \(|z|<1\) and \(0\) for \(|z|>1\).

The index-divisibility lemma was checked by tracking all roots \(w\) of points on the mid-circle. Fredholmness of \(X^k-w^k\) implies Fredholmness of \(X-w\) via Calkin spectral mapping, and its index is constant on the connected root circle. Factorization then gives divisibility by \(k\). The Neumann-series homotopy is valid under the strict essential-norm bound \((a-b)/2\). At equality the explicit merged-scale operator supplies attainment, but the argument does not claim uniqueness.

The separate cases \(k\mid p\), \(k\nmid p\), \(a=b\), and interchange of unequal scales were each checked in the proof. The main statement is analytic and requires no computational certificate or finite enumeration.
