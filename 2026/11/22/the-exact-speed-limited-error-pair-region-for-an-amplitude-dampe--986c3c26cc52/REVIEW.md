# Self-review

**Correctness — PASS.** For a fixed physical amplitude-damping channel, the exact first-order survival-fidelity slopes are the time integrals of \((1\mp r_z)^2/4\). The drive-speed cap forces every successful north-to-south inversion to spend at least \(d\theta/\Omega\) crossing each latitude. Subtracting that mandatory residence produces a positive measure of total mass \(\Delta\). A bounded scalar moment problem gives the exact inequalities, and a two-atom representation is physically implemented by meridian movement plus two dwells. The endpoint \(T=\pi/\Omega\), nonmonotone paths and all boundary moment cases are included. The packaged numerical verifier passed 4,445 checks, but finite checks alone are not a proof.

**Originality — PASS, with publication-priority qualification.** Patra's arXiv:2611.18755v1 derives the general first-order moment relation and analyzes particular pole wait–flip–wait controls, including one-sided dwelling. Those known ingredients are explicitly credited. The present claim is different: a necessary-and-sufficient all-control region of two error coefficients for a fixed amplitude-damping bath under a Hamiltonian norm and duration cap, together with a constructive converse for every region point. Lin, Sels and Wang's 2020 full text addresses time-optimal transfer, not this fixed-duration joint-error feasibility set. An implicit statement in wider geometric-control literature cannot be ruled out by a finite literature search.

**Value — PASS.** This is a complete, physically interpretable reachable-set classification for the two fidelity costs, not a restatement of the first-order fidelity formula or a single special case. It yields explicit implementable pulse witnesses and a sharp global ratio benchmark, with no assumed restrictions on the number of control switches. The value is mathematical design/diagnostic value rather than a claim of experimentally validated performance.

**Closest literature and limits.** Initiating source: A. Patra, arXiv:2611.18755v1, Sections 2–3, 7.2 and Appendix F. Related control source: C. Lin, D. Sels, Y. Wang, Phys. Rev. A 101, 022320 (2020). Scope is leading weak-damping error for one fixed zero-temperature jump. Additional noise channels, finite damping, bandwidth and slew limits, and robustness to calibration errors lie outside the proved claim.
