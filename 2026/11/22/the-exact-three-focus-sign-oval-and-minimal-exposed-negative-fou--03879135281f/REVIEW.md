# Scientific review: four-site microscopic-weight sign oval

## Correctness — PASS
The mass-one three-site gauging has explicitly positive coefficients obtained from the three strict triangle-inequality limbs. Euclidean distance has strict negative type on every finite set of distinct points, as follows directly from the Fourier representation of \(\|z\|\). The four-site energy, maximized first at fixed new mass, is a strictly concave quadratic \(\lambda+2(\Phi-\lambda)t-Q t^2\) with \(Q>0\). Its exact optimizer establishes the sign equivalence without approximation. Positivity of the three focus masses makes the distance-sum potential coercive and strictly convex; all original vertices lie on its level \(\lambda\), and the open edges lie strictly below it. The explicit outward site makes a convex quadrilateral and has strictly negative mass. One, two, and three sites cannot have negative mass by direct formula and the collinear endpoint calculation.

The executable check independently solves the full four-site gauging system across 381 seeded nondegenerate cases, agrees with the derived fourth-mass formula and tests the exposed-vertex example. This is supporting evidence, not a substitute for the infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS (provisional historical priority)
Roff and Willerton's public paper [1], examined in full text, proves the existence and the energy interpretation of microscopic weighting for finite Euclidean spaces, and describes general Schoenberg-simplex barycentric coordinates. Those facts do not explicitly give an exact geometric sign locus for adding a point to every Euclidean triangle, or the minimal number of exposed vertices needed for a negative microscopic weight. Devriendt's magnitude and similarity-embedding paper [3], examined at its introduction and subspace matrix theorem, concerns positive-scale similarity matrix weightings and does not provide this planar sign-oval threshold.

The closest specifically exposed-negative published result [2] uses two staggered regular polygons, at least eight exposed vertices. Its recorded theorem does not include four sites or a universal varying-fourth-site classification. A related geometric construction for a negative *interior* center attached to a spherical finite rim has a distinct center-in-hull hypothesis; it cannot imply negative mass at an exposed vertex. A direct historical search found no stated quadrilateral equivalence, but incomplete historical indexing and the uninspected full text of [2] remain residual risks.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The small-scale weighting is often understood as emphasizing boundary points; the smallest *exposed* negative-mass configuration tests this geometric intuition at its first possible size. The result answers that natural minimum-cardinality question sharply and classifies every placement of a fourth point relative to every fixed nondegenerate triangle by a canonical convex oval, including strict sign transitions and exterior points. The oval itself is a standard weighted distance-sum sublevel: what matters is its exact equivalence to a signed distance-energy optimizer and the sharp minimal exposed-negative witness, not a purported invention of weighted ellipses. No general theory of arbitrary finite sets is claimed.

## Limitations and closest literature
The result classifies one designated site when the other three form a triangle. It does not enumerate all four simultaneous signs, non-Euclidean sites, degenerate repeated points, or arbitrary cardinality. Historical priority remains subject to an independent literature audit, which has not been performed.
