# Mathematical review

**Correctness — PASS.** Distinct near-perfect matchings on a tree have a single even alternating path as their symmetric difference. Flipping successively along it both attains and lower-bounds the half-distance formula. An exposed-neighbour argument, a tree edge count and matching saturation force the unique core to have subdivision-degree two. Every remaining component has a unique perfect matching and one attachment at a subdivision vertex. The converse construction yields all configurations and realizes each finite tree. The standalone checker exhaustively tests labelled trees through seven vertices and separately tests all six-vertex realization bases. There is no extrapolation from finite tests to the general theorem.

**Originality — PASS, qualified.** General one-hole matching flips, alternating-path transport and a linear diameter bound appear in Aichholzer et al. (arXiv:2508.18457), and broad complexity results in Dorfer (arXiv:2602.09573). The maximal number of near-perfect matchings and equality for once-subdivided trees are established by Cambie et al. (2025), and are not claimed here. Bousquet et al. (2018) imply the exact metric via their no-detour result for even-hole-free line graphs (all forest line graphs are chordal); the metric component is credited as prior-implied. The searched primary results do not state the unique exposed core, forced perfect-matching fringe and exact flip-tree classification for *all* tree hosts. The strongest risk is an unindexed specialization of Gallai–Edmonds decomposition or an older tree matching-exchange result. The 2018 primary PDF was inspected at its relevant sections but not cover to cover.

**Value — PASS.** The result classifies every connected nonempty one-hole matching-flip graph arising from a tree and its full metric, reveals a unique forced-fringe/skeleton decomposition, and proves a converse realization theorem. It replaces general linear diameter bounds with a canonical graph isomorphism and exact all-pairs distances for a mathematically natural host class. These are structural facts beyond a small example or restatement of the known extremal number of matchings.

**Closest prior work and limits.** Aichholzer et al. (2025), Dorfer (2026), Cambie et al. (2025), Bousquet et al. (2018) and Brenner et al. (2026). Inputs without near-perfect matchings are outside scope; cyclic hosts and labelled/weighted flips are not covered; historical priority remains provisional.
