# Scientific review: Fano-incidence NOR attraction census

**Correctness — PASS.** The update uses precisely the three open incidence neighbors of each point or line. The Galois identities give \(F^3=F\), the Fano axiom that two points determine one line yields exactly 37 closed sets, and the five fiber pairs determine the complete basin multiplicity table. The basin-weighted total is \(16,384\), and two separately written exact enumerators agree on the orbit counts, basin sizes, and recurrent-entry layers. There is no extrapolation from a finite sample to a larger graph family.

**Originality — PASS, with residual priority risk.** The known negative-conjunctive network/maximal-independent-set correspondence covers the existence and fixed-point interpretation, but it neither tabulates Heawood NOR two-cycles nor provides their basin sizes. Published work on Heawood majority uses a different rule, while published work on NOR leaf coronas treats a family with leaves, hence not the cubic Heawood graph. General max/minterm studies do not supply the Fano-specific 11-row basin spectrum. Searches of accessible records and external literature found no directly covering claim. Primary full text was checked for the most plausible fixed-point and periodic-point results, while two indexed related case-study full texts were inaccessible; historical priority is therefore not independently established.

**Value — PASS.** The Heawood graph is the unique minimal cubic girth-six cage and the smallest nontrivial finite-projective-plane incidence graph, rather than an ad hoc parameter slice. The theorem solves its complete recurrent graph including *individual attraction basins*, not just orbit numbers. The striking structural split—21 completely isolated equilibria versus a unique oscillation attracting 4,097 states—and the exact closed-fiber explanation make the finite invariant useful as a reproducible benchmark for inhibitory Boolean dynamics and for comparisons to other graph-update rules. The 37-symbol classification is reusable within the analysis, not a mere rephrasing of the known fixed-point bound.

**Closest literature and limits.** Aracena–Richard–Salinas, arXiv:1507.06141, establishes the fixed point/MIS correspondence; Aledo et al., doi:10.1155/2020/9708347, treats broader Boolean max/minterm periodic-point problems but not these basin fibers; indexed results on NOR leaf coronas and Heawood majority concern distinct hypotheses. A general Galois-closure reduction is elementary; only the exact Fano geometry/basin enumeration is presented as this finding. Equivalent undocumented computations cannot be ruled out.
