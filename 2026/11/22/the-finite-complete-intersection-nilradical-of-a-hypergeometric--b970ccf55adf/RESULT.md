# The finite complete-intersection nilradical of a hypergeometric Kleinian critical scheme

## Finding

For every integer \(n\ge 4\), let \(P_n\) be the hypergeometric Kleinian potential of Kang [1] and let \(A_n=\mathbb C[x,y,z,w]/(\partial P_n)\) be its Jacobian ring. Write \(N_n=\sqrt{0}\subset A_n\). With \(B=\mathbb C[z,w]\), weights \(\deg z=1\), \(\deg w=2\), and \(G_n\) as defined below, there is an **explicit graded \(A_n\)-module isomorphism**

\[
N_n\cong (B/(G_n,\partial_zG_n))(-2),\qquad xN_n=yN_n=0.
\]

Here \((-2)\) shifts generator degree to \(2\), with \(z,w\) acting by ordinary multiplication. The two equations form a zero-dimensional weighted homogeneous regular sequence of degrees \(n\) and \(n-1\). Thus the *entire* intrinsic-graded nilradical Hilbert series, nilpotent colength and minimal graded free resolution over \(B\) are

\[
H_{N_n}(t)=\frac{t^2(1-t^{n-1})(1-t^n)}{(1-t)(1-t^2)},\qquad
\dim_\mathbb C N_n=\binom n2,
\]

\[
0\to B(-2n-1)\to B(-n-2)\oplus B(-n-1)\to B(-2)\to N_n\to0.
\]

The **first nilpotent layer**, a module over the reduced Kleinian surface, is

\[
N_n/N_n^2\cong\bigl(\mathbb C[z]/(z^{n-1})\bigr)(-2),
\qquad H_{N_n/N_n^2}(t)=t^2(1+t+\cdots+t^{n-2}),
\]

where \(x,y,w\) act by zero. The nilradical is supported only at the Kleinian vertex and satisfies \(N_n^n=0\). The reduced quotient is \(A_n/N_n\cong\mathbb C[x,y,z]/(xy+z^n)\). The full ring Hilbert series is the sum of the reduced contribution and \(H_{N_n}\):

\[
H_{A_n}(t)=\frac{1-t^n}{(1-t^{n/2})^2(1-t)}
+\frac{t^2(1-t^{n-1})(1-t^n)}{(1-t)(1-t^2)}.
\]

For odd \(n\), the grading on \(x,y\) uses half-integer weights; the nilradical grading remains integer-valued.

## Assumptions and scope

Work over \(\mathbb C\). Fix \(n\ge4\), weights \(\deg(x)=\deg(y)=n/2\), \(\deg(z)=1\), \(\deg(w)=2\), and put

\[
G_n(z,w)=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}
\frac{2^k n!}{(n-2k)!\,[n(n-1)]^k\,k!\,(k+1)!}\;z^{n-2k}w^k,\qquad
P_n=w(xy+G_n).
\]

By Kang's Theorem 3.1 and Proposition 3.2 [1], this is the normalized syzygy-selected potential and its Jacobian ring has nilradical \((w)\), with reduced locus \(w=xy+z^n=0\). These are *inputs*, not newly claimed results. The new claim concerns the precise graded nilpotent module and first successive quotient, not all higher power quotients.

## Proof

**1. Derivative presentation and the annihilator.** Set \(G=G_n\), \(G_z=\partial_zG\), and \(R=G+w\partial_wG\). Differentiating \(P_n\) gives

\[
A_n=\frac{B[x,y]}{(xw,yw,wG_z,xy+R)}.
\]

Weighted Euler homogeneity \(zG_z+2wG_w=nG\) gives

\[
R=\tfrac{n+2}{2}G-\tfrac z2G_z,
\quad\text{so}\quad (R,G_z)=(G,G_z)\ \text{in }B.
\]

The \(B\)-algebra \(D=B[x,y]/(xy+R)\) is free as a \(B\)-module with basis \(1,x,x^2,\ldots,y,y^2,\ldots\): eliminating the monomial \(xy\) preserves these and only these normal monomials. In \(D\), the ideal generated by \(wx,wy,wG_z\) meets the distinguished \(B\)-summand in precisely \((wR,wG_z)\): factors \(wx\) or \(wy\) reduce to a scalar term only after pairing with an opposite variable, yielding a factor \(-wR\), and any subsequent pairing multiplies by a further \(R\). Consequently, the multiplication map \(B\to(w)\subset A_n\), \(b\mapsto wb\), has kernel exactly \((R,G_z)=(G,G_z)\). Since \(xw=yw=0\), this proves the stated graded \(A_n\)-module isomorphism.

**2. Regular sequence for every integer parameter.** Let \(g_n(z)=G_n(z,1)\) and \(\alpha=8/[n(n-1)]\). The explicit coefficient recurrence

\[
\frac{c_{n,k+1}}{c_{n,k}}
=\frac{2(n-2k)(n-2k-1)}{n(n-1)(k+1)(k+2)}
\]

implies the differential equation

\[
(\alpha-z^2)g_n''+(2n+1)zg_n'-n(n+2)g_n=0.
\]

A common root of \(g_n\) and \(g_n'\) at a point other than \(\pm\sqrt\alpha\) would force the polynomial solution to vanish identically by uniqueness for a regular second-order ODE. At \(\pm\sqrt\alpha\), it cannot vanish: all coefficients \(c_{n,k}\) are positive, so \(g_n(\sqrt\alpha)>0\) and \(g_n(-\sqrt\alpha)=(-1)^n g_n(\sqrt\alpha)\ne0\). Therefore \(\gcd(g_n,g_n')=1\).

If \(G_n=G_{n,z}=0\) with \(w\ne0\), choose a square root \(s\) of \(w\) and write \(z=su\). Weighted homogeneity gives \(g_n(u)=g_n'(u)=0\), contradiction. At \(w=0\), the equations are \(z^n=nz^{n-1}=0\), so their only common affine zero is \((0,0)\). The two nonzero weighted homogeneous polynomials therefore form a regular sequence in the two-variable polynomial ring \(B\), with weights \(n\) and \(n-1\).

**3. Hilbert series, length, resolution and nilpotency.** The weighted complete-intersection Koszul resolution of \(B/(G,G_z)\), shifted by the generator degree \(2\), yields the displayed minimal resolution and Hilbert series. Taking the limit as \(t\to1\), its dimension is \(n(n-1)/2\). Its one-dimensional socle has graded degree \(2+[n+(n-1)]-(1+2)=2n-2\), the maximal degree of \(N_n\). Since every element of \(N_n\) has degree at least \(2\), each homogeneous product of \(n\) such elements has degree at least \(2n\); hence \(N_n^n=0\). The presentation from [1] gives the reduced quotient, and the exact sequence \(0\to N_n\to A_n\to A_n/N_n\to0\) gives the full ring series.

**4. First layer.** Since \(N_n=w\,B/(G,G_z)\), multiplication by \(w\) identifies

\[
N_n/N_n^2\cong (B/(G,G_z,w))(-2)
\cong(\mathbb C[z]/(z^{n-1}))(-2),
\]

using \(G_n(z,0)=z^n\) and \(G_{n,z}(z,0)=nz^{n-1}\). The \(x,y,w\) actions vanish in this quotient. This is an exact first-layer calculation for the entire family, not finite interpolation.

## Verification

The attached `verify.py` reproduces the coefficient identity, differential equation, root coprimality, zero-dimensionality, complete-intersection length, first-layer specialization, and nilpotence for \(2\le n\le10\), using exact rational arithmetic and Gröbner bases. These finite computations only check the proof: the ODE and weighted complete-intersection arguments establish the result for every \(n\ge4\). The ODE singular-endpoint check and the scalar-term kernel argument are nonstandard steps proved above rather than inferred from the script.

## Relationship to prior work

Kang [1], Theorem 3.1 and Remark 3.3, supply the entire potential, the reduced Kleinian locus, and the fact that the nilradical is generated by \(w\); none of those facts is claimed as new. Section 8.1 of [1] identifies successive nilradical modules as a further question. The new contribution gives an exact annihilator, a zero-dimensional complete-intersection realization, an all-parameter Hilbert series and minimal module resolution, and the first of those successive modules. The related work of Kang–Lawrie–Song [2] establishes a general nilradical-filtration program for other Argyres–Douglas critical rings; it does not give these specific closed graded-module invariants. The result is a partial, not complete, solution of the nilradical-power filtration question.

## Limitations

The theorem **does not compute** the modules \(N_n^k/N_n^{k+1}\) for all \(k\ge2\), their symmetry decompositions beyond trivial \(x,y\)-action, or the filtered arc-space index. The bound \(N_n^n=0\) is not asserted to be sharp. It also does not assert any independent validation or publication-priority certificate. An explicit presentation is known from [1], so the novelty lies in extracting and proving these uniform graded and local numerical invariants, not in constructing the ring itself.
