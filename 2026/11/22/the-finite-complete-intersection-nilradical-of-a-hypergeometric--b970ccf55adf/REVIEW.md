# Mathematical review

**Correctness — PASS (same-model).** The proof starts from the explicit derivative presentation, establishes the exact annihilator by the two-variable normal-form decomposition of the monic relation \(xy+R\), and derives the ODE for \(G_n(z,1)\) from the published coefficient recurrence. Positive coefficients eliminate singular-ODE endpoint roots, giving a genuine regular sequence for every \(n\ge4\). The Koszul resolution then proves the full series and length, and specialization modulo \(w\) gives the first layer. An attached exact-arithmetic checker covers small parameters; this is corroboration, not proof by extrapolation. Risk: the graded shift and intersection-of-ideals arguments are important algebraic steps; both are written explicitly in RESULT.md.

**Originality — PASS (qualified).** The closest source is Kang, arXiv:2611.14631v1, Theorem 3.1, Proposition 3.2, Remark 3.3 and the open module question in Section 8.1. It establishes the ring presentation and the nilradical generator \(w\) but does not state the nilradical's complete-intersection annihilator, graded module resolution, length or first power quotient. Kang–Lawrie–Song arXiv:2610.33151v1 describes the broader filtration construction for a different type of family, without the numerical formulas here. Mathematically related geometric critical schemes with nilpotents are distinct objects; indexed record search did not find an implication furnishing these formulas. Risk: an equivalent unpublished or unindexed computation may exist, and a complete ring presentation makes these results algorithmically recoverable. Search absence is not a novelty proof.

**Value — PASS (limited but substantive).** Kang explicitly motivates the modules of successive nilpotent powers as the scheme-theoretic input to a refined filtration. The theorem gives an exact all-parameter module realization, its first entire layer, its length growing quadratically with \(n\), its Gorenstein socle degree, and an elementary free resolution. This is more than a one-off numerical check: it isolates precisely the finite nonreduced defect at the Kleinian vertex and supplies a building block for higher layers. Risk: higher filtration layers and filtered arc-space series are not calculated, so the result is a partial structural answer.
