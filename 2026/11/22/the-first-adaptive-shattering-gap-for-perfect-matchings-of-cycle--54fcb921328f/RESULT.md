# The first adaptive shattering gap for perfect matchings of cycle prisms

## Finding
Let \(P_n=C_n\square K_2\) for \(n\ge3\). Regard every perfect matching of \(P_n\) as a Boolean hypothesis on its \(3n\) **edges**, with answer one when the queried edge is in the matching. For the first four cycle prisms, the exact static and adaptive dimensions are

| \(n\) | Perfect matchings | VC dimension | Littlestone dimension |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 3 | 4 | 2 | 2 |
| 4 | 9 | 2 | 2 |
| 5 | 11 | 3 | 3 |
| 6 | 20 | 3 | 4 |

In particular, the **hexagonal prism** \(P_6\), a three-vertex-connected, cubic, planar, bipartite graph on twelve vertices, has \(\operatorname{VCdim}(\mathcal P(P_6))=3<4=\operatorname{Ldim}(\mathcal P(P_6))\). This is the first strict adaptive advantage *within the cycle-prism family*, not a claim of global minimality among all cubic planar graphs. Every queried edge is contained in a perfect matching.

The complete shattered-support face counts through degree four for \(P_6\) are \((1,18,117,138,0)\); all higher counts vanish. A fully specified depth-four query tree with sixteen distinct matching witnesses is included.

## Assumptions and scope
The vertex set is \(\{u_i,v_i:i\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\}\); edges are rungs \(r_i=u_iv_i\), top rail edges \(a_i=u_iu_{i+1}\), and bottom rail edges \(b_i=v_iv_{i+1}\). All indices are cyclic. A shattered fixed support of size \(k\) must realize every one of the \(2^k\) Boolean labels on those edges; queries are distinct. Littlestone dimension is the largest depth of a fully binary adaptive query tree whose root-to-leaf answer words have perfect-matching witnesses. The graph has a geometric planar embedding as two concentric cycles joined by rungs. Cubicity is immediate; deleting any two vertices leaves the graph connected, since two rail cycles and the remaining rungs provide a surviving connection, establishing three-vertex-connectivity. For \(n=3\) the rails are triangles but the graph is simple.

## Proof
**1. Exact finite hypothesis lists.** The attached `verify_hexagonal_prism.py` enumerates every size-\(n\) subset of the \(3n\) edges for \(n=3,4,5,6\), and retains one exactly when each of the \(2n\) vertices has degree one. This exhaustively (not statistically) produces respectively \(4,9,11,20\) perfect matchings. The same checker exhaustively computes trace sets on every support of sizes zero through four. Its face-count vectors are \((1,9,3,0,0)\), \((1,12,42,0,0)\), \((1,15,60,5,0)\), and \((1,18,117,138,0)\), respectively. In particular \(P_6\) shatters 138 triples, no quadruple, and hence no larger support. The certificate lists all twenty actual six-edge matchings by bit masks.

**2. Structural obstruction for quadruples.** There is also a short combinatorial explanation specific to \(P_6\). Of its twenty perfect matchings, eighteen correspond to independent subsets of \(C_6\): a selected cut uses both rail edges between adjacent columns, and every otherwise unused column uses its rung. Two further perfect matchings have staggered top and bottom rail phases and no rungs. Each rung occurs in exactly eight perfect matchings: excluding both adjacent selected cuts leaves an independent set of the four-vertex path, with eight possibilities. Each individual rail edge occurs in exactly six: five synchronized perfect matchings (independent subsets of the remaining three-vertex path) plus one staggered matching. If four edge queries were shattered, each query would be positive in at least eight different matching concepts (one for each labeling of the other three queries). Consequently all four must be rungs. If four chosen rungs include three consecutive columns, the labels \(1,0,1\) on those three rungs are impossible: the middle column cannot be matched to either occupied neighbor. Otherwise, up to cyclic rotation, their indices are \(\{0,1,3,4\}\). The assignment \(r_0=r_4=1\), \(r_1=r_3=0\) is impossible: since rung zero is occupied and rung one is absent, the two vertices of column one must be horizontally matched to column two; then column three has neither an available adjacent column two nor available adjacent column four (rung four is occupied). Thus no four-rung support is shattered. This reconstructs the all-order-of-labels obstruction independently of exhaustive four-support computation.

**3. Adaptive lower certificate.** The included `prism6_certificate.json` specifies the actual eighteen edges, twenty matchings, and a nested depth-four tree. An internal node names a queried edge index; its `absent` and `present` branches encode the respective outcomes. Each of its sixteen leaves specifies a matching index. The standalone verifier checks recursively that every witness in a descendant branch satisfies *all* accumulated edge labels and that the sixteen leaf indices are distinct; the tree has fifteen internal nodes. Hence the Littlestone dimension is at least four. Conversely every complete adaptive tree of depth five needs \(32\) different hypotheses, but \(P_6\) has only twenty perfect matchings; its Littlestone dimension is therefore exactly four.

**4. Smaller prisms and graph properties.** For the first three lengths the exhaustive face census gives static dimensions \(2,2,3\). A separately computed full-version-space minimax recurrence (or the exact binary search over subsets of their at-most-eleven concepts) gives adaptive dimensions \(2,2,3\). The attached depth verifier includes an exact recurrence check for these three cases. Every rung occurs in the all-rung perfect matching, and every rail edge occurs in the perfect matching consisting of the matching pair of top and bottom rail edges at that cut and rungs elsewhere. Since \(n=6\) is even, the cycle prism is bipartite. This finishes the precise first-in-family assertion.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify_hexagonal_prism.py` next to `prism6_certificate.json` with Python 3 and no external packages. The checker independently reconstructs the graph and its perfect matchings through an exhaustive size-\(n\)-edge combination census; it checks every support of size at most four for all four small prisms, all twenty certificate matching masks, and every branch and leaf of the explicit adaptive depth-four tree. It also checks the smaller adaptive depths by full version-space recursion. The output marker is `HEXAGONAL_PRISM_ADAPTIVE_GAP_PASS`. This finite enumeration is exhaustive for the four named graphs; no extrapolation to larger prisms is asserted.

## Relationship to prior work
Lekshmi and West (first publicly circulated March 21, 2020) determine the number of perfect matchings of the entire prism and Möbius families, but the cardinality does not fix a concept class's static or adaptive query dimension. Earlier analyses of matching edge queries on open ladders give an adaptive gap in a ten-vertex ladder with two-valent corners, and longer ladders with only two-vertex connectivity; their graph objects and computed capacities do not settle the three-connected cubic hexagonal prism. The block-bridge examples of adaptive gaps contain bridges that never belong to a perfect matching. Here the graph is a natural homogeneous, three-connected, matching-covered cubic planar graph. Previous studies of perfect-matching complexes on hexagonal prisms compute topology, a different invariant from shattering edge-label traces. The present result is limited to the sharp first-in-family finite boundary and not to an unknown all-length formula.

## Limitations
The exact dimension table is established only for \(3\le n\le6\); the data through other sizes are not part of the claim. There is no assertion that the hexagonal prism is the smallest three-connected planar cubic graph with such a gap among all cubic graphs, nor a claim that the adaptive gap is unbounded in cycle prisms. Priority beyond the inspected literature and accessible indexed records is not certified.
