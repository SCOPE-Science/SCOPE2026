# Review of the nine-fibre axial Bergman zero threshold

## Correctness — PASS
The cylinder fibre volume is \(\pi^m(1-|z|^4)^m\), and direct beta integration gives the exact monomial reciprocal \(A_m(n)/(\pi^{m+1}2^m m!)\). The reproducing-kernel series therefore reduces to a degree-\(m\) polynomial divided by \((1-s)^{m+2}\). Applying the Möbius disk-to-right-half-plane map and exact rational Routh arrays yields no disk zeros for \(1\le m\le8\), and two for \(m=9\). Exact gcds exclude boundary crossings; a real-axis positivity argument and the multiplicity count establish that both zeros are simple, nonreal and conjugate. The packaged standalone checker recomputes the coefficients, boundary exclusion and Routh signs. This is not a numerical extrapolation beyond \(m=9\).

## Originality — PASS, best-knowledge
Searches for the precise quartic central power, independent quadratic cylinder fibres, radial weight \((1-r^4)^m\), exact dimension thresholds, and Cayley/Routh zero counting did not identify the nine-fibre threshold. Perälä (2017) supplies the radial-zero motivation but not this family; Beberok (2016) treats a distinct exponent geometry. Park (2021) is closest and includes a general higher-dimensional cylindrical-intersection kernel formula that specializes to the present axial series. That general formula is acknowledged as covering the kernel derivation; the claimed new content is the sharp finite dimension threshold and complete equality-free zero census through it. The publisher's full text was not available, so it remains a meaningful possibility that a stronger statement or appendix covers the threshold; indexed sections examined did not. A four-fibre variable-exponent classification concerns a different parameter and does not imply the nine-fibre quartic threshold.

## Value — PASS
The number of equal quadratic cylinder fibres is the natural geometric dimension parameter, and the first failure of central-axis Lu Qi-Keng zero-freeness is a direct geometric question, not an arbitrary slice of polynomial coefficients. The exact cutoff at nine fibres and first nonreal conjugate pair give a dimension-level obstruction in a canonical intersection family and a reproducible analytic–algebraic method. Only axial zero-freeness is claimed for smaller dimensions; global Lu Qi-Keng implications are asserted only for the nine-fibre counterexample.

## Principal limitations and comparison risks
The result is scoped to \(1\le m\le9\), and it does not assert full-domain zero-freeness below the first axial transition. Park's general formula covers the objects and makes the initial series accessible, but the inspected indexed material does not report the zero threshold. Complete historical exclusion remains unverified because its full publisher PDF could not be accessed; independent mathematical audit remains outstanding.
