# The first excess locating-dominating sensor in length-two Kautz networks

## Finding

Let \(q\) be an integer with \(2\le q\le7\), and let \(U_q\) be the **simple undirected** graph with vertices \((a,b)\), where \(a,b\in\{0,\ldots,q-1\}\) and \(a\ne b\). Distinct vertices \((a,b)\) and \((c,d)\) are adjacent exactly when \(b=c\) or \(d=a\). Thus \(U_q\) is the orientation-forgetting version of the length-two \(q\)-letter Kautz shift graph, not the directed Kautz graph.

A locating-dominating set \(S\) is a vertex set for which each vertex outside \(S\) has a nonempty, unique open-neighborhood intersection with \(S\). Let \(\lambda_q\) be its minimum size, let \(F_q\) count labeled minimum sets, and let \(R_q\) count their orbits under simultaneous symbol relabeling and word reversal \((a,b)\mapsto(b,a)\). Then:

| \(q\) | \(\lvert V(U_q)\rvert\) | \(\lambda_q\) | \(F_q\) | \(R_q\) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | 2 | 1 | 2 | 1 |
| 3 | 6 | 3 | 20 | 3 |
| 4 | 12 | 4 | 168 | 7 |
| 5 | 20 | 5 | 84 | 2 |
| 6 | 30 | **7** | **11,040** | **16** |
| 7 | 42 | **8** | **13,860** | **5** |

In particular \(q=6\) is the first alphabet size \(q\ge3\) where \(q\) locating-dominating sensors do **not** suffice. The excess remains one at \(q=7\). The full natural-symmetry orbit-size multisets at \(q=6\) and \(q=7\) are, respectively,
\[
\{120^{[2]},360^{[4]},720^{[7]},1440^{[3]}\},\qquad
\{630^{[2]},2520,5040^{[2]}\},
\]
where \(s^{[r]}\) means \(r\) separate orbits of size \(s\), **not** an exponent.

## Assumptions and scope

The alphabet is labeled and finite; \(2\le q\le7\) is essential. Every ordered pair of unequal symbols is a vertex. Edges ignore the direction of the ordinary Kautz shift but do not add loops or identify reversed words. A sensor located at a vertex is distinguished by its presence in \(S\), so only unsensored vertices must have distinct nonempty signatures. This is **ordinary locating domination**, not all-vertex identifying codes, total locating domination, or directed identification. All counts are of vertex subsets, not ordered lists of sensors. The symmetry quotient includes only the specified group generated by the alphabet permutations and reversal; no statement about the full abstract graph automorphism group is needed.

## Proof

For a vertex \(v\), write \(N(v)\) for its open neighborhood and \(I_S(v)=N(v)\cap S\). Directly from the definition, \(S\) is locating-dominating if and only if the \(I_S(v)\), for \(v\notin S\), are all nonempty and pairwise different. This is the complete test used in `checker.cpp`.

**Monotonicity.** If \(S\) passes this test and \(S\subseteq T\), then \(T\) passes: every vertex outside \(T\) was outside \(S\), its old nonempty signature persists inside its new signature, and two different old signatures remain different on coordinates in \(S\). Hence to establish the minimum \(\lambda_q=k\), it suffices to exhibit one valid \(k\)-set and exclude every \((k-1)\)-set.

**Finite exhaustion and witnesses.** In `checker.cpp`, `Instance(q)` builds every vertex in lexicographic order and computes exactly the adjacency condition in the first paragraph. Its `qualifies` routine checks all outsiders and rejects empty or repeated signatures. `enumerate(g,k,...)` visits only bitmasks with exactly \(k\) selected vertices, in strictly increasing numerical order. The standard next-combination arithmetic increases the mask and retains the popcount; the checker verifies that the number visited equals the exact binomial coefficient \(\binom{q(q-1)}k\). Consequently every \(k\)-subset occurs exactly once and none can be omitted. For \(q=2,\ldots,7\), the respective counts of qualifying \((k-1)\)-sets are all zero, while the qualifying \(k\)-set counts are exactly the \(F_q\) in the table. This establishes both minima and complete labeled counts.

Explicit attaining sets (concatenated pairs denote vertices, not binary numerals) are:

| \(q\) | One minimum set \(S\) |
|---:|---|
| 2 | `01` |
| 3 | `01 02 10` |
| 4 | `01 03 10 12` |
| 5 | `02 03 14 20 30` |
| 6 | `02 03 04 15 20 30 40` |
| 7 | `04 05 12 13 21 31 40 50` |

The verifier reconstructs and checks each witness directly rather than inferring feasibility from the enumeration count.

**Natural-symmetry census.** Adjacent transpositions of alphabet labels generate all \(q!\) relabelings. Reversal is also an automorphism of \(U_q\), commutes with relabeling, and has order two. Starting from each unvisited minimum set, the checker closes it under these generators. The resulting orbits are disjoint and exhaust the enumerated minimum sets. The orbit-size multiplicities for \(q=2,\ldots,7\) are:

| \(q\) | Orbit size: multiplicity |
|---:|---|
| 2 | `2:1` |
| 3 | `2:1, 6:1, 12:1` |
| 4 | `6:2, 12:1, 24:2, 48:2` |
| 5 | `24:1, 60:1` |
| 6 | `120:2, 360:4, 720:7, 1440:3` |
| 7 | `630:2, 2520:1, 5040:2` |

Each orbit-size weighted sum equals \(F_q\); every listed orbit size divides \(2q!\), a separate consistency check.

## Verification

Compile with a standard C++17 compiler and run:

```sh
g++ -O3 -std=c++17 checker.cpp -o checker
./checker
python3 secondary_check.py
```

The first program enumerates all relevant subsets without heuristics, using only integers, and prints `VERIFY_OK` after checking the exact minima, counts, and orbit-size histograms for every \(2\le q\le7\). At \(q=7\) its exhaustive trials are precisely \(\binom{42}{7}=26{,}978{,}328\) and \(\binom{42}{8}=118{,}030{,}185\). The independent Python program constructs neighborhoods as sets of ordered pairs, enumerates the cases through \(q=6\), and checks the \(q=7\) witness, ending in `ALTERNATE_OK`. The independently implemented integer linear formulation with a coverage constraint for every vertex and a separation constraint for every unordered vertex pair also reproduced \(\lambda_6=7\) and \(\lambda_7=8\). That solver is a corroboration, not the exhaustive proof.

## Relationship to prior work

Kautz shift digraphs and their line-digraph construction are standard. Araki (2009) optimizes connected \(k\)-tuple twin domination in **directed** Kautz graphs: those sensor sets cover incoming and outgoing neighborhoods, but do not require pairwise-unique outsider signatures. Boutin, Goliber, and Pelto (arXiv:1412.5842, first posted 2014) study all-vertex identifying codes in **directed de Bruijn** graphs. Their direction, graph family and identification quantifiers differ from the present problem. Stronger all-vertex identifying-code results on length-two undirected Kautz graphs can furnish upper bounds on \(\lambda_q\), but neither such an upper bound nor an enumeration of the stricter codes establishes the lower bounds and exact weaker-code census here.

The classification was also compared against published network-code and locating-domination records, including directed ternary Kautz identification and locating-domination in other graph families. No inspected statement yields these exact ordinary locating-domination numbers and natural-symmetry counts. This is a bounded priority assessment, not a claim of exhaustive historical uniqueness.

## Limitations

All optimization and counting claims are finite and stop at \(q=7\); the data alone do not establish a formula or lower bound for arbitrary \(q\ge8\). A fast reproducible exhaustive computation is a proof only of the stated finite cases, not an asymptotic theorem. No physical device noise, multiple faults, or sensing beyond graph-distance one is modeled. The natural relabeling-and-reversal orbit partition is not claimed to be a quotient by the complete graph automorphism group. Historical coverage outside inspected sources remains possible. No independent mathematical audit has been completed.
