# The first lattice holes of ternary cyclic-substitution Parikh spectra

## Finding
Let \(\sigma(0)=001\), \(\sigma(1)=112\), \(\sigma(2)=220\), and let \(s=\lim_{j\to\infty}\sigma^j(0)\). Write \(\mathcal P_m\) for the Parikh vectors of its contiguous factors of length \(3^m\). For \(m=0,1,2,3\), respectively,
\[
(|\mathcal P_m|,\ |\operatorname{conv}(\mathcal P_m)\cap\mathbb Z^3|)
=(3,3),\ (10,10),\ (37,37),\ (115,136).
\]
Here the convex hull is in the fixed-sum plane \(x_0+x_1+x_2=3^m\). The first nonsaturated substitution-power Parikh spectrum occurs at rank three, with exactly 21 missing lattice vectors. They form seven cyclic-coordinate orbits generated by
\[
(3,13,11),\ (3,15,9),\ (4,15,8),\ (5,7,15),\ (5,10,12),\ (6,6,15),\ (6,10,11).
\]
Every listed orbit has size three. This is a finite, exact classification at the first nonsaturated substitution-power rank; it does not assert a general all-rank formula.

## Assumptions and scope
A factor is contiguous. Its Parikh vector is \((|u|_0,|u|_1,|u|_2)\). A factor-Parikh set is *lattice-saturated* when it contains every integer point of its convex hull in its fixed-sum affine plane. Only the four substitution-power lengths \(1,3,9,27\) are considered. All 21 holes are integer points in that plane; they are not necessarily missing factors at other lengths. Cyclic coordinate rotation means \((a,b,c)\mapsto(b,c,a)\). All enumerations use exact integers.

## Proof
**Complete finite factor reduction.** The fixed point contains all three letters. Each \(\sigma(a)\) contains both adjacent pairs \(aa\) and \(a(a+1)\), with letters computed modulo three. Since the pair \(aa\) occurs, its substitution contains \((a+1)a\) at the junction of \(\sigma(a)\sigma(a)\). These three types, as \(a\) ranges over three letters, give all nine ordered adjacent pairs, and every one occurs in \(s\).

For each nonnegative integer \(m\) put \(U_{m,a}=\sigma^m(a)\), all of length \(L=3^m\). Every contiguous length-\(L\) factor of \(s\) lies inside some pair \(U_{m,a}U_{m,b}\), where \(ab\) occurs in \(s\). Conversely, each of the nine pairs occurs as consecutive level-\(m\) blocks. Therefore the exact, exhaustive identity is
\[
\mathcal P_m=\bigcup_{a,b\in\{0,1,2\}}
\{\Psi((U_{m,a}U_{m,b})[t:t+L]):0\le t\le L\}.
\tag{1}
\]
This is an equality, rather than a numerical sampling assertion. It reduces all four assertions to finitely many explicit windows: at most \(9(L+1)\) per rank.

**Exact evaluation.** Starting with the three one-letter blocks, repeatedly apply \(a\mapsto aa(a+1)\) to produce the blocks at ranks zero through three. Evaluate (1) by taking the Parikh vector of each pair's first length-\(L\) window, then subtracting the outgoing symbol and adding the incoming symbol at every successive offset. This gives distinct vector totals \(3,10,37,115\).

For each rank, take the ordinary planar convex hull of pairs \((x_0,x_1)\) and inspect its lattice points, reconstructing \(x_2=L-x_0-x_1\). Its exact lattice-point counts are \(3,10,37,136\). To certify the last figure without floating-point geometry, the rank-three hull has counterclockwise vertices
\[
(2,12),\ (4,8),\ (10,2),\ (13,2),\ (15,4),\ (15,10),\ (12,13),\ (8,15),\ (2,15).
\]
The shoelace sum gives twice the area \(237\). The sum of lattice step counts (coordinate gcds) along the nine edges is \(33\). Pick's theorem therefore gives \((237+33)/2+1=136\) total lattice points, boundary included. The integer window enumeration produces exactly \(115\) different Parikh vectors. Direct subtraction yields the 21 holes, all and only the three cyclic-coordinate rotations of each of the seven triples in the Finding. This last equality is a finite set identity verified by exact integer comparisons in the attached program. For ranks zero, one and two, the computed hull lattice counts equal the exact factor-Parikh counts, so there are no holes at those three earlier substitution-power ranks. This completes the classification.

## Verification
The standalone `verify_parikh_hole.py` recomputes the substituted blocks and all nine ordered block-pair windows from exact integers; it builds each planar hull with an integer-orientation monotone-chain algorithm and uses integer point-in-polygon tests to identify holes. It independently scans a long directly substituted one-sided prefix for each rank and asserts equality with the nine-pair factor spectrum. It checks the four pairs of reported counts, the rank-three nine vertices and \(136\) lattice points, the seven precise rotation orbits, and the three maximal-zero compositions. Run `python3 verify_parikh_hole.py`; the expected output is supplied as `verification_output.txt`. These computations are an exhaustive finite proof of the four specified ranks because the preceding pair-block reduction covers **all** factors, not a sample. The certificate does not establish any all-rank or asymptotic claim.

## Relationship to prior work
Allouche, Rampersad, Rigo, Shallit and Stipulanti [1] investigate binary abelian complexity for a related cyclic substitution with three repeated letters, and their weighted-factor method determines support-function extrema. The present ternary substitution has two repeated letters and the invariant is instead the **entire integral Parikh image**, including lattice holes invisible to an extremal support function. The closely related published cyclic-morphism study [2] concerns binary projections and their nonregular abelian complexity for larger repetition counts; its convex support-function recurrence does not imply which interior lattice points are attained in this exact rank-three ternary spectrum. Previously published prefix-abelian complexity and general Parikh-image methods [3] provide context but were not found to enumerate this 21-hole set. The novelty comparison is evidence-based and cannot exclude inaccessible or unindexed equivalent computations.

## Limitations
Only the finite substitution-power ranks zero through three are classified. No statement is made that length 27 is the earliest *arbitrary* length with holes, or that holes persist at every higher rank. The classification does not by itself imply a new formula for full abelian complexity at arbitrary lengths. Independent mathematical and priority review have not been performed.
