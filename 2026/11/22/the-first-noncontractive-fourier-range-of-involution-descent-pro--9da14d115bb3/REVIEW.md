PASS. The definition uses every involution, with multiplicities, on the full Boolean descent-position group. Classical Robinson--Schensted tableau transposition gives exact complement symmetry and odd-weight cancellation. Finite all-case recursion exhausts every involution through order eight; the independent Young-tableau corner-growth histogram and direct character sums agree. A translation-averaging proof establishes that the endpoint projection constant is optimal even among arbitrary non-translation-invariant real and complex projections. The extracted verifier passes 2,429 exact assertions. No all-order spectral pattern is claimed.

## Originality

PASS under a bounded literature comparison. Chow (2008) already gives formulas for the complete prescribed descent-set counts, hence the input combinatorial distribution is **not new**. Barnabei--Bonetti--Silimbani (2007) and Chow also supply the RSK/tableau complementary symmetry, so the odd Walsh zeros are **not new**. Barnabei--Bonetti--Elizalde--Silimbani (2014) focus on 321-avoiding involutions rather than the unrestricted class. The inspected primary works do not state the earliest noncontractive full-involution descent-set spectral range, its four independent extra null frequencies, or the universally projection-optimal endpoint norm \(9/4\). A recomputation of known descent-set counts alone would not support originality; the distinct Fourier-range and optimal-projection conclusion is the assessed claim. Residual risk: an equivalent functional-analytic restatement might occur in unindexed literature.

## Value

PASS. Descent profiles encode correlated combinatorial statistics. Complement symmetry predicts a norm-one conditional-expectation range projection, but at order seven that picture fails sharply: 36 of 64 Fourier directions are lost, with four independent extra zero modes and minimum possible endpoint projection amplification \(9/4\). The norm-one projection returns in order eight, so loss of contractivity is genuinely nonmonotone. This is a natural, complete first-transition boundary for stable Fourier reconstruction, not an arbitrary numerical slice. The limitation is finite-order scope; no classification beyond order eight is claimed.
