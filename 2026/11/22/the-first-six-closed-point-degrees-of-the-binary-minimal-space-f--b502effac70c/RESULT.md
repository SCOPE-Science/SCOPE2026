# The first six closed-point degrees of the binary minimal space-filling genus-\(19\) curve

## Finding
Let \(k=\mathbb F_2\) and let \(C\subset\mathbb P^3_k\) be any geometrically smooth complete intersection of a cubic and a quartic containing all \(15\) rational points of the ambient space. Using the published projective uniqueness theorem for these curves, the following counts are **independent of the choice of \(C\)**:

\[
\bigl(\#C(\mathbb F_{2^n})\bigr)_{n=1}^{6}=(15,15,15,19,50,15).
\]

The complete spectrum of closed points of degrees at most six is
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrr}
\text{degree}&1&2&3&4&5&6\\\hline
\text{number of closed points}&15&0&0&1&7&0.
\end{array}
\]

The unique degree-four point is precisely the geometric branch locus of the rational cyclic order-five action. The cyclic group acts on the seven degree-five closed points in cycle type \((1,1,5)\). Consequently the genus-three quotient \(B=C/C_5\) has precisely five rational points: three have completely split fibers of five rational points and two have inert fibers, each a degree-five closed point. The first six coefficients of the Hasse–Weil numerator, with \(P_C(0)=1\), are
\[
P_C(T)\equiv 1+12T+77T^2+350T^3+1261T^4+3827T^5+10157T^6\pmod {T^7}.
\]
These coefficients do not specify the full degree-\(38\) numerator.

## Assumptions and scope
The base field is \(k=\mathbb F_2\). A closed point has degree \(d\) when its residue field is \(\mathbb F_{2^d}\). All geometric smoothness assertions are over an algebraic closure. The source classification concerns complete intersections of type \((3,4)\) containing every point of \(\mathbb P^3(k)\), **not arbitrary space-filling curves**. The arithmetic computation is on an explicit representative; universality uses the separately published projective-transitivity theorem.

Write \(\phi(a,b)=a^2b+ab^2\). We use the representative from Campbell–Dedvukaj–McCormick–Moon–Morales:
\[
\begin{aligned}
G={}&\phi(X,Y)+\phi(Z,W),\\
F={}&(X+Z)\phi(X,Z)+(Y+Z+W)\phi(X,W)\\
 &+(X+Y+Z+W)\phi(Y,Z)+(Z+W)\phi(Y,W)+Y\phi(Z,W).
\end{aligned}
\]
The companion exact script verifies these equations afresh, so the arithmetic conclusions do not depend on a table of point counts.

## Proof
**1. Geometric soundness.** In each of the four standard affine charts, take the ideal generated by \(G,F\) and all six \(2\times2\) minors of the Jacobian matrix. Exact Gröbner reduction over \(k\) gives the unit ideal in every chart. Thus the complete intersection is geometrically smooth. By connectedness of projective complete intersections it is geometrically integral, of degree \(12\), and adjunction gives genus \(1+\frac{3\cdot4(3+4-4)}2=19\).

**2. Exhaustive rational points.** For \(1\le n\le6\), form \(K_n=\mathbb F_2[t]/(m_n(t))\), where the irreducible polynomials, represented by their nonzero monomials, are
\[
\begin{aligned}
m_1&=t+1,&m_2&=t^2+t+1,&m_3&=t^3+t+1,\\
m_4&=t^4+t+1,&m_5&=t^5+t^2+1,&m_6&=t^6+t+1.
\end{aligned}
\]
Irreducibility is checked by testing every potential proper monic factor of degree at most half the degree. Enumerate each projective point exactly once by requiring its first nonzero coordinate to equal \(1\), and evaluate the two homogeneous polynomials using exact binary polynomial arithmetic. This exhausts respectively \(15\), \(15\), \(15\), \(19\), \(50\), and \(15\) points on \(C\) over these six fields. If \(b_d\) is the number of closed points of degree \(d\), then \(\#C(\mathbb F_{2^n})=\sum_{d\mid n}d b_d\). Möbius inversion gives \((b_1,\ldots,b_6)=(15,0,0,1,7,0)\). Explicit Frobenius orbit formation over the degree-four and degree-five fields independently recovers one length-four orbit and seven length-five orbits.

**3. Deck action and quotient fibers.** The linear projectivity
\[
A:[X:Y:Z:W]\longmapsto[Y+Z+W:X+Y:Z+W:X+Y+W]
\]
obeys \(A^5=I\), \(G\circ A=G\), and \(F\circ A=F+ZG\); its characteristic polynomial is \(t^4+t^3+t^2+t+1\), irreducible over \(k\). Since its four eigenlines are distinct, they are exactly its four geometric fixed points. Each lies on \(C\): the homogeneous degrees three and four, together with \(A\)-invariance of the ideal, force both equations to vanish at an eigenvector with primitive fifth-root eigenvalue. Its eigenlines form one Frobenius orbit of size four. Direct substitution in the exact arithmetic verifier identifies this orbit with the unique degree-four closed point, and hence with the entire tame ramification divisor.

No rational point is fixed, so \(A\) acts on the \(15\) rational points as three five-cycles. On the seven degree-five Frobenius orbits, the same projectivity gives exactly two fixed places and one five-cycle of places. A rational point on \(B=C/\langle A\rangle\) is unramified (the four branch points lie over a degree-four place), and its degree-five étale fiber is either five rational points or one invariant closed point of degree five. The three rational five-cycles therefore give three split rational base points and the two invariant degree-five places give two inert rational base points, proving \(\#B(k)=5\). This does not assert the full zeta function of \(B\).

**4. The truncated numerator.** Write \(P_C(T)=\sum_{j=0}^{38}a_jT^j\) with \(a_0=1\). The zeta identity \(Z_C(T)=P_C(T)/((1-T)(1-2T))\) gives for \(1\le n\le6\)
\[
n a_n=\sum_{j=1}^n\bigl(\#C(\mathbb F_{2^j})-1-2^j\bigr)a_{n-j}.
\]
Recursion on the six verified extension counts yields the displayed coefficients.

**5. Universality.** The published full projective classification proves every geometrically smooth type-\((3,4)\) space-filling complete intersection over \(k\) lies in one \(\operatorname{PGL}_4(k)\)-orbit. Its projective transformations preserve point counts, closed places, and the conjugacy class of the unique order-five rational projective stabilizer. Therefore all above arithmetic invariants apply to each such curve. The projective classification itself is cited, not claimed as new.

## Relationship to prior work
Campbell et al. construct minimal complete-intersection space-filling curves, and supply the explicit defining equations. The later published projective classification shows their binary smooth minimal examples form one orbit and identifies an order-five action with a genus-three quotient and a four-point branch locus. A separate fixed-cubic census proves smoothness/ordinarity for all its smooth quartic sections. Another published general theorem already excludes quadratic closed points in minimal space-filling complete intersections. **None of these results implies the new exact seven degree-five places, absence of degree-three and degree-six places, the deck permutation \((1,1,5)\), the five quotient rational fibers, or the stated truncated numerator.** The degree-four branch statement and the first two numerator coefficients are credited, not claimed as independent novelty; the additional coefficients start at degree three.

## Limitations
The full degree-\(38\) Hasse–Weil numerator is not determined. There is no claim about closed places of degree greater than six or about complete splitting of every extension. The projective-uniqueness step relies on an earlier published computational theorem. This is a finite exact calculation with source code and algebraic checks; it has not received independent external audit. An equivalent unindexed earlier point table could still affect priority.
