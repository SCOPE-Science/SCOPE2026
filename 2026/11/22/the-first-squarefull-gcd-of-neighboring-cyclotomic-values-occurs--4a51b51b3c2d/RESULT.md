# The first squarefull gcd of neighboring cyclotomic values occurs at index \(29\)

## Finding
For each integer \(m\ge1\), let \(\Phi_m(X)\) be the monic cyclotomic polynomial and, for \(n\in\mathbb Z\), put
\[
G_m(n)=\gcd\bigl(|\Phi_m(n)|,|\Phi_m(n+1)|\bigr).
\]
Then **every** \(G_m(n)\) is squarefree for \(1\le m\le28\). The assertion is false at index \(29\):
\[
59^2=3481\mid\gcd\bigl(\Phi_{29}(298),\Phi_{29}(299)\bigr).
\]
Consequently, the smallest cyclotomic index admitting a squarefull gcd of consecutive values is exactly \(29\).

## Assumptions and scope
The index \(m\) is a positive integer; the argument \(n\) ranges over all integers, including negatives. Squarefree means no prime square divides the positive integer. The gcd is positive because the two polynomials have no common complex root and hence cannot both vanish at an integer. No restriction on the size or sign of \(n\) is imposed. The claim concerns the least **index**, not the least integer argument producing a counterexample at index \(29\).

## Proof
Fix \(m\). Write \(f(X)=\Phi_m(X)\), \(g(X)=f(X+1)\), and \(R_m=|\operatorname{Res}_X(f,g)|\). Both polynomials are monic with integer coefficients and have no common complex root: if both \(z\) and \(z+1\) lie on the unit circle, then \(z=-1/2\pm i\sqrt{3}/2\) is a primitive third root while \(z+1\) is a primitive sixth root, so they cannot both be primitive roots of one order. The resultant is therefore a nonzero integer and belongs to the ideal generated by \(f\) and \(g\) in \(\mathbb Z[X]\). In particular any common divisor of \(f(n)\) and \(g(n)\) divides \(R_m\). Thus if \(p^2\mid G_m(n)\) for a prime \(p\), necessarily \(p\mid R_m\) and the residue \(a=n\bmod p\) satisfies
\[
f(a)\equiv g(a)\equiv0\pmod p.
\]
Every integer congruent to \(a\) modulo \(p\) has, modulo \(p^2\), a unique form \(a+pt\) with \(0\le t<p\). For each common root \(a\in\{0,\ldots,p-1\}\), exact polynomial Taylor expansion yields
\[
f(a+pt)\equiv f(a)+ptf'(a),\qquad
 g(a+pt)\equiv g(a)+ptg'(a)\pmod {p^2}.
\]
Therefore simultaneous divisibility by \(p^2\) occurs if and only if these two linear congruences in \(t\) have a common solution modulo \(p\):
\[
\frac{f(a)}p+tf'(a)\equiv0,\qquad
\frac{g(a)}p+tg'(a)\equiv0\pmod p.
\]
The quotients are integers at the common roots. This criterion remains valid for singular roots because it never divides by a derivative.

The finite certificate `certificate.json` lists the **complete prime factorizations** of \(R_m\) for every \(1\le m\le28\). The accompanying `checker.py` independently constructs every \(\Phi_m\), recomputes each exact integer resultant, checks primality of every listed factor by exhaustive trial division, checks that the factor product is the entire resultant, enumerates all residues \(a\bmod p\) satisfying both root congruences, and tests **all** \(t\bmod p\) in the two linear equations. There are 97 distinct index–prime entries, no prime exceeds \(19927\), and 178 simultaneous root instances. No simultaneous prime-square lift occurs. Because the resultant restricts every possible common prime, the finite enumeration rules out **every** prime square for **all integers** \(n\) at each lower index, not merely integer inputs up to a cutoff.

For \(m=29\), the prime-index formula gives
\[
\Phi_{29}(X)=1+X+\cdots+X^{28}.
\]
Direct Horner evaluation of its integer coefficients modulo \(3481\) gives \(\Phi_{29}(298)\equiv\Phi_{29}(299)\equiv0\pmod{3481}\). Hence \(G_{29}(298)\) is not squarefree. Combined with the preceding exclusion, this proves the claimed first index. \(\square\)

## Verification
Run `python checker.py` in a directory containing `certificate.json`, with Python 3 and SymPy. It performs all exact determinant and modular checks and prints a PASS statement only after every assertion. It does not use probabilistic primality certificates, numerical approximation, or a search over bounded integer arguments. The stored prime-factor table can be regenerated and checked by multiplication against the exact resultant. The run tested 178 common roots, as detailed above.

## Relationship to prior work
De Nápoli's 2020 account of cyclotomic identities, including earlier work of Cheng–McKay–Wang (1995), describes index-product identities, not neighboring-input prime-square gcds. Sanna and Szikszai (2017) study coprimality patterns among long runs of quadratic/cubic polynomial values; their existence theorem does not decide a uniform index threshold for squarefull gcds of **two adjacent** cyclotomic values. Earlier exact classifications of neighboring values for degree-two and degree-six cyclotomic polynomials concern particular indices; even combined, they do not establish the complete exclusion for all indices below \(29\) or exhibit the squarefull transition at \(29\). These are implication comparisons rather than inference from absent matching titles.

## Limitations
The claim is a sharp finite **index** cutoff and a specific squarefull witness; it does not classify all arguments at index \(29\), all higher indices, or the maximal prime-power multiplicity. The nontrivial finite exclusion relies on exact reproducible symbolic resultants and modular enumeration, not on a closed parametric identity in \(m\). Although no equivalent cutoff was located in the inspected literature, unindexed tables or consequences of earlier general resultant identities could anticipate it. Independent validation has not been performed.
