# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The objects are the genuine ordered-word semisimplicial realizations, with words rather than unordered subsets as cells. Exact derivation of cellular deletion boundaries, full enumeration of 265 six-letter derangements, and deterministic integer unit-pivot elimination of all boundary matrices give chain ranks \(5,25,95,255,245\). Each reduction leaves zero residual entries, proving unit Smith factors, not merely ranks modulo a prime. Chain-square checks, Euler identities, modular crosschecks and full smaller-case repeats passed. The integer chain computations establish the theorem exactly; they do not imply an unclaimed homotopy wedge decomposition.

**Originality — PASS with bounded priority risk.** The first public motivating paper (Govc, arXiv:2611.11362) proves freeness of the fundamental group of an arbitrary permutation complex, not integral higher homology of the derangement-only six-letter complex. Chachólski–Levi–Meshulam treat two-generator and general/full complexes; Libman treats the different complex generated by *non-derangements*; Elizalde–Hanlon–Hersh treat relative-order pattern avoidance. The closest indexed computations concern alternating-group and projective-linear permutation families, which have different facet sets. No source inspected or indexed result established this precise statement. An unindexed finite computation remains possible.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The fixed-point-free permutations form a canonical, previously motivated selection of facets of the classical injective-word complex. At sizes \(n=2,3,4,5\), the resulting nonzero homology occupies at most two degrees; the computed six-letter case is the *first* instance of three adjacent nonzero positive homology groups. This is a meaningful smallest-size boundary for a natural combinatorial-topological family rather than an arbitrary selected parameter, and the integral computation rules out torsion in that instance. The result does not assert any unproved pattern at larger sizes.
