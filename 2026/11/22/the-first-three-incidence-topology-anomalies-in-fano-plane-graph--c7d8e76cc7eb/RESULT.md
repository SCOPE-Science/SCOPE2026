# The first three-incidence topology anomalies in Fano-plane graph powers

## Finding
Let \(H\) be the 14-vertex point–line incidence graph of the Fano plane, with 21 flags as its edges. For a set \(F\) of at most three deleted flags, require that \(H-F\) remains connected, use its **new shortest-path metric**, and put \(K_F=\operatorname{VR}_{\le2}(H-F)\). All homology in this result has coefficients \(\mathbb F_2\).

**Theorem.** For every such \(F\), \(\widetilde H_i(K_F;\mathbb F_2)=0\) for all \(i\ne2\). If \(|F|\le2\), then
\[\dim_{\mathbb F_2}\widetilde H_2(K_F;\mathbb F_2)=8-|F|.\]
If \(|F|=3\), set \(\delta(F)=0\) unless the three deleted flags are an **induced matching** in the original graph \(H\): their six endpoints are distinct and there are no other incidences between selected point and line endpoints. For such an induced matching, let \(a(F)\) be one when its three point endpoints lie on a Fano line and zero otherwise, and let \(b(F)\) be one when its three line endpoints have a common Fano point and zero otherwise. Then
\[\boxed{\dim_{\mathbb F_2}\widetilde H_2(K_F;\mathbb F_2)=5+\delta(F),\quad\delta(F)=a(F)+b(F)\text{ for induced matchings}.}\]
Among the \(\binom{21}{3}=1330\) three-edge failures, exactly 14 disconnect the graph. Of the other 1316, exactly **1232, 56 and 28** have degree-two Betti numbers **5, 6 and 7**, respectively. The 84 exceptional deletions are precisely the 84 maximum induced matchings, split by their known three geometric types of size 28. In particular, the 28 configurations with collinear points **and** concurrent lines give a two-class rebound above the expected rank five. No such rebound occurs after one or two edge failures.

## Assumptions and scope
The seven points are \(0,\dots,6\) and the seven lines are \(L_j=\{j,j+1,j+3\}\), with all indices modulo seven. The edge set consists of the 21 pairs \((p,L_j)\) with \(p\in L_j\). The graph \(H-F\) has the same fourteen vertices but the edges in \(F\) are removed. For connected cases only, graph distances are recomputed after removal. A simplex in \(K_F\) is a nonempty subset of the fourteen vertices with every pair at graph distance at most two. The coefficient field \(\mathbb F_2\) is essential: no assertion about integral homology, torsion, persistence maps or homotopy wedge decompositions is made.

## Proof
This is a finite exhaustive proof, not an induction or extrapolation. The complete standalone verifier `verify.py` is part of the result. Its construction and the properties of its loops can be independently checked as follows.

First, the seven triples \(L_j\) form the Fano plane: their 21 incidence pairs are distinct, and any two distinct points belong to precisely one triple. The verifier forms *every* set \(F\subseteq E(H)\) with \(0\le|F|\le3\) by the standard combination generator. For each, it builds the adjacency sets of \(H-F\) and computes all connected components by an exhaustive graph traversal. It then runs breadth-first search from each of the fourteen vertices, obtaining the exact shortest-path metric. This checks the quantifier over every connected failure pattern, rather than sampling isomorphism types.

For each connected graph, the `visit` recursion constructs the clique complex of the graph square: starting with the empty tuple, it adds each subsequent vertex in ascending order only if adjacent in the distance-two graph to every already-chosen vertex. Every clique has a unique ascending order and is therefore generated exactly once, so `bydim[d]` lists **all** \(d\)-simplices. For each \(d\ge1\) the program writes each mod-two simplicial boundary as an integer whose bits are the \((d-1)\)-faces of the simplex. The function `rank_mod2` performs exact Gaussian elimination by successively canceling the leading nonzero bit. The number of distinct pivots equals the rank of the boundary operator over \(\mathbb F_2\). The rank of the reduced zero-chain augmentation is one because the vertex set is nonempty. Therefore the dimension of reduced homology in degree \(d\) is exactly
\[f_d-\operatorname{rank}(\partial_d)-\operatorname{rank}(\partial_{d+1}).\]
This identity proves that the computed Betti numbers exhaust all homological degrees, including the highest simplicial dimension.

The independent combinatorial predicate `expected_rank` first recognizes whether three deleted flags are pairwise noninteracting, using all six forbidden cross-incidences; it then tests collinearity against the seven \(L_j\) and concurrency against the seven points. For every connected deletion pattern, the program asserts both the proposed degree-two formula and the vanishing of all other reduced Betti numbers. Finally, it asserts the exact distribution over all \(\binom{21}{t}\) failure patterns for each \(t=0,1,2,3\). Since the code contains no probabilistic step, symbolic oracle, external package or finite-precision arithmetic, these assertions provide a finite certificate for the stated theorem. It is **not** a certificate for stronger integral or homotopy assertions.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py` with a standard Python 3 interpreter; compare stdout with `verification_output.txt`. The verifier enumerates **1+21+210+1330=1562** distinct deletion patterns, of which 1548 have connected surviving graphs. It checks all faces, all binary boundary ranks and the incidence predicate for every connected pattern. The expected output identifies ranks 8 (one intact case), 7 (21 single failures), 6 (210 double failures), and, for triple failures, ranks 5 (1232), 6 (56), and 7 (28), alongside the 14 disconnected excluded cases. A second exploratory implementation using a graph library and independent clique enumeration reproduced the same three-edge distribution; the delivered script is self-contained and sufficient without that exploratory implementation.

## Relationship to prior work
Adams and Coskunuzer (2021) treat persistent homology of graph-power filtrations and provide the natural graph-metric setting, but not a failure census for the Fano incidence graph. Feng and Nukala (2023) classify other scale-two complexes on layers of Boolean subset spaces, not the Fano incidence metric or deleted incidences. A published classification of scale-two Vietoris–Rips complexes of finite projective-plane incidence graphs establishes that the **unmodified** Fano incidence graph has the homotopy type of eight two-spheres; its proof relies on every two points and every two lines being at graph distance two, a property that fails for the present damaged graphs. A separate published study of the one-flag exchange graph of maximum induced matchings of the Heawood graph already classifies its 84 induced matching triples into three 28-element types; that enumeration and orbit classification are **prior work**, not claimed here. The new assertion is the exact topological response of the **recomputed** radius-two graph powers to all connected failure patterns and its correlation with those known three types. Neither the intact classification nor the matching-exchange distances directly gives these 1548 boundary-rank calculations or the anomalous ranks.

## Limitations
The theorem is restricted to the Fano plane and to deleting at most three incidences with connected residual graph. The result determines mod-two Betti numbers only, not integral groups, torsion, homotopy type, barcodes or analogous ranks for higher-order projective planes. The verification is finite and fully exhaustive for the stated domain. A matching statement buried in unindexed literature remains possible; source comparisons are not proofs of uniqueness.
