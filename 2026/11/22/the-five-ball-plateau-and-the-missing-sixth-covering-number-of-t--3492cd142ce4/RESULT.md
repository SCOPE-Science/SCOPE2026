# The five-ball plateau and the missing sixth covering number of the Manhattan square torus

## Finding

Let \(T=(\mathbb R/\mathbb Z)^2\) carry the intrinsic sum metric
\[
d((x,y),(x',y'))=\min_{k\in\mathbb Z}|x-x'-k|+\min_{l\in\mathbb Z}|y-y'-l|.
\]
Write \(N(r)\) for the smallest number of freely placed, closed intrinsic radius-\(r\) balls covering \(T\). The exact low-multiplicity spectrum is
\[
N(r)=\begin{cases}
1,&r\ge1,\\
2,&1/2\le r<1,\\
4,&3/8\le r<1/2,\\
5,&1/3\le r<3/8,\\
\ge7,&0<r<1/3.
\end{cases}
\]
In particular, neither \(3\) nor \(6\) occurs as a covering number, and at \(r=1/3\) the covering number jumps by at least two. No assertion of an exact value is made below \(r=1/3\).

## Assumptions and scope

The circles in \(T\) have circumference one. The metric is the intrinsic \(\ell_1\) product of the two circle geodesic metrics, **not** the chordal or Euclidean flat-torus metric. Centers are unrestricted in \(T\). All balls are closed. The threshold equalities \(r=1/3,3/8,1/2,1\) are included in the upper-radius intervals as displayed. Classification: MSC2020 52C17.

## Proof

**A fiber lower bound.** Put \(u=x+y\) modulo one and \(v=x-y\) modulo two. The linear map \((x,y)\mapsto(u,v)\) identifies the plane with the \(\ell_\infty\) metric because \(|a|+|b|=\max\{|a+b|,|a-b|\}\). Its period lattice is generated by \((1,1)\) and \((1,-1)\). For a fixed value of \(u\) modulo one, the fiber is a circle of circumference two in the \(v\)-coordinate. When \(0<r<1/2\), one radius-\(r\) ball has a nonempty intersection with that fiber only when the \(u\)-coordinate lies in an arc of length \(2r\) about the ball's projected center, and then the intersection is a single interval of \(v\)-length at most \(2r\). Consequently, every fiber requires at least \(\lceil1/r\rceil\) active balls. Integrating the number of active balls around the \(u\)-circle gives the rigorous necessary bound
\[
2rN(r)\ge\lceil1/r\rceil,\qquad
N(r)\ge\left\lceil\frac{\lceil1/r\rceil}{2r}\right\rceil\quad(0<r<1/2).
\]
For \(1/3\le r<3/8\) this is at least five; for \(3/8\le r<1/2\) it is at least four. For \(0<r<1/3\) it is strictly greater than six. This estimate was already established in the earlier work identified in the prior-work section; it is reproduced here to make the new endpoint constructions independently checkable.

**Four-center upper bound.** The four centers
\[
C_4=\{(0,0),(1/2,0),(1/4,1/2),(3/4,1/2)\}\pmod{\mathbb Z^2}
\]
cover \(T\) with radius \(3/8\). The following finite exact certificate verifies the entire continuum rather than a sampled grid. C_5=\{(0,0),(1/3,1/3),(2/3,1/3),(0,2/3),(1/2,5/6)\}\pmod{\mathbb Z^2}

cover \(T\) with radius \(1/3\). Divide \([0,1]^2\) into \(6\times6\) squares and use the same diagonal convention, now with

**Remaining phases.** Two radius-\(1/2\) balls centered at \((0,0)\) and \((1/2,1/2)\) cover: put \(a=\min\{|x|,1-|x|\}\) and \(b=\min\{|y|,1-|y|\}\) for \(x,y\in[0,1]\). The two distances are \(a+b\) and \(1-a-b\); their minimum is at most \(1/2\). One ball must have radius at least the torus diameter \(1\). For \(r\ge1\), one ball suffices. Monotonicity of \(N(r)\), the lower bound and the three explicit configurations give exactly the stated classification. In particular \(N(r)=3\) and \(N(r)=6\) are impossible at every positive radius.

## Verification

`checker.py` uses Python standard-library `fractions.Fraction`; it checks all \(128+72=200\) triangles against all relevant integer translates of every displayed center. Each triangle is accepted only if one *same translated diamond* contains all its vertices, a sufficient condition for covering the continuous triangular region by convexity. Running `python3 checker.py` prints two counts and `All 200 rational triangle inclusion certificates passed.` The checker does not search for optimal center sets; optimality follows analytically from the fiber lower bound. This finite exact proof certificate is a complete verification of the two explicit covering constructions, not statistical evidence.

## Relationship to prior work

Cieliebak and Cieliebak, *Covering numbers of metric spaces* (2026), introduce and study the sum-metric covering-number setting and product inequalities. Their paper does not state these torus threshold values or the five-center diamond covering. A prior research record (finding ID `824df36e-a7bb-4bc5-94fb-f2acb41a752a`) derived the sharp fiber lower bound and the optimal two-center phase, along with all even-reciprocal exact values \(N(1/(2n))=2n^2\). The present statement credits those facts, and adds the **complete four- and five-center phases**, the previously undetermined endpoint \(N(1/3)=5\), and the resulting exclusion of the value six. The exact constructions are not implied by a one-sided lower bound. Studies of Euclidean-circle packing on flat tori concern a different norm and packing rather than covering, and do not directly imply the stated thresholds.

## Limitations

This establishes neither exact \(N(r)\) for \(0<r<1/3\) nor uniqueness of optimal four- or five-center configurations. The geometric certificates are specific to the square torus and intrinsic Manhattan metric. Historical priority is provisional: unindexed geometric covering literature using periodic-square or diamond-cover terminology may contain equivalent arrangements. No independent audit has been performed.
