# Scientific review: Steiner-triplet Frobenius reconstruction

## Correctness
PASS. The exact domain is every smooth cubic surface over a finite field, with triplets understood as *unordered* three-part partitions of its \(27\) geometric lines into three nine-line Steiner-pair supports. The classical correspondence with the Weyl group gives the finite reduction. A fresh standalone verifier enumerates all \(27\) line classes, \(45\) anticanonical triples, \(120\) Steiner-pair supports, \(40\) triplets, \(51{,}840\) Weyl elements, and exactly \(25\) conjugacy classes. It separately verifies the Weyl orbit of a triplet has size \(40\). Every class has a distinct triplet cycle partition; the prefix count signature sequence is \(10,14,20,22,23,24,24,24,25\). The order-twelve versus order-nine representatives prove that eight consecutive counts do not suffice. All results are exact finite computations; the certificate is independently regenerated from the included source code. The geometric incidence-to-Frobenius interpretation is standard and is cited.

## Originality
PASS with residual risk. The fully examined arXiv full texts of Elsenhans–Jahnel (2010 and 2011) establish the Steiner pair, triplet, stabilizer and Brauer contexts, not the full triplet zeta spectra or inverse identification of all \(25\) Weyl types. Das (2020) classifies finite-field Weyl types and markings but does not state the triplet reconstruction and sharp nine-degree cutoff. Targeted database and literature searches for the exact and equivalent permutations found no decisive prior theorem. The previously established \(72\)-sixer reconstruction neither proves nor subsumes the assertion that the different \(40\)-triplet action separates the same classes. A historical or unindexed \(40\)-point character table could encode this comparison implicitly; that is the principal remaining originality risk.

## Mathematical value
PASS. Steiner triplets are natural rather than arbitrary finite subsets: they are canonical decompositions of all \(27\) lines, and over number fields they play a structural role in order-three Brauer phenomena. The precise arithmetic inverse problem—how much of Frobenius on the Picard lattice their \(40\)-point splitting detects—therefore has a geometric motivation independent of the computation. The result shows complete identification from a smaller configuration than the \(72\)-sixer marking, distinguishes otherwise identical \(27\)-line splitting types with one rational-triplet count, and gives the exact first-nine-observations cutoff and obstruction at degree eight. It is a natural complete classification, not an arbitrary coefficient slice. It does not reconstruct the surface's isomorphism class and does not provide an independent mathematical audit.
