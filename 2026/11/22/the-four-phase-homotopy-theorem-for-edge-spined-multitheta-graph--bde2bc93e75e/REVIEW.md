# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The exact two-cone decomposition follows by partitioning independent sets by their intersection with the adjacent pole pair. Each base is a join of standard path independence complexes. The decisive all-one-residue attachment is an isomorphism on homotopy because endpoint inclusion of \(\operatorname{Ind}(P_{3a-1})\) into \(\operatorname{Ind}(P_{3a})\) has a contractible cofiber. In all other cases, cone attachments have contractible source or contractible target. This proves the four homotopy alternatives without extrapolating from examples. A standalone mod-two chain verifier agrees on 70 finite instances, including disconnected zero-dimensional cases.

## Originality — PASS against the inspected evidence

The most relevant 2024 theta-graph calculation has only three pole-to-pole routes, whereas the theorem concerns at least four, including the pole edge. The 2023 vertex-wedge classification has only one identified vertex, and the general 2012 mapping-cone results are methodology rather than this family-specific statement. Publicly indexed unspined equal-length multitheta calculations address a different graph and do not imply the classification after adding an edge. Targeted searches found no stronger covering theorem. An unindexed equivalent, or a general theorem not retrieved under these terms, remains a genuine bibliographic risk.

## Value — PASS

The graphs are the natural generalized theta networks with a direct pole edge. The theorem gives one exact integral homotopy classification for an arbitrary number of routes and all length assignments, including a sharp four-phase mod-three transition and a uniform two-sphere-summand bound independent of graph size. These are natural parameters and a complete classification, not a selected finite experiment or a renaming of the cycle case.

## Scientific limits

The proof is homotopy-theoretic, not a claimed simple-homotopy certificate. Finite mod-two checks corroborate but do not establish the infinite result; they cannot independently rule out integral torsion. Bibliographic completeness is not guaranteed.
