# The full center of the type-A 0-Hecke monoid is a truncated monogenic monoid

## Finding

Let \(n\ge 2\) be an integer, let \(H_n\) be the type-A 0-Hecke **monoid** indexed by permutations in \(S_n\), and write \(\pi_w\) for its element indexed by \(w\). Set \(m=\lfloor n/2\rfloor\). For each integer \(0\le k\le m\), let \(w_k\) be the permutation with one-line notation
\[
w_k=(n,n-1,\ldots,n-k+1,\ k+1,k+2,\ldots,n-k,\ k,k-1,\ldots,1).
\]
Empty lists are omitted. Equivalently, \(w_k\) swaps each of the \(k\) outermost pairs \(i,n+1-i\), leaving the remaining middle positions fixed. Then
\[
Z(H_n)=\{\pi_{w_0},\pi_{w_1},\ldots,\pi_{w_m}\},\qquad
\pi_{w_j}\star\pi_{w_k}=\pi_{w_{\min(j+k,m)}}
\quad(0\le j,k\le m).
\]
In particular, \(Z(H_n)\) has exactly \(m+1\) elements and is isomorphic to the monoid \(\{0,\ldots,m\}\) under truncated addition. It is generated by \(\pi_{w_1}\), indexed by the endpoint transposition \((1\ n)\), whose \(m\)-th power is the longest element \(\pi_{w_m}\). Only the identity and the longest element are central idempotents. The case \(n=1\) has the singleton center.

## Assumptions and scope

The 0-Hecke generators \(\pi_i\), for \(1\le i<n\), satisfy \(\pi_i^2=\pi_i\), the usual adjacent braid relations, and commutation for nonadjacent indices. There is one monoid element \(\pi_w\) per permutation \(w\in S_n\). Multiplication is the (increasing-length) Demazure product: \(\pi_w\star\pi_i=\pi_{ws_i}\) if \(w(i)<w(i+1)\), and \(\pi_w\) otherwise; analogously \(\pi_i\star\pi_w=\pi_{s_iw}\) if \(w^{-1}(i)<w^{-1}(i+1)\), and \(\pi_w\) otherwise. Here \(s_i\) is the adjacent transposition. The center means the set of individual monoid elements commuting with every monoid element, **not** the vector-space center of a 0-Hecke algebra over a field.

## Proof

**Step 1: a complete generator test.** For a permutation \(w\), define its right and left descent sets by
\[
D_R(w)=\{i:w(i)>w(i+1)\},\qquad
D_L(w)=\{i:w^{-1}(i)>w^{-1}(i+1)\}.
\]
The two products \(\pi_w\star\pi_i\) and \(\pi_i\star\pi_w\) are equal precisely when either \(i\in D_R(w)\cap D_L(w)\) or neither is a descent and \(ws_i=s_iw\). Indeed, if just one side is a descent the two products have unequal Coxeter lengths. If neither is a descent then both increase length, and equality of their indexing permutations is necessary and sufficient. Finally, \(ws_i=s_iw\) means that \(w\) preserves the two-element set \(\{i,i+1\}\). It cannot exchange the pair, since that would create a right descent. Consequently
\[
\pi_w\in Z(H_n)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\forall i\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\},\quad
\big(i\in D_R(w)\cap D_L(w)\big)\ \text{or}
\big(w(i)=i\ \text{and}\ w(i+1)=i+1\big).
\tag{1}
\]
Sufficiency follows because the simple generators generate \(H_n\).

**Step 2: outer-pair peeling.** If a central \(w\) fixes \(1\), then index \(1\) cannot be a right descent, since the entry following \(1\) is larger. Condition (1) forces \(w(2)=2\). Continuing, if \(w(1),\ldots,w(i)\) are fixed, then \(w(i+1)>i\) and condition (1) forces \(w(i+1)=i+1\). Hence \(w\) is the identity.

Otherwise condition (1) at index \(1\) forces a double descent. Let \(j=w(1)>1\). If \(j<n\), then \(w^{-1}(j)=1<w^{-1}(j+1)\), so \(j\notin D_L(w)\); condition (1) would require \(w(j)=j\), contradicting \(w^{-1}(j)=1\) and \(j>1\). Thus \(w(1)=n\). Condition (1) is invariant under inversion, so the same argument for \(w^{-1}\) gives \(w(n)=1\). The middle positions and values \(\{2,\ldots,n-1\}\) are preserved. Their restricted permutation satisfies (1) with shifted indices; conversely, every central permutation on these middle positions extends by \(w(1)=n,w(n)=1\) to a central permutation on \(\{1,\ldots,n\}\), because both boundary indices are double descents. Starting at dimensions one and two, induction proves that the complete list is \(w_0,\ldots,w_m\), with all distinct.

**Step 3: exact product law.** The endpoint transposition \(w_1\) has the reduced Coxeter expression
\[
w_1=s_1s_2\cdots s_{n-1}s_{n-2}\cdots s_1.
\]
For \(0\le k<m\), start with the one-line array for \(w_k\). Under right Demazure multiplication by the ascending sweep \(s_1,\ldots,s_{n-1}\), the smallest entry \(k+1\) of the ascending middle block moves to the end of that block, immediately before the descending right block. Under the descending sweep \(s_{n-2},\ldots,s_1\), the largest remaining middle entry \(n-k\) moves to the front of that block, immediately after the descending left block. All other entries retain their relative order. The result is exactly \(w_{k+1}\). At \(k=m\), \(w_m\) is the longest permutation, so every simple generator is a descent on both sides and \(\pi_{w_m}\) absorbs any further multiplication. Thus
\[
\pi_{w_k}\star\pi_{w_1}=\pi_{w_{\min(k+1,m)}}.
\]
Inductively \(\pi_{w_k}=\pi_{w_1}^{\star k}\), so associativity gives the stated formula for all \(j,k\). Distinctness of the powers and the idempotent claim follow immediately from their indices. This completes the proof.

## Verification

The accompanying standalone `verify.py` independently implements left and right Demazure multiplication by simple generators, enumerates **all** permutations through \(n=8\), and compares the full generator-commuting set against the proposed formula. Separately, it constructs reduced words by inversion sorting and checks the entire multiplication table of the proposed central elements. The tested center cardinalities at \(n=1,2,3,4,5,6,7,8\) are \(1,2,2,3,3,4,4,5\), respectively. The verifier prints `TYPE_A_HECKE_CENTER_VERIFIED` on success. These are finite checks; the general conclusion follows from the preceding proof, not from enumeration.

## Relationship to prior work

König's 2021 article gives a combinatorial basis and enumeration for the **linear center of the type-A 0-Hecke algebra**, a larger and different object. It does not directly enumerate which *individual monoid basis elements* commute with every simple generator. He and subsequent authors also studied centers of the algebra in general finite Coxeter types. Berenstein, Greenstein and Li (2026) give powerful product laws for **parabolic elements** and classify **commuting pairs of idempotents** in Hecke monoids; in particular, their parabolic-product laws subsume the multiplication calculation once the particular elements here are recognized as parabolic. The independent claim made here is the *necessary-and-sufficient criterion for commuting with every generator and the exhaustive description of the entire monoid center*; the truncated product law is included to identify its resulting algebraic structure, not presented as a new general parabolic-multiplication theorem. The earlier published type-A maximal-clique classification concerns only monoid idempotents and explicitly excludes nonidempotent commuting submonoids. This center contains nonidempotent elements for \(n\ge4\), so it is not determined by those idempotent-only results.

## Limitations

The argument applies to the finite type-A 0-Hecke monoid with increasing-length Demazure multiplication, not to centers of its monoid algebras, other finite Coxeter types, affine types, or arbitrary Hecke–Kiselman quotients. The claims are supported by a complete proof and a finite independent checker; they have not received an independent specialist audit. Historical priority is provisional: implicit descriptions in earlier general work on Hecke monoids or their parabolic submonoids remain possible, even though the inspected sources do not assert the full monoid-center classification.
