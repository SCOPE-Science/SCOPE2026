# Scientific review: Crofton caustic distribution of an ellipse

## Correctness — PASS

The invariant line measure and billiard collision measure are matched via the incidence-flux Jacobian; the caustic label is independently derived using the rank-one identity \(\operatorname{diag}(a^2,b^2)-qq^T=q'q'^T\). Conditioning on arclength and uniform tangent momentum gives a globally normalized CDF. Both elliptic-integral density branches are obtained by exact changes of variables. The complete elliptic integral's logarithmic expansion has the same leading coefficient from both sides; the circular case and support endpoints are treated separately. Independent numerical integration and two independently sampled line ensembles agree with these formulas. Simulations are diagnostic, not a substitute for the analytic derivation.

## Originality — PASS (priority provisional)

The closest prior material is Casas–Ramírez-Ros (2011, section 4.1), which explicitly identifies the caustic label as a phase-space function, and Bialy (2022, section 3.2), which develops equivalent conserved quantities. Corvaja–Zannier (2023, section 2.8) describes conditional invariant measures on fixed-caustic leaves. None of the inspected sections gives the *marginal probability law* obtained by pushing forward the full Crofton/Birkhoff collision measure, nor its two-sided sharp logarithmic coefficient and identification of both axes from distribution singularities. The focal-segment mass is an elementary classical Crofton consequence and is not claimed as a separately new result. A past paper may contain an equivalent distribution implicitly or in other notation; a literature search cannot establish universal priority.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The caustic label is the canonical first integral classifying every generic billiard ray in an ellipse. Giving its entire probability law under the natural invariant-line ensemble is a complete, non-arbitrary integral-geometric invariant: it separates the elliptic/hyperbolic dynamical regimes, specifies the exact singular density at their separatrix, and establishes geometric recovery of both semiaxes from the law. The circular limit exhibits a genuine change from linear to square-root extreme tails. These facts go beyond a normalization or isolated parameter substitution and are relevant to random-ray sampling and inverse integral geometry.

## Scientific limitations and remaining priority risks

The statement is confined to planar ellipses and exact invariant-line sampling, and does not imply distributional laws for other chord samplings. A general density-of-states/coarea treatment of integrable billiards may subsume the formula after specializing its invariant and measure normalization. Especially check older billiard-action and classical integral-geometry monographs in an independent audit. The calculation has not received an independent mathematical audit.
