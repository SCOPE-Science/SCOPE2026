# The full dihedral quotient of the heptagon associahedral sphere is a three-sphere

## Finding
Let \(\mathcal A_7\) be the simplicial complex whose fourteen vertices are the diagonals of a regular convex heptagon and whose simplices are sets of pairwise noncrossing diagonals. Let \(D_7=\langle r,s\mid r^7=s^2=1,\ srs=r^{-1}\rangle\), of order fourteen, act by all polygon rotations and reflections. The **ordinary geometric orbit space**, not a Borel quotient or the simplicial complex on vertex orbits, satisfies
\[
\boxed{|\mathcal A_7|/D_7\ \cong\ S^3.}
\]
The identification is a homeomorphism. In particular, the orbit space is a closed simply connected three-manifold and has integral homology \(H_0\cong H_3\cong\mathbb Z\), with all other groups zero. The contrast is sharp: the **rotation-only** quotient is the previously determined nontrivial lens space \(L(7;2,3)\), whose fundamental group is cyclic of order seven.

## Assumptions and scope
Label the heptagon's vertices cyclically by \(j\in\mathbb Z/7\mathbb Z\), place vertex \(j\) at \(a_j=(\cos(2\pi j/7),\sin(2\pi j/7))\), and call a nonboundary chord a diagonal. Two diagonals cross only if they meet in their relative interiors. An \(m\)-element noncrossing set is an \((m-1)\)-simplex. Maximal sets are four-diagonal triangulations, so \(\mathcal A_7\) is a three-dimensional simplicial sphere (the type-\(A_4\) associahedral sphere). The actions are \(r:j\mapsto j+1\) and \(s:j\mapsto-j\). The statement concerns this complete dihedral group, not the rotation subgroup alone, a general polygon order, or a choice of barycentric quotient abstract simplicial complex.

## Proof
**1. An equivariant four-dimensional sphere model.** For a heptagon triangulation \(T\), define its secondary-polytope (GKZ) vector \(v(T)\in\mathbb R^7\) by
\[
v_j(T)=\sum_{\substack{\tau\text{ a triangle of }T\\a_j\text{ a vertex of }\tau}}\operatorname{area}(\tau).
\]
Let \(P\) be the convex hull of these vectors. The classical secondary-polytope theorem for a convex polygon says that \(P\) is a simple four-dimensional associahedron and its face poset is anti-isomorphic to noncrossing-diagonal sets, compatibly with polygon relabelings. Every triangulation of a convex polygon is regular. The dihedral group permutes the seven GKZ coordinates since polygon isometries preserve triangle areas.

Summing the GKZ coordinates, and then summing the coordinates weighted by their planar vertices, gives the triangulation-independent equations
\[
\sum_{j=0}^6 v_j(T)=3\operatorname{area}(Q),\qquad
\sum_{j=0}^6a_jv_j(T)=3\int_Q y\,dy=0.
\]
The second equality holds because \(Q\) is centered at the origin and each triangle contributes three times its area times its centroid. Since \(P\) has dimension four, its affine difference space is exactly
\[
W=\left\{x\in\mathbb R^7:\sum_jx_j=0,\quad
\sum_j x_j\cos(2\pi j/7)=0,\quad
\sum_jx_j\sin(2\pi j/7)=0\right\}.
\]
The average of the vertices of \(P\) is a dihedral-fixed relative interior point. Translate \(P\) by this center, take its polar in \(W\), and radially project the polar boundary to \(S(W)\). The polar is simplicial, has boundary combinatorially identified with \(\mathcal A_7\), and all identifications commute with \(D_7\). Consequently there is a \(D_7\)-equivariant homeomorphism \(|\mathcal A_7|\cong S(W)\cong S^3\).

**2. The two real Fourier modes, including reflections.** Write \(\zeta=e^{2\pi i/7}\). Real Fourier coordinates
\[
z_2(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{6}x_j\zeta^{2j},\qquad
z_3(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{6}x_j\zeta^{3j}
\]
give a real-linear isomorphism \(W\cong\mathbb C^2\), made orthogonal by a common positive rescaling. The omitted modes are the constant and first cosine/sine modes fixed by the three affine constraints. In these coordinates the generating symmetries act on \(S^3\subset\mathbb C^2\) as
\[
r(z_2,z_3)=(\zeta^2z_2,\zeta^3z_3),\qquad
s(z_2,z_3)=(\overline{z_2},\overline{z_3}).
\]
This verifies the reflection action as well as the already known cyclic weights, rather than deducing a dihedral statement solely from a cyclic classification.

**3. The full quotient is a closed three-manifold.** No nonidentity rotation fixes a point of \(S^3\), since neither weight \(2\) nor \(3\) vanishes modulo seven. Each reflection \(sr^k\) has a real two-dimensional fixed subspace in \(W\), hence fixes a great circle \(S^1\subset S^3\). Two distinct reflections cannot fix a common point, because their product is a nontrivial rotation. Therefore every point stabilizer is trivial or generated by a single reflection. At a reflection-fixed point, the reflection acts on the three-dimensional tangent space with a one-dimensional \(+1\) eigenspace and a two-dimensional \(-1\) eigenspace. A local quotient is modeled on \(\mathbb R\times(\mathbb C/\{z\sim-z\})\cong\mathbb R^3\), via the local squaring coordinate. Thus \(S^3/D_7\) is a compact, connected three-manifold without boundary.

**4. Its fundamental group vanishes.** Armstrong's orbit-space fundamental-group theorem applies to the finite action on the simply connected, locally compact metric space \(S^3\). It gives
\[
\pi_1(S^3/D_7)\cong D_7/N,
\]
where \(N\) is the normal subgroup generated by group elements having nonempty fixed-point sets. All seven reflections have fixed circles and generate \(D_7\). Hence \(N=D_7\) and \(\pi_1=1\). By the established three-dimensional Poincaré theorem, every closed connected simply connected three-manifold is homeomorphic to \(S^3\). Applying it here proves the stated homeomorphism.

## Verification
The standalone file `verification/check_heptagon_quotient.py` uses Python's standard library to reconstruct all fourteen diagonals and all \(196\) nonempty noncrossing faces, with face-size counts \((14,56,84,42)\). It checks the fourteen distinct dihedral permutations, the absence of rotation-fixed faces, and the barycentric flag-orbit counts \((19,91,144,72)\) in dimensions zero through three. The quotient incidence maps satisfy \(\partial^2=0\) and have ranks \((18,73,71)\) over each of the fields of orders \(2,3,5,7,11,101,1009\), yielding one-dimensional zeroth and third homology and zero first and second homology over these fields. It also reconstructs each reflection-fixed barycentric subcomplex as a connected ten-cycle. Running `python3 verification/check_heptagon_quotient.py` prints the success marker documented in `VERIFICATION.md`.

The finite computations corroborate the structure but are not substitutes for the secondary-polytope representation, local manifold argument, Armstrong theorem or Poincaré theorem in the proof. No claim about all polygon sizes is inferred from finite enumeration.

## Relationship to prior work
Fomin and Zelevinsky's 2001 preprint establishes the classical type-A associahedral sphere. The secondary-polytope method is standard and is described in full by Ceballos, Santos and Ziegler. Davis, Januszkiewicz and Scott identify the full dihedral combinatorial automorphism group of the associahedron, while Reiner studies equivariant secondary **fixed** polytopes, not geometric orbit spaces. The closely related published classification *Prime rotational quotients of associahedral spheres are weighted lens spaces* identifies \(|\mathcal A_7|/C_7\cong L(7;2,3)\) but expressly does not classify reflection-containing subgroups. The present claim treats the full order-fourteen quotient and proves a three-manifold homeomorphism using the reflection action, fixed-circle local models and orbit fundamental group. The even-polygon full dihedral fundamental-group classification does not cover an odd seven-gon. Noncrossing-partition, cyclohedron and cyclic nonmatching complexes are different spaces even when they carry the same polygonal group action.

## Limitations
The result concerns the ordinary topological orbit of one natural three-dimensional complex. It does not identify the knot type of the branched fixed-circle image, classify higher-dimensional dihedral quotients, or give a specific piecewise-linear equivalence. Its use of classical secondary polytopes, Armstrong's orbit theorem and the three-dimensional Poincaré theorem is explicit; no claim of a new proof of those results is made. A historical source could have recorded an equivalent consequence using secondary-polytope representation language, so bibliographic priority has a residual risk. Independent mathematical validation is not asserted.
