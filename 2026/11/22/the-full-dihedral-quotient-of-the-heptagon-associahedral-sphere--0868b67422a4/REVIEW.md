# Review of the heptagon associahedral dihedral quotient

## Correctness — PASS
The convex heptagon GKZ secondary polytope has four-dimensional affine difference representation equal to Fourier modes two and three after removing the constant and first modes. Equivariant polar duality and radial projection identify the noncrossing-diagonal sphere with the unit sphere of this representation. Explicit transformation laws give rotations with weights two and three and simultaneous conjugation for reflections. All nonidentity rotations act freely. Each reflection fixes a great circle, and distinct reflection-fixed circles are disjoint. Therefore all stabilizers are either trivial or order-two rotations of the two-plane normal to a fixed circle. This yields a closed three-manifold quotient. Armstrong's fundamental-group theorem kills the full dihedral group because reflections with fixed points generate it. The three-dimensional Poincaré theorem identifies the simply connected closed quotient with a three-sphere. These steps account for group action, topology, boundary behavior, and hypotheses. The finite reconstruction verifies face and flag counts, orbit incidence boundaries and seven reflection-fixed circles; it is supplementary rather than an infinite proof.

## Value — PASS
The noncrossing-diagonal sphere of the regular heptagon is a canonical three-dimensional associahedral boundary. Its rotation orbit space is a lens space with nontrivial fundamental group and seven-torsion in first homology. Whether adjoining geometric reflections removes this torsion *while preserving a manifold rather than producing a space with boundary or singular nonmanifold links* is a geometrically meaningful natural invariant. The proof identifies the local codimension-two fixed-circle structure and resolves the complete homeomorphism type, rather than only ranks or rational acyclicity. It supplies an exact natural finite classification in symmetry topology without claiming a family-wide extension or new proof of classical results.
