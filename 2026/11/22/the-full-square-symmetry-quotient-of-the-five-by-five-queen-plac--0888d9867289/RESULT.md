# The full square-symmetry quotient of the five-by-five queen placement complex is four two-spheres

## Finding

Let \(Q_5\) be the graph whose vertices are the \(25\) squares of the ordinary nonperiodic five-by-five chessboard; two squares are adjacent precisely when queens on them would attack each other along a row, column, or diagonal. Let \(K=\operatorname{Ind}(Q_5)\) denote its nonattacking-queen placement complex. The eight geometric symmetries of the square form \(D_4\) and act simplicially on \(K\). The **ordinary geometric orbit space** has complete integral homotopy type

\[
\boxed{|K|/D_4\simeq S^2\vee S^2\vee S^2\vee S^2.}
\]

In particular, the quotient is simply connected, its only nonzero reduced integral homology is \(\widetilde H_2\cong\mathbb Z^4\), and it has no integral homology torsion. This statement concerns the geometric quotient, not the quotient of the face set, equivariant homotopy, or merely invariant rational homology.

## Assumptions and scope

Index board squares by \((r,c)\) with \(r,c\in\{0,1,2,3,4\}\), represented in code by \(5r+c\). A nonempty finite set of board squares is a simplex when every distinct pair has different rows, different columns, and different values of both \(r-c\) and \(r+c\). All partial queen placements are included; no maximality requirement is imposed. The group \(D_4\) is generated by a quarter-turn and a reflection; the full eight-element group acts by board-square permutations. Every use of homotopy equivalence refers to ordinary geometric realization.

The archival source is the original March 8, 2013 arXiv release of *A q-Queens Problem. I. General Theory*, which studies nonattacking queen placements. A published calculation of the underlying complex already gives \(K\simeq S^1\vee\bigvee^{17}S^2\); this previously established fact is background, not the new claim.

## Proof

**The canonical quotient cell structure.** Enumerate every legal nonempty placement with increasing square labels. There are exactly \(25\) one-queen faces, \(140\) two-queen faces, \(204\) three-queen faces, \(82\) four-queen faces, and \(10\) five-queen faces; no larger placements exist. Form the barycentric subdivision using chains of nonempty original faces, including non-saturated chains. Its simplex counts in dimensions \(0,1,2,3,4\) are

\[
(461,2952,5676,4368,1200).
\]

Every vertex of a subdivision simplex retains the distinct cardinality of its original face. Consequently no group element can permute vertices within a simplex; moreover distinct facets in the boundary of a particular flag have distinct omitted cardinalities, hence distinct orbits. Therefore orbits of barycentric flags give a finite regular cell structure on the actual geometric quotient. Exact canonicalization of each flag under the eight group elements yields the quotient cell counts

\[
(c_0,c_1,c_2,c_3,c_4)=(63,373,711,546,150).
\]

The boundary of a flag is its alternating sum of omitted-face flags. The verifier checks that these incidence coefficients are \(\pm1\) with no boundary facet identifications and that all boundary compositions vanish. It then performs \(823\) elementary free-face collapses; each removed face has a unique live immediate coface that is maximal at the time of removal. This leaves a connected two-dimensional regular CW complex with

\[
(c_0,c_1,c_2)=(30,96,71),\qquad\chi=30-96+71=5.
\]

**Fundamental group, checked on the full quotient two-skeleton.** Choose an explicit breadth-first spanning tree on the \(63\)-vertex, \(373\)-edge quotient one-skeleton. Its \(62\) tree edges are collapsed, leaving \(311\) circle generators. The \(711\) oriented quotient triangles give attaching relators of cyclically reduced lengths \(1,2,3\), with respective counts \(94,190,427\). For each relator containing a generator exactly once with exponent \(+1\) or \(-1\), solve for that generator, substitute in every remaining relator, and freely and cyclically reduce the words. Such a step is a reversible Tietze elimination, preserving the presented fundamental group. The deterministic algorithm performs exactly \(311\) eliminations and finishes with no generators and no nonempty relators. Thus

\[
\pi_1(|K|/D_4)=1.
\]

**Integral homology and homotopy.** The elementary collapses show the space is a connected simply connected two-dimensional CW complex. Thus its first integral homology is zero, its second integral homology is a subgroup of the free group of two-cell chains, and all higher homology vanishes. Euler characteristic \(5\) implies \(H_2\cong\mathbb Z^4\). By the Hurewicz isomorphism, choose four based sphere maps representing an integral basis of \(H_2\). Their wedge defines a map \(\bigvee^4S^2\to|K|/D_4\) that is a homology isomorphism between simply connected CW complexes, hence a homotopy equivalence by Whitehead's theorem.

As an independent arithmetic check, the Euler characteristics of the eight fixed barycentric flag complexes are \((17,5,1,5,1,5,1,5)\). Their Burnside average is \(5\), agreeing with the quotient Euler characteristic from orbit counts and collapses.

It also computes the independent fixed-point Euler check. Its expected terminal line is `pi1=1, Euler_characteristic=5, homotopy_type=vee_4_S2`, following the marker `QUEENS5_D4_QUOTIENT_VERIFIED`. `verification_output.txt` records an actual execution from the delivered script. Every enumeration is finite and exhausted; no extrapolation to larger boards is used.

## Relationship to prior work

Chaiken, Hanusa and Zaslavsky develop counting and inside-out-polytope methods for nonattacking queen placements on general and square boards; their examined results do not classify the homotopy type of the full dihedral geometric quotient. The closest exact same-board calculation of \(K\) establishes the nonequivariant wedge \(S^1\vee\bigvee^{17}S^2\) and expressly does not establish an equivariant splitting. That nonequivariant homotopy type does not imply the present quotient homotopy type, since it does not specify the group action or its isotropy. Chessboard complexes allowing nonattacking rooks, and studies of bishops and kings, use different attack graphs and do not imply this statement. Searches for the precise five-by-five queen-complex dihedral quotient, alternate orbit-space and graph-independence terminology, and homology/fundamental-group equivalents found no directly dominating result; absence of a hit is not proof of publication priority.

## Limitations

This theorem is restricted to the ordinary five-by-five board with the full eight-element square-symmetry group. It does not classify intermediate subgroups, larger boards, or equivariant homotopy types of the original complex. The main proof is a finite, fully exhaustible cellular calculation with source-code verification rather than a symbolic uniform-family argument. Literature indexing can miss equivalent results; an unindexed earlier homotopy classification remains a publication-priority risk. Independent expert validation has not been performed.
