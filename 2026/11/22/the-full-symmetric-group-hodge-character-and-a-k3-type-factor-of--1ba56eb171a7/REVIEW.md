# Scientific review

## Correctness

PASS. The transposition, full symmetric and alternating-subgroup quotient descriptions give three distinct invariant-dimension constraints on the second rational cohomology. The alternating fixed-count formula is \((12+2\cdot18)/3=16\), and the absence of invariant one-forms makes its second Betti number fourteen. This reconstructs \(H^2\) without assuming a character. The same argument for holomorphic two-forms, followed by Hodge symmetry and the Hodge–Riemann bilinear relations, yields the complete Hodge diamond and the K3-type multiplicity. Exact standalone checks verify all character and Lefschetz identities.

## Originality

PASS (qualified). The closest primary source [1] gives the surface and its one-form representation but not the entire second-cohomology character or its polarized multiplicity Hodge structure. Grossi's thesis [2, Chapter 6] directly covers the eighteen fixed points and the quotient invariants; these are explicitly excluded from the novelty claim. The present result combines independent subgroup-quotient geometries to determine a natural full equivariant Hodge realization and identify a rank-six K3-type factor. A related Galois-closure paper [3] concerns different surfaces. Search-based priority remains incomplete and a short unpublished deduction from the known quotient data cannot be ruled out.

## Value

PASS. The standard-isotypic component supplies a geometrically motivated, canonical six-dimensional polarized weight-two Hodge structure with a two-dimensional positive period plane, which is not apparent from the nonequivariant Betti numbers or the known quotient invariants alone. The exact sign/standard splitting distinguishes two different kinds of transcendental and algebraic Hodge variation and provides a usable input for period and motivated-correspondence questions. This is a natural complete equivariant classification of the construction, rather than an arbitrary finite slice.

## Closest work and limitations

The numerical quotient theorem [2, Theorem 6.13] is a strong source of premises and must not be presented as an original quotient theorem. This result concerns rational Hodge structures only and does not construct a K3 surface, determine an integral lattice or prove a new Hodge conjecture instance.
