For any smooth, positively curved plane body of constant width, the probability that two independent arclength-uniform boundary points are within \(\varepsilon\) of the diameter has a sharp \(\sqrt\varepsilon\) law whose normalized coefficient is exactly the Hellinger affinity between the curvature-radius densities at opposite normals. The circle uniquely maximizes this coefficient, while smooth constant-width bodies realize every positive smaller value, with zero as an unattained sharp infimum.
