# Review of the icosahedral geodesic general-position complex

## Correctness

PASS. The twelve-vertex, thirty-edge diameter-three model and every forbidden triple were independently regenerated using the embedded exhaustive verifier. The 30 distance-two edges and 20 pairwise-distance-two triangles form a connected combinatorial two-sphere (all vertex links are five-cycles). Every original graph edge acquires a free triangle pairing to this core. Removing six isolated antipodal one-cells leaves a simply connected complex, which collapses in 30 separate tetrahedron/triangle pairs to a two-complex of Euler characteristic 22. Therefore the 2-sphere wedge rank is exactly 21; the six antipodal cells contribute six circles. This is an integral homotopy proof, not merely experimental homology evidence.

## Originality

PASS against inspected evidence. The original geodesic general-position literature (Manuel--Klavžar; Anand et al.) specifies the admissible sets and extremal cardinalities but supplies no homotopy classification of the full icosahedral complex. The closest indexed icosahedral independence-complex calculation has one two-sphere and six circles; this is a proper subcomplex with a different face condition, not a covering result. Two-independence and distance-neighborhood complexes likewise do not cover this face system. Some indexed records were available in summary form only, and unindexed equivalent work remains possible.

## Value

PASS. The smallest regular icosahedral graph is a natural symmetric test case. The new classification distinguishes the geodesic configuration topology from ordinary independence topology by precisely twenty additional two-sphere classes and gives an explicit structural proof of why those classes occur. Such an integral homotopy result is not recoverable from a maximum general-position cardinality or the face counts alone.

## Limitations

No result is asserted for other graphs or for the integral symmetry action. The work has not undergone independent external validation.
