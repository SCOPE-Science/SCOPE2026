# The idempotent commuting graph of a rank-two transformation variant is a subdivided biclique multigraph

## Finding
Let \(X\) be a finite set of cardinality \(n\geq2\), and let \(a:X\to X\) have rank exactly two, with fiber sizes \(u,v\geq1\), \(u+v=n\). In the sandwich variant \(T_X^a\), multiplication is \(f\star g=f\circ a\circ g\), where maps act on the right of the argument and the rightmost map is applied first. Define \(\Gamma_a\) to be the *simple idempotent commuting graph*: its vertices are **all** elements \(e\in T_X^a\) with \(e\star e=e\); two distinct vertices are joined exactly when \(e\star f=f\star e\). This graph includes constant transformations, and excludes loops.

Put \(t=2^{n-2}\) and \(m=tuv\). Then \(\Gamma_a\) is the once-subdivided multigraph of \(tK_{u,v}\): take the complete bipartite graph on the two kernel fibers of \(a\), replace each edge by \(t\) parallel edges, and subdivide every edge exactly once. Consequently it has \(n+m\) vertices and \(2m\) edges, and its entire adjacency characteristic polynomial is
\[
\boxed{\chi_{\Gamma_a}(z)=z^{m-n+2}(z^2-nt)(z^2-vt)^{u-1}(z^2-ut)^{v-1}.}
\]
The adjacency matrix has rank \(2(n-1)\). In particular, the adjacency spectrum of \(\Gamma_a\) determines the unordered pair \(\{u,v\}\), and thus determines the isomorphism type of \(T_X^a\) *within the class of rank-two full-transformation variants*: two such variants are isomorphic exactly when their idempotent commuting graphs are cospectral.

For \(n\geq4\), the degree-two vertices are precisely the nonconstant idempotents, and the graph automorphism group is
\[
\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma_a)\cong(S_t)^{uv}\rtimes\operatorname{Aut}(K_{u,v}),
\]
where \(\operatorname{Aut}(K_{u,v})\cong S_u\times S_v\) when \(u\ne v\), and \(\operatorname{Aut}(K_{u,u})\cong(S_u\times S_u)\rtimes C_2\). The semidirect action permutes the \(uv\) parallel-edge sets. No graph-automorphism statement is asserted for smaller degrees.

## Assumptions and scope
All transformations are *total* functions on the labeled set \(X\). Choose the two distinct values of \(a\) and call them \(p,q\); let \(F_p=a^{-1}(p)\) and \(F_q=a^{-1}(q)\) with sizes \(u,v\). The values \(p,q\) need not be fixed by \(a\) and may lie in either fiber. The graph has no central-element deletion convention: every sandwich idempotent, including all constants, is a vertex. Repeated multigraph edges are introduced only to describe the resulting **simple** graph after subdivision.

The present assertion concerns structural commutation and adjacency spectra. The total count \(n+2^{n-2}uv\) of sandwich idempotents and the classification of transformation variants by kernel-fiber sizes were already known; they are not claimed as discoveries here.

## Proof
**Step 1: classify the idempotents.** A star-idempotent \(f\) satisfies \(f\circ a\circ f=f\), so \(\operatorname{rank}(f)\leq\operatorname{rank}(a)=2\). Every constant \(c_b:X\to\{b\}\) is a star-idempotent, giving \(n\) vertices. Suppose \(f\) has rank two. Necessarily \(f(p)\ne f(q)\), because otherwise \(f\circ a\circ f\) has rank at most one. Write \(b=f(p)\), \(c=f(q)\). The condition at points mapped to \(b\) and \(c\) shows \(f(a(b))=b\) and \(f(a(c))=c\); since \(f(p)=b\ne c=f(q)\), this is equivalent to \(b\in F_p\) and \(c\in F_q\). Conversely these two conditions, together with arbitrary assignments \(f(x)\in\{b,c\}\) for each \(x\notin\{p,q\}\), imply \(f\circ a\circ f=f\). Therefore there are exactly \(uvt\) rank-two star-idempotents, parameterized by an ordered fiber pair \((b,c)\in F_p\times F_q\) and a binary string of length \(n-2\).

**Step 2: classify all commuting pairs.** For any rank-two star-idempotents \(f,g\), both \(f\star g\) and \(g\star f\) are rank two, with respective images \(\{f(p),f(q)\}\) and \(\{g(p),g(q)\}\). If they are equal, their common images identify the unique points in the disjoint fibers \(F_p,F_q\), giving \(f(p)=g(p)\) and \(f(q)=g(q)\). Under this equality, \(f\star g=g\) and \(g\star f=f\), so commutation forces \(f=g\). Two distinct constants do not commute because \(c_b\star c_d=c_b\) and \(c_d\star c_b=c_d\). Finally, \(f\star c_b\) is the constant map with value \(f(a(b))\), whereas \(c_b\star f=c_b\). For a rank-two star-idempotent \(f\), equality holds exactly when \(b=f(p)\) or \(b=f(q)\). Hence the commuting graph is bipartite: its one part is the \(n\) constant maps, the other part consists of \(m\) rank-two idempotents; each rank-two vertex is adjacent precisely to the two constants indexed by its image points. For each pair \((b,c)\in F_p\times F_q\), there are exactly \(t\) rank-two vertices with those two neighbors. This is precisely the claimed edge-subdivision description.

**Step 3: compute the full characteristic polynomial.** In the indicated vertex order, the adjacency matrix is \(A=\begin{pmatrix}0&B\\B^{\mathsf T}&0\end{pmatrix}\), where \(B\) is the unsigned vertex-edge incidence matrix (of size \(n\times m\)) of the \(t\)-fold complete bipartite multigraph. Ordering the first \(u\) rows in \(F_p\) gives
\[
BB^{\mathsf T}=t\begin{pmatrix}vI_u&J_{u,v}\\J_{v,u}&uI_v\end{pmatrix}.
\]
This matrix has eigenvalue \(vt\) on the \((u-1)\)-dimensional sum-zero subspace of the first fiber, eigenvalue \(ut\) on the \((v-1)\)-dimensional sum-zero subspace of the second, eigenvalue \(nt\) on the remaining positive mode, and one zero eigenvalue on the bipartition-sign vector. (The last two eigenvalues are the eigenvalues of \(t\begin{pmatrix}v&v\\u&u\end{pmatrix}\).) The nonzero eigenvalues of \(A\) are the positive and negative square roots of the nonzero eigenvalues of \(BB^{\mathsf T}\), with equal multiplicities. The \(m+n-2(n-1)=m-n+2\) remaining eigenvalues vanish. This proves the characteristic polynomial, including \(n=2\) and the cases \(u=1\) or \(v=1\).

**Step 4: recover the sandwich type.** The largest positive adjacency eigenvalue equals \(\sqrt{n2^{n-2}}\). Since \(n2^{n-2}\) is strictly increasing for integral \(n\geq2\), the spectrum identifies \(n\). The order \(n+m\) of the adjacency matrix then identifies \(uv=m/2^{n-2}\). The unordered roots of \(\xi^2-n\xi+uv=0\) are exactly \(u,v\). If two rank-two sandwich maps have identical unordered kernel sizes, choose bijections aligning their kernel classes and their two image labels. These give permutations \(s,r\in S_X\) with \(a'=s\circ a\circ r\). Then \(\Phi(f)=r^{-1}\circ f\circ s^{-1}\) satisfies \(\Phi(f\star_a g)=\Phi(f)\star_{a'}\Phi(g)\). Conversely a variant isomorphism preserves idempotence and commutation, hence preserves the graph and its adjacency spectrum. This proves the equivalence without claiming that the original variant isomorphism criterion was previously unknown.

**Step 5: graph automorphisms in stable degrees.** For \(n\geq4\), \(t\geq4\), so every constant vertex has degree at least \(4\), while every subdividing vertex has degree \(2\). Any graph automorphism must therefore preserve the constant-vertex set, whose pairwise numbers of common neighbors reconstruct the complete bipartite simple graph \(K_{u,v}\). It acts on this base graph by a member of \(\operatorname{Aut}(K_{u,v})\), and can independently permute the \(t\) degree-two vertices attached to each of its \(uv\) edges. Conversely all such permutations preserve adjacency. The claimed semidirect product follows.

## Verification
The attached standalone Python program `check.py` uses only the standard library. For \(2\leq n\leq6\), every nonempty pair of fiber sizes was tested (including two placements of the sandwich map when \(n>2\)), totaling \(29\) configurations. The program enumerated every function \(X\to X\), extracted idempotents directly from the triple-composition equation, checked every pair for commuting, checked every neighborhood and exact edge count, and compared the first \(n\) power traces of \(BB^{\mathsf T}\) with the predicted eigenvalues. All configurations passed. This is finite corroboration only; the unrestricted theorem follows from Steps 1–5, not numerical extrapolation. The exact output is `check-output.txt`; run `python3 check.py` from the extracted directory.

## Relationship to prior work
Dolinka and East, *Variants of finite full transformation semigroups* (arXiv:1410.5253, first version 2014-10-20), Proposition 6.1, already characterize and count star-idempotents for arbitrary sandwich rank. Their formula specializes to \(n+2^{n-2}uv\) when the sandwich rank is two. The same work credits earlier classifications of transformation-variant isomorphism types by kernel partition. The new assertion is **not** that idempotents can be counted or that kernel partitions distinguish variants; it is the explicit complete commuting-edge structure, all adjacency eigenvalues, and the corresponding graph-automorphism description, which are not statements in that proposition.

Araújo, Kinyon and Konieczny, *Minimal paths in the commuting graphs of semigroups* (arXiv:1003.2809), study standard commuting graphs of ordinary transformation semigroups and their ideals rather than the idempotents in the rank-two sandwich variant. Araújo, Bentz and Konieczny, *The Commuting Graph of the Symmetric Inverse Semigroup* (arXiv:1205.1664), concern a different inverse semigroup and a graph convention deleting central elements. Broader structural treatments of sandwich transformation semigroups are relevant background but do not provide the precise characteristic polynomial above by direct statement. The graph theorem is compatible with, rather than a replacement for, these prior descriptions.

## Limitations
The claim is restricted to finite **full** transformation variants with a rank-two sandwich map. The graph is defined on all star-idempotents, not on all noncentral elements of the variant, and may differ from standard commuting graphs that delete centers. The result does not assert that the known classification of variant isomorphism types is new, and does not classify graph automorphisms at \(n=2,3\). Although the cited papers and published semantic comparisons did not exhibit the same graph spectrum, related literature on rectangular bands and subdivision graph spectra could contain implicit equivalent formulations; that historical priority risk remains. The reported tests cover finite degrees only.
