# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** Every sandwich idempotent has rank at most two. The complete rank-two parameterization follows from its action on the two sandwich image points. Direct computation of the products proves that distinct rank-two idempotents do not commute, distinct constants do not commute, and each rank-two idempotent commutes with exactly its two constant-image maps. The resulting subdivision graph has an explicit incidence matrix whose Gram eigenvalues are \(nt\), \(vt\), \(ut\), and zero, including their multiplicities. Monotonicity of \(n2^{n-2}\), then the vertex count, recovers the kernel-fiber sizes. The graph-automorphism assertion uses the strict degree separation for \(n\ge4\). A standalone exhaustive computation of \(29\) configurations through \(n=6\) confirmed all finite testable identities but is not used as proof of the general theorem.

**Originality — PASS, with priority risk.** Dolinka–East (arXiv:1410.5253), Proposition 6.1, **already provides the idempotent parameterization and total count**, and earlier work already classified isomorphism types of transformation variants. These facts are credited and not represented as novel. The assessed claim is instead a full commuting-edge graph identification, complete adjacency spectrum, and graph-automorphism group, none asserted in the closely inspected paper. The commuting-graph literature by Araújo–Kinyon–Konieczny (arXiv:1003.2809) and Araújo–Bentz–Konieczny (arXiv:1205.1664) treats different underlying multiplication/graph conventions. Semantic comparisons against published nearby records covered transformations, commutation, sandwich variants, and adjacency spectra, without finding an equivalent theorem. Classical subdivision and multigraph spectra may imply the spectral factor once the new adjacency identification is supplied; no priority claim is made for those generic linear-algebra steps. Unlocated historical equivalents remain a risk.

**Value — PASS.** Commuting idempotents are intrinsic to a semigroup's algebraic structure, while graph adjacency eigenvalues and graph automorphism groups are natural, computable invariants. The subdivision theorem gives a complete graph classification, all eigenvalues and multiplicities, and an exact spectral recovery principle for an infinite algebraic family. Unlike the known idempotent count, it records *which* idempotents commute and their global graph symmetries. The graph theorem has substantially more content than re-evaluating the prior cardinality formula; it does not rely on a purportedly new variant-isomorphism classification.

**Limitations.** Rank-two finite full-transformations only, no classification of rank-three variants. The graph contains all idempotents; alternate commuting-graph conventions change the problem. No independent audit has been performed. Implicit coverage by older sources not retrieved cannot be ruled out.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
