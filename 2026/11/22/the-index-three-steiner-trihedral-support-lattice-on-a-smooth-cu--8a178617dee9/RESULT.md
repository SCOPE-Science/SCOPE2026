# The index-three Steiner-trihedral support lattice on a smooth cubic surface

## Finding

Let \(X\) be a smooth cubic surface over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. Let \(F=\mathbb Z^{\mathcal L}\) be the free lattice on its \(27\) geometric lines, and let
\[
\pi:F\longrightarrow \operatorname{Pic}(X),\qquad e_\ell\longmapsto [\ell]
\]
be the divisor-class homomorphism. Write \(A=-K_X\), and denote by \(\mathcal S\) the \(120\) unordered pairs of Steiner trihedra. Each pair has a support of exactly nine *distinct* lines. Define the unsigned nine-line support lattice
\[
L_{\mathrm{St}}=\left\langle\sum_{\ell\text{ in the nine-line support of }P}e_\ell:P\in\mathcal S\right\rangle_{\mathbb Z}\subseteq F.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{L_{\mathrm{St}}=\pi^{-1}(3\mathbb ZA).}
\]
In particular, this is a complete **integral**, not merely rational, generation theorem. The associated \(27\)-by-\(120\) unsigned incidence matrix has
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{SNF}=\operatorname{diag}(1^{20},3,0^6),\qquad
F/L_{\mathrm{St}}\cong\mathbb Z^6\oplus\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z.}
\]
Equivalently, if \(L_{\mathrm{Tr}}\) is the integral lattice generated by the \(45\) tritangent three-line supports, then \(L_{\mathrm{St}}\subset L_{\mathrm{Tr}}\) and
\[
\boxed{L_{\mathrm{Tr}}/L_{\mathrm{St}}\cong\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z.}
\]
Thus passing from tritangent supports to nine-line Steiner supports incurs exactly one order-three obstruction and **no additional two-primary obstruction**.

## Assumptions and scope

Work with geometric line labels, not arbitrary rational divisors or the Picard group itself. Write the cubic surface as the blowup of six general-position points of \(\mathbb P^2\). Let \(H\) be the pullback of a plane line and \(E_1,\ldots,E_6\) the exceptional divisors. The line classes, in a fixed ordering, are
\[
E_1,\ldots,E_6;\quad L_{ij}=H-E_i-E_j\ (1\le i<j\le6)\text{ in lexicographic order};\quad Q_1,\ldots,Q_6,\quad Q_i=2H-\sum_{j\ne i}E_j.
\]
The anticanonical class is \(A=3H-\sum_{i=1}^6E_i\). The \(45\) tritangents are the \(30\) triples \(\{E_i,L_{ij},Q_j\}\) for \(i\ne j\), and the \(15\) triples \(\{L_{ij},L_{kl},L_{mn}\}\) indexed by partitions of six labels into three pairs. A Steiner pair is a pair of mutually conjugate three-plane trihedra; equivalently its nine-line support has exactly two partitions into three disjoint tritangent triples (the rows and columns of a \(3\)-by-\(3\) grid). These are standard geometric descriptions, not claims of discovery here.

No sign, orientation, multiplicity two, or normalization by a number of planes enters the nine-line support matrix. Its columns contain nine ones. The statement is uniform for all smooth cubic surfaces in the stated characteristic-zero setting and makes no arithmetic descent or Brauer-group assertion.

## Proof

**Step 1: The primitive tritangent lattice.** All \(27\) line classes span \(\operatorname{Pic}(X)\cong\mathbb Z^7\), because they include \(E_1,\ldots,E_6\) and \(H=E_1+E_2+L_{12}\). The class \(A\) is primitive. Consequently \(\pi^{-1}(\mathbb ZA)\) is a saturated sublattice of rank \(27-6=21\) in \(F\). Every tritangent triple has divisor class \(A\), so its support belongs to this lattice.

We now exhibit \(21\) such triples with a unit determinant on \(21\) specified line-coordinate rows. Use the deterministic lexicographic enumeration described in Verification, with all indices zero-based. Choose the \(21\) tritangent-column indices
\[
(2,37,39,41,42,38,34,43,24,18,27,12,11,10,30,32,22,16,25,6,0)
\]
and the \(21\) line-coordinate row indices
\[
(11,6,8,2,10,23,15,18,22,16,13,4,17,9,24,12,5,14,25,7,26).
\]
The corresponding integer determinant is exactly \(-1\), as established by the explicit fraction-free certificate. Thus those columns generate a primitive rank-\(21\) lattice. Because this is contained in the saturated rank-\(21\) lattice \(\pi^{-1}(\mathbb ZA)\), the two coincide. In particular,
\[
L_{\mathrm{Tr}}=\pi^{-1}(\mathbb ZA).
\]
This also admits an independent integral elimination proof via the tritangent presentation of the \(E_6\) weight lattice, referenced below.

**Step 2: The index-three upper bound.** Enumerate all triples of pairwise line-disjoint tritangents. Precisely \(120\) nine-line supports occur with two distinct such partitions; these are exactly the supports of Steiner pairs. Each is the disjoint union of three tritangent supports and therefore is an element of \(L_{\mathrm{Tr}}\) with divisor class \(3A\). Sort the \(120\) nine-line supports lexicographically. On the same \(21\) line-coordinate rows as in Step 1, choose the \(21\) Steiner columns
\[
(86,6,16,97,87,107,79,105,52,10,43,109,89,103,5,50,15,37,27,59,21).
\]
Their \(21\)-by-\(21\) determinant is exactly \(+3\). Relative to the unit-determinant tritangent basis, these Steiner vectors therefore generate a sublattice of index \(3\) in \(L_{\mathrm{Tr}}\). Hence \([L_{\mathrm{Tr}}:L_{\mathrm{St}}]\le3\).

**Step 3: The matching lower bound.** The map \(\pi:L_{\mathrm{Tr}}\to\mathbb ZA\) is surjective, since any tritangent maps to \(A\). Composition with the coefficient of \(A\) modulo \(3\) is a surjection \(L_{\mathrm{Tr}}\to\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z\). Every Steiner column maps to zero because it has class \(3A\). Therefore \([L_{\mathrm{Tr}}:L_{\mathrm{St}}]\ge3\). Together with Step 2 this gives equality and identifies \(L_{\mathrm{St}}\) with the kernel of that mod-three coefficient map, namely \(\pi^{-1}(3\mathbb ZA)\).

**Step 4: The cokernel.** The line-class homomorphism \(\pi\) is surjective, so the first isomorphism theorem gives
\[
F/L_{\mathrm{St}}\cong \operatorname{Pic}(X)/(3\mathbb ZA).
\]
Since \(A\) is primitive in the free rank-seven Picard lattice, this quotient is \(\mathbb Z^6\oplus\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z\). The stated Smith normal form follows from the structure theorem for finitely generated abelian groups. No rational-rank computation is substituted for this integral argument.

## Verification

The included standalone Python standard-library script `verify_steiner_lattice.py` constructs all \(27\) line divisor classes, enumerates all \(45\) tritangent triples by anticanonical class sum, and checks all \(\binom{45}{3}\) potential triples of tritangents for disjoint line supports. It obtains exactly \(5{,}040\) single-partition and \(120\) double-partition nine-line supports, and checks that every line occurs in exactly \(40\) Steiner supports. The script verifies that each support has class \(3A\), and uses fraction-free Bareiss elimination to evaluate the two exact determinants \(-1\) and \(+3\) above. On completion it prints `VERIFY_OK`.

As a separate numerical-independent check of the finite certificate, an exact SymPy Smith reduction of the full \(27\)-by-\(45\) tritangent and \(27\)-by-\(120\) Steiner matrices yielded, respectively, \(1^{21},0^6\) and \(1^{20},3,0^6\). The proof does not rely on a numerical test over finitely many surfaces: it reduces all geometric cases to the same finite universal line configuration and gives exact integer certificates there.

## Relationship to prior work

Elsenhans--Jahnel (2010, §2) give the classical geometry of the \(120\) Steiner pairs and explicitly list their nine-line supports in three combinatorial types \(20+10+90\). Betten--Hirschfeld--Karaoglu (2017, §§1–3) also use Steiner nine-line configurations in the finite-field classification of cubic surfaces with 27 lines. These combinatorial configurations are input, not claimed discoveries. McKean--Minahan--Zhang (first posted 2020-02-24) address the geometric recovery of the \(27\) lines and Galois monodromy from three skew lines, not the integral nine-line support lattice.

The published *Integral tritangent presentation of the \(E_6\) weight lattice* determines the saturated lattice generated by the \(45\) three-line tritangent supports, but does not determine the **sub**lattice generated by the \(120\) nine-line Steiner supports. Published results on the tritangent toric ideal determine the \(120\) cubic Steiner exchanges; those are relations among monomials in tritangent variables, not the integral image of nine-line support columns. Similarly the Schläfli double-six incidence theorem addresses \(36\) different twelve-line columns and has an order-four obstruction. These results neither state nor directly force the index-three equality above. A six-plane-support incidence map also differs from this map: each line occurs *twice* in the six planes of its Steiner pair, while each line occurs *once* here.

## Limitations

The result concerns formal integer sums of geometric line labels. It does not assert torsion in \(\operatorname{Pic}(X)\), a theorem on rationality over nonclosed fields, or a claim about the full toric ideal of the tritangent algebra. The geometry and the \(120\) supports are classical; only their integral generation and exact index are assessed here. An unknown historical integral-design or coding-theory table might contain an equivalent computation. Independent external validation has not been performed.
