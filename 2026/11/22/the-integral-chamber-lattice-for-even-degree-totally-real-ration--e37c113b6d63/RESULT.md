# The integral chamber lattice for even-degree totally real rational plane curves

## Finding
Let \(\mathcal A=\{C_1,\ldots,C_r\}\) be a nonempty totally real arrangement of distinct geometrically irreducible rational real projective plane curves, in the sense specified below, and suppose **every component degree is even**. Set \(d_i=\deg C_i\) and \(d=\gcd(d_1,\ldots,d_r)\), so \(d\) is even. Let \(U_{\mathbb C}=\mathbb P^2(\mathbb C)\setminus\bigcup_i C_i(\mathbb C)\), and let
\[
\gamma:\bigoplus_{R\text{ orientable real chamber}}\mathbb Z[R]\longrightarrow H^{\mathrm{BM}}_2(U_{\mathbb C};\mathbb Z)
\]
be the integral real-region cycle map, with arbitrary choices of chamber orientations. Define \(\epsilon\in\{0,1\}\) as the rank of the image of \(H_1(C_{\mathbb R};\mathbb Z)\to H_1(\mathbb {RP}^2;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z/2\), where \(C_{\mathbb R}=\bigcup_i C_i(\mathbb R)\). If \(\epsilon=0\), precisely one real chamber is nonorientable and is excluded from the domain; if \(\epsilon=1\), all chambers are orientable.

**Theorem.** Let \(\alpha_i\in H_1(C_{\mathbb R};\mathbb Z)\) be the pushforward of the real equator of the normalization of \(C_i\), oriented as a boundary of a complex hemisphere. Under the real boundary isomorphism
\[
\partial_{\mathbb R}:\bigoplus_{R\text{ orientable}}\mathbb Z[R]
 \xrightarrow{\ \simeq\ }
\ker\bigl(H_1(C_{\mathbb R};\mathbb Z)\to\mathbb Z/2\bigr),
\]
the integral kernel of \(\gamma\) is exactly
\[
\boxed{\quad\partial_{\mathbb R}(\ker\gamma)=
\left\{\sum_{i=1}^r a_i\alpha_i:\ \sum_{i=1}^r a_i\frac{d_i}{d}\equiv0\pmod2\right\}.\quad}
\]
It is an index-two sublattice of the free lattice spanned by the \(r\) normalization circles. Writing \(T\cong\mathbb Z/d\) for the torsion subgroup of \(H_2^{\mathrm{BM}}(U_{\mathbb C};\mathbb Z)\), the intersection \(T\cap\operatorname{im}\gamma\) is its unique order-two subgroup. The cokernel fits into the exact sequence
\[
\boxed{\quad 0\longrightarrow\mathbb Z/(d/2)
\longrightarrow\operatorname{coker}\gamma
\longrightarrow(\mathbb Z/2)^{\epsilon}\longrightarrow0.\quad}
\]
In particular, the **exact finite index** of the subgroup generated by orientable real chambers is
\[
\boxed{\quad[H^{\mathrm{BM}}_2(U_{\mathbb C};\mathbb Z):\operatorname{im}\gamma]
=\frac d2\,2^{\epsilon}.\quad}
\]
When \(\epsilon=0\), the cokernel is canonically cyclic of order \(d/2\); when \(\epsilon=1\), the exact sequence states the order but does not assert whether the extension splits. As a sharp consequence, an arrangement of even degrees has **integral chamber generation if and only if** \(d=2\) and \(\epsilon=0\). This holds despite the existence of one nonorientable real chamber in that case.

## Assumptions and scope
All homology groups use integral singular chains. A *totally real rational arrangement* means: (i) the complex normalization of each component is \(\mathbb P^1\), with real normalization isomorphic to \(\mathbb P^1_{\mathbb R}\); (ii) every complex point with at least two local branches is real and all of its branches lift to real normalization points. These are exactly the hypotheses of Yoshinaga's Definition 3.1. Singularities with a single local branch (real or nonreal) are allowed. Every \(C_i\) is reduced and geometrically irreducible. The ambient surface is the complex projective plane with its standard real structure.

A real chamber is a connected component of \(\mathbb {RP}^2\setminus C_{\mathbb R}\). Orientable chambers have integral locally finite fundamental classes. No orientation choice affects the kernel's index or the cokernel's order; changing one chosen orientation negates the corresponding generator.

The integer \(\epsilon\) depends on the embedding of the real curve graph and can differ between two arrangements with the same degree list and the same numbers of complex branches. The total-realness hypotheses are essential for the normalization-circle exact sequence used in the proof. In this theorem, \(d\ge2\) is even; no assertion is made about odd \(d\).

## Proof
**1. Real graph and complex relative groups.** Normalize the curves, obtaining \(r\) real circles whose complexifications are spheres. As all identifications between distinct local branches occur at real branch points, the real union \(C_{\mathbb R}\) is a connected graph. Attaching two complex hemisphere disks along every normalization circle recovers the complex topological curve. The attaching loops \(\alpha_i\) have disjoint edge supports and hence are independent. The resulting homology exact sequence is
\[
0\longrightarrow L:=\bigoplus_i\mathbb Z\alpha_i
\longrightarrow H_1(C_{\mathbb R};\mathbb Z)
\xrightarrow{j_*}H_1(C_{\mathbb C};\mathbb Z)\longrightarrow0.
\tag{1}
\]
Here \(C_{\mathbb C}=\bigcup_i C_i(\mathbb C)\). Since \(H_2(\mathbb {RP}^2;\mathbb Z)=0\), the long exact sequence of the real pair identifies its second relative homology with \(\ker\eta\), for \(\eta:H_1(C_{\mathbb R};\mathbb Z)\to\mathbb Z/2\). Excision and the classification of complement components identify the real relative group with the free abelian group on the orientable chambers. This proves the displayed boundary isomorphism.

The parity of the degree gives \(\eta(\alpha_i)=d_i\bmod2=0\). Therefore \(L\subseteq\ker\eta\), and \(\eta\) factors through the quotient \(j_*\) in (1). The induced character \(\bar\eta:H_1(C_{\mathbb C};\mathbb Z)\to\mathbb Z/2\) has image of order \(2^\epsilon\). Thus
\[
\partial_{\mathbb C}(\operatorname{im}\gamma)=j_*(\ker\eta)=\ker\bar\eta,
\quad[H_1(C_{\mathbb C};\mathbb Z):\partial_{\mathbb C}(\operatorname{im}\gamma)]=2^\epsilon.
\tag{2}
\]

**2. The two-torsion half-degree lemma.** The complex relative long exact sequence gives
\[
0\longrightarrow T\longrightarrow H_2(\mathbb {CP}^2,C_{\mathbb C};\mathbb Z)
\xrightarrow{\partial_{\mathbb C}}H_1(C_{\mathbb C};\mathbb Z)\longrightarrow0,
\quad T=\mathbb Z/(d_1,\ldots,d_r)\cong\mathbb Z/d.
\tag{3}
\]
Fix a component. Since \(\eta(\alpha_i)=0\), take a real integral two-chain \(R_i\subset\mathbb {RP}^2\) with boundary \(\alpha_i\). Let \(H_i\) be a complex hemispherical chain of the normalized \(\mathbb P^1\), pushed forward to \(C_i\), with boundary the same \(\alpha_i\). The difference \(R_i-H_i\) is an absolute integral two-cycle of \(\mathbb {CP}^2\), representing \(k_i[\ell]\) for a complex line \(\ell\) and some integer \(k_i\). Let \(c\) be complex conjugation. The real chain is pointwise fixed, \(c_*[\ell]=-[\ell]\), and the difference of the hemisphere and its conjugate is the complex fundamental cycle of \(C_i\). Hence
\[
-2k_i[\ell]=[H_i-cH_i]=[C_i]=d_i[\ell],
\quad k_i=-d_i/2.
\tag{4}
\]
The equality in (4) is an equality of homology classes; the real chain need not lie on the algebraic curve. Modulo the subgroup \(\langle d_1[\ell],\ldots,d_r[\ell]\rangle\) in (3), equation (4) shows
\[
\gamma(\partial_{\mathbb R}^{-1}\alpha_i)
=-d_i/2\pmod d\ \in T.
\tag{5}
\]
This computation is valid for singular rational curves because it is made on their normalizations and then pushed forward as singular chains. Since \(d_i=d m_i\) with \(\gcd(m_1,\ldots,m_r)=1\), the classes (5) generate precisely \(\{0,d/2\}\subset\mathbb Z/d\). In particular,
\[
T\cap\operatorname{im}\gamma=\langle d/2\rangle\cong\mathbb Z/2.
\tag{6}
\]
The intersection statement follows because any real class with complex boundary zero has, by (1), real boundary in \(L\), and the reverse containment is supplied by (5).

**3. Kernel and cokernel.** If a real relative class maps to zero, its real boundary is in \(L\) by the compatibility of the boundary maps and (1). Conversely, the class having boundary \(\sum a_i\alpha_i\in L\) maps by (5) to \(-\sum a_i(d_i/2)\pmod d\). This vanishes exactly when \(\sum a_i m_i\) is even. Because the \(m_i\) have gcd one, the parity functional on \(L\) is nonzero and its kernel has index two. This gives the kernel formula and shows that the kernel has rank \(r\).

Finally, quotient the short exact sequence (3) by \(\operatorname{im}\gamma\), using (2) and (6). The snake-lemma (or direct quotient) exact sequence is
\[
0\longrightarrow T/(T\cap\operatorname{im}\gamma)
\longrightarrow\operatorname{coker}\gamma
\longrightarrow H_1(C_{\mathbb C};\mathbb Z)/\ker\bar\eta
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{7}
\]
The left term is cyclic of order \(d/2\); the right term is trivial if \(\epsilon=0\) and cyclic of order two if \(\epsilon=1\). Equations (7) and (6) prove the exact sequence and index. Integral surjectivity holds precisely for \(d/2=1\) and \(\epsilon=0\), as claimed. \(\square\)

## Verification
The topological argument above proves the theorem for every arrangement satisfying the stated hypotheses; it is not inferred from finite enumeration. The accompanying standard-library-only script `checker.py` independently evaluates the elementary torsion subgroup and computes maximal-minor gcds (Smith determinantal divisors) of abstract integer-module models for degree lists with one to three components and degrees from \(\{2,4,6,8,10,12\}\), in both \(\epsilon\) cases. It checks the index \((d/2)2^\epsilon\) in 1,290 consistent models, including arbitrary torsion offsets on free lifts. Run `python checker.py` with Python 3; no packages or network access are needed. This is a supplementary finite consistency check, not a substitute for the conjugation calculation (4).

Two degree-two examples in Yoshinaga's Example 4.2 have the same four complex transverse intersections but different real embedding types: the affine conics \(4x^2+y^2=1\) and \(x^2+4y^2=1\) have \(\epsilon=0\), so their five orientable chambers generate all of \(H_2^{\mathrm{BM}}\) integrally. The affine conics \(xy=1\) and \(x^2+y^2=4\) have \(\epsilon=1\), so their five orientable chambers span an index-two subgroup, despite having the same complex Betti numbers. Both statements are about **integral** homology, not its rationalization.

## Relationship to prior work
Yoshinaga (arXiv:2611.00861v1, Definition 3.1, Proposition 3.3, Lemma 3.4 and Theorems 5.1–5.2) establishes the totally real hypotheses, graph exact sequences and rational spanning, and computes the integral chamber cokernel \(\mathbb Z/d\) when \(d\) is **odd**. His integral theorem explicitly assumes odd \(d\); it does not supply equations (4)–(7) for even \(d\). The new point is the half-degree torsion class of each real normalization circle and its interaction with the embedding character \(\epsilon\), which together decide the exact even-degree index and kernel. The worked two-conic configurations are in his paper, but the integral chamber indices asserted here are additional.

Brown–Dupont (arXiv:2501.03202v3) studies canonical forms and relative homology of genus-zero pairs with rational coefficients; it does not assert this even-degree integral lattice. Fomin–Shustin (arXiv:2006.14066) concerns expressive real algebraic curves and critical points, rather than the integral chamber-map cokernel. The classical homology of plane-curve complements determines the torsion group \(T\), but does not alone determine its intersection with real-region classes. Related work can have overlapping objects without containing the stated half-degree calculation.

## Limitations
The abstract isomorphism type of the cokernel for \(\epsilon=1\) beyond its displayed short exact sequence is not decided (a nontrivial extension is possible). There is no claim for arrangements that fail total reality or for positive-genus component normalizations. The theorem is topological/algebro-geometric over \(\mathbb R\) and \(\mathbb C\); it makes no assertion about arithmetic over finite fields. It does not classify which degree sequences can realize each \(\epsilon\). The computation in `checker.py` verifies lattice algebra only. The literature comparison is targeted and cannot exclude unindexed equivalent historical homological arguments. Independent external audit has not been performed.
