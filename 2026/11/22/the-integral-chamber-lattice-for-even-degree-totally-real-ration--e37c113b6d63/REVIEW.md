# Mathematical assessment of the even-degree integral chamber-lattice theorem

## Correctness — PASS
The claim is a statement for every totally real arrangement of rational plane curves with all component degrees even, with no generic-position assumption. The proof verifies both relative long exact sequences, the real-to-complex graph map, the role of the embedding character, and a nonstandard conjugation lemma: a real two-chain bounding the normalization equator, compared with one hemisphere of the complex normalization, represents precisely a half-degree class modulo the gcd of the curve degrees. That lemma is proved by subtracting conjugate absolute cycles in the complex projective plane, not guessed from finite data. The kernel parity formula and short exact sequence for the cokernel then follow by diagram chasing. The standalone checker executes 1,290 finite integer-module determinantal-divisor computations, supplementing rather than proving the all-degree theorem.

Potential mathematical risks checked: component normalization orientation affects signs but not the parity statement; even degrees are used to ensure each normalization equator bounds integrally in the real plane; no inference is made from the abstract group order to the splitting of the two-primary extension; the torsion group \(\mathbb Z/d\) comes from the degree map on integral second homology. The result does not hold under a silently weaker reality hypothesis.

## Originality — PASS, targeted and qualified
Yoshinaga's source (arXiv:2611.00861v1) supplies the relevant graph and relative-homology framework, proves rational spanning in every degree, and gives a specific integral-cokernel calculation only when the degree gcd is odd (Theorem 5.2). The new theorem addresses the excluded even-degree case, calculates the previously unrecorded half-degree torsion image, determines all integral relations and the exact finite chamber index, and characterizes integral generation for even-degree arrangements. These facts are not a special case of the cited odd-degree theorem. Brown–Dupont's mixed-Hodge formulation (arXiv:2501.03202v3) is over rational and complex coefficients and does not imply integral torsion indices. Fomin–Shustin's expressive-curves study (arXiv:2006.14066) has different invariants. Mathematical-record and literature searches found no directly equivalent claim, but absence of hits is not proof of historical priority. A pre-existing unindexed topological account of the half-degree comparison remains possible.

## Value — PASS
Yoshinaga's integral theorem leaves the parity boundary of the natural chamber map untreated. This result completes that boundary at the level of the full kernel and precise image index, not merely rational rank. It distinguishes the topology of the two real forms of a pair of transverse conics that have the same complex complement: the configuration whose real graph is nullhomologous has integral chamber generation, whereas the other has index two. The general index depends on both the degree gcd and the embedding character and can be arbitrarily large. This is a natural structural invariant of real algebraic-curve complements, with direct relevance to integral lifts of rational region and canonical-form relations.

## Scope, literature and limits
The theorem concerns the integral Borel–Moore second homology of a complex projective-plane complement, not arbitrary higher-dimensional arrangements. It does not determine the extension class of the cokernel when the embedding character is nontrivial, nor does it classify realizable characters for every degree tuple. The relevant first-public-source date is 2026-10-31 (the date of Yoshinaga's first arXiv version). The proof and supplementary checker were reviewed together; no separate independent mathematical validation has been undertaken.
