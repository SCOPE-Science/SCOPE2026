# The integral conic-pencil support lattice of degree-two del Pezzo surfaces

## Finding

Let \(X\) be a smooth del Pezzo surface of degree two over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. Let \(\mathcal E\) denote its 56 exceptional curves and \(\mathcal F\) its 126 geometric conic-bundle classes. For \(F\in\mathcal F\), let \(\operatorname{Supp}(F)\subset\mathcal E\) be the twelve exceptional curves that occur as components of its six reducible conic fibers. Define the **unsigned support map**
\[
\Phi:\mathbb Z^{\mathcal F}\longrightarrow\mathbb Z^{\mathcal E},\qquad
[F]\longmapsto\sum_{E\in\operatorname{Supp}(F)}[E].
\]
Its Smith normal form has exactly 35 nonzero entries, namely
\[
\operatorname{SNF}(\Phi)=\operatorname{diag}(1^{28},2^6,6,0^{21}),
\qquad
\operatorname{coker}\Phi\simeq\mathbb Z^{21}\oplus
(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^6\oplus\mathbb Z/6\mathbb Z.
\]
Thus the image is of index \(384\) in its saturation, its torsion exponent is six, and an order-three obstruction survives integral saturation. The free rank 21 is important: this does **not** claim that arbitrary individual exceptional-curve labels are rational combinations of pencil supports.

## Assumptions and scope

All incidences are over the algebraic closure. We use the standard marking \(X\cong\operatorname{Bl}_{P_1,\ldots,P_7}\mathbb P^2\), with seven points in general position. Write divisor classes as
\[
(d;m_1,\ldots,m_7)=dH-\sum_{i=1}^7m_iE_i,
\quad (d;m)\cdot(d';m')=dd'-\sum_i m_im'_i,
\quad K_X=(-3;-1,\ldots,-1).
\]
This notation is *coordinate data*, not a claim that an exceptional curve with negative \(m_i\) is ineffective. The 56 exceptional classes have four forms:
\[
E_i=(0;\,m_i=-1),\quad H-E_i-E_j,\quad
2H-\sum_{i\in I}E_i\ (|I|=5),\quad
3H-2E_i-\sum_{j\ne i}E_j,
\]
with multiplicities \(7,21,21,7\), respectively. A conic-pencil class is \(F=-K_X+r\), where \(r\) is a root in the \(E_7\) root lattice \(K_X^\perp\). Its support is exactly \(\{E\in\mathcal E:F\cdot E=0\}\), of size twelve.

There are precisely 126 roots in this realization: 42 directed differences \(E_i-E_j\); the positive and negative 35 roots \(H-E_i-E_j-E_k\); and the positive and negative seven roots \(2H-\sum_{j\ne i}E_j\). Signs here mean additive inverses of their divisor classes. Orderings of the 56 columns and 126 rows do not affect the asserted Smith factors.

## Proof

**Completeness of exceptional and conic classes.** The classical classification of exceptional curves on the blow-up of seven general points gives the displayed four types; alternatively, the inequalities \(E^2=K_X\cdot E=-1\), \(\sum m_i=3d-1\), \(\sum m_i^2=d^2+1\), and Cauchy–Schwarz confine the degree to \(0\le d\le3\) and force those four patterns. For roots, \(r^2=-2\), \(K_X\cdot r=0\) imply \(\sum m_i=3d\) and \(\sum m_i^2=d^2+2\); Cauchy–Schwarz gives \(|d|\le2\), and the 126 listed classes exhaust the solutions. The included checker also exhaustively verifies this last integer classification.

**Geometric interpretation of the twelve supports.** Every class \(F=-K_X+r\) above satisfies \(F^2=0\) and \((-K_X)\cdot F=2\). Direct intersections with the 56 exceptional curves yield exactly twelve zeros, 32 ones, and twelve twos. Hence \(F\) is nef (the effective cone is generated by exceptional curves), and its complete linear system is a conic fibration. For each \(E\) with \(F\cdot E=0\), the class \(F-E\) is another exceptional curve and \(E\cdot(F-E)=1\); the twelve components pair into the six reducible fibers. Within these twelve curves the disjointness graph is \(K_{12}\) minus a perfect matching (the thin \(D_6\) graph), connecting this presentation with the classical 126-block Gosset design. Conversely, if \(F\) is any conic-bundle class, then \(r=F+K_X\) satisfies \(r^2=-2\) and \(K_X\cdot r=0\), so \(r\) is one of these 126 roots. Distinct roots yield distinct conic classes. The support matrix is therefore the canonical geometric unsigned incidence map.

**Integral computation, not a modular-rank guess.** Enumerate the 126 roots and 56 exceptional classes in the exact order specified in `verify.py`. Set the \((r,E)\) matrix entry to one if \((-K_X+r)\cdot E=0\), and to zero otherwise. Every row has sum twelve and every column sum 27. Among the \(\binom{56}{2}\) column pairs, exactly 28 have overlap zero, 756 overlap one, and 756 overlap ten. In particular this is a structured \(E_7\) incidence map rather than an arbitrary 0–1 table.

This \(126\times56\) support table is the **transpose** of the \(56\times126\) matrix of \(\Phi\). Transposition preserves the nonzero Smith invariant factors, but the cokernel of the defined map has free rank \(56-35=21\), not \(126-35\).

The file `operations.json` gives **2,313 explicitly listed unimodular integer row or column operations**. The standard-library program `verify.py` regenerates the incidence matrix from divisor classes, checks every operation's unimodularity, performs all operations in order, and verifies that the resulting \(126\times56\) matrix is strictly diagonal with entries
\[
\underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{28},\quad
\underbrace{2,\ldots,2}_{6},\quad6,\quad
\underbrace{0,\ldots,0}_{21}.
\]
Since these positive entries divide their successors, this is the Smith normal form over \(\mathbb Z\), not merely a diagonalization over \(\mathbb Q\) or a finite field. The elementary divisor theorem immediately gives the stated cokernel and torsion order \(2^6\cdot6=384\).

## Verification

Place `verify.py` and `operations.json` together, then execute:

```text
python3 verify.py
```

Only Python 3's standard library is required. Successful execution prints `CERTIFICATE_OK`, checks all 126 conic classes and their component partners, and replays the full integer certificate. The zero factors are verified directly by checking that **all** off-diagonal entries vanish and the remaining 21 diagonals are zero. A separate symbolic integer Smith computation and an exact Gram characteristic-polynomial calculation produced the same answer, but the packaged proof relies on the replayable unimodular certificate and on the geometric class enumeration.

## Relationship to prior work

The underlying 56-vertex Gosset geometry and 126 twelve-vertex \(D_6\) supports occur in Van Maldeghem–Victoor's 2019 account of exceptional buildings (Section 3.3): their results use **signed quadratic equations** in the same support sets. They do not give the unsigned integral support cokernel computed here. Demeio–Streeter's 2021 preprint (in particular its background treatment of conic fibrations) and Demeio–Streeter–Winter's 2023 preprint develop conic-bundle and exceptional-curve geometry but do not assert this integer Smith decomposition. Previously published integral incidence computations for degree-four del Pezzo pencils, cubic-surface double-sixes, and plane-quartic bitangents use different sets and different maps; their Smith factors neither imply nor subsume this one.

## Limitations

This theorem concerns **formal integral labels** of geometric exceptional curves. The torsion is in the cokernel of the unsigned support map, **not** torsion of the Picard group or the Brauer group. It supplies neither arithmetic Frobenius orbit-sum invariants nor assertions about rational conic fibrations over a nonclosed ground field. Although exact and reproducible, a previously unlocated equivalent integral calculation of the \(E_7\) incidence design could affect priority.
