# The integral dihedral homology lattice of the ordinary three-divisible cycle independence complex

## Finding
For every integer \(k\ge1\), let \(X_k=\operatorname{Ind}(C_{3k})\) be the simplicial complex of independent subsets of the simple cycle with vertices \(\mathbb Z/(3k)\mathbb Z\). Its dihedral automorphisms are \(r(i)=i+1\) and \(s(i)=-i\). The previously known nonequivariant homotopy type is a wedge of two \((k-1)\)-spheres, with the conventional interpretation of the three-point complex when \(k=1\). The new integral equivariant classification is:

\[
\widetilde H_{k-1}(X_k;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Za\oplus\mathbb Zb,\qquad
[r]_{a,b}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&(-1)^k\end{pmatrix},\qquad
[s]_{a,b}=(-1)^{k(k+1)/2}\begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\(-1)^k&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The generators are explicit simplicial cycles
\[
 a=[z_0],\quad b=[z_1],\qquad
 z_j=\bigstar_{t=0}^{k-1}\bigl([3t+j]-[3t+j+1]\bigr),\quad j=0,1,2,
\]
with vertex indices modulo \(3k\) and joins ordered by increasing \(t\). All other reduced integral homology groups vanish. In particular the cyclic and full-dihedral **module coinvariants** are respectively
\[
\bigl(\widetilde H_{k-1}(X_k;\mathbb Z)\bigr)_{\langle r\rangle}\cong
\begin{cases}\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z&k\text{ odd},\\0&k\text{ even},\end{cases}
\]
\[
\bigl(\widetilde H_{k-1}(X_k;\mathbb Z)\bigr)_{D_{3k}}\cong
\begin{cases}\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z&k\equiv3\pmod4,\\0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}
\]
These coinvariants are **not** claimed to be integral homology groups of the geometric orbit spaces.

## Assumptions and scope
The graph \(C_{3k}\) has edges between successive residues modulo \(3k\), with \(k\ge1\); for \(k=1\) it is the three-cycle. The independence complex is the ordinary unaugmented geometric complex, while reduced simplicial chains use the usual augmentation. Simplicial automorphisms act with the standard alternating sign from sorting image vertices. The notation \(\bigstar\) means the simplicial join product of oriented zero-chains supported on mutually nonadjacent pairs. We use \(D_{3k}=\langle r,s\mid r^{3k}=s^2=1,\ srs=r^{-1}\rangle\). The theorem classifies the complete integral action on the one nonzero reduced homology group, not the homotopy type or homology of any quotient space.

## Proof
**1. Existence of the three integral cycles.** For each \(j\in\{0,1,2\}\), the pairs \(\{3t+j,3t+j+1\}\) lie in disjoint, mutually nonadjacent blocks. Choosing one vertex from each pair always gives an independent set. Consequently the displayed join is an integral \((k-1)\)-cycle with exactly \(2^k\) signed simplices, as it is a join of \(k\) reduced zero-cycles. The same definition is valid for \(k=1\).

**2. A unimodular dual pairing.** Let \(F_0=\{0,3,\ldots,3k-3\}\) and \(F_1=\{1,4,\ldots,3k-2\}\). Each is a *maximal* independent set: any excluded vertex is adjacent to one of its vertices, so neither has a proper coface in the independence complex. Thus the oriented simplex-coefficient functionals \(\phi_0\) and \(\phi_1\) are integral cocycles of degree \(k-1\), even though larger independent sets may exist elsewhere. Direct expansion gives, with the standard increasing-vertex orientation,
\[
(\phi_0(z_0),\phi_0(z_1),\phi_0(z_2))=(1,0,-1),
\quad
(\phi_1(z_0),\phi_1(z_1),\phi_1(z_2))=((-1)^k,1,0).
\]
The matrix of \((\phi_0,\phi_1)\) on \(([z_0],[z_1])\) is \(\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\(-1)^k&1\end{pmatrix}\), of determinant one. The classical cycle-independence homotopy classification proves \(\widetilde H_{k-1}(X_k;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^2\) and vanishing in all other degrees [1]. The integer cocycle pairing therefore establishes that \(a=[z_0]\), \(b=[z_1]\) are a full *integral* basis, not just a rational basis. It also gives \([z_2]=-a+(-1)^k b\).

**3. Exact rotational and reflection action.** Cyclically translating vertices shifts \(z_0\to z_1\to z_2\), while translation of \(z_2\) returns \(z_0\) with sign \((-1)^{k-1}\) from cycling the \(k\) join factors. Thus \(ra=b\), \(rb=-a+(-1)^k b\), yielding the displayed rotation matrix. Reflection reverses the order of the \(k\) pair factors (sign \((-1)^{k(k-1)/2}\)) and reverses each directed zero-cycle (sign \((-1)^k\)). Their product is \(h=(-1)^{k(k+1)/2}\). Reflection interchanges \(z_0,z_2\) and fixes \(z_1\), up to the common sign \(h\); hence \(sa=h(-a+(-1)^k b)\), \(sb=hb\), yielding the reflection matrix. Direct multiplication gives \(r^3=(-1)^{k-1}I\), \(r^{3k}=I\), \(s^2=I\), and \(srs=r^{-1}\).

**4. Exact coinvariants.** Let \(M=\mathbb Za\oplus\mathbb Zb\). The cyclic coinvariants are \(M/(r-I)M\), and \(\det(r-I)=3\) if \(k\) is odd and \(1\) if it is even. Since \(r-I\) contains an entry equal to \(1\), its integer Smith invariants are \((1,3)\) or \((1,1)\), proving the cyclic assertion. When \(k\) is odd, the cyclic coinvariants are generated by the common image of \(a,b\) and have order three. On this group \(s\) acts by multiplication by \(h=(-1)^{k(k+1)/2}\). For odd \(k\), \(h=1\) exactly when \(k\equiv3\pmod4\); otherwise \(h=-1\), and imposing \(s=1\) kills the order-three group. This proves the full-dihedral assertion.

## Verification
The standalone standard-library script `verify.py` reconstructs all \(2^k\) signed summands of each \(z_j\) and their simplicial boundaries for \(1\le k\le7\). It independently applies both vertex permutations with orientation signs, evaluates maximal-face cocycles, verifies the four dihedral matrix relations and computes the Smith determinants for both coinvariant groups. Separately it enumerates all independent faces, builds boundary ranks over \(\mathbb F_2,\mathbb F_3,\mathbb F_5\), and confirms top reduced Betti rank two for \(1\le k\le5\). Run `python3 verify.py` from a directory containing this RESULT.md and the script; the last line is `CYCLE_DIHEDRAL_INTEGRAL_LATTICE_VERIFIED`. The computations test finite cases; the preceding integer-chain argument and published nonequivariant classification establish the theorem for all integers \(k\ge1\).

## Relationship to prior work
Kozlov's classical nonequivariant cycle-independence homotopy classification is explicitly recalled in Adamaszek's 2011 preprint [1], which establishes the rank-two integral homology but does not state the dihedral module matrices or their full-dihedral coinvariant behavior. Conant and Thistlethwaite [2] introduced the Alexander-dual theta complex, which is a separate object. The closest accessible dihedral study of the theta complex determines its *rational* character and explicitly leaves the integral lattice in the three-divisible case undetermined [3]; Alexander duality at the rational level does not imply the integral reflection matrix or the order-three module coinvariants. The finite geometric-dihedral quotient classification of the cycle matching complex for \(3\le n\le13\) [4] studies quotient *spaces*, not the module for all \(k\). An integral representation theorem for \(\operatorname{Ind}(C_{3m}^{m-1})\) treats powers of cycles rather than ordinary cycles [5] and does not cover the present claim. The formulas here are compatible with the previously known rational characters but distinguish integral information they do not determine.

## Limitations
There is no claim about the homotopy type, integral homology, or fundamental group of the geometric quotient \(|X_k|/D_{3k}\). The integral basis and matrices depend on the explicit labeling and orientation convention; a change of basis conjugates the matrices. The finite script checks only \(1\le k\le7\), and the general result uses the established nonequivariant cycle-independence homotopy theorem. Exhaustive publication priority is not established; further unindexed or inaccessible equivalent integral-equivariant work may exist. No independent audit is claimed.
