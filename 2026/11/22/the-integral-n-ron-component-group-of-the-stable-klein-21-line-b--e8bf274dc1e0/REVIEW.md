# Review of the integral group for the stable Klein boundary

## Correctness — PASS

The 21 explicit Klein lines are defined over the quadratic order with relation \(a^2+a+2=0\). Their complete pairwise incidence has been reconstructed exactly, yielding 21 quadruple and 28 triple points with no additional nodes. The blowup construction therefore gives a stable nodal curve with 70 rational components, 168 edges, and genus 99. The determinant is computed independently via a 20-by-20 weighted Schur complement and its verified spectral polynomial, and by exact fraction-free elimination. The prime-local Smith calculations cover precisely every determinant prime, agree for two rooted Laplacians, and recover the determinant. The component-group interpretation invokes the classical Raynaud theorem only for regular unit-thickness semistable models.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk

The main historical comparisons were Seceleanu (2014), Bauer et al. (first public 2016), Gévay–Pokora (2022/2023), and Innamorati (2024); their inspected relevant material treats the classical incidence, geometric rigidity, symbolic powers, or derived point-line configurations, rather than the full integral critical group of the complete stable boundary. Existing related critical-group results on Klein's quadruple-only and triple-only Levi graphs treat strictly smaller/different graphs and do not imply the complete 70-vertex invariant factors. The general Raynaud theorem identifies the arithmetic interpretation but does not supply numerical Smith factors. An unindexed older numerical table remains a historical priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

This is the complete integral Néron degeneration invariant for the canonical stable boundary obtained by resolving *all* singularities of an unusually symmetric classical line arrangement, rather than an arbitrary slice. It refines the spanning-tree count with explicit cyclic factors and reveals substantial \(719\)-primary torsion absent from both known partial-configuration critical groups. The exact integral decomposition is therefore mathematically motivated by the intrinsic stable curve and is not a renaming of its spectrum or previously known subgraph invariants.

## Limitations

Only the stated stable boundary and regular thickness-one smoothings are covered. Neither the original nonnodal plane divisor nor arbitrary nonregular models or thickened nodes are addressed. Numerical priority still needs independent specialist examination.
