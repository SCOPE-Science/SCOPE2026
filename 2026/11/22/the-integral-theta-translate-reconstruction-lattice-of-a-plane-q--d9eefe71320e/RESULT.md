# The integral theta-translate reconstruction lattice of a plane quartic

## Finding

For every smooth plane quartic \(C\) over an algebraically closed field of characteristic different from \(2\), the integral lattice generated by the 64 two-torsion translates of an even theta divisor, restricted to the 64 two-torsion points, has the exact quotient

\[
\mathbb Z^{J[2]}/L \cong (\mathbb Z/2)^{14}\oplus(\mathbb Z/4)^{20}\oplus(\mathbb Z/8)^{14}\oplus(\mathbb Z/16)^7\oplus\mathbb Z/112.
\]

Every point basis vector has exact order \(112\) in this quotient. The 64 incidence vectors are linearly independent over \(\mathbb Q\). The quotient index is \(7\cdot2^{128}\).

## Assumptions and scope

Let \(J=\operatorname{Jac}(C)\) and choose an **even** theta characteristic \(\kappa\). The translated theta divisor is the set of degree-zero classes \(u\) for which \(h^0(C,\kappa\otimes u)>0\). For smooth nonhyperelliptic genus-three curves, exactly the 28 odd theta characteristics are effective, while the 36 even theta characteristics are ineffective. Thus for each \(t\in J[2]\) the indicator \(b_t\) of the set \((\Theta_\kappa+t)\cap J[2]\) has 28 entries equal to one. Here \(L\) is the lattice generated by all these 64 vectors in the free formal group \(\mathbb Z^{J[2]}\). It is **not** the Néron–Severi lattice, Chow group, or torsion subgroup of the Jacobian.

A symplectic basis of \(J[2]\cong\mathbb F_2^6\) and the chosen even theta characteristic identify points with \(x=(u,v)\in\mathbb F_2^3\times\mathbb F_2^3\), with parity \(q(u,v)=u\cdot v\). The incidence matrix has entries \(M_{t,x}=1\) exactly when \(q(t+x)=1\). The conclusion is independent of the basis and even-theta choice, because they only relabel its rows and columns through affine symplectic transformations.

## Proof

1. Write \(f(x)=(-1)^{q(x)}\). The three independent hyperbolic planes give \(\sum_x f(x)=8\). Hence the support \(q(x)=1\) has size \(28\). For each nonzero \(d\in\mathbb F_2^6\), the polarization of \(q\) is a nondegenerate symplectic pairing and \(f(x)f(x+d)=(-1)^{q(d)+\langle x,d\rangle}\); summing over \(x\) gives zero. Expanding \((1-f(x))(1-f(x+d))/4\) gives intersection size \(12\). Consequently the symmetric incidence matrix satisfies the **exact** identity

   \[M^2=16I_{64}+12\mathbf1\mathbf1^\mathsf{T},\qquad M\mathbf1=28\mathbf1.\]

   On the constant line, \(M\) has eigenvalue \(28\), while on its orthogonal complement it has eigenvalues of absolute value \(4\). Thus \(M\) has full rank, and \(|\det M|=28\cdot4^{63}=7\cdot2^{128}\).

2. The complete two-primary Smith data are obtained by the finite unit-pivot algorithm described and implemented in `VERIFY.py`, applied to the specified 64-by-64 zero-one matrix modulo \(2^7\). At each stage a unit pivot is cleared by invertible row and column operations over the current ring \(\mathbb Z/2^r\). When the entire remaining block is even, division by two reduces the modulus and records one power of two in each eventual invariant factor. The algorithm terminates with the following exponents, each strictly below the precision cutoff:

   | Exponent \(e\) | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
   |---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
   | Multiplicity of \(2^e\) | 8 | 14 | 20 | 14 | 8 |

   The sum of exponents is \(128\), exactly the known valuation of \(|\det M|\). No larger two-primary factor is possible. The determinant has one factor \(7\) and no other odd prime factor. Uniqueness and divisibility ordering of Smith invariants therefore force the complete diagonal \(1^8,2^{14},4^{20},8^{14},16^7,112\).

3. The matrix identity yields the exact rational inverse

   \[M^{-1}=\frac7{112}M-\frac3{112}\mathbf1\mathbf1^\mathsf{T}.\]

   Every column has 28 entries \(1/28\) and 36 entries \(-3/112\). Therefore for any point \(x\), the integer vector \(m e_x\) lies in \(M\mathbb Z^{64}\) if and only if \(mM^{-1}e_x\) is integral, which occurs precisely when \(112\mid m\). This proves the sharp uniform multiplier.

## Verification

Run `python3 VERIFY.py` using a standard Python 3 interpreter. The standalone script reconstructs every matrix entry, checks all 4,096 entries of \(M^2=16I+12J\), independently performs modular unit-pivot reduction, checks the determinant-derived Smith invariants, and verifies \(M(7M-3J)=112I\) exactly. The modular algorithm uses only invertible elementary operations over powers of two, not probabilistic or floating-point rank estimates. An independent computer-algebra Smith-form calculation was compared during preparation, with the same invariant factors.

## Relationship to prior work

Bergvall (first public preprint 2021) describes the bitangents and the 64-element theta-characteristic torsor in symplectic terms, but does not compute this 64-by-64 integral theta-translate incidence quotient. Polujan–Pott (2020) explicitly define the equivalent translation design \(\mathrm{dev}(D_f)\) for a quadratic Boolean bent function, and tabulate Smith forms for the **128-by-128 graph-development incidence matrix** \(\mathrm{dev}(G_f)\); the latter is not the matrix considered here, and its reported invariant factors do not state the 7-primary obstruction or this integral quotient. Work on the classical 64-point Menon/symmetric-difference-property design and 2021 switching constructions establishes the same underlying design parameters and other invariants, not the complete Smith factors identified here in the inspected materials. The construction and its combinatorial parameters are classical; the specific integral calculation, not their discovery, is the assessed contribution.

## Limitations

This is a theorem about formal integral incidence of two-torsion points with theta translates; it neither classifies plane quartics nor proves that an arbitrary combination of geometric divisor classes can be recovered by these formal sums. The finite Smith calculation is supported by a reproducible exhaustive algorithm, not by a hand-displayed 64-by-64 unimodular certificate. Previously uninspected classical design-theory tables may contain the same Smith form; in particular the full text of the 1994 64-point symmetric-difference-property paper was not available for complete comparison.
