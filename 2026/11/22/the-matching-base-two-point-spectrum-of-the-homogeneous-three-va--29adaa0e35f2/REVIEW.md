# Scientific review: matching-base metric pair types

## Correctness

PASS. The finite metric-extension criterion is exactly the triangle inequalities. Partitioning the old vertices into matched pairs gives four exhaustive local support classes whose counts are \((16,5,5,9)\), \((16,12,12,9)\), and \((16,12,12,2)\). Across different pairs, distance three forbids two separate value-one witnesses for the same new point and imposes no other restriction. The resulting exact product-support count proves all three formulas, their strict ordering, and the rational limiting laws. Direct independent enumeration of every unary profile and pair-distance feasibility through three old pairs confirms the numbers. The infinite formulas are not inferred from finite experiments.

## Originality

PASS, with historical-priority risk. Mubayi–Terry (arXiv:1502.01212, Section 7) constructs short-distance matchings as a source of globally many finite metric spaces but does not enumerate conditioned complete two-point types over this fixed base. The closest published one-type polynomial counts individual Katětov vectors without joint pair compatibility; an earlier equilateral-base two-point theorem has a different old metric hypothesis and different limiting fractions. Targeted published-finding corpus queries and searches for labelled matching-base Katětov extension matrices and rational limiting distance ratios did not identify a dominating claim. Historical literature or a nonindexed table could contain an equivalent specialized enumeration.

## Mathematical value

PASS. Short-distance matchings are a motivated extremal construction in odd-alphabet discrete-metric enumeration. Their fixed-base model-theoretic extension behavior reveals a two-witness separation mechanism, yields the exact distance-resolved pair spectrum for every matching size, and changes the asymptotic mutual-distance law from the equilateral-base reference value. The precise rational distribution and the \(313/144\) mean feasible-distance invariant encode information not contained in the one-point count alone.

## Limits

The result is restricted to complete logical types of two distinct new coordinates over the stated parameter matching. The limiting probability is type-uniform, not a spatial or global random-metric measure. A historical priority uncertainty remains, and no independent validation is claimed.
