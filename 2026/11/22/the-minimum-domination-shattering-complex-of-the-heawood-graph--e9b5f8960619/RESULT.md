# The minimum-domination shattering complex of the Heawood graph

## Finding
Let \(H\) be the Heawood graph, the 14-vertex point–line incidence graph of the Fano plane, and let \(\mathcal D\) consist of the characteristic functions of **all minimum-cardinality ordinary dominating sets** of \(H\). The complete shattered-support complex is the one-dimensional simplicial complex whose **edge graph is the complement** \(\overline H\): its nonempty faces are precisely the single vertices and the nonadjacent pairs of \(H\). Consequently its shattered-support polynomial is
\[
\Phi_{\mathcal D}(z)=1+14z+70z^2,
\]
and its Vapnik–Chervonenkis dimension is \(2\). The complex is connected and has integral homotopy type \(\bigvee^{57}S^1\). Under adaptive vertex-membership queries its Littlestone dimension is \(3\). Thus minimum dominating sets of this highly symmetric graph exhibit the strict separation \(\operatorname{VCdim}(\mathcal D)=2<3=\operatorname{Ldim}(\mathcal D)\).

## Assumptions and scope
The seven points and seven lines of the Fano plane are distinct types of vertices. A point is adjacent to a line precisely when the point lies on that line, and there are no edges between two points or between two lines. Every vertex of \(H\) has degree three. A subset \(D\subseteq V(H)\) is an **ordinary dominating set** if every vertex lies in \(D\) or is adjacent to some vertex of \(D\). The hypothesis class \(\mathcal D\) uses only sets of minimum possible cardinality, not all inclusion-minimal dominating sets, all maximal independent sets, minimum secure dominating sets or total dominating sets.

A vertex support \(S\subseteq V(H)\) is shattered if every labeling \(S\to\{0,1\}\) equals the vertex-membership labeling induced by at least one \(D\in\mathcal D\). The Littlestone dimension is the largest depth of a complete binary tree whose internal nodes are vertex-membership queries and whose every root-to-leaf binary answer sequence is realized by some \(D\in\mathcal D\). Repeated queries along a path are unnecessary and cannot improve a shattered tree. Homotopy is that of the geometric realization of the abstract simplicial complex on shattered supports. All claims concern this single finite graph.

## Proof
**Minimum dominating sets and their parametrization.** Any vertex of the cubic graph dominates at most itself and its three neighbors, so three vertices dominate at most twelve of the fourteen vertices. Hence the domination number is at least four. Let \(D\) be a dominating four-set, and write \(a\) for its number of selected points and \(b=4-a\) for its number of selected lines. The seven points can only be dominated by the \(a\) selected points and the three points on each selected line; dually for lines. Thus \(a+3b\ge7\) and \(b+3a\ge7\). The only possibility with \(a+b=4\) is \(a=b=2\).

Choose the two selected points \(p,q\), and denote by \(r\) the third point on their unique Fano line. The remaining five points must be covered by the two selected lines. Each line contains three points; their union has at most five points, exactly five if the lines are distinct, and thus both must avoid \(p,q\) and intersect at one of the remaining points. If their intersection \(s\) were not \(r\), then the distinct lines \(sp\) and \(sq\) would use two of the three lines through \(s\), leaving only one line through \(s\) that avoids both \(p,q\), contradicting the existence of two selected lines. Therefore \(s=r\), and the selected lines are uniquely the **two lines through \(r\) other than \(pq\)**. Conversely, for every unordered pair \(\{p,q\}\), this four-set dominates all seven points and all seven lines: the two chosen lines cover the five unselected points, and the two selected points lie on five distinct lines, covering all lines except precisely the other two lines through \(r\), which are themselves selected. Therefore it is dominating. There are exactly \(\binom72=21\) minimum dominating sets, one for each point pair; all of them are independent vertex sets.

**One- and two-query traces.** Each point vertex is present in exactly six concepts, indexed by its six possible partners. Every line vertex is also in six concepts: for each of its three points \(r\), two choices of a different line through \(r\) yield the pair \(\{p,q\}\) on that other line. Any two distinct point vertices occur together in exactly one concept. By incidence duality the same is true of two distinct line vertices. An incident point–line pair occurs together in no concept because each minimum set is independent. If \(p\) is not incident with line \(\ell\), then the three possible intersection points \(r\in\ell\) give exactly three concepts containing both: choose the second selected point \(q\) as the third point on the line \(pr\). The number of concepts containing the pair is therefore \(1\) for same-type vertices, \(3\) for nonincident opposite-type vertices, and \(0\) for incident opposite-type vertices.

In the first two cases, with pair-cooccurrence count \(t\in\{1,3\}\), the four Boolean traces occur with multiplicities \(t,6-t,6-t,21-12+t\), respectively. All four counts are positive. Incident pairs cannot realize the all-positive trace. Every singleton is shattered because it occurs in six but not all twenty-one concepts. Hence exactly the \(\binom{14}{2}-21=70\) nonedges of \(H\) are shattered pairs.

**Triple obstruction.** Any three vertices include two of the same type. That same-type pair belongs to exactly one concept, say \(D\). If the third vertex is contained in \(D\), the trace that is positive precisely on the first two vertices is impossible; otherwise the all-positive trace is impossible. Therefore no triple is shattered, and by downward closure no larger support is shattered. This proves the full polynomial and VC dimension. The complex is exactly the graph \(\overline H\) regarded as a one-dimensional CW complex. Because all seven point vertices form a clique in \(\overline H\), and every line vertex is adjacent in \(\overline H\) to every other line vertex, it is connected. With fourteen vertices and seventy edges, its cycle rank is \(70-14+1=57\), so it is homotopy equivalent over the integers to a wedge of fifty-seven circles.

**Online lower bound.** Label the seven points by the nonzero three-bit vectors \(1,\ldots,7\) (vertex indices \(0,\ldots,6\)) and the seven lines by the nonzero linear functionals \(1,\ldots,7\) (indices \(7,\ldots,13\)). A point \(x\) lies on functional-line \(h\) exactly when \(x\cdot h=0\) over \(\mathbb F_2\). Query point index \(0\) at the root, followed at both depth-one nodes by line index \(7\), which is nonincident with that point. The four answer branches \((0,0),(0,1),(1,0),(1,1)\) have, respectively, \(12,3,3,3\) surviving concepts. At their depth-two nodes query indices \(1,2,1,2\), respectively. In each of the four surviving families these third queries attain both answers (for the families of sizes \(12,3,3,3\), the positive-branch sizes are \(5,2,1,1\)). Hence every one of the eight depth-three leaves has a witness, yielding \(\operatorname{Ldim}\ge3\).

**Online upper bound.** Any first vertex query partitions the class of twenty-one concepts into a positive branch of exactly six and a negative branch of fifteen, by the vertex-incidence count above. A complete depth-four binary mistake tree would require at least eight distinct surviving concepts in **each** branch of its root (one per leaf in each depth-three subtree). Its six-element positive branch cannot support this, so \(\operatorname{Ldim}\le3\). The online dimension is exactly three.

## Verification
The accompanying standalone `verify_binary.py` builds the Fano plane from the seven nonzero binary vectors and nonzero dual vectors. It checks every possible four-set against all fourteen domination constraints, all \(2^{14}\) possible query supports against all twenty-one concepts, the exact edge/nonedge trace criterion, the explicit eight-leaf decision tree, and an exhaustive memoized adaptive-dimension recurrence. A distinct `verify_cyclic.cpp` implementation builds the incidence graph from the seven cyclic translates of the difference set \(\{0,1,3\}\) modulo seven, independently enumerates all four-sets and query supports, and independently computes the adaptive rank. Both outputs are included. The proof above does not rely on finite numerical extrapolation: all objects quantified are subsets of the stated fourteen-vertex graph.

Run in the directory containing the files:

```sh
python3 verify_binary.py
c++ -std=c++17 -O2 -Wall -Wextra verify_cyclic.cpp -o verify_cyclic
./verify_cyclic
```

## Relationship to prior work
Héger and Nagy study domination of projective-plane point–line incidence graphs, including the established \(2q\) domination size and general primal minimum structures; their central sharp structural theorem assumes projective-plane order at least five, so it does not state this fourteen-vertex shattering or online-learning result. Alipour and Jafari explicitly use the Heawood graph as a distinguished incidence example, but analyze a different \((2,2)\)-domination variant and bounds. Knor, Škrekovski and Tepeh study domination versus independent domination ratios of regular graphs, not membership traces. Earlier enumerations of twenty-one ordinary minimum dominating sets and studies of all maximal independent sets in the Heawood graph supply the hypothesis-space background; this result does not claim the twenty-one-set census as independently new.

The relevant comparison for originality is the **Boolean trace family**, not the cardinality of dominating sets. The full inclusion-maximal independent-set hypothesis class is strictly larger (thirty-seven concepts); shattering and online dimensions do not pass unchanged from a class to a subclass, so an existing classification for the larger class does not imply these formulas. Existing work on minimum secure domination and total domination of the Heawood graph uses distinct selection constraints and likewise does not state the complement-graph shattering complex or the three-level adaptive tree. These distinctions are based on definitions and inspected theorems, not merely absent search keywords.

## Limitations
This is an exact finite theorem for one highly symmetric projective-plane incidence graph. No result is claimed for projective planes of higher order, arbitrary cubic graphs, or every inclusion-minimal dominating set. The fact that twenty-one minimum dominating sets exist is background, not a novelty claim. Historical priority of the new shattering polynomial, topological identification and adaptive separation has not been independently certified; specialized or unindexed enumeration tables could duplicate some coefficients. A separate independent mathematical audit has not been performed.
