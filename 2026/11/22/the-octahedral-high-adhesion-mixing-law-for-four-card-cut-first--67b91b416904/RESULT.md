# The octahedral high-adhesion mixing law for four-card cut-first riffles

## Finding

For the **card-weighted** cut-first adhesive riffle on four labelled cards, let \(q=1-p\) be the probability that an internal packet contact breaks. Start from a fixed order and let \(d_{C,q}(m)\) be the total-variation distance from the uniform deck after \(m\) rounds. For every fixed \(t>0\), the full law after \(\lfloor t/q\rfloor\) rounds converges in total variation to the uniform-within-class lift of a six-state continuous-time law on cyclic orders, started at the initial cyclic class. Its generator is
\[
Q_C=\frac9{64}(A_{\rm oct}-4I),
\]
where the octahedral graph connects each cyclic order to every other order except its reversal. In particular, writing \(a_C=9/64\),
\[
\lim_{q\downarrow0}d_{C,q}(\lfloor t/q\rfloor)=\tfrac12 e^{-4a_Ct}+\tfrac13e^{-6a_Ct}.
\]
For \(0<\varepsilon<5/6\), if \(m_{C,q}(\varepsilon)\) is the first round at distance at most \(\varepsilon\), then \(q\,m_{C,q}(\varepsilon)\to s_\varepsilon/a_C\), where \(\tfrac12 e^{-4s_\varepsilon}+\tfrac13 e^{-6s_\varepsilon}=\varepsilon\) with \(s_\varepsilon>0\).

The block-weighted and midpoint cut-first rules are included only for comparison: their four-card generators have the same form with rates \(a_B=7/48\) and \(a_I=7/16\), by specializing the general rotation-coset theorem [2]. At the stated thresholds, the card-weighted mixing-time constant is \(28/27\) times the block-weighted constant and \(28/9\) times the midpoint constant.

## Assumptions and scope

Each round independently cuts at \(K\sim\mathrm{Bin}(4,1/2)\); an empty cut leaves the deck unchanged. In a nonempty cut the two internal packet gaps break independently with probability \(q\), yielding contiguous blocks with their internal order unchanged. Blocks are released from the two packet heads. In the card-weighted model, choose the next head with probability proportional to the number of remaining **cards** in its packet and update the counts after releasing its entire block. The comparison rules are block-weighted head selection (using remaining block counts) and midpoint release (sort by the key \((a+b-1)/(2m)\) for packet positions \(a,\ldots,b\) in a packet of length \(m\), breaking ties independently and fairly). These are the three cut-first definitions in [1], not its correlated-label inverse model. A cyclic class consists of the four rotations of an order, and the reversed order specifies the opposite class.

## Proof

**Rare moves for card-weighted release.** Let \(P(q)\) be the 24-state row-stochastic kernel. Each increment permutes positions independently of the labels, so every \(P(q)\) is doubly stochastic. The entries are polynomials of degree at most two, with \(P(q)=P_0+qR+O(q^2)\). At \(q=0\) the two packets are intact, and choosing the right packet first at cuts \(1,2,3\) gives each of the three nonidentity rotations with positive probability; empty cuts ensure positive holding. Thus \(P_0\) acts within each cyclic class, with its uniform law as the unique equilibrium and a strict spectral gap in modulus off equilibrium.

Only one-break events contribute to an off-class entry at order \(q\). Suppose a particular break splits a packet into \(X,Y\), and \(L\) is the intact other packet. Releasing \(X,Y,L\) or \(L,X,Y\) produces a rotation; the only other possibility, \(X,L,Y\), leaves the class. Its conditional card-weighted probability is
\[
\frac{|X|+|Y|}{4}\frac{|L|}{|L|+|Y|}.
\]
For either cut \(1,3\) the two breaks in the three-card packet give probabilities \(1/4,3/8\), and the cut has weight \(4/16\). For cut \(2\) each of the two breaks gives \(1/3\), with cut weight \(6/16\). A prescribed one-break event has coefficient one conditional on the cut, so the off-class coefficient is \(5/32+1/4+5/32=9/16\).

The new circular order cannot be the opposite: with a singleton packet the cyclic order of the three cards in the split packet is preserved, and with two cards in each packet, \(X,L,Y\) exchanges just the two split singletons in the original circular order. For the latter claim one may rotate the input to \(X,Y,L\); rotating the output gives \(Y,X,L\). Averaging over rotations gives a relabelling-equivariant quotient generator. The stabilizer of a cyclic class, acting by relabelling, is generated by its 4-cycle and is transitive on the four nonopposite neighbours. Indeed, identify cyclic orders with 4-cycles; the square of the given 4-cycle centralizes only two 4-cycles, the given one and its inverse. To see this, a 4-cycle commuting with the square conjugates that square to itself; the centralizer of the double transposition is the order-eight group preserving its two pairs, and its two elements of order four are this inverse pair. Therefore the other four cycles form a single orbit of length four. The four off-diagonal rates are equal, each \((9/16)/4=9/64\). Row sums determine the diagonal.

**Time rescaling.** Let \(\Pi\) be uniform averaging within each rotation class. Then \(P_0\Pi=\Pi P_0=\Pi\). For the law \(\mu_k\), put \(x_k=\mu_k\Pi\) and \(y_k=\mu_k(I-\Pi)\). Use an equivalent contraction norm on the complementary subspace, with \(\|P_0\|\le\rho<1\) there. Since the laws are bounded, the \(O(q)\) perturbation gives \(y_k=O(\rho^k+q)\) in norm. Also
\[
x_{k+1}=x_k+q x_k\Pi R\Pi+O(q^2+q\|y_k\|).
\]
The sum of the errors for \(k\le T/q\) is \(O_T(q)\); the discrete propagation of each error is bounded by \((1+O(q))^{T/q}\). Euler convergence then gives \(x_{\lfloor t/q\rfloor}\to(\mu_0\Pi)e^{t\Pi R\Pi}\), while \(y_{\lfloor t/q\rfloor}\to0\) for every \(t>0\). On class masses the exponential is precisely \(e^{tQ_C}\), and lifting preserves distance from uniform.

**Profile and thresholds.** Opposite-pair antisymmetric modes have generator eigenvalue \(-4a_C\), and opposite-pair symmetric modes of zero total mass have eigenvalue \(-6a_C\); the uniform mode has eigenvalue zero. With \(u=e^{-4a_Ct}\), \(v=e^{-6a_Ct}\), deviations from uniform starting at one class are \(u/2+v/3\) at the starting class, \(-u/2+v/3\) at its opposite, and \(-v/6\) at each other class. Since \(u\ge v\), only the starting class has positive deviation, giving the stated profile. This profile decreases strictly from \(5/6\) at the right limit of zero to zero at infinity. Since the finite-\(q\) distance is nonincreasing in the round number by stochastic contraction, evaluating at rescaled times just below and above the unique crossing proves the mixing-time limit. At \(t=0\) the initial rotation layer has not occurred: the full-deck distance is \(23/24\).

**Comparison with the other two cut-first release rules.** The general rotation-coset generator in [2] weights each one-break \(X,L,Y\) move by its cut probability times \(1/3\) for block weighting and three times that for midpoint release. At four cards there are two choices of a break at each nonempty cut, whose weights sum to \(14/16\). The preceding circular-order and symmetry argument yields rate \((2/3)(14/16)/4=7/48\) for block weighting and \(7/16\) for midpoint release. Applying the same spectrum gives the comparison constants. These two cases, including their threefold ratio, are specializations of [2], not a separate novelty claim.

`verification/check_four_card.py` enumerates the 24 states, cuts, gap masks, and block merges, checks the exact rational six-state generators for all three release rules, and checks stochasticity and support confinement at zero break probability. ## Relationship to prior work and limitations

Jiang [1] defines the three rules and proves fixed-deck \(\Theta(q^{-1})\) mixing-time bounds in the stated regime there (Theorem 2.5; see also Theorem 10.1 for the high-adhesion comparison). The sharp result here is the exact **card-weighted** quotient law, which [2] excludes. The block-weighted and midpoint formulas above identify the four-card comparison values explicitly, without a priority claim between the four-card and all-fixed-size treatments. The result does not address simultaneous growth of deck size and adhesion, the correlated-label model, rescaled time zero, or mixing thresholds \(\varepsilon\ge5/6\). The literature comparison is not a guarantee against earlier unindexed work.
