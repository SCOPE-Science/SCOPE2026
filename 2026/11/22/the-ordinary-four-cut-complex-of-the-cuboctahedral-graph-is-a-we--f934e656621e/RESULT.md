# The ordinary four-cut complex of the cuboctahedral graph is a wedge of sixty-three seven-spheres

## Finding
Let \(Q_3\) be the three-dimensional cube graph and \(C=L(Q_3)\) its twelve-vertex line graph, or the cuboctahedral graph. Let \(\Delta_4(C)\) be the **ordinary** four-cut complex, generated by complements of disconnected induced four-vertex sets. Then its combinatorial Alexander dual and the original cut complex have the integral homotopy types
\[
\boxed{\Delta_4(C)^\vee\simeq\bigvee^{63}S^2,
\qquad\Delta_4(C)\simeq\bigvee^{63}S^7.}
\]
In particular, the only nonzero reduced integral homology of \(\Delta_4(C)\) is \(\widetilde H_7\cong\mathbb Z^{63}\). The complex is pure of dimension seven. This establishes the previously unclassified middle threshold between the published three-cut and five-cut classifications for the same graph. No shellability assertion is made.

## Assumptions and scope
All graphs are finite, simple and undirected. Label the cube vertices by binary integers \(0,\ldots,7\) and join vertices differing in one bit. Lexicographically order the twelve cube edges \((a,b)\), \(a<b\); these, in that order, are vertices \(0,\ldots,11\) of \(C\). Two vertices of \(C\) are adjacent precisely when the corresponding cube edges share an endpoint. Thus \(C\) is four-regular with 24 edges.

Let \(V=\{0,\ldots,11\}\). For every four-element set \(T\subset V\), declare \(T\) *disconnected* when the induced graph \(C[T]\) has at least two components. By definition, \(\Delta_4(C)\) has facets \(V\setminus T\) over all such \(T\), each with eight vertices. Its combinatorial Alexander dual \(D=\Delta_4(C)^\vee\) consists of exactly the vertex sets containing no disconnected four-subset. This uses disconnected, not independent, four-sets: the latter would define a different, total cut complex. All homotopy equivalences are non-equivariant integral homotopy equivalences.

## Proof
**1. Complete face enumeration.** Exact enumeration of all \(\binom{12}{4}=495\) four-sets by induced-graph connectivity gives 369 disconnected and 126 connected sets. Therefore \(K=\Delta_4(C)\) has exactly 369 distinct eight-vertex facets. Its face vector in dimensions zero through seven is
\[
(12,66,220,495,792,924,768,369).
\]
Its dual \(D\) has face vector in dimensions zero through four
\[
(12,66,220,126,24),
\]
and no faces of greater dimension. Both vectors come from full inclusion tests on all \(2^{12}\) vertex subsets, as reproduced by the attached deterministic verifier. In particular, \(D\) contains the entire two-skeleton of the eleven-simplex, while \(K\) contains the entire five-skeleton but misses 24 of the 792 seven-vertex subsets. Its top dimension is exactly seven.

**2. Verified elementary collapses of the dual.** Represent each vertex subset \(S\subseteq V\) by the nonnegative integer \(\sum_{i\in S}2^i\). The standalone file `verification/collapse_pairs.tsv` lists 132 ordered pairs \((a,b)\), meaning the simplex with vertex mask \(a\) is a free codimension-one face of the simplex with mask \(b\). Beginning with every face of \(D\), delete the listed pairs consecutively. The verifier re-creates \(D\) from scratch and checks, at each stage, that \(a\) belongs to the current complex, that \(b\) is its **only proper coface of any dimension**, and that \(b\) has one more vertex than \(a\). Consequently these are genuine elementary simplicial collapses, not merely acyclic matching proposals. They leave a two-dimensional subcomplex \(D'\) with face vector
\[
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(12,60,112).
\]
Thus \(D\simeq D'\). Since \(D\) originally contains the complete two-skeleton of a simplex, it is simply connected; elementary collapses preserve this property. The reduced Euler characteristic of \(D'\) is \(12-60+112-1=63\). Any simply connected two-dimensional CW complex has free second homology, and here \(H_2(D';\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{63}\), with all other reduced homology zero. Selecting cellular sphere maps representing a basis and applying the simply connected homology version of Whitehead's theorem gives \(D'\simeq\bigvee^{63}S^2\).

**3. Integral Alexander duality and original homotopy.** On twelve vertices, combinatorial Alexander duality gives
\[
\widetilde H_i(K;\mathbb Z)\cong\widetilde H^{9-i}(D;\mathbb Z).
\]
Since \(D\simeq\bigvee^{63}S^2\), only \(i=7\) survives, with rank 63 and no torsion. The complete five-skeleton of the simplex in \(K\) makes \(K\) at least four-connected. Hurewicz, first in degree five and then in degree six, together with the vanishing integral homology in both degrees, proves that \(K\) is six-connected. Finally, \(K\) is seven-dimensional with \(H_7(K;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{63}\). A wedge of 63 seven-spheres mapping by an integral basis of \(\pi_7(K)\cong H_7(K;\mathbb Z)\) is a homology equivalence between simply connected CW complexes and therefore a homotopy equivalence. This proves the stated homotopy type rather than just a Betti-number count.

## Verification
The standard-library Python program `verification/check_fourcut.py`, the explicit 132-line collapse certificate `verification/collapse_pairs.tsv`, and the matching actual execution transcript `verification/check_output.txt` are included. Run `python3 verification/check_fourcut.py` from this package's root; the program resolves the certificate relative to its own path. It reconstructs the cube and its line graph; tests every one of the 495 four-subsets; verifies all 449 faces of the dual and all 3,647 faces of the original including the empty face; and replays all 132 elementary collapses with a full unique-coface test. Its separate mod-two boundary elimination computes ranks 306 and 462 for the boundary maps from dimensions seven and six of \(K\), and ranks 49 and 11 for the residual dual maps from dimensions two and one. These independently yield Betti rank 63 in the top degree and no mod-two lower-degree homology. The integral conclusion, however, follows from the exact collapses and homotopy argument, not from mod-two ranks alone.

## Relationship to prior work
Bayer, Denker, Jelić Milutinović, Rowlands, Sundaram and Xue [1] defined ordinary cut complexes and studied several graph families. Their Theorem 6.1 requires the complete next-to-top skeleton for its particular face-lattice shortcut, but here 24 seven-vertex faces are absent, so that shortcut does not classify this case. Their other explicit families are not the twelve-vertex cuboctahedral line graph. Bayer and collaborators [2] address disjoint unions, squared paths and rectangular grid graphs, likewise without the cuboctahedral line graph.

The published cuboctahedral three-cut result [3] gives \(\Delta_3(C)\simeq S^7\vee\bigvee^6S^8\); the published five-cut result [4] gives \(\Delta_5(C)\simeq\bigvee^{12}S^5\vee\bigvee^{118}S^6\). Their facet definitions, dimensions and homology statements do not determine the intermediate four-cut complex. The present theorem closes this natural three-stage cut-threshold gap and reveals a top-dimensional sphere wedge between two mixed-dimensional sphere wedges. Published results for the cube \(Q_3\), its line graph's matching complex and various Rips/Čech complexes concern different graph constructions or distinct simplices and do not imply this classification.

## Limitations
The theorem concerns only the ordinary four-cut complex of this twelve-vertex graph; no classification for larger line graphs, no equivariant symmetry representation, and no shellability claim is established. The finite certificate is exhaustive for the stated finite graph and does not prove any infinite family by extrapolation. Prior-work searches and available full-text inspections do not rule out unindexed mathematically equivalent work. Independent external review has not been performed.
