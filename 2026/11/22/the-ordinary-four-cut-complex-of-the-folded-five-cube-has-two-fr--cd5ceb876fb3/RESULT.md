# The ordinary four-cut complex of the folded five-cube has two free homology degrees

## Finding

Let \(C\) be the five-regular, sixteen-vertex Clebsch graph (the folded five-cube). Its **ordinary** four-cut complex \(\Delta_4(C)\) has exactly \(1140\) facets, all of dimension \(11\). The combinatorial Alexander dual has the integral homotopy type
\[
\Delta_4(C)^\vee\simeq S^4\vee\bigvee^{34}S^3.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\widetilde H_j(\Delta_4(C);\mathbb Z)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&j=9,\\
\mathbb Z^{34},&j=10,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
There is no integral torsion. In particular, \(\Delta_4(C)\) is pure but not shellable, because its integral homology below its facet dimension does not vanish.

## Assumptions and scope

The graph \(C\) is defined without relying on graph-library conventions: take the \(16\) even-parity binary strings of length five, and join two strings if they differ in exactly four positions. The resulting graph is strongly regular with parameters \((16,5,0,2)\). Labels \(0,\ldots,15\) enumerate the even-parity strings in increasing binary-value order. This selects the **five-regular** graph, not its ten-regular complement.

For a graph \(G=(V,E)\), its ordinary \(k\)-cut complex is the simplicial complex generated by the sets \(V\setminus Q\), where \(|Q|=k\) and the induced graph \(G[Q]\) is disconnected. For a complex \(L\) on the ground set \(V\), define its combinatorial Alexander dual by \(L^\vee=\{\sigma\subseteq V:V\setminus\sigma\notin L\}\). All homology and cohomology are reduced and integral, unless explicitly stated otherwise. The statement concerns this single graph and cutoff, not a graph-family classification.

## Proof

**1. Identify the dual without interchanging connected and disconnected sets.** Write \(K=\Delta_4(C)^\vee\). By the definition of the cut complex, \(V\setminus\sigma\) is a face of \(\Delta_4(C)\) exactly when \(\sigma\) contains a disconnected induced four-vertex subset. Thus \(K\) consists of those vertex sets **none of whose four-subsets induces a disconnected graph**. This makes every subset of size at most three a face, whereas a four-subset is a face precisely when it induces a connected graph.

**2. Enumerate the complete, finite chain groups.** The attached certificate enumerates all \(\binom{16}{4}=1820\) induced four-vertex graphs using two separate connectivity algorithms. Exactly \(680\) are connected and \(1140\) disconnected. Exactly \(192\) five-subsets have every four-subset connected, and no six-subset does. Therefore \(K\) has the full two-skeleton of the fifteen-simplex, with face numbers \((16,120,560,680,192)\) in dimensions zero through four. Its reduced Euler characteristic is \(-33\). The \(192\) five-subsets are induced pentagons; each has five graph edges. Because no six-subset is a face, this enumeration specifies the entire complex, not just a truncated skeleton.

**3. Prove integral surjectivity of the third differential.** Fix vertex \(0\). In the complete two-skeleton, the cycles \(\partial[0,a,b,c]\) for \(1\le a<b<c\le15\) form an integral basis of the two-cycle lattice, which has rank \(\binom{15}{3}=455\). Coordinates of a tetrahedron boundary in this basis are simply its coefficients on triangles not containing vertex \(0\).

Select \(455\) connected tetrahedra as columns, using the exact bounded construction in `verify_clebsch_cut4.py`. The first \(170\) contain vertex \(0\) and give distinct coordinate-unit vectors. Among the other \(285\) coordinates, \(140\) admit connected tetrahedron columns supported at just one of these \(285\) coordinates; choose one per coordinate. Among the last \(145\), each admits a connected tetrahedron column supported at just one of those \(145\); again choose one per coordinate. Ordering rows and columns by these three rounds produces a triangular integral \(455\)-by-\(455\) boundary submatrix with each diagonal entry \(\pm1\), checked from the actual oriented simplex boundaries. Its determinant is \(\pm1\). Thus \(\partial_3\) maps **onto the entire integral two-cycle lattice**, not merely onto a finite-index subgroup. It follows that \(\widetilde H_2(K;\mathbb Z)=0\), while the rank of the three-cycle lattice is \(680-455=225\).

**4. Determine the fourth differential integrally.** Each of the \(192\) oriented four-simplices is a five-vertex face. Precisely \(480\) distinct tetrahedra occur on their boundaries, each on exactly two, while the other \(200\) connected tetrahedra occur on none. Form the graph whose \(192\) vertices are five-vertex faces and whose \(480\) edges represent shared tetrahedra. Direct enumeration verifies that it is connected. The same enumeration propagates orientations consistently, so the two incidence signs on every shared tetrahedron are opposite after multiplying each four-simplex by its propagated sign. Hence the fourth cellular differential is the transpose of the oriented incidence matrix of a connected graph, with \(200\) additional zero rows. A spanning tree gives a unit \(191\)-by-\(191\) minor and its Smith invariants are all one, with rank \(191\). Therefore its image is primitive and its kernel is \(\mathbb Z\). The two potentially nonzero reduced homology groups of \(K\) are precisely \(\widetilde H_3(K;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z^{225-191}=\mathbb Z^{34}\) and \(\widetilde H_4(K;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z\).

**5. Upgrade to homotopy and transfer to the original complex.** The full two-skeleton makes \(K\) simply connected. The vanishing of its second integral homology therefore makes it two-connected by Hurewicz. Its three-skeleton is consequently homotopy equivalent to \(\bigvee^{225}S^3\). Under Hurewicz, attaching maps of the \(192\) four-cells correspond to the integral \(225\)-by-\(192\) fourth boundary matrix. Its primitive rank-\(191\) image allows integral change-of-basis operations, realized by homotopy equivalences of wedges of three-spheres, to cancel \(191\) three/four-cell pairs. The remaining one four-cell attaches nullhomotopically. Thus \(K\simeq \bigvee^{34}S^3\vee S^4\). Integral combinatorial Alexander duality on the sixteen-vertex ground set yields \(\widetilde H_i(\Delta_4(C);\mathbb Z)\cong\widetilde H^{13-i}(K;\mathbb Z)\), giving the displayed groups in degrees nine and ten. Every facet of the cut complex is the complement of a disconnected four-subset, and hence has twelve vertices; nonzero homology in degree nine or ten obstructs shellability of this pure eleven-dimensional complex.

The verifier reconstructs the five-bit graph, checks strongly regular parameters, enumerates every four-, five-, and six-subset, independently recomputes four-subset connectivity, creates and verifies all \(455\) oriented third-boundary columns and their triangular unit pivots, and verifies all \(480\) fourth-boundary incidences together with graph connectivity and coherent orientations. The verification is an exhaustive finite **proof certificate**, not statistical evidence or an extrapolation from bounded graph sizes.

## Relationship to prior work

Bayer et al. introduced the ordinary cut complex and developed the general Alexander-duality framework and several graph-family calculations in their public April 26, 2023 preprint, `arxiv:2304.13675`. Their 2024 sequel, `arxiv:2407.08158`, studies disjoint unions, squared paths, and grids. Neither inspected text states this graph's ordinary four-cut homology or the present dual homotopy type. Other published records distinguish the Clebsch **total** four-cut complex (whose facets instead correspond to *independent* four-subsets), the Clebsch ordinary **three**-cut complex (which has two-torsion), and the ordinary four-cut complex of the **unfolded** four-cube. None gives this result by implication without an additional bridge theorem. This calculation sharpens the contrast with the earlier torsion-bearing Clebsch cut topology, while making no assertion about maps between cut parameters. The standard folded-cube model is documented in MathWorld and DistanceRegular.org.

## Limitations

This is a finite exact classification for one named graph and one cut parameter. The homotopy equivalence is proved for the **Alexander dual**, while the statement for the original ordinary cut complex is its integral homology and nonshellability; no homotopy wedge claim for the original complex is made. The computation does not determine symmetry characters, persistence maps, or a classification of all folded hypercubes. Originality remains subject to potential unindexed or inaccessible equivalent sources. No independent external validation is asserted.
