# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The line measure reduces exactly to a uniform unsigned offset; subtracting the two disk chords gives a piecewise-monotone total-length function. Both inverse branches are algebraically explicit. Dominated convergence proves all subcritical moments, and the grazing offset change of variables yields an integrable majorant for every fixed supercritical order. The borderline second moment reduces to complete elliptic integrals; their displayed expansions reproduce the logarithmic coefficient and finite constant. A standalone verifier reproduced the finite-radius CDF and the moment/crossover checks. Numerical tests do not stand in for proof.

## Originality — PASS, provisional
The closest inspected primary texts concern motion-invariant chords of convex polygons and finite random segment intersections with convex bodies. Neither treats the **total sum of material intercepts** across two disconnected components of a nonconvex annulus, nor establishes the thin-shell moment phase transition, marked grazing layer, or explicit coefficients. Searches for annular line-length distributions and the published finding database produced related but non-covering results. General integral-geometric normalization is previous work and is not claimed as new. An inaccessible 2011 nonconvex-polygon seminar paper and possible specialist shell-transport references remain residual historical risks; priority is not independently certified.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Thin annuli are natural test objects for line-intercept stereology. The complete elementary law exposes a genuine singular limiting-statistics phenomenon: an ordinary finite-mean weak limit has infinite second moment, a sharp critical moment order, a computable logarithmic variance divergence and nontrivial rare-grazing crossover with explicit conditional geometry. These jointly explain why replacing finite-thickness line paths by local-thickness geometry fails for higher moments. The claim is not just a reparametrization or an isolated numerical constant.

## Sources and scope
The primary dated starting source is Bäsel, *Random chords and point distances in regular polygons* (arXiv:1204.2707, 12 April 2012). Related literature: Harutyunyan and Ohanyan (2009) on regular-polygon chords; Aharonyan, Harutyunyan and Ohanyan (2010) on finite random segments through convex domains; Santaló (1976) on classical integral geometry. These are comparative context, not asserted to prove the present annulus result. Only planar concentric annuli under the specified invariant line law are covered.
