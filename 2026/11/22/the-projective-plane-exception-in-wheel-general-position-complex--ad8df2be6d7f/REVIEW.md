## Originality: PASS against inspected evidence
The closest mathematically controlling work, Deshpande and Singh (arXiv:2001.05448, Theorem 4.6), already determines the higher-independence complex of cycles, and this is expressly credited as prior rather than claimed. Its assertion does not itself determine the wheel complex: the center cone is an additional topological attachment whose effect must be proved and is nontrivial for the pentagonal wheel. Yao–He–Ji–Li (arXiv:2002.03108, Section 4) characterizes maximum general-position cardinalities and remarks on selected induced rim paths, without a homotopy result. Iršič–Klavžar–Rus–Tuite (arXiv:2401.05696) treats counting polynomials without this identification. Indexed comparisons to wheel transversals, unrelated wheel matching complexes, geodesic general-position complexes of coronas, and familiar projective-plane triangulations do not imply the claimed complete wheel classification. The possible existence of an unindexed equivalent calculation remains a residual bibliographic risk, not evidence of uniqueness.

## Value: PASS
This is a complete topology classification of a natural and well-established graph-geodesic family, not an arbitrary finite instance. It detects a sharp, small exceptional projective-plane torsion phenomenon that neither the general-position number nor the cyclic higher-independence homotopy by itself describes. The center attachment becomes trivial precisely after the first nontrivial small cases, and thereafter a single extra two-sphere interacts with an explicit four-periodic cycle family, yielding mixed-dimensional sphere wedges for infinitely many sizes. The exceptional real projective plane makes the low-order boundary qualitatively significant.
