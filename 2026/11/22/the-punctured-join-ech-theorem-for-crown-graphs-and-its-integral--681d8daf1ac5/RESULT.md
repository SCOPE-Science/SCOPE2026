# The punctured-join Čech theorem for crown graphs and its integral symmetry module

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge3\), let \(Q_n=K_{n,n}\setminus M_n\) be the crown graph obtained by removing a perfect matching from the complete bipartite graph. Let \(\mathcal N(Q_n,r)\) denote the intrinsic vertex-centered Čech nerve for closed radius-\(r/2\) balls in the unit-edge geometric realization of \(Q_n\). Then
\[
\left|\mathcal N(Q_n,3)\right|\simeq\bigvee^{n-1}S^{2n-4},\qquad
\left|\mathcal N(Q_n,4)\right|\cong S^{2n-2},\qquad
\left|\mathcal N(Q_n,5)\right|\simeq\{*\}.
\]
The middle equivalence is an identification with a simplicial boundary, not merely a homotopy equivalence. The same descriptions hold throughout the respective half-open real parameter intervals \([3,4)\), \([4,5)\), and \([5,\infty)\).

The complete reduced integral homology of the scale-three complex is concentrated in degree \(2n-4\), where it is free of rank \(n-1\). More precisely, the natural group \(S_n\times C_2\) simultaneously permutes the matched-pair indices and exchanges the two shores. As an **integral** module,
\[
\widetilde H_{2n-4}(\mathcal N(Q_n,3);\mathbb Z)
\cong\left(\mathbb Z^n/\mathbb Z(1,\ldots,1)\right)\otimes\chi_n,
\]
where \(S_n\) permutes coordinate classes and \(\chi_n\) is trivial on \(S_n\) and takes the shore-exchange involution to \((-1)^{n-1}\). Thus if \(f(\pi)\) denotes the number of fixed indices of \(\pi\in S_n\), the traces on top homology are \(f(\pi)-1\) for \(\pi\) and \((-1)^{n-1}(f(\pi)-1)\) for its composition with shore exchange. The integral module is the **quotient permutation lattice**, not the generally non-isomorphic augmentation sublattice. No equivariant homotopy wedge decomposition is claimed.

## Assumptions and scope
Write the shores \(A=\{a_1,\ldots,a_n\}\) and \(B=\{b_1,\ldots,b_n\}\), with edges precisely \(a_i b_j\) when \(i\ne j\). Each graph edge has geometric length one. A simplex of \(\mathcal N(Q_n,r)\) is a finite set of graph vertices whose closed radius-\(r/2\) metric balls have nonempty intersection in the **geometric realization** of the graph. In particular, this is not the Čech nerve of an arbitrary Euclidean embedding or merely a nerve in the discrete vertex metric. All homology groups below use \(\mathbb Z\) unless explicitly indicated.

The graph Čech generator formula in [1, Lemma 2.2] says that at even integer parameter \(2s\), maximal faces are generated by closed distance-\(s\) vertex balls; at odd parameter \(2s+1\), they are generated by unions of closed distance-\(s\) vertex balls at the two ends of each edge. It also shows the nerve is constant between consecutive integers. The proof below applies this formula for \(s=1,2\).

## Proof
**1. Exact scale-three facets.** The closed distance-one neighborhoods are
\[
N_1[a_i]=\{a_i\}\cup(B\setminus\{b_i\}),\qquad
N_1[b_j]=\{b_j\}\cup(A\setminus\{a_j\}).
\]
For an edge \(a_i b_j\), necessarily \(i\ne j\), their union is the complementary vertex set \(V(Q_n)\setminus\{a_j,b_i\}\). Relabeling indices, the scale-three facets are **exactly**
\[
F_{i,j}=V(Q_n)\setminus\{a_i,b_j\}\qquad(i\ne j).
\]
No additional facets arise because [1, Lemma 2.2] exhausts all edge-centered witnesses.

**2. A sphere with precisely \(n\) top cells omitted.** Put
\[
J=\partial\Delta(A)*\partial\Delta(B)\cong S^{2n-3}.
\]
Its top simplices are \(F_{i,j}\) for all ordered \((i,j)\), including the diagonal. The scale-three complex \(K=\mathcal N(Q_n,3)\) is the subcomplex generated by the off-diagonal top simplices. For every \(i\), each codimension-one face of the omitted \(F_{i,i}\) is still in \(K\): the complement of such a face has three vertices, either \(\{a_i,b_i,a_j\}\) or \(\{a_i,b_i,b_j\}\) for some \(j\ne i\); this contains an off-diagonal pair \(a_j,b_i\) or \(a_i,b_j\), respectively. Hence the face lies in an available off-diagonal top simplex. All proper faces of \(F_{i,i}\) are therefore in \(K\). Consequently, \(J\) is obtained from \(K\) by attaching exactly \(n\) top-dimensional simplices, of dimension \(d=2n-3\), along their entire boundaries.

**3. Integral homology and homotopy.** The relative cellular chain complex \(C_*(J,K;\mathbb Z)\) is \(\mathbb Z^n\) in degree \(d\) and zero elsewhere. Orient the top simplices coherently with the oriented fundamental class of \(J\). Its image in \(H_d(J,K;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^n\) is the primitive diagonal element \((1,\ldots,1)\). The long exact sequence of the pair gives
\[
0\longrightarrow\mathbb Z\xrightarrow{\,1\mapsto(1,\ldots,1)\,}\mathbb Z^n\longrightarrow\widetilde H_{d-1}(K;\mathbb Z)\longrightarrow0,
\]
with all other reduced homology groups of \(K\) zero. In particular, \(H_d(K)=0\) because the displayed first map is injective.

Because attaching \(d\)-cells does not change homotopy groups below degree \(d-1\), and \(J\cong S^d\), the complex \(K\) is \((d-2)\)-connected. Here \(d\ge3\), so \(K\) is simply connected. By the Hurewicz theorem, a basis for its free group \(H_{d-1}(K)\cong\mathbb Z^{n-1}\) is realized by maps from \((d-1)\)-spheres. Wedge these maps to obtain a map \(\bigvee^{n-1}S^{d-1}\to K\) inducing isomorphisms on all integral homology groups. Both spaces are simply connected CW complexes, so the homological Whitehead theorem makes this map a homotopy equivalence. Thus the claimed wedge decomposition is an actual **integral homotopy classification**, not an inference solely from finite-field Betti numbers.

**4. The integral symmetry action.** The permutation \(\pi\in S_n\) acts on each of the two factors \(\partial\Delta(A)\) and \(\partial\Delta(B)\) with degree \(\operatorname{sgn}(\pi)\), hence acts on \(H_d(J;\mathbb Z)\) with degree \(\operatorname{sgn}(\pi)^2=1\). Shore exchange interchanges the two join factors of dimension \(n-2\), so its degree on their join is \((-1)^{(n-1)^2}=(-1)^{n-1}\). Choose the relative top-cell basis so that the image of the oriented fundamental cycle is \((1,\ldots,1)\). Naturality and coherent orientations then give the same global sign times the ordinary permutation of the \(n\) diagonal omitted cells. The exact sequence in step 3 is equivariant, yielding the asserted quotient permutation lattice with precisely that shore-exchange twist. Taking traces gives the formulas in the finding.

**5. The following two scales.** Every two vertices on the same shore have distance two, whereas matched opposite vertices \(a_i,b_i\) have distance three. Therefore
\[
N_2[a_i]=V(Q_n)\setminus\{b_i\},\qquad N_2[b_i]=V(Q_n)\setminus\{a_i\}.
\]
The scale-four generator formula produces exactly all codimension-one faces of the full \((2n-1)\)-simplex, hence \(\mathcal N(Q_n,4)=\partial\Delta(V(Q_n))\cong S^{2n-2}\). For any edge \(a_i b_j\), the two distance-two neighborhoods together contain every vertex. The odd-scale formula therefore makes \(\mathcal N(Q_n,5)\) the full simplex, which remains so at larger scales.

## Verification
The standalone `verify_crown_scale3.py` requires Python 3 and no external packages. It constructs the crown graph explicitly, independently computes all-pairs shortest-path distances, forms odd/even intrinsic Čech generators, verifies the off-diagonal-facet and subsequent full-boundary/full-simplex identities, and exhaustively computes mod-two reduced simplicial homology for \(n=3,4,5,6\). It also computes simplicial Lefschetz numbers under all permutations for \(n=3,4\) and sampled permutations for \(n=5,6\), with and without shore exchange. The code returned `ALL_FINITE_CHECKS_PASS`. The tested scale-three reduced ranks in degrees \(2,4,6,8\) were respectively \(2,3,4,5\), with zero other reduced ranks. The all-order integral and symmetry assertions come from the proof, not extrapolation from these tests.

## Relationship to prior work
Adams, Shukla and Singh [1] prove the graph-geometric Čech facet description and investigate hypercube graphs; their result supplies the definitions here but not this crown-graph calculation. Choi, Kim and Park [2] study non-cover complexes of graphs and general collapsibility bounds. In fact the present scale-three complex is **also** the non-cover complex of \(Q_n\): its maximal faces are complements of graph edges. The general bounds in [2] do not determine this crown family's exact homotopy type or integral symmetry module. These are substantive equivalent-formulation comparisons, not merely title searches.

The published crown-graph closed-neighborhood classification [3] concerns scale **two**, with \(\bigvee^{n^2-3n+1}S^2\vee S^{n-2}\vee S^{n-2}\) for \(n\ge4\); its full proof was inspected. It does not determine the new scale-three wedge, the transition to the scale-four boundary, or the present quotient permutation module. The complete-multipartite graph Čech classification [4], likewise inspected in full, proves that \(\mathcal N(K_{n,n},3)\) is contractible. The perfect-matching deletion changes the facets, and this covering theorem therefore cannot be applied to \(Q_n\). Searches of relevant published statements and the exact non-cover reformulation did not find an implication covering the entire claim, but incomplete indexing and other unexamined literature remain risks.

## Limitations
The homotopy and integral symmetry statements require \(n\ge3\). They are for the graph-geometric intrinsic Čech nerve, not a Euclidean Čech complex. The wedge equivalence is nonequivariant; the module classification is equivariant on homology only. No closed-form description of the scale-two-to-three inclusion map is asserted. Finite tests cannot replace the all-order proof; database searches cannot prove absolute novelty, and no independent mathematical audit is claimed.
