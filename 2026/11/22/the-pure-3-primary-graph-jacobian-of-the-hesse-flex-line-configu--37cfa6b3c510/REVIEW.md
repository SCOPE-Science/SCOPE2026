# Scientific review: the Hesse flex–line graph Jacobian

**Correctness — PASS.** The graph is the classical 9-point/12-line affine-plane incidence, with an independently checked complete list of twelve three-point lines and 36 incidences. Its integral incidence Gram identity gives the 21-vertex Laplacian characteristic polynomial and \(3^{18}\) tree count. A dependency-free exact checker gives the full reduced-Laplacian Smith valuations \(0^{12},1,2^4,3^3\) at six different sinks and independently evaluates each determinant over the integers. This is sufficient to reconstruct the claimed critical group. A rational-component nodal curve with the specified graph is stable and genus 16; its Néron interpretation requires the stated regular split thickness-one smoothing.

**Originality — PASS, with historical priority risk.** The classical Hesse incidence appears in Artebani–Dolgachev (2006). Chandler–Sin–Xiang (2003) prove general results about the different rectangular point–line incidence matrix; those results do not state the graph-Laplacian critical group. Busonero–Melo–Stoppino (2008) give the general stable-curve critical-group framework without this specific numerical group. Targeted exact-object, alias, and broader-result comparisons did not locate the particular invariant-factor decomposition. This does not exclude older unpublished or hard-to-index graph calculations. Previously recorded distinct Hesse divisor, Hesse-pencil boundary, and critical-group computations do not state the present 21-vertex/36-edge graph's Smith form.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The nine-flex/twelve-line Hesse configuration is a canonical geometric incidence object. Its graph has a nonobvious entirely 3-primary Jacobian with exponent 27 and eight nonunit invariant factors, rather than an order alone; this integral torsion refines the classical rational spectrum. The precise group gives a complete component-group invariant for an explicitly realizable genus-16 stable-curve boundary type. This is a narrow exact invariant, not a claimed general theorem or large moduli classification.

**Limitations and risks.** Historical priority is not exhaustive, and an inaccessible or unindexed design-theoretic Smith computation could cover the result. The stable curve is auxiliary. Independent validation has not occurred.
