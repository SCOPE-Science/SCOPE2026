# The rational weight-four Weierstrass point on the symmetric Fermat quotient of degree eleven

## Finding

Let \(F_{11}:X^{11}+Y^{11}+Z^{11}=0\) be the smooth Fermat curve over \(\mathbb Q\), let \(D_{11}=F_{11}/S_3\) be the smooth projective quotient by coordinate permutations, and let \(Q\) be the image of \(P=(1:-1:0)\). The canonical vanishing sequence of the genus-five curve \(D_{11}\) at \(Q\) is
\[
(0,1,2,5,6).
\]
Consequently, its gap sequence, Weierstrass weight and Weierstrass pole semigroup are
\[
G(Q)=(1,2,3,6,7),\qquad w(Q)=4,\qquad H(Q)=\langle4,5,11\rangle.
\]
The point \(Q\) is \(\mathbb Q\)-rational and the non-gap \(4\) gives a degree-four morphism \(D_{11}\to\mathbb P^1\) over \(\mathbb Q\) with totally ramified fiber at \(Q\). The claim is about this distinguished point, not a census of all Weierstrass points.

## Assumptions and scope

Work in characteristic zero. The quotient denotes the unique smooth projective curve with function field \(\mathbb Q(F_{11})^{S_3}\). A Weierstrass gap at a point is a positive integer \(m\) not realizable as the pole order of a rational function with no other poles; the canonical vanishing orders of a genus-\(g\) curve are \(m_i-1\) for the \(g\) gaps \(m_i\). A pole semigroup includes \(0\).

The three points \((1:-1:0)\) and its coordinate permutations form one \(S_3\)-orbit and are defined over \(\mathbb Q\); hence \(Q\) is a rational point. The stabilizer of \(P\) has order \(2\), generated by swapping \(X\) and \(Y\), so the quotient map \(\pi:F_{11}\to D_{11}\) has tame local ramification index \(2\) at \(P\).

The Fermat curve has genus \(45\). Each transposition fixes eleven points with its two swapped coordinates equal, together with the projective point on its negative eigenspace; each three-cycle fixes the two nontrivial eigenpoints. Thus the ramification degree in the degree-six quotient is \(3\cdot12+2\cdot2=40\). Riemann–Hurwitz gives \(88=6(2g(D_{11})-2)+40\), so \(g(D_{11})=5\).

## Proof

Write \(e_1=X+Y+Z\), \(e_2=XY+YZ+ZX\), \(e_3=XYZ\), and \(\Delta=(X-Y)(X-Z)(Y-Z)\). The adjunction description of holomorphic differentials of the plane curve uses degree-eight numerator forms in \(\operatorname{Res}(A\Omega/F)\). The ambient form \(\Omega\) transforms by the sign of a coordinate permutation, so an invariant differential has an alternating numerator. In characteristic zero this numerator is divisible by \(\Delta\), and the quotient is symmetric of degree five. This invariant adjunction description also agrees with the Schur-differential basis in [1, §§2.1–2.2 and §4]. A basis of the symmetric polynomial space is
\[
 e_1^5,\quad e_1^3e_2,\quad e_1e_2^2,\quad e_1^2e_3,\quad e_2e_3.
\]
The five-dimensional invariant differential space is exactly the pullback of \(H^0(D_{11},\Omega^1)\), since pullback is injective and this genus is five.

In the affine chart \(X=1\) at \(P\), take \(t=Z\) and put \(y=Y\). The Fermat equation gives the formal branch
\[
 y(t)=-(1+t^{11})^{1/11}=-1-\frac{t^{11}}{11}+O(t^{22}).
\]
Up to sign, the residue for numerator \(\Delta B\) is \(\Delta(1,y,t)B(1,y,t)\,dt/(11y^{10})\). The factor multiplying \(B\,dt\) is a unit, since \(\Delta(P)=-2\) and \(y(P)=-1\).

Along the branch, \(e_1\) starts with \(t\), \(e_2\) starts with \(-1\), and \(e_3\) starts with \(-t\). The important cancellation follows from
\[
K=e_1e_2-e_3=(X+Y)(X+Z)(Y+Z).
\]
Indeed, \(1+y\) starts with \(-t^{11}/11\) while \(1+t\) and \(y+t\) are units with constants \(1,-1\). Thus \(K\) starts with \(t^{11}/11\). Five symmetric degree-five sections are
\[
 B_0=e_2e_3,\quad B_1=e_1^3e_2,\quad B_2=e_1^5,\quad
 B_3=e_2K,\quad B_4=e_1^2K.
\]
They have distinct exact orders \(1,3,5,11,13\), respectively, with leading coefficients \(1,-1,1,-1/11,1/11\). Thus they are independent and give all orders in the **invariant** canonical differential space upstairs at \(P\): the smallest order in a nontrivial linear combination cannot cancel. These are not the vanishing orders of the full canonical space of \(F_{11}\).

Since \(\pi\) has local ramification index \(2\), a downstairs differential of order \(a\) pulls back to order \(2a+1\). The five downstairs orders are \(0,1,2,5,6\). The gap theorem gives \(G(Q)=(1,2,3,6,7)\), of weight \((6-4)+(7-5)=4\). The generators \(4,5,11\) give the nongaps \(4,5\) and the four consecutive integers \(8,9,10,11\); adding multiples of \(4\) gives all remaining nongaps.

Since \(4\) is the smallest non-gap, \(\dim H^0(D_{11},\mathcal O(4Q))=2\), while \(\dim H^0(D_{11},\mathcal O(3Q))=1\). These dimensions hold over \(\mathbb Q\) by flat base change, since \(Q\) is rational. A nonconstant \(\mathbb Q\)-rational function in the former space has pole divisor exactly \(4Q\), giving a degree-four morphism with total ramification at \(Q\).

## Verification

The standard-library-only program `d11_weierstrass_check.py` uses rational truncated-polynomial arithmetic through order \(13\) to verify the Fermat branch equation, the five exact leading orders of the invariant sections, and the jet rank jumps of the symmetric monomial basis at exponents \(1,3,5,11,13\). The selected jet matrix has determinant \(1/121\). The script checks the local Taylor certificate, while the invariant-differential and ramification arguments establish the claim on the quotient.

## Relationship to prior work

Tafazolian [1] proves the quotient's genus, absolute simplicity and CM structure, trivial automorphism group, and exhibits the invariant Schur differential basis. The gap set here numerically matches the CM-type exponent set in [1, Proposition 4.1], but the two sets describe different invariants; no general identity between them is claimed. Tzermias [2, §2] gives gaps at the same coordinate point \(P\) **upstairs on \(F_{11}\)** and [2, §4] constructs a quotient model. The calculation here uses the cancellation in the invariant basis and the quotient ramification to determine the vanishing sequence **downstairs at \(Q\)**.

## Limitations

The result is confined to \(D_{11}\) over characteristic zero and its one distinguished rational point. It does not classify the other ramification points, all Weierstrass points, or the curve's global gonality. The literature comparison does not rule out unpublished or unindexed equivalent calculations.
