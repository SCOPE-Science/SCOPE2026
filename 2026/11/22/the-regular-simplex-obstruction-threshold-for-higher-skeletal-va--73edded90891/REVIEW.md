# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The vertex Gram matrix gives all codimension-face affine distances, including the vertex and facet endpoints. Counting regular-simplex faces gives the exact ratio and the consecutive ratio establishes a single integer crossing. The endpoint comparison is strict by AM–GM and \((1+1/n)^n<e<n+1\). Uniform Stirling expansion near the unique simple root of the continuum rate gives the second-order crossing displacement and the universal obstruction follows by direct substitution \(j=n-k\). The independent finite checker executes from the packaged path and checks 63,012 arithmetic instances; it is not substituted for the proof.

## Originality
PASS (historical priority provisional). The full publicly available motivating paper was examined in its introductory statement, Section 5, Proposition 5.1 and Question 5.5; it supplies one four-dimensional simplex counterexample, but not an all-dimensional exact crossover and asymptotic coefficient. The published product-of-simplices ridge result only guarantees a codimension-two inequality; planar-triangle product thresholds concern different geometric families. Targeted mathematical searches did not locate the same exact regular-simplex ratio transition or its resulting coefficient for the open question. The older fixed-volume simplicial inequality paper was not inspected fully and remains a priority risk; lack of a search hit is not a novelty certificate.

## Mathematical value
PASS. The explicit infinite-dimensional family gives a quantitative upper bound with a nontrivial fractional face-dimension cutoff for the range asked in a named open question. It improves on the previously exhibited isolated dimension-four failure and fixed-dimension-low-skeleton obstructions by proving failure for a linearly growing range of skeletal dimensions. The exact integer threshold and constant displacement distinguish the result from a mere leading-order estimate. No resolution of the unrestricted question is claimed.

## Closest literature and residual limitations
The motivating 2026 paper contains the governing constraints, the surface-area theorem and a single regular four-simplex failure. Published ridge-skeleton theorems for products of simplices do not state the higher-codimension obstruction classification. The 1996 Dalla–Tamvakis skeletal inequality uses a different volume normalization and could not be compared in full. External historical priority and independent review remain open.
