# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

An interior point gives positive support distance p=q−d cosθ in every direction, so M+p uθ is an embedded regular analytic polar curve. Its tangent has one full turn because its argument equals θ plus the argument of p′+ip with p>0. Thus the curvature numerator N=p²+2p′²−pp″ is everywhere nonnegative exactly when the bounded region is convex. For h=q/a, e=1−b²/a², x=cosθ/h and z=d/a, use h²+(h′)²=1−e+ex², h cosθ−h′ sinθ=x and h+h″=(1−e)/h³ to obtain the frozen cubic for N/a². The minor-axis reflection permits z≥0. The minimum is on x∈[0,1]; 2ex/(1+ex²) is strictly increasing, so in the interior-minimum regime substitution gives sign ex²−2e+1, whereas at x=1 the minimum is (1−z)(1+e−2z). The join 2e/(1+e) lies strictly below (1+e)/2 and, for e>1/2, above the lower threshold. These tests give exactly the closed window and the stated curvature-zero cases. Analyticity excludes boundary segments; outside the window the minimum is negative. The focal pedal satisfies |M+p uθ|²=a², with z=√e strictly between the thresholds. Expanding √((1−2τ)/(1−τ)) at τ=b²/a² verifies the width coefficient 5/8.

## originality

PASS

The classical center and focus cases are covered and should not receive novelty credit. The inspected sources do not give the global axial window on strictly interior points, with its lower and upper thresholds, reentrance and thin-ellipse width. This is a bounded statement comparison, not a claim to settle historical priority.

## value

PASS

The major axis is geometrically canonical and includes both the center and the foci. A complete convexity test there is more than interpolation between two classical examples: sufficiently eccentric ellipses have a nonconvex center pedal, then a convex band, then another nonconvex regime before the boundary. The sharp interval and its fourth-order width give a useful exact shape criterion where an approximate rule based on focal distance can fail.
