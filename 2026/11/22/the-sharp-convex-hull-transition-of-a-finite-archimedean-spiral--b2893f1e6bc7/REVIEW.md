# Scientific review: finite Archimedean spiral hull

**Correctness — PASS.** The support-normal map is strictly increasing, its stationary support height is strictly increasing, and the tangent contact equation has one upward-crossing root precisely beyond the sharp half-turn. The global support comparison excludes all old windings, not merely candidate local geometry. Green's theorem gives exact intrinsic volumes, and Taylor expansions give the sharp transition and long-arc coefficients. The extracted numerical checker constructs actual convex hulls and verifies independent geometric outputs. Remaining risk: numerical tests cannot establish all real-parameter claims; the proof supplies that step.

**Originality — PASS, subject to priority risk.** Archimedean radial and tangent formulas have been standard since antiquity. Homan et al. (arXiv:1504.04996, first posted 2015-04-20) use the linear-radius spiral but do not discuss its convex hull. MathWorld gives classical curvature and length formulas, not the sharp convexification threshold. A closely related published proof for a *logarithmic* spiral in a Gaussian Fock overlap problem gives a terminal tangent-chord and exact area/perimeter; it was inspected in full and is acknowledged. The exponential spiral cannot be reparametrized into the linear-radius finite spiral by a Euclidean similarity, and that record does not assert the half-turn onset or the long-arc polynomial expansions. Searches did not surface another full linear-spiral hull classification. Residual risk: some historical support-envelope literature was not fully accessible or indexed.

**Value — PASS.** A classical curve has a natural, globally determined convexification transition: the first occlusion occurs sharply at half a turn; the area and perimeter react in distinct orders; every later turn leaves exactly one terminal exposed arc; and its approach to a disk has an explicit square-root contact boundary layer. These facts are not simply the already-known polar-sector area or the arclength primitive, and give quantitative shape reconstruction and geometric control for truncated spiral envelopes.

**Closest literature and scope.** The earlier logarithmic-spiral theorem is a methodological antecedent, not an implication. The result is restricted to origin-started linear radial pitch, Euclidean convexity, and fixed finite arc length; no claim is made for general spirals. Historical-priority uncertainty and lack of independent validation remain.
