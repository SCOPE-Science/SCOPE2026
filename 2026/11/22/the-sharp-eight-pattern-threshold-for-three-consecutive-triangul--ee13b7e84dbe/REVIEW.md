## Correctness (C): PASS
The proof evaluates seven nonconstant moments with all four zero-index exclusions, establishes a universal eight-pattern formula, and applies Hasse to the smooth quartic with two rational points at infinity. The bound is positive for every prime at least 37. Every remaining prime from five through 31 is checked by exact exhaustive enumeration; the independent checker also verifies every formula for primes through 997. The proof, not the finite experiment alone, establishes the infinite theorem.

## Originality (O): PASS on inspected evidence
Conrad's general consecutive-linear-pattern indicator method and square-root estimate already cover the *technique* of expanding signs; neither proves the sharp eight-word cutoff for three consecutive triangular numbers. Wang–Fang (2023) and Wang–Li (2023) concern ordinary arithmetic progressions of residues; Ulness (2022) concerns single triangular-value maps. A variety of related mathematical-record searches produced no exact corresponding theorem. The specifically sharp triangular threshold and evaluated eight-frequency law were not found in the inspected material. Risk remains from inaccessible full Chinese-language Wang–Li text and specialized unindexed studies; no independent novelty certification is asserted.

## Mathematical value (V): PASS
A sharp finite prime threshold for realization of *every* ordered sign word is a natural arithmetic classification; it requires simultaneously handling the elliptic quartic trace, all eight sign choices, exceptional roots and the last failing prime. The complete count identifies how the eight patterns deviate from uniformity, beyond a generic error estimate. The simple indicator identity alone would not qualify as a contribution, but the universal sharp classification does.

## Limits
The prime range begins at five and excludes zero-valued symbols. The exact formula still contains a fixed elliptic trace; the result is not a characterization of individual primes or an independent certification of priority.
