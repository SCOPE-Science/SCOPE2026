# The sharp eighth-order three-excitation thermal fingerprint of the Shrikhande and rook networks

## Finding

Let \(R\) and \(S\) be the \(4\times4\) rook and Shrikhande graphs. In the exactly-three-excitation, hard-core unit-hopping XY model, the normalized entire trace functions
\[
F_G(z)=\frac{1}{560}\operatorname{Tr}\!\left(e^{zH_G}\right),\qquad G\in\{R,S\},
\]
have **identical Taylor coefficients through order seven**, but
\[
\boxed{F_R(z)-F_S(z)=\frac{17}{175}z^8+O(z^9)}\qquad(z\to0).
\]
Equivalently, the first unequal power trace is
\[
\operatorname{Tr}(H_R^8)-\operatorname{Tr}(H_S^8)=2,193,408.
\]
In particular, the normalized three-excitation canonical partition functions with \(z=-\beta\) first separate at order \(\beta^8\); the coherent trace amplitudes with \(z=-it\) first separate at order \(t^8\). These are two interpretations of the same analytic identity, not independent results.

## Assumptions and scope

Take \(V=\mathbb Z_4\times\mathbb Z_4\), with arithmetic modulo four. In \(R\), two **distinct** vertices are adjacent when one coordinate agrees; in \(S\), two vertices are adjacent when their difference belongs to
\[
\{(1,0),(-1,0),(0,1),(0,-1),(1,1),(-1,-1)\}.
\]
The model consists of three indistinguishable hard-core excitations with equal **positive unit** exchange amplitudes and no onsite or interaction energies beyond the exclusion constraint. For the occupation basis indexed by the complete collection \(\binom{V}{3}\), let
\[
(H_G)_{X,Y}=\begin{cases}1,&|X\triangle Y|=2\text{ and the exchanged pair is an edge of }G,\\0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}
\]
There are \(\binom{16}{3}=560\) basis states. This agrees with the number-conserving three-excitation block of the standard spin-exchange Hamiltonian \(\sum_{\{u,v\}\in E(G)}(\sigma_u^+\sigma_v^-+\sigma_u^-\sigma_v^+)\). There is no additional bosonic occupancy normalization.

## Proof

**1. Base graphs and quantum block.** The above definitions give symmetric zero-diagonal matrices on sixteen vertices with six neighbors per vertex. Direct multiplication gives \(A_G^2=4I+2J\) in both cases; thus both are strongly regular with parameters \((16,6,2,2)\). This also verifies a common one-particle spectrum. The graphs are not isomorphic: \(R\) has eight four-cliques (four coordinate rows and four coordinate columns), whereas \(S\) has none. The three-particle block has exactly one undirected edge per admissible one-particle move, hence its adjacency matrix has \(8,736\) nonzero directed entries, in both cases.

**2. Complete exact finite recurrence.** Order the \(560\) three-vertex subsets lexicographically and define \(\mathcal N_G(X)\) as all subsets obtained by replacing one occupied vertex with an adjacent unoccupied vertex. For each \(X\), define integers
\[
c_0^X(Y)=\mathbf1_{X=Y},\qquad c_{j+1}^X(Y)=\sum_{Z\in\mathcal N_G(Y)}c_j^X(Z).
\]
By induction, \(c_j^X(Y)\) counts all length-\(j\) allowed three-excitation walks from \(X\) to \(Y\), including multiplicity. Since \(H_G\) is exactly the zero-one adjacency matrix of these moves, \(\operatorname{Tr}H_G^j=\sum_X c_j^X(X)\). The induction proves the recurrence; enumerating all \(\binom{16}{3}\) starting configurations is exhaustive rather than a sample.

**3. Exact recurrence output.** Iterating the stated integer recurrence, or independently multiplying the exact sparse matrices, gives the following integer certificates:

| \(j\) | \(\operatorname{Tr}H_R^j\) | \(\operatorname{Tr}H_S^j\) |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 560 | 560 |
| 1 | 0 | 0 |
| 2 | 8,736 | 8,736 |
| 3 | 17,472 | 17,472 |
| 4 | 407,040 | 407,040 |
| 5 | 2,680,320 | 2,680,320 |
| 6 | 39,058,944 | 39,058,944 |
| 7 | 437,090,304 | 437,090,304 |
| 8 | 6,117,992,448 | 6,115,799,040 |

Every displayed integer is generated by `check_walks.py`, with no floating-point computation, and independently by `check_matrix.py`. Their algorithms build the graphs differently and compute the traces by different recurrence routes. All intermediate integer counts fit within exact signed 64-bit arithmetic through the claimed eighth order.

**4. Sharp Taylor order.** Since matrix exponentiation is entire in finite dimension,
\[
F_R(z)-F_S(z)=\frac1{560}\sum_{j\ge0}\frac{z^j}{j!}\Big(\operatorname{Tr}H_R^j-\operatorname{Tr}H_S^j\Big).
\]
The table cancels orders zero through seven and gives \(2,193,408/(560\,8!)=17/175\) at order eight. The coefficient is nonzero, so eight is the *first* distinguishing order, with analytic \(O(z^9)\) remainder. This completes the proof.

## Verification

The accompanying `check_walks.py` is Python standard-library only; it explicitly constructs the full adjacency lists of all three-excitation states and, for each of \(560\) roots, counts every closed walk of lengths zero through eight by exact-integer dynamic programming. The independently authored `check_matrix.py` constructs the rook Hamiltonian using Kronecker sums, the Shrikhande Hamiltonian using cyclic-shift matrices, and computes the resulting \(560\times560\) symmetric-power traces with exact integer SciPy sparse multiplication. It also verifies the two strongly regular identities and the contrasting counts of four-cliques (eight versus zero). Both programs were executed and asserted the moment equality and exact difference. Run `python check_walks.py` or `python check_matrix.py`; the second requires NumPy and SciPy. No stochastic simulations, approximate eigenvalues or external files are needed.

## Relationship to prior work

Rudolph introduced excitation-exchange graph invariants and displayed a level-three spectral distinction on **a different pair of 24-vertex graphs** [1]. Audenaert, Godsil, Royle and Rudolph rigorously proved the common symmetric-square spectrum of equal-parameter strongly regular graphs and reported that symmetric-cube spectra distinguished tested strongly regular graphs through order 36 [2]; the qualitative three-excitation separation of the present sixteen-vertex pair is therefore **not** claimed as new. The claim here is the much sharper first nonzero trace/Taylor order **eight** and its exact coefficient \(17/175\), which is not stated in the inspected primary article's introduction, computational-results section or physics appendix. Shiau, Joynt and Coppersmith [3] study different dynamical graph invariants; Gamble and collaborators [4] distinguish quantum walks using two interacting bosons and Green's functions, a different comparison from the three-hard-core-excitation normalized trace. Reyes, Dalfó and Fiol [5] study token-graph spectral radii and known cospectrality, without this exact eighth coefficient. The result does not settle general isomorphism testing or symmetric-cube uniqueness.

## Limitations

The computation is exact but applies to this specified nonisomorphic pair, exactly three hard-core excitations, and unit uniform hopping. It does not classify the higher moments, make a universal claim about three-token graphs, or imply that every physical measurement requires eighth-order resolution: individual transition amplitudes can differ at lower order, even when their **trace averages** agree. The prior-paper comparison leaves residual risk of unindexed numerical tables or unpublished prior computations giving the same eight-moment data. No independent external audit has been performed.
