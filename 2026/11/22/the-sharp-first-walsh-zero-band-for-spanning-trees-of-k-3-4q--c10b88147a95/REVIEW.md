# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The signed tree sum is exactly a cofactor of a \(\pm1\)-weighted graph Laplacian. Every right-vertex weighted degree belongs to \(\{-3,-1,1,3\}\), so the stated Schur complement has no forbidden division. An eight-case computation gives \(Q=A-(1-4r)B\succeq0\), with \(Q\succ0\) when at least two local signs are negative, and three distinct null lines in the one-negative cases. Therefore the total Schur matrix is positive definite when \(4|S|<n\); at equality its precise singular cases are exactly same-left stars. The zero count follows from disjoint labelled configurations. A direct 432-tree enumeration independently reproduces all 4,096 signed Fourier coefficients at \(n=4\); separate exact sparse enumerations confirm the complete first bands at \(n=8,12,16\). The infinite theorem does not depend on these finite checks.

## Originality — PASS under bounded comparison
Martin and Reiner (2003) supply weighted matrix-tree methodology, not a sharp edge-parity zero threshold for \(K_{3,n}\). Kassel and Wu (2013) explain transfer-current determinantal formulas for general local spanning-tree statistics but do not identify the first vanishing degree or characterize its star extremizers for this graph family. Klee and Stamps (2019) treat vertex-factorizable weights on complete multipartite graphs; arbitrary edge sign matrices are generally not vertex-factorizable. The closest published binary Fourier result gives the full transform for \(K_{2,n}\) using its one-double-right-vertex tree decomposition, and explicitly limits scope to that family. The exact \(K_{3,4q}\) low-degree rigidity statement is neither that theorem's corollary nor the same determinant formula in renamed variables. Searches did not locate a covering zero-location theorem. The residual risk is an unindexed earlier specialized signed-tree parity analysis.

## Value — PASS
A first vanishing Walsh band is a natural, sharp measure of how far local parity statistics can be recovered by convolution with a graphical-matroid basis indicator. In the first complete bipartite family beyond two left vertices, the all-width \(n/4\) lower bound is saturated exactly along one explicitly classified geometrical motif, yielding \(3\binom{4q}{q}\) first singular frequencies. The theorem provides a structural and dimension-growing recovery boundary rather than a fixed small numerical census or restatement of an existing tree count.

## Closest literature and limitations
The weighted Laplacian and transfer-current formulas are established ingredients; the new contribution is the sharp positive-semidefinite comparison and equality classification for binary edge signs. This analysis does not claim a complete spectrum, all-width nullity, generalization to \(K_{m,n}\) for larger left sides, or independent validation. Relevant published searches were bounded; an inaccessible or unindexed equivalent result could change the originality assessment.
