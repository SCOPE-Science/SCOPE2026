# Self-review

## Correctness
PASS. Explicit analytic Bergman moments yield the stated Jacobi entries. The AM-GM identity provides the global upper bound at and above one half, and essential-spectrum comparison proves it sharp. For smaller exponents, the first inverse-index correction to the Jacobi weights is positive; discrete sine vectors on blocks of length \(N^{3/4}\) produce arbitrarily many independent Rayleigh quotients above the essential edge. Simplicity and reflection of the discrete spectrum follow from the Jacobi recurrence and alternating-sign conjugation. The included finite checker is not substituted for these infinite-dimensional arguments.

## Originality
PASS, best-knowledge, subject to independent literature priority. Simanek's 2018 theorem establishes necessity at every exponent but sufficiency only for \(s\ge2\), explicitly leaving smaller exponents unresolved. Simanek (2020) and Le–Simanek (2020) characterize the optimal coefficient through an implicit Jacobi norm, and already supply the essential spectral interval, but do not establish the threshold \(1/2\) or infinite subcritical outliers. The original and follow-up full-text theorems, examples, and relevant proofs were inspected, alongside later polynomial-symbol comparisons. The actual coefficient and exact spectral implication, not merely similar paper titles, were compared. Any unindexed older equivalent spectral calculation remains a historical-priority risk.

## Value
PASS. The theorem resolves the sharp constant for a natural Bergman adjacent-coefficient form through an entire exponent half-line, closes the gap of a cited open small-exponent regime, and identifies a qualitative infinite discrete-spectrum bifurcation. Below the cutoff the norm is still given implicitly rather than by elementary closed form, and the result is not claimed for general weights or higher shifts.
