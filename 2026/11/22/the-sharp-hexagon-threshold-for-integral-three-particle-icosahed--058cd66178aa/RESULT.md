# The sharp hexagon threshold for integral three-particle icosahedral homology

## Finding

Let \(I\) be the one-skeleton of the regular icosahedron, regarded as a finite topological graph, and let \(B_3(I)\) be the space of unordered configurations of three pairwise distinct points of \(I\). For an integer \(d\geq 3\), define \(T_d\subseteq H_3(B_3(I);\mathbb Z)\) to be the subgroup generated by geometric product three-tori obtained from three *pairwise vertex-disjoint* simple cycles in \(I\), each of length at most \(d\). All cycles are unoriented for counting purposes; either orientation gives the same subgroup.

The complete top-degree integral homology and its cycle-length filtration through the sharp threshold are

\[
H_3(B_3(I);\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{110},\qquad
T_3=T_4=T_5\cong\mathbb Z^{100},\qquad
H_3(B_3(I);\mathbb Z)/T_5\cong\mathbb Z^{10},\qquad
T_6=H_3(B_3(I);\mathbb Z).
\]

More precisely, **all 100 product tori from triples of disjoint triangles** are an integral basis of \(T_5\), and **ten selected products of two triangles and one hexagon** extend them to an integral basis of \(H_3\). No shorter cycle can replace this hexagon requirement.

## Assumptions and scope

The graph is the connected, 12-vertex, 30-edge, 5-regular icosahedral graph. Its complete labeled edge set is fixed in `verify_icosa.py` and `BASIS_CERTIFICATE.json`; every vertex link is a five-cycle. The configuration space permits particles in edge interiors, not just at graph vertices. Its homology is calculated with ordinary integer coefficients. The result concerns **three unordered particles**, degree-three *ordinary* homology, and the sublattice generated by products of **vertex-disjoint simple graph cycles**. It does not assert a homotopy decomposition, calculate other homology degrees, or classify the full symmetry action.

## Proof

**1. Faithful integral chain model.** Apply the reduced Świątkowski graph-configuration chain complex in weight three (An–Drummond-Cole–Knudsen, Theorem 4.5 and Proposition 4.9). Choose a base incident edge \(e_{v,0}\) at each vertex \(v\). The reduced local degree-one generators are the four differences \(h_{v,e}-h_{v,e_{v,0}}\) for non-base incident edges \(e\), with differential \(\partial(h_{v,e}-h_{v,e_{v,0}})=e-e_{v,0}\). Multiply with the Koszul sign convention when sorting vertex factors. At total weight three, chain degree cannot exceed three, so \(H_3\) is exactly the integral kernel of \(\partial_3\), with no quotient by degree-four boundaries. Explicit enumeration yields

\[
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Z}C_3=\binom{12}{3}4^3=14080,
\qquad \operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Z}C_2=30\binom{12}{2}4^2=31680.
\]

**2. Upper bound by an exact finite-field certificate.** Encode each signed \(\partial_3\)-column as a binary integer whose set bits mark its nonzero entries modulo two. Exhaustive bitwise Gaussian elimination over \(\mathbb F_2\), in fixed lexicographic generator order, produces **13,970 distinct pivot rows**. Therefore the rational rank is at least 13,970 and the integral rank of the top homology is **at most 110**. This lower bound on boundary rank is logically sufficient; no unproved inference of integral ranks from a single finite field is made.

**3. Explicit closed geometric tori.** For an oriented simple cycle \(C=(v_0,\ldots,v_{m-1})\), the reduced degree-one representative of its traveling-particle loop is the signed sum over its vertices of \(h_{v_i,e_{\mathrm{out}}}-h_{v_i,e_{\mathrm{in}}}\). Its differential vanishes because every cycle edge occurs once with each sign. For three vertex-disjoint cycles, the product of their three degree-one representatives is a degree-three cycle; sorting local factors contributes the Koszul sign. The verifier constructs these chains explicitly, checks every integral boundary coefficient to be zero, and enumerates exactly **700** geometrically distinct triple-cycle products with maximum cycle length at most six:

| Cycle lengths | Number of products |
|---|---:|
| \((3,3,3)\) | 100 |
| \((3,3,4)\) | 210 |
| \((3,3,5)\) | 120 |
| \((3,4,4)\) | 120 |
| \((3,3,6)\) | 70 |
| \((3,4,5)\) | 60 |
| \((4,4,4)\) | 20 |

The cycles involved are among 20 triangles, 30 four-cycles, 72 five-cycles, and 240 six-cycles. No triple of pairwise vertex-disjoint simple cycles can have a length exceeding six among its members: each of the other two cycles requires at least three different vertices, leaving at most six of the twelve vertices.

**4. Integral basis and saturation.** Put the 700 signed torus vectors in deterministic order by maximum cycle length, length triple, then their enumerated cycle indices. Run exact integer row elimination on their coordinates in the free group \(C_3\). A nonzero reduced vector is retained only at a coordinate with coefficient \(+1\) or \(-1\), normalized to \(+1\); all preceding pivot coordinates have already been cleared using integral row operations. The verifier obtains **110 unit pivots**, exactly the first 100 triangle-triple products and ten products of type \((3,3,6)\). All other tori reduce to zero without division; every torus whose maximum cycle length is at most five already reduces to zero against the 100 triangle pivots alone.

The 110 chosen torus rows and their 110 chosen coordinate columns therefore have a triangular integer minor with diagonal entries \(+1\), after unimodular row operations. The determinant is \(+1\): these product cycles generate a **primitive** rank-110 sublattice \(T_6\) of the free degree-three chain group. By Step 2, \(\operatorname{rank}H_3\le110\), while these 110 cycles are independent in \(H_3\), forcing equality. Because \(T_6\subseteq\ker\partial_3\subseteq C_3\), the quotient \(\ker\partial_3/T_6\) is both finite (equal ranks) and a subgroup of the free group \(C_3/T_6\) (primitivity). It is consequently zero. The first 100 chosen basis elements generate \(T_5\) and extend to the 110-element integral basis; hence their quotient is freely generated by the other ten. This proves every assertion.

## Verification

The files `verify_icosa.py` and `BASIS_CERTIFICATE.json` are sufficient, with Python 3 standard library and no network or third-party packages. Run `python3 verify_icosa.py` in the directory containing both. The program exactly reconstructs the edges, local generators, differential, all simple cycles of length at most six, every disjoint-cycle triple, and their signed product chains. It verifies all 700 cycles are closed; checks the complete unit-pivot certificate against `BASIS_CERTIFICATE.json`; verifies the short-torus integer span; and recomputes the rank-13,970 mod-two boundary certificate. Successful execution ends with `EXACT_ICO_3PARTICLE_TORUS_THRESHOLD_OK`. No floating-point rank, heuristic search, or sample-to-infinite extrapolation is used.

## Relationship to prior work

Farley and Sabalka introduced graph braid computations through discrete Morse theory. Maciążek and Sawicki developed product-cycle methods, including calculations for one-connected graphs and selected graph families. An, Drummond-Cole and Knudsen proved that the reduced Świątkowski complex computes the required graph configuration homology. Maciążek and Sawicki's later product-cycle classification treats wheels, \(K_{3,3}\), and certain degree-restricted cases; the icosahedral graph has twelve degree-five vertices and is not among those classes. The published dodecahedral three-particle homology result and two-particle icosahedral calculations concern different graph/particle combinations; neither determines this exact integral basis or the six-edge threshold. These comparisons are scoped to identified literature, not claims of universal novelty.

## Limitations

This is a single icosahedral-graph computation, not a universal theorem for Platonic graphs, all particle numbers, or arbitrary 5-regular graphs. The fundamental group, lower homology, and equivariant action are not computed. The integer basis relies on an exhaustive finite exact certificate that can be independently executed, not on a short conceptual hand classification. The primary literature and indexed records do not exclude unindexed equivalent work, and independent mathematical audit has not been performed.
