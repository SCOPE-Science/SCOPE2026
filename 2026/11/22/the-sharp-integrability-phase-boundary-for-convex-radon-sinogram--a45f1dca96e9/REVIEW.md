# Scientific assessment: convex Radon sections of the unit ball

**Correctness — PASS.** For all dimensions, the radial Beta integral gives the exact quadratic sinogram in the only integrable candidate dimensions. For arbitrary, possibly nonradial, integrable data, averaging the coarea formula over sphere directions supplies a boundary-layer inequality with exponent \((d-3)/2\). When \(d\ge5\), the shell mass tends to zero; when \(d=4\), the additional square-root shell factor from \(L^2\) makes the decay strictly \(o(1-t)\). Convexity and endpoint zero limits imply that a nonzero sinogram must carry at least linear terminal mass. That contradiction and Fourier-slice injectivity prove the universal impossibility. The proof addresses a.e. definitions, endpoints, normalization and the infinite-dimensional quantifiers. The exact symbolic checker is supplementary and not used as an infinite proof.

**Originality — PASS relative to inspected evidence.** Hansen's March 2026 theorem explicitly covers the radial power-weight forward mapping law, so this part alone is not new. It does not impose convexity or derive a universal \(L^p\) nonexistence theorem. Iosevich–Liflyand's November 17, 2026 manuscript gives the planar quadratic-sinogram witness (so that witness is not new) and Fourier bounds conditional on convexity, not the sharp dimension-four and dimension-five obstruction. The general all-dimensional boundary-layer rigidity is the distinguishing statement. Database and literature searches did not reveal a dominating theorem; older range and positivity theorems could still hide an equivalent statement.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The classification resolves a natural question raised by the interaction of boundary-singular Radon mapping laws with a global shape constraint on the measured sinogram. It identifies a sharp, nontrivial and dimension-dependent dividing line—dimension four is possible below, but impossible at, the Hilbert exponent—and proves dimension-five absolute nonexistence without symmetry assumptions. This is an exact natural range/regularity invariant, rather than an arbitrary parameter slice or a small numerical gain.

The result is limited to real functions on a Euclidean ball and convexity on the full interior chord-parameter interval for almost every direction. There is no classification of individual admissible functions, no general convex-domain extension, and no claim of fully exhaustive bibliographic novelty.
