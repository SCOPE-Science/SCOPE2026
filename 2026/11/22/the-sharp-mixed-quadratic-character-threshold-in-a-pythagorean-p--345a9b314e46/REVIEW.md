# Scientific review: mixed quadratic-character Pythagorean patterns

## Correctness — PASS

The claim specifies every odd prime, every nonzero triple and exactly the six nonconstant sign words. For each word, the product indicator has value eight at matches, zero at admissible mismatches and at most four at a zero-factor parameter. The five-parameter exclusion bound is valid. The single quadratic sums are exactly \(-1,0,-1\). The two cubic products are squarefree and have genus one, the quartic has two rational points at infinity and genus one, and the quintic has genus two. Hasse–Weil bounds yield \(8N\ge p-23-10\sqrt p>0\) for every prime at least 149. The remaining 33 primes have been exhausted by two independent exact residue computations and the six exceptions confirmed. The program and actual output are attached. The finite checks are not used to infer the infinite part.

## Originality — PASS on the inspected evidence

Hu–Zhan's 2021 primary full text establishes an asymptotic over a two-parameter Euclidean family and consequently eventual occurrence in the related normalized family, but no sharp finite mixed threshold or exception list. Xi–Zheng's 2024 abstract treats existence in the full Pythagorean solution space for sufficiently large primes, not this specific one-parameter sharp classification. Li–Wang's 2025 article directly targets the same one-parameter family, so it is the greatest priority risk; the accessible abstract and first-page preview state explicit formulas for precisely the two monochromatic patterns. The six complementary mixed patterns and their exact cutoff are not stated in that material. The rest of that article was inaccessible; an unstated implication from its Jacobi-sum derivations remains a material risk. OEIS A344620 has a separate integer height restriction and only the positive monochromatic word. No matching mathematical claim was found among inspected published-index search returns; this is a limited search, not proof of priority.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The sharp cutoff \(31\) is an exact natural invariant for the six nonmonochromatic quadratic-residue patterns in the classical normalized Euclid parametrization. It is motivated by published all-eight and two-monochromatic problems, separates the mixed regime from a genuinely different monochromatic one, identifies exactly which primes and which mixed words obstruct universality, and resolves an infinite family with a finite analytic certificate. This is not an arbitrary parameter restriction or an unmotivated single numerical count. It does not claim any broader arithmetic-geometry theorem.
