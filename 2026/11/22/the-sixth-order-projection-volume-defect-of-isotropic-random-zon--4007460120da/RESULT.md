# The sixth-order projection-volume defect of isotropic random zonotopes

## Finding

For any integer \(n\ge1\), let \(U_1,\ldots,U_n\) be independent uniform unit vectors on \(S^2\subset\mathbb R^3\). Independently choose nonnegative, mutually independent radial variables \(R_i\) satisfying \(\mathbb E R_i<\infty\), with no assumption on their second moments. Form the zonotope
\[
Z_n=\sum_{i=1}^n[0,R_iU_i].
\]
The projection body \(\Pi K\) is defined by \(h_{\Pi K}(u)=\operatorname{area}(K|u^\perp)\); let \(V(K)\) denote ordinary three-dimensional Euclidean volume. The nonnegative *projection-volume deficit*
\[
\Delta_n=V(Z_n)^2-\frac18V(\Pi Z_n)
\]
is integrable even if the two terms defining it are individually nonintegrable, and has the exact finite-sample expectation
\[
\boxed{\mathbb E\Delta_n=
\frac{5\pi^2(32-3\pi^2)}{512}
\sum_{1\le i_1<\cdots<i_6\le n}\prod_{j=1}^6\mathbb E R_{i_j}.}
\tag{1}
\]
In particular, for unit-length generators it is exactly \(\binom n6\,5\pi^2(32-3\pi^2)/512\), and for \(n\le5\) the deficit is identically zero. Formula (1) arises from a *pointwise nonnegative six-generator decomposition* of the projection deficit, not merely cancellation of expectations.

## Assumptions and scope

Vectors live in the fixed Euclidean space \(\mathbb R^3\). Each \(U_i\) has normalized rotation-invariant surface measure on \(S^2\), independently; the lengths \(R_i\ge0\) are mutually independent and independent of the directions, but need not be identically distributed. The only radial integrability hypothesis is \(\mathbb E R_i<\infty\). There are finitely many generators. Volumes of lower-dimensional bodies, and projection bodies of lower-dimensional bodies, are interpreted by the same support-function definition; any associated three-volume is zero. There is no restriction on the possible signs of coordinate components of the generators.

We use Saroglou's established three-dimensional sharp zonoid inequality \(V(\Pi K)\le8V(K)^2\), with equality for every zonotope generated by at most five segments. These two previously known statements are *inputs*, not new claims.

## Proof

**1. Determinantal formulas with exact normalization.** For arbitrary vectors \(v_1,\dots,v_n\in\mathbb R^3\) and \(Z=\sum_i[0,v_i]\), the usual zonotope formula is
\[
V(Z)=\sum_{i<j<k}|\det(v_i,v_j,v_k)|.
\tag{2}
\]
The planar shadow perpendicular to a unit vector \(u\) is the sum of the projected segments, so
\[
h_{\Pi Z}(u)=\sum_{i<j}|u\cdot(v_i\times v_j)|.
\]
Consequently \(\Pi Z=\sum_{i<j}[-v_i\times v_j,v_i\times v_j]\). Since the centered segments have twice their generating-vector length, applying (2) to \(\Pi Z\) gives
\[
\frac18V(\Pi Z)=\sum_{\{(i,j),(k,l),(p,q)\}}
|\det(v_i\times v_j,v_k\times v_l,v_p\times v_q)|,
\tag{3}
\]
where the summation runs over unordered triples of distinct unordered index pairs; repeated indices are permitted between different pairs. All factors \(1/8\) and \(2\) in these identities are fixed by the centered-segment convention.

**2. Cancellation through five generators.** Fix the directions \(v_i\), replace them by \(t_iv_i\), and consider \(F(t)=V(Z(t))^2-V(\Pi Z(t))/8\). Equations (2)–(3) show that \(F\) is a polynomial homogeneous of total degree six in nonnegative \(t_i\). Whenever at most five \(t_i\) are nonzero, the aforementioned sharp equality for five-segment zonotopes gives \(F(t)=0\). Thus every monomial coefficient supported on at most five labels vanishes identically: restrict \(F\) to any corresponding coordinate subspace and use polynomial uniqueness. A degree-six monomial involving at least six labels must use exactly six different labels, each to the first power. It follows that
\[
\boxed{V(Z)^2-\frac18V(\Pi Z)
=\sum_{S\subseteq[n],\ |S|=6}\delta(v_i:i\in S),}
\tag{4}
\]
where \(\delta(w_1,\ldots,w_6)=V(\sum_{i=1}^6[0,w_i])^2-V(\Pi\sum_{i=1}^6[0,w_i])/8\). Every summand is nonnegative by the sharp zonoid inequality. Equations (2)–(3) also prove that \(\delta\) scales linearly with the length of *each* of its six arguments.

**3. The exact six-label coefficient.** Let \(T\sqcup T^c=[6]\) range over the ten unordered partitions into two triples. The coefficient of the squarefree monomial \(t_1\cdots t_6\) in the squared-volume term in step 2 equals
\[
A=2\sum_{T\sqcup T^c=[6]}
|\det(v_i:i\in T)|\,|\det(v_j:j\in T^c)|.
\]
The corresponding coefficient in the projection-body term (3) is
\[
B=\sum_{\mathcal M}\bigl|\det(v_{a}\times v_{b},v_{c}\times v_{d},v_{e}\times v_{f})\bigr|,
\]
where \(\mathcal M=\{\{a,b\},\{c,d\},\{e,f\}\}\) ranges over the fifteen perfect matchings of the six labels. Therefore \(\delta(v_1,\ldots,v_6)=A-B\). This exact identity requires no generic-position assumptions.

**4. Rotational averages.** If \(U,V,W\) are independent uniform directions on \(S^2\), then
\[
\mathbb E|U\times V|=\frac12\int_{-1}^1\sqrt{1-s^2}\,ds=\frac\pi4,
\qquad
\mu:=\mathbb E|\det(U,V,W)|=\frac12\cdot\frac\pi4=\frac\pi8.
\]
Two disjoint triples use independent directions. Thus the ten terms of \(A\) have expectation \(2\mu^2\) each, and \(\mathbb EA=20(\pi/8)^2=5\pi^2/16\). Each of the fifteen terms of \(B\) contains cross products formed from three disjoint *pairs*. Those cross products are independent, rotationally invariant random vectors; each has mean length \(\pi/4\), with an independent uniformly distributed direction. It follows that
\[
\mathbb EB=15\left(\frac\pi4\right)^3\frac\pi8
=\frac{15\pi^4}{512},
\quad
\mathbb E\delta(U_1,\ldots,U_6)
=\frac{5\pi^2(32-3\pi^2)}{512}>0.
\tag{5}
\]

**5. General lengths and integrability.** Substituting \(v_i=R_iU_i\) in (4), separate homogeneity of each six-label term gives \(\delta(R_iU_i:i\in S)=(\prod_{i\in S}R_i)\delta(U_i:i\in S)\). Independence and nonnegativity permit taking expectations using only first radial moments, and equations (4)–(5) give (1). Boundedness of the unit-vector coefficient, finite \(n\), and the finite first moments show \(\Delta_n\in L^1\). For instance, if all radial distributions have tails with finite first moments but infinite second moments, \(\mathbb E\Delta_n\) remains finite although \(\mathbb E V(Z_n)^2\) and \(\mathbb E V(\Pi Z_n)\) are both infinite for \(n\ge3\), because a single triple of nondegenerate generators gives a nonnegative lower bound involving squared radii. This is a cancellation result for the random *difference*, not a subtraction of divergent expectations.

## Verification

The standalone, standard-library program `verify_projection_defect.py` evaluates determinants and cross products with exact integer arithmetic on configurations of six and seven vectors; it checks equation (4) and the five-generator cancellation independently using formulas (2)–(3), including full 21-pair enumeration for seven vectors. It separately samples 18,000 sets of six independent uniform directions with a fixed seed and compares the empirical coefficient against (5), reporting a Monte Carlo standard error. The exact finite checks validate normalization and combinatorial implementation; the finite simulation is corroborative and is **not** a proof of an expectation identity. Reproduce using `python3 verify_projection_defect.py`.

## Relationship to prior work

Saroglou (2011) proved the sharp three-dimensional zonoid projection-body inequality, whose equality cases include sums of five segments; Saroglou (2014) explicitly restates that equality theorem. Those established facts provide the nonnegativity and lower-support cancellation in (4). Breiding, Bürgisser, Lerario and Mathis (2021/2022) developed an algebra of zonoids and expected-determinant formulas, including an abstract projection-body representation; these provide a broader framework but do not evaluate the specific six-way defect expectation in (1). Averkov, von Dichter, Richard and Soprunov (2026) classify inequalities on six mixed volumes in three dimensions, but do not give the projection-body deficit's isotropic expectation or its radial first-moment universality. The new claimed contribution is the *exact six-generator decomposition of the deficit* coupled to its *finite-sample, mean-length-only expectation* (1), including the cancellation at infinite individual second moments. Standard determinant formulas and the sharp prior inequality are explicitly not asserted to be new.

## Limitations

All directions must be independent and Haar-uniform; the result does not apply to correlated or anisotropic directions. It concerns finite segment sums in \(\mathbb R^3\), not arbitrary dimensions. The expectation formula refers to the deficit, rather than to the expectation of the quotient \(V(\Pi Z_n)/V(Z_n)^2\). The separate expected volumes may be infinite under heavy-tailed lengths, so they must not be subtracted as extended-real quantities. The proof uses the established sharp five-generator equality theorem. Its historical novelty has been checked against identified close sources but is not independently certified.
