# Review of the sixth-order isotropic projection-volume defect

## Correctness — PASS

The result uses the established sharp three-dimensional zonoid inequality and its full five-segment equality family (Saroglou 2011; explicitly reproduced as Theorem A in Saroglou 2014). The zonotope volume formula and the support-function formula for the projection body determine the exact normalizations. A homogeneous degree-six polynomial vanishing whenever at most five length parameters are nonzero must be squarefree of support six; this rigorously yields the positive six-generator decomposition. Its expected coefficient is computed by ten complementary triple partitions and fifteen perfect matchings. The spherical integral \(\mathbb E|U\times V|=\pi/4\) and scalar product mean \(1/2\) give the closed formula. The integrability assertion follows from separate linearity in each six-generator radial factor. Integer determinant enumerations and independent seeded sampling check arithmetic, not the infinite-quantifier argument.

## Originality — PASS (historical priority provisional)

The comparison is specifically with both formulations, namely the pointwise six-generator projection-body deficit and its exact mean under arbitrary independent isotropic radial generators. Saroglou 2011/2014 proves the upper bound and characterizes equality; it does **not** state the new averaged defect formula. Breiding et al. 2021/2022 provides the general zonoid algebra, expected determinant apparatus and projection-body representation, but no evaluation of this six-way deficit or its first-moment-only radial universality. The published 2026 six-zonoid mixed-volume cone result gives strong general multilinear inequalities, but does not evaluate the random projection-volume deficit. The complete accessible prior result on random three-dimensional zonotope volumes evaluates \(\mathbb E V\), \(\operatorname{Var}V\), and the fluctuation law, not \(\operatorname{Vol}(\Pi Z)\) or the six-generator cancellation. Known computations of fixed polyhedral projection-body volumes address individual solids, not a stochastic all-sample-size formula.

The full text of Saroglou's 2011 article could not be accessed; its 2014 full-text restatement establishes the required theorem. The above is a best-knowledge statement, not an assertion of absolute uniqueness or independent historical certification.

## Value — PASS

The exact mean of a natural nonnegative gap to the sharp three-dimensional projection-body volume inequality supplies an all-finite-sample statistic, reveals the *first genuinely six-body interaction*, and identifies the universal constant governing independently isotropic generators. Its dependence only on first radial moments, even when the separate squared-volume expectations diverge, is a substantive integrability phenomenon. This is a structured stochastic-geometric consequence relevant to projection-body comparisons, not an arbitrary small enumeration or a reproduction of the known five-generator equality case.

## Evidence and limitations

The proof covers arbitrary finite generator count and independent rotationally invariant directions with independent nonnegative radial lengths of finite means. It does not handle anisotropic or correlated directions, infinite generator series, dimensions other than three, or expected ratios of the two volumes. The standalone checker uses exact integer determinants on three six/seven-vector configurations and a seeded 18,000-replicate Monte Carlo normalization test. The 2011 article's paywalled full text is a residual priority risk; no independent mathematical audit was performed.
