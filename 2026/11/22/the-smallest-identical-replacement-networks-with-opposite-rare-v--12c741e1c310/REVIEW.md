# Mathematical review

## Correctness

**PASS.** The group-normalized encounter matrix is reconstructed directly from the probability of a focal individual being alone; the rational witness has identical diagonal and off-diagonal weights despite differing pairwise encounter probabilities. The BDB fixation law follows from the two-interior-state birth–death recurrence with positive transition rates. Exact coefficient inequalities prove opposite signs for every \(0<w\le1\), not merely at sampled values. Minimality is established for every two-player anonymous independent-site model and for all three-player models visiting at most two sites. The extracted standalone exact-arithmetic checker also enumerates all twenty-seven three-site placements and confirms the rational identities.

## Originality

**PASS, with priority limitations.** Pires and Broom (2026) provide a general occupancy/payoff/fixation calculus from which concrete numerical instances can be derived, but the inspected paper does not state or imply a same-entire-matrix all-intensity reversal or its sharp two-player/two-site obstructions as a derived theorem. Haq, Schimit and Broom (2025) report strong temperature predictors within particular coordinated-movement models, not the exact same-matrix counterexample. The 2024 isolated-community work and the 2020 framework study are broader background but do not give this finite classification. Ohtsuki, Pacheco and Nowak (2007) already articulate the conceptual distinction between interaction and replacement structures; this general insight is acknowledged, not claimed. The result's additional content is a same-game, same-kernel, exact smallest-instance obstruction with an all-intensity sign certificate. The possibility of an earlier equivalent small-instance example remains a historical-priority risk. Independent external audit has not been performed.

## Value

**PASS.** The complete weighted replacement graph is a standard compressed observable for evolutionary simulations. The theorem proves that even this entire observable can fail to identify the sign of selection on a rare volunteer, and establishes the sharp minimum model complexity at which the failure begins. Because the selection verdict differs for every positive intensity, the distinction is not a fragile infinitesimal effect. This specifically motivates collecting pairwise co-occupation information when interpreting cooperation experiments or calibrating multiplayer mobility games.

## Scope and residual risks

The result is about fixation against neutrality, not the stronger comparison of mutant and resident fixation required in some definitions of selection favoring cooperation. The full-paper literature comparison is substantial but not exhaustive; an earlier treatment of the same anonymous occupancy inverse problem could exist. The theorem does not address correlated movement or update rules other than BDB.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
