# Review of the anticanonical ring theorem

## Correctness — PASS

The source gives the exact octagonal-prism fan and polar vertices [1, Theorem 4.1]. The dual of this prism is a *bipyramid*, giving the monomial condition \(|c|\le m\), \((a,b)\in(m-|c|)Q\). Ten explicit boundary rays plus the central ray triangulate the planar degree cone into determinant-one cones, proving generation in all degrees; the low-degree lattice sets prove irredundancy. The height-coordinate reduction proves the exact thirteen-element Hilbert basis. The rational Ehrhart theorem and eighteen certified initial values give the all-degree formula, from which the Hilbert series follows algebraically. Torus-character ratios prove the degree-one/conic and degree-two/birational statements. The index is the least common multiple of the facet support denominators. The bundled checker verifies critical exact arithmetic, but does not replace the infinite semigroup and Ehrhart arguments.

## Originality — PASS, with priority risk

Petracci–Villa [1, §4] give the defining fan, polar vertices, volume and deformation spaces, but not the exact anticanonical ring, its generating weights, the complete Hilbert series or the birational threshold. Standard toric polar/Ehrhart correspondences [2] make the quantities computable but do not themselves state these evaluated invariants. Kaloghiros–Petracci [3] have different examples, including volume twelve rather than the present \(11/3\). Targeted mathematical-record searches for the exact family, generator profile, Hilbert numerator and map behavior identified no dominating statement. Unindexed polytope databases, unpublished notes or alternative presentations might still contain equivalent values; searches are not a proof of global priority.

## Value — PASS

The minimal anticanonical ring and the sharp transition from a one-dimensional first-anticanonical image to a birational second-anticanonical map are natural projective-model invariants of a recently introduced K-polystable Fano degeneration. They supplement the already known anticanonical volume and deformation obstruction with an exact, finite algebraic presentation *in terms of minimal generator degrees*, and an all-degree section count. The rational-map threshold is a geometric fact not obtained by merely restating the volume; it can matter when constructing explicit projective models and studying the K-moduli degeneration. No claim is made for general deformations or minimal relations.
