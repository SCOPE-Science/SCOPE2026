# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The incidence enumeration is finite and exhaustive: every \((9_3)\) configuration can normalize its lines through point zero to three fixed triples, and the verified 176 completions are each isomorphic to exactly one of the three displayed representatives, as distinguished by their point noncollinearity cycle types. For each representative, the entire 512-input majority map on each shore is evaluated, establishing \(H^3=H^2\), \(K^3=K^2\), the inverse-image phase bijection and the exact weights of each 4-step retraction fiber. The proof then algebraically derives every basin size for all \(2^{18}\) starting configurations. Explicit witnesses show the sharp entrance-time bounds. Independent exact C++ enumeration of all 786432 states across the three graphs confirms every orbit, basin and entrance-time count. No step extrapolates a finite sample to an unbounded family.

## Originality — PASS, with historical-priority qualification
Adam–Dahleh–Ozdaglar establish the general eventual period-two phenomenon and complexity bounds, not the exact three-type \((9_3)\) phase spectrum. Bokowski–Pilaud enumerate incidence configurations, not binary-majority attraction dynamics. The closest published index matches include a critical-group comparison of all three \((9_3)\) Levi graphs (different invariant), and a majority projection on the fourteen-vertex Heawood graph (different incidence type and size). Earlier exact majority results on the sixteen-vertex Möbius–Kantor and twenty-vertex Desargues graphs similarly cannot imply the exact nine-point phase histograms without additional work. Searches of the mathematical literature and indexed findings did not locate an equivalent nine-point full basin classification. Unindexed equivalents may remain.

## Value — PASS
This is a complete, naturally delimited classification of the three abstract realizable nine-point, three-lines-per-point incidence geometries, not an arbitrary selection of graph sizes. The noncollinearity geometry predicts distinct dynamical symbols and fiber spectra, and singles out the Pappus graph by a sharper universal settling time and absence of singleton fixed-state basins. The exact fiber formula resolves *individual attractor basins*, which is strictly more informative than the classical statement that periods divide two or a simple fixed-point census. It supplies reproducible benchmark data for how incidence geometry influences nonlinear dynamics.
