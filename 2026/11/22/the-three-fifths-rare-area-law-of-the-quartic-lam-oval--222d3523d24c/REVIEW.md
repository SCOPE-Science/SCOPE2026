# Scientific review: quartic Lamé rare-area law

## Correctness — PASS
The geometric sample space, arclength measure, area normalization and quartic tip charts are explicit. A deterministic finite rational checker verified 77 quartic determinant identities and two independent integral normalizations. The analytical proof controls all non-tip configurations, compares the exact local convex graph to the positive homogeneous quintic via a positive divided-difference kernel, and proves finiteness of the limiting three-dimensional sublevel integral. Dominated convergence yields both the sharp asymptotic and the stated marked total-variation limit. Computation supports arithmetic but does not replace the proof.

## Originality — PASS (provisional priority)
The closest full published rare-area result proves exponent \(2/3\) only with strictly positive curvature, explicitly excluding the four flat tips. Its curvature integral diverges in this case and does not imply the \(3/5\) law. Older studies of maximal polygons on a circle, affine curvature measures, and random points sampled from convex interiors do not cover the exact boundary probability law. published-finding corpus alias and curvature-degeneracy searches and previous-record claim comparisons did not show overlap. Silverman and Brown (1978) was only available as an abstract; historical priority is therefore not certified.

## Mathematical value — PASS
A smooth strictly convex classical quartic Lamé oval falls outside positive-curvature asymptotic theory. The complete leading constant, new exponent, four-point localization and explicit conditional configuration law supply a structural classification of a natural finite-type degeneracy, rather than a numerical recomputation or routine change of names.

## Boundaries and verification status
The claim concerns only the fixed quartic oval with three independent Euclidean-arclength-uniform points. It does not assert uniformity in the Lamé exponent, near-maximal area, interior sampling or general flat-point curves. Independent mathematical validation has not occurred.
