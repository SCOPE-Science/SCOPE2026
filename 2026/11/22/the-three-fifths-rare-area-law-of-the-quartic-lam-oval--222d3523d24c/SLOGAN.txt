Three independent arclength-uniform points on the quartic Lamé oval form a triangle of area at most \(t\) with probability asymptotic to \(1.7794049442086\ldots\,t^{3/5}\), not the regular-curvature \(t^{2/3}\) law. Exceptionally small triangles concentrate at the four zero-curvature tips and, after \(t^{1/5}\) rescaling, have an explicit uniform-on-a-quintic-sublevel limit in total variation.
