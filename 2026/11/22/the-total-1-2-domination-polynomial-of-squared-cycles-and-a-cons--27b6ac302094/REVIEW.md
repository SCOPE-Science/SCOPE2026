# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The de Bruijn trace counts exactly the cyclic binary words satisfying the four-neighbor interval predicate. The cyclic gap bound is sharp by explicit feasible constructions. Equality cases establish all four extremal coefficient formulas; in the \(7q+1\) class, the unique short-short junction makes the two forbidden shortening positions unambiguous. The transfer and exhaustive adjacency implementations passed and agreed in their common finite range. Those checks do not replace the infinite proofs.

## Originality — PASS, with historical limitations
The closest same-object 2014 paper enumerates **ordinary** total-dominating sets of squared cycles. The original 2013 paper defines total \([1,2]\)-domination as an open variant; the accessible Lv–Wu 2015 preprint treats global bounds rather than a squared-cycle load-capped polynomial. An earlier seven-periodic total-domination optimum/exchange result is antecedent and not claimed as new. The at-most-two-neighbor filter is not implied by ordinary domination counts: for order eight the optimum coefficient falls from twenty-four to eight, and for order fifteen from seventy-five to forty-five. No equivalent complete enumeration emerged from indexed searches; unindexed earlier priority remains possible.

## Value — PASS
The new exact cardinality polynomial gives the entire availability spectrum for bounded-load total domination on a canonical family of quartic circulants. The residue-one theorem identifies a surprising scale-free loss of exactly two configurations per labelled base vertex at every scale, despite the number of ordinary optima growing quadratically. This quantifies a meaningful distinction between coverage-only and bounded-load placement, rather than recomputing a known scalar optimum.
