# Review of the four-by-four transpose quotient

## Correctness: PASS
The definition of the underlying rook complex, the ordinary orbit action, and every cellular indexing convention are explicit. The packaged checker reconstructs 16, 72, 96, 24 original faces and 125, 586, 768, 300 orbit-flag cells, verifies signed boundary squares, and replays 300 prescribed elementary collapses leaving a two-dimensional finite CW complex of Euler characteristic seven. Independently of imported connectivity statements, it forms the complete van Kampen presentation from the quotient two-skeleton, contracts a spanning tree, and eliminates all 462 generators by checked unit-occurrence Tietze transformations. Simple connectivity and cellular Euler characteristic then give integral second homology \(\mathbb Z^6\); Hurewicz and homological Whitehead imply the six-sphere wedge. Exhaustive finite verification is appropriate for this exact finite object. No general-size claim is made.

## Originality: PASS within inspected evidence
The 1994 Björner–Lovász–Vrećica–Živaljević theorem proves chessboard connectivity but not this quotient. A published symmetric-board result classifies the transpose **fixed-point** complex, not its ordinary orbit space. The closely related five-by-five transpose result computes its three-local type and torsion cancellation rather than the integral four-by-four homotopy type. Published work on alternative row, column or diagonal actions is also not equivalent. Searches for chessboard, rook placement, inverse-partial-permutation, bipartition-swapping matching complex, transpose quotient, and the six-sphere formula found no result with the claimed object, action and implication. Absence from indexed searches is not proof of universal publication priority: older unindexed work and equivalences remain risks.

## Value: PASS
This is the smallest balanced simply-connected chessboard case and its row–column exchange is a canonical involution with a noncontractible fixed locus. The exact quotient classification settles its full integral homotopy type, not just a rational invariant or Euler characteristic, and exhibits a contrast between its \(15\)-dimensional covering degree-two homology, the three-circle fixed space, and the six-sphere orbit space. The naturality of the involution and the finite exact homotopy classification motivate the precise invariant without needing a uniform theorem for all boards.

## Limits and comparison
The result depends on a standalone explicit exact-combinatorial certificate; the source code and data must both be retained to reproduce its finite part. Fixed loci, Borel quotients and larger boards are outside the scope. The published fixed-locus and five-by-five quotient findings are not implications of the new claim and do not imply it, based on their inspected statements and proofs. A differently named unindexed equivalent remains a publication-priority risk; no independent audit has occurred.
