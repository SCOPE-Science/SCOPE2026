# The two-quadric compactification of the sextic composition locus

## Finding

Let \(W=\langle t,t^2,t^3,t^4,t^6\rangle_{\mathbb C}\) and let \(R\subset\mathbb P(W)\cong\mathbb P^4\) be the **reduced projective closure** of the normalized monic centered sextics whose invariant Bezoutian is reducible. Write a point of \(\mathbb P(W)\) as \([h:A:B:C:D]\), representing \(ht^6+At^4+Bt^3+Ct^2+Dt\); the normalized affine slice is \(h=1\). Then

\[
R=V(AB-2hD,\;2BC-AD)\subset\mathbb P^4.
\]

This is a reduced, reducible complete intersection of two quadrics. Its two irreducible components are the plane \(P=V(B,D)\) and the smooth rational cubic scroll \(S\cong\mathbb F_1\), whose ideal is generated by the \(2\times2\) minors of

\[
\begin{pmatrix}A&2C&D\\2h&A&B\end{pmatrix}.
\]

They meet scheme-theoretically along the smooth conic

\[
K=V(B,D,A^2-4hC)\subset\mathbb P^4.
\]

At every point of \(K\), including the unique point of \(K\) at infinity \([0:0:0:1:0]\), the completed local ring of \(R\) is analytically a smooth one-dimensional factor times an ordinary node, \(\mathbb C[[s,u,v]]/(uv)\). There are no other singularities, and \(\operatorname{Sing}(R)\) is scheme-theoretically the reduced conic \(K\). The components have generic intersection multiplicity one along \(K\).

The degree is four, the section-ring Hilbert series is \((1+z)^2/(1-z)^3\), the Hilbert polynomial is \(2n^2+2n+1\), and the dualizing sheaf is \(\omega_R\cong\mathcal O_R(-1)\). Thus this is a (reducible and nonnormal) Gorenstein anticanonically polarized degree-four surface with hyperplane sectional arithmetic genus one.

## Assumptions and scope

The ground field is \(\mathbb C\); all closures and singularity schemes are taken in the fixed coefficient projective space \(\mathbb P(W)\). The polynomial has no \(t^5\) term and no constant term. Composition is by monic centered factors of degrees at least two. The previously known criterion for reducibility of the invariant Bezoutian identifies the affine locus set-theoretically with the two distinct composition components \(\mathcal D_{3,2}\) and \(\mathcal D_{2,3}\). We use the reduced closure of that locus and do not assert that an independently chosen nonreduced Bezoutian resultant scheme equals it.

## Proof

**1. The two parameter spaces.** A normalized composition of an outer cubic with an inner quadratic has the form \(G(t^2)\), where \(G(u)=u^3+\alpha u^2+\beta u\). Its coordinates are \([1:\alpha:0:\beta:0]\), dense in the plane \(P=V(B,D)\).

An outer quadratic applied to an inner cubic has the form

\[
(t^3+bt)^2+a(t^3+bt)
=t^6+2bt^4+at^3+b^2t^2+abt.
\]

Consequently its affine component is parameterized by \([1:2b:a:b^2:ab]\). Put

\[
F=A^2-4hC,\quad Q_1=AB-2hD,\quad Q_2=2BC-AD.
\]

All three vanish on the parameterization. They are precisely the minors of the displayed \(2\times3\) matrix. The projective rank-one locus of that matrix is a hyperplane section of the Segre threefold \(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^2\subset\mathbb P^5\), where the hyperplane equates two diagonal matrix entries. Its defining bilinear form has rank two, so this is a smooth irreducible cubic scroll \(S\cong\mathbb F_1\). On \(h=1\) its defining equations give \(C=A^2/4\) and \(D=AB/2\); hence this open subset is exactly the affine quadratic-after-cubic component. In particular the determinantal scroll is its whole projective closure, not an additional component at infinity.

**2. Exact union ideal.** Let \(I=(Q_1,Q_2)\). The polynomial identities

\[
AQ_1-2hQ_2=BF,\qquad 2CQ_1-AQ_2=DF
\]

show that any zero of \(I\) either has \(B=D=0\) or has \(F=0\), in which case it belongs to \(S\). Conversely \(S\cup P\subset V(I)\). The quadrics \(Q_1,Q_2\) are relatively prime, so \(I\) has height two and is a homogeneous complete-intersection ideal. On the open set where \(F\ne0\), the pair \((Q_1,Q_2)\) is an invertible linear system in \((B,D)\) with determinant \(-F\), giving the reduced plane \(P\). On the open set where \((B,D)\ne(0,0)\), the displayed identities imply \(F\in I\) locally, giving the reduced smooth scroll \(S\). Thus \(I\) is generically reduced on both of its irreducible components. A complete-intersection quotient is Cohen–Macaulay and has no embedded associated primes; generic reducedness therefore implies it is radical. We conclude \(I=I(P)\cap I(S)\), with the given reduced scheme structure.

**3. Intersection and singularities.** On the plane \(P\), the determinantal equations reduce to \(F=A^2-4hC=0\). This cuts out a smooth conic in \(\mathbb P^2_{[h:A:C]}\), and the equality of ideals above shows it is the scheme-theoretic intersection. Outside the conic exactly one of the two smooth components is present, hence \(R\) is smooth.

In the affine chart \(h=1\), eliminate \(D=AB/2\) from \(Q_1\). The remaining equation is \(B(4C-A^2)=0\). Setting \(u=B\) and \(v=4C-A^2\) gives the completed local normal-crossing model \(\mathbb C[[A,u,v]]/(uv)\) at every point of \(K\) in this chart. The conic has exactly one point on \(h=0\), namely \([0:0:0:1:0]\). In the chart \(C=1\), eliminate \(B=AD/2\) from \(Q_2\). The remaining equation is \(D(A^2-4h)=0\). Since \(A^2-4h\) is a local coordinate near that point, this is again \(\mathbb C[[A,u,v]]/(uv)\), now with \(u=D\), \(v=A^2-4h\). The Jacobian ideal of the normal-crossing equation is \((u,v)\), hence its singular scheme is the smooth reduced conic. This also proves intersection multiplicity one, including at infinity.

**4. Invariants.** A type-\((2,2)\) complete intersection in \(\mathbb P^4\) has degree \(4\) and homogeneous section-ring Hilbert series \((1-z^2)^2/(1-z)^5=(1+z)^2/(1-z)^3\). Expansion gives Hilbert polynomial \(2n^2+2n+1\). Adjunction for Gorenstein complete intersections gives \(\omega_R\cong\mathcal O_R(2+2-5)=\mathcal O_R(-1)\). The difference of consecutive Hilbert polynomials is \(4n\), giving a general hyperplane section of degree four and arithmetic genus one.

## Relationship to prior work

Piene–Shapiro establish the normalized invariant-Bezoutian reducibility criterion, identify the two degree-six components, compute their separate degrees one and three, and describe their affine collision locus. Their Section 10 explicitly separates the singularities at infinity and intersection multiplicities of projective components as further questions. These established results are the starting hypotheses, not claimed as new. The additional result here is the exact two-quadric ideal of the *combined projective locus*, its component gluing along a smooth conic (including infinity), its normal-crossing scheme structure, and Gorenstein Hilbert data.

The classical geometry of a plane and cubic scroll inside a degenerate intersection of two quadrics is not claimed to be new in isolation. The identification of that geometry with the specific normalized sextic composition locus is the mathematical assertion. In prior literature on decomposable polynomial spaces, von zur Gathen–Matera study affine composition parametrizations and density rather than this particular projective singular scheme.

## Limitations

This theorem concerns only degree six in characteristic zero, the reduced scheme closure, and the specified monic-centered normalization; it is not a universal result for arbitrary composition degree or other compactifications. The invariant-Bezoutian polynomial's own scheme-theoretic resultant structure is not determined. Historical priority remains qualified because unindexed classical small-degree elimination and degeneration calculations may exist. No independent mathematical audit is claimed.
