# Mathematical assessment: the sextic composition compactification

## Correctness — PASS

The object is the reduced closure of a monic centered characteristic-zero composition locus in the specified coefficient \(\mathbb P^4\). Both component maps are reconstructed coefficient by coefficient. The scroll's three determinantal equations are verified; the two displayed syzygies imply its union with the plane is exactly the support of a height-two complete intersection. Generic reducedness plus Cohen–Macaulayness establishes equality of ideals, rather than merely comparing degrees. Explicit affine charts show analytically the ordinary crossing at every conic point, including the single point at infinity; this suffices for the exact singular scheme and multiplicity one. The dualizing and Hilbert assertions follow from complete-intersection formulas. The exact symbolic checker verifies identities and samples, but a finite sample is not used in lieu of a proof.

## Originality — PASS, with historical priority risk

Piene–Shapiro (arXiv:2611.16763v1, §§5–7, 9–10) provide the reducibility-components, their individual degrees and affine Ritt intersections, while expressly singling out projective singularities at infinity and intersection multiplicities as separate questions. The new precise contribution is the *combined projective* two-quadric scheme and reduced conic gluing, including the point at infinity. General prior plane-plus-scroll complete intersections do not establish this identification. von zur Gathen–Matera (arXiv:1407.0906) describe affine decomposition densities, not this claimed projective singular scheme. Targeted mathematical literature and accessible research-record searches checked name variants and equivalent complete-intersection, del Pezzo and determinantal formulations, with no matching theorem. Generic classical linkage and unindexed small-degree calculations remain publication-priority risks.

## Value — PASS

The first case in which distinct sextic polynomial decomposition components coexist is a canonical test for the recent degree-and-intersection theory's explicit projective boundary question. Identifying the entire reducible locus as an anticanonically polarized Gorenstein complete intersection, rather than two separately counted components, establishes its scheme, normal-crossing boundary, exact intersection multiplicity and Hilbert invariants. These are structural and naturally motivated geometric facts, not an arbitrary numerical slice.

## Limits

Only the reduced closure in the stated coefficient compactification and characteristic zero are covered. No result on nonreduced presentation by Bezoutian resultants, general degrees, or independent validation is asserted.
