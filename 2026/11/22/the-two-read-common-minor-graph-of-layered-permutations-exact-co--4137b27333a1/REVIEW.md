# Scientific review: common-minor graph of layered permutations

**Correctness — PASS.** Layered permutation minors map precisely to the two operations of decrementing a non-singleton composition part or deleting a singleton. The terminal-one binary encoding reduces all two-read edges to the unrestricted alternating-interval lemma together with two necessary boundary conditions. Both local transformations follow, and all subsequent generating functions count unique marked moves or component signatures. Independent direct-composition and binary-word programs agreed through \(n=11\). Risks: the infinite result rests on the stated boundary-admissibility argument rather than on finite enumeration; this argument and the two endpoint cases were reconstructed explicitly.

**Originality — PASS, qualified.** Full-deck reconstruction (Vatter, 2006), unrestricted binary two-read intersections (Cai et al., 2020), generalized-subword-order Möbius functions (McNamara–Sagan, 2011), and interval topology (McNamara–Steingrímsson, 2013) do not state the restricted graph's complete component signatures, Fibonacci-product component sizes or component-count rational series. Searches included published mathematical records and published-finding corpus with composition, layered-permutation, common-lower-cover, alternate-interval and Fibonacci aliases. The edge coefficient sequence \(1,3,8,20,48,\ldots\) is already OEIS A001792/A049610; numerical novelty is **not** asserted. Risks: an unindexed rank-level graph description could subsume the classification, and the Raykova full text was not available for inspection in this review.

**Value — PASS.** A natural previously studied reconstruction source has an exact all-length ambiguity component classification, a constructive canonical form for every nontrivial gap, and exact component/isolated-vertex enumeration. These are global facts about how local two-read ambiguities propagate across the entire source, not arbitrary finite small cases, and support decomposition of further codebook/decoding questions. The result does not imply optimal independent-set sizes.
