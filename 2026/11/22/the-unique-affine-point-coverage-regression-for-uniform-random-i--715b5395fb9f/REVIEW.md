# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The claim has a direct finite-sample proof from the independent three-way classification of intervals relative to a fixed point. Conditional pair intersection probabilities \(1,2/3,2/3-c^2/(3s^2)\) yield the exact regression. One-edge, shared-vertex-edge, and disjoint-edge configurations give the exact covariance and variance. The unique affine point follows from a strictly positive second difference off the midpoint; the unique optimal predictor follows from strict monotonicity of \(c^3/(1-c)\). A separate dependency-free exact-rational checker passed 522 assertions, including finite state enumeration. The asymptotic statement is a direct limit of the proven rational formula.

## Originality — PASS, with disclosed historical uncertainty
The strongest inspected background is Diaconis–Holmes–Janson (2011), *Interval graph limits*, Sections 2.3, 4 and 7, and Iliopoulos (2015), *Random Interval Graphs*, Sections 3.3, 4.2 and 5.1. They provide the model, the known edge density, and midpoint coverage maximum, not the claimed conditional finite-size polynomial or the precise one-location prediction efficiency. Searches for alternative formulations including midpoint coverage, depth, clique count, conditional edge density, and the coefficient \(5/8\) revealed no dominating statement. Scheinerman's 1988 original article remained unavailable for full comparison, so possible earlier coverage is a substantive priority risk; novelty is not independently certified.

## Value — PASS
Point coverage is the natural local clique statistic of interval graphs. The theorem exhibits an exact conditional linearization only at the spatial median and shows it explains an asymptotically nontrivial \(5/8\) of fluctuations of a global edge statistic. This is a quantitative local-to-global prediction statement beyond the classical marginal mean or clique-size law, with a complete parameter classification rather than one arbitrary parameter value.

## Constraints and further validation
The result concerns iid endpoint intervals and fixed interior locations; it is not a claim about all random interval graph ensembles. An independent specialist literature audit, especially of the 1988 original article, remains warranted. No infinite-sample convergence is inferred from enumeration.
