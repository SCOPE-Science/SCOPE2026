# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The type-to-face bijection is justified by the finite-diagram extension property of the countable homogeneous \(K_4\)-free graph. Exhaustive labelled enumeration tests all \(2^{\binom n2}\) simple graphs at every base order \(0\le n\le6\), filters forbidden four-cliques, counts every possible named adjacency subset, and applies exact cross-multiplied integer inequalities. It produces exactly \(72\) bad labelled six-vertex bases, with degree sequence \((3,3,3,3,3,5)\), necessarily the pentagonal wheel. The wheel's polynomial and the rational \(1/72\) gap follow independently by its hub/rim decomposition. No inference is made beyond six vertices.

## Originality — PASS, with historical-priority risk
The closest source for the model-theoretic side is Conant's `arxiv:1401.1570v1`, whose Section 3 provides diagram-realization facts rather than the new normalized rank classification. Ghosh–Selvaraja's `arxiv:2602.09623v1` introduces the exact \(\mathrm{CF}_3\) viewpoint but studies shellability and chordality; its result does not imply this sharp cutoff. Xie–Xu's `arxiv:2408.09152v2` concerns a different edge-dependence polynomial and does not cover triangle-free induced subsets. A published Shrikhande clique-free-complex calculation concerns homotopy, not rank-sequence log-concavity. Several searches for the wheel, Henson one-type polynomials, binomial normalization, and clique-free f-vector counterexamples found no dominating prior statement. Undetected or historically unindexed exact tables are still possible; this is not independently established priority.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Uniformly choosing a size-\(k\) potential neighbor set produces an elementary and natural success probability, namely \(f_k/\binom nk\). Binomial log-concavity is a structural constraint of interest for combinatorial independence systems. This theorem determines its first failure among all bases of a natural homogeneous structure and classifies its unique minimal obstruction, rather than reporting a single arbitrary coefficient or computational sample.

## Limitations
The complete census stops at six named vertices. The theorem is about binomial normalization by the ambient vertex number, not the polynomial degree. The unnormalized wheel coefficient sequence is log-concave. No claim is made for all larger bases, other forbidden-clique sizes, or probability sampling of elements from the infinite structure. The mathematical review here is not an independent audit.
