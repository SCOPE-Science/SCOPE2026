# The unique six-point source of nonunique quotient weak cores

## Finding
Let \(X\) range over all finite \(T_0\) Alexandrov spaces with at most six points. A *quotient weak core* is the codomain \(Y\) of a surjective quotient map \(X\to Y\) inducing a weak homotopy equivalence, with \(Y\) admitting no non-homeomorphic further weak-equivalence quotient. Then two non-homeomorphic quotient weak cores of \(X\) exist **if and only if** \(X\) is the six-point height-two poset whose covering graph is \(K_{3,3}\) with two disjoint edges removed. This source has exactly two quotient weak cores, up to homeomorphism, namely the five-point posets \(P_{2,3}\) and \(P_{3,2}\) in which all elements in the first antichain are below all elements of the second. Moreover, it has exactly one quotient kernel onto each of these two five-point spaces.

In particular six points are necessary and sufficient for the failure of quotient weak-core uniqueness among finite \(T_0\) Alexandrov spaces; at this first size, the source is unique up to homeomorphism. The existence of a six-point example with two cores was established by Barmak--Galotto (Example 23). The sharpness, classification of the *source*, and the uniqueness of the two reduction kernels at the threshold are the claims here.

## Assumptions and scope
The order on every finite Alexandrov space is the specialization partial order; open sets are downsets. A quotient topology is the **final topology** of the surjection, not merely the topology induced from an arbitrary monotone map. All homotopy groups are understood componentwise; nonconnected spaces are included. Two quotient maps with the same partition of source points have the same quotient topology and are counted as one kernel. The theorem concerns spaces with \(1\) through \(6\) points; the empty space, if admitted, has only its identity quotient and causes no exception. The primary MSC2020 class is 55P99.

Write \(P_{a,b}=A_a\oplus A_b\) for the ordinal sum of two antichains. The exceptional source can be displayed with minimal elements \(\{0,1,2\}\), maximal elements \(\{3,4,5\}\), and all relations generated by
\[
0<3,\;0<4,\;0<5,\;1<3,\;1<5,\;2<3,\;2<4.
\]
In the first quotient identify \(1\sim2\), producing \(P_{2,3}\); in the second identify \(4\sim5\), producing \(P_{3,2}\). Both target order complexes are the graph \(K_{2,3}\simeq S^1\vee S^1\). They are not homeomorphic as \(T_0\) spaces because they have different numbers of minimal points.

## Proof
**1. Minimal targets.** Every quotient weak core is \(T_0\): otherwise passing to its Kolmogorov quotient is a proper quotient homotopy equivalence. It has no Stong beat point: deleting a beat point is a proper quotient homotopy equivalence. The enumeration below is exhaustive up to cardinality five, because every finite poset admits a linear extension, hence one of the upper-triangular relation masks checked by the script. Repeated beat deletion yields, on at most five points, only the following cores: discrete antichains, the four-point circle poset \(P_{2,2}\), the disjoint union \(P_{2,2}\sqcup\{*\}\), and the two five-point connected posets \(P_{2,3}\), \(P_{3,2}\). The listed spaces have different weak homotopy types, **except** for the last pair, both modeling two circles wedged at a point. Thus two non-homeomorphic quotient weak cores of a space with at most six points must be exactly \(P_{2,3}\) and \(P_{3,2}\). In particular a space of at most five points cannot map onto both: a five-point quotient to either must be a homeomorphism, and the targets differ.

For clarity, the targets are minimal even among arbitrary finite Alexandrov quotients: both have \(H_1\cong\mathbb Z^2\) and are connected. A finite \(T_0\) space on at most four points has \(\mathrm{rank}\,H_1\le1\), as the exhaustive beat-free list also shows. Any non-\(T_0\) proper weak-equivalence quotient would admit a further Kolmogorov quotient. Consequently a proper weak-equivalence quotient of either target cannot exist.

**2. Complete six-point search.** If a six-point \(X\) admits both target cores, each quotient onto a five-point target identifies exactly one pair of its points. For any chosen pair, first collapse the pair in the underlying set, then take the transitive closure of the induced order relation on the five classes. This is exactly the quotient preorder for the finite Alexandrov final topology. Discard preorders that fail \(T_0\). The embedded checker enumerates **all \(4{,}824\) naturally labeled six-point posets** (representatives across linear extensions, not all labeled posets), and all their \(15\) single-pair partitions. Exactly \(33\) extension-labeled inputs have quotients of *both* shapes. They form **three** isomorphism classes of connected height-two posets: one with seven covering edges, one with eight, and one with nine. Their order complexes are connected graphs with respective first Betti ranks \(2,3,4\), since for each \(\beta_1=|E|-|V|+1\). Because either target has first Betti rank \(2\), only the seven-edge class can have both weak-equivalence quotients. The checker independently canonicalizes these inputs under all \(6!\) vertex permutations and finds precisely one seven-edge isomorphism class, the stated two-edge deletion of \(K_{3,3}\). In that class there is precisely one pair-identification kernel onto each target, verified uniformly across the enumerated inputs.

**3. Actual weak equivalences.** Both quotient maps from the seven-edge graph induce isomorphisms of fundamental groups, not just equal Betti ranks. To see this for \(1\sim2\), use the source graph spanning tree with edges \((0,3),(0,4),(0,5),(1,3),(2,3)\) and the target spanning tree \((0,3),(0,4),(0,5),(\{1,2\},3)\). The two source non-tree edges \((1,5),(2,4)\) map bijectively to the two target non-tree edges, and their fundamental loop generators map to the respective fundamental loop generators (up to orientation). Thus the induced map is a free-group isomorphism. Both order complexes are connected graphs, so it is a weak homotopy equivalence. The second quotient \(4\sim5\) follows by interchanging the two levels of the displayed graph. Naturality of McCord's weak equivalences transfers these facts to the finite Alexandrov quotient maps. Both targets are quotient weak cores by step 1.

## Verification
Run `python3 check_weak_core_threshold.py` using only the Python 3 standard library. It checks transitively closed orders for each upper-triangular mask; recursively removes beat points; constructs every one-pair quotient by explicit transitive closure; rejects nonantisymmetric quotient preorders; canonicalizes all possible six-point double-target sources under full permutation of vertices; counts covering edges and graph Betti numbers; and tests the exact two target kernels for every extremal source. Its expected terminal output ends in `PASS`. The printed counts for sizes \(1\) through \(6\) are \(1,2,7,40,357,4824\), respectively; among the \(33\) double-target inputs, ranks \(2,3,4\) occur \(22,10,1\) times, respectively. Counts are across linear-extension-compatible labelings; full relabeling distinguishes the three isomorphism classes.

The finite enumeration is exhaustive **because** each finite poset admits an extension-compatible labeling, the mask loop traverses all such relations, and the code imposes transitivity explicitly. It is not a replacement for the separate graph-level weak-equivalence argument.

## Relationship to prior work
Barmak--Galotto, *Directed systems of A-spaces and weak cores* (2026), define quotient weak cores and present the six-point nonuniqueness example in their Example 23. Their example directly implies **existence** at six points; existence is not claimed as new here. Their paper does not assert that six is smallest or that this is the only six-point source, nor does it classify its two single-pair quotient kernels. Barmak--Minian, *Minimal Finite Models* (2007), Theorem 4.7, characterize minimal finite models of wedges of circles; this gives the five-point minimum for a wedge of two circles and is consistent with the two small targets, but does not classify their common six-point quotient sources. Mosquera-Lois (2026) studies Whitehead-type properties of minimal finite models, not this simultaneous quotient classification. The nonuniqueness threshold and source/kernel rigidity are the additional assertions.

## Limitations
No classification is claimed beyond six points, or for different notions of *weak core* based on deleting weak points instead of minimal weak-equivalence quotients. The source is unique only **at the first cardinality of nonuniqueness**. The analysis concerns weak homotopy equivalence, not finite-space homotopy equivalence. A mathematically equivalent classification could occur in unindexed earlier literature; no independent mathematical audit has been performed.
