# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The graph and four face predicates are explicit. The four actual packaged operation lists were checked by an independent standalone reconstruction of all faces, every free codimension-one simplicial pair, every critical maximal face, and the entire directed Hasse graph for acyclicity. The signatures force the four claimed integral homotopy types by discrete Morse theory, not by field-homology inference. The filtration's positive-degree maps vanish because successive stages support their homology in different dimensions. Signed Lefschetz sums over all sixteen symmetries yield the stated dihedral character tables and irreducible rational decomposition. No unproved extension beyond this finite graph is used.

**Originality — PASS against inspected evidence.** The 2023 primary forest-filtration paper handles different families and contains no Wagner stage-two or terminal classification. The 2021 primary higher-independence paper concerns chordal graphs and trees, not the nonchordal Wagner graph's forest stages. The closest publicly indexed 2026 five-Möbius-ladder computation already proves the degree-one forest stage and its character: these prior results are explicitly excluded from novelty. Semantic searches over the accessible mathematical-record index and existing claim ledger did not produce an equivalent or dominating statement for the two new stages plus dihedral characters. Risk remains from unindexed or inaccessible equivalents; absence of a search hit is not a proof of uniqueness.

**Value — PASS.** The exact seven-sphere burst at the degree-two stage, followed by a single four-sphere, resolves the entire vertex-induced forest filtration on a canonical nonplanar cubic graph and determines its integer persistence. The seven-dimensional dihedral character supplies structural information not captured by sphere counts, including a nonzero full-group invariant at degree three and a rotationally odd terminal generator. This is a motivated finite classification, not an arbitrary parameter slice or a recomputation of the already known degree-one stage.
