# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

For unweighted wheels the exact forbidden geodesic triples give a simplicial union of the two-independence complex of the rim and the cone on the embedded rim cycle. This is a homotopy cofiber, with no unverified identification of homology with homotopy. Deshpande and Singh's Theorem 4.6 gives simply connected rim complexes for all rim orders at least six, making the attaching circle nullhomotopic and yielding the integral wedge splitting. The four-rim case kills a primitive free generator in a graph. The five-rim case has six vertices, fifteen edges, ten triangles, every edge in two triangles and every link a circle, hence is a closed connected surface of Euler characteristic one and therefore the real projective plane. The three-rim case is a simplex. Independent exhaustive shortest-path and mod-two chain checks corroborate the first ten rim orders.

## Originality — PASS (best-knowledge, residual risks)

The closest two earlier published wheel general-position studies (2026-11-02 and 2026-11-08) were inspected in full: they classify maximal sets and their enumerators, not the topology of the full general-position complex. The full cycle homotopy classification of Deshpande–Singh (2020) is credited, and the general-position join polynomial theorem of Iršič–Klavžar–Rus–Tuite (2024) counts subsets but does not specify homotopy attachments. The explicit suspension-cofiber identification and the exceptional homeomorphism with the projective plane are distinct from those published statements. Because the stable splitting becomes short once the rim theorem is known, an equivalent unpublished or unindexed corollary is a real priority risk, not a claimed proof of uniqueness.

## Value — PASS

The classification resolves a natural topological invariant of an entire wheel family investigated in the current literature for enumerative reasons. The five-rim wheel becomes the unique genuine integral two-torsion case, a six-vertex triangulated real projective plane, whereas longer wheels have a four-periodic torsion-free sphere-wedge pattern. This is a structural topological transition that a facet polynomial or mod-two census alone does not determine, not an arbitrary small-graph table entry. The result supplies a reusable cone-attachment description and an exact natural boundary for simple connectivity of the rim subcomplex.
