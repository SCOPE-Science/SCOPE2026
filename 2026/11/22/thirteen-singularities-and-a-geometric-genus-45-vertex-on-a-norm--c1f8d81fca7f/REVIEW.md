# Review of a degree-nine abelian surface singularity basket

## Correctness
PASS. The nine dual-Hesse lines have precisely twelve triple points; the projective Jacobian calculation gives precisely those twelve singularities plus the vertex. All twelve are cubic cones over smooth elliptic curves, with local genus one. The finite cyclic triple cover has Euler characteristic \(12\), and its resolution has characteristic zero. A degree-nine hypersurface has Euler characteristic \(57\); since the two models are birational, the local Leray defect at the remaining vertex is \(45\). The proof states the needed smoothness and avoidance assumptions on the conic.

## Originality
PASS with priority limitations. The explicit normal degree-nine model, dual-Hesse branch arrangement and existence of the vertex are already in the 2026 source. The 2015 paper supplies earlier dual-Hesse geometry. The comparison is of implications, not titles: neither inspected source reports the vertex's local geometric genus or the full 13-point basket of the 2026 model. Targeted indexed searches found no identical or stronger calculation. Residual risk: unindexed singularity calculations or uninspected appendices could contain an equivalent formula.

## Value
PASS. The new normal abelian-surface model carries a large local defect concentrated at one point, and its exact geometric genus \(45\) explains how the arithmetic genus \(57\) of a degree-nine hypersurface reconciles with a resolution birational to an abelian surface. The full basket is a natural local invariant of the explicitly introduced model; the number is not an arbitrary choice of coefficients or dimension.

## Limitations
The analytic resolution graph at the vertex is not computed. Numerical local genus is established for every quadratic in the declared open set, rather than by checking one specialization. Local proof and finite arithmetic tests are separate. Absolute literature priority and independent external audit are not claimed.
