# Three embedded wild fixed points of a transposition on the quintic del Pezzo surface

## Finding

Let \(S=\overline M_{0,5}\) be the split degree-five del Pezzo surface over \(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z\), and let \(\tau=(45)\) exchange the last two markings. Its **complete scheme-theoretic fixed locus** \(F=\operatorname{Fix}_S(\tau)\) is the scheme-theoretic union of a horizontal rational curve \(D\cong\mathbb P^1_{\mathbb Z}\) and three horizontal sections \(Q_0,Q_1,Q_\infty\cong\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z\). The sections are mutually disjoint, and
\[
D\cap Q_a\cong\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb F_2\quad(a\in\{0,1,\infty\}).
\]
The total fixed scheme is flat over \(\mathbb Z\). Over every algebraically closed field of characteristic different from two, its geometric fixed locus consists of the rational curve and three reduced isolated points. Over a field \(k\) of characteristic two, its underlying topological space is only the rational curve, but at each of three distinct points it has the local algebra
\[
\mathcal O_{F_k,P}\cong k[x,t]_{(x,t)}/(xt,t^2),
\]
with completed local algebra \(k[[x,t]]/(xt,t^2)\). Thus each point contributes a length-one embedded nilradical \((t)\cong k\), and the special fiber has three embedded associated points. The ambient derivative of \(\tau\) at each collision is the identity, although \(\tau\) is nontrivial as an automorphism of the formal surface. Consequently this action is not formally linearizable at any of these three wild points.

In particular, for a finite field \(\mathbb F_q\),
\[
\#F(\mathbb F_q)=\begin{cases}q+1,&\operatorname{char}\mathbb F_q=2,\\q+4,&\operatorname{char}\mathbb F_q\ne2.\end{cases}
\]
These are counts of geometric points over the field, not scheme lengths.

## Assumptions and scope

We use the standard integral model
\[
S=\operatorname{Bl}_{\{(0,0),(1,1),(\infty,\infty)\}}
\bigl(\mathbb P^1_{\mathbb Z}\times\mathbb P^1_{\mathbb Z}\bigr).
\]
It is the stable five-pointed-rational-curve compactification: after sending the first three marks to \(0,1,\infty\), the remaining two coordinates are \(u,v\). The three blowups resolve simultaneous collisions of these two coordinates with the fixed marks. The ten stable boundary divisors are the six coordinate fibers, the three exceptional curves and the strict transform of the diagonal. The involution \(\tau\) is induced by \((u,v)\mapsto(v,u)\). The assertion is integral and includes the ramified prime \(2\); it is not restricted to reduced geometric fixed points. No claim is made for non-split forms of the surface or for quotients by \(\tau\).

## Proof

**1. Exhaustive fixed-point support.** Before blowing up, the exchange of factors of \(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1\) fixes exactly its diagonal, scheme-theoretically. At a blown-up center \((a,a)\), use identical local coordinates \(x=u-a\), \(y=v-a\) (at infinity use reciprocal coordinates) and the blowup chart \(y=rx\). On the chart where \(r\) is invertible, the lifted exchange acts by
\[
\tau(x,r)=(rx,r^{-1}).
\]
On the exceptional divisor, a fixed point is an invariant direction of the coordinate swap, hence satisfies \(r^2=1\); such directions automatically have \(r\) invertible, so the displayed chart covers every exceptional fixed point. Outside the exceptional divisors, the fixed scheme is the strict transform of the diagonal. There are no additional points in the other blowup charts.

**2. Exact integral ideal.** In this invertible-slope chart, the defining equalizer ideal is generated by \(rx-x\) and \(r^{-1}-r\). Since \(r\) is a unit, it is exactly
\[
I_F=(x(r-1),(r-1)(r+1)).
\]
Write \(t=r-1\). Near the intersection of the diagonal's strict transform with the exceptional curve, \(1+t\) is a unit, and the equations become
\[
I_F=(xt,t(t+2))=(t)\cap(x,t+2)
\quad\text{inside }\mathbb Z[x,t,(1+t)^{-1}].
\]
The last ideal equality uses the fact that multiplication by \(2\) is injective in \(\mathbb Z\): if \(tg\) belongs to \((x,t+2)\), evaluating at \(x=0,t=-2\) gives \(-2g(0,-2)=0\), hence \(g\in(x,t+2)\). The ideal \((t)\) defines the proper transform \(D\) of the diagonal, and \((x,t+2)\) defines a section \(Q_a\) at the opposite exceptional direction \(r=-1\). The global fixed scheme is their scheme-theoretic union, with one such \(Q_a\) at each of the three disjoint blowup centers. The sections meet the diagonal only above \(2=0\), and those intersections are \(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb F_2\). In particular \(D\cong\mathbb P^1_{\mathbb Z}\), while each \(Q_a\cong\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z\).

**3. Flatness and fibers.** As a \(\mathbb Z\)-module the local coordinate algebra
\[
A=\mathbb Z[x,t]/(xt,t^2+2t)
\]
is the direct sum of \(\mathbb Z[x]\) and the free abelian summand \(\mathbb Zt\), using \(xt=0\) and \(t^2=-2t\). Thus it has no integral torsion. Other charts are smooth diagonal charts or disjoint reduced sections; all local rings are torsion-free over the PID \(\mathbb Z\), proving flatness. When \(2\) is invertible, \(r=1\) and \(r=-1\) are disjoint, giving one smooth fixed divisor and three isolated reduced sections. In characteristic two the local ideal is \((xt,t^2)\), whose nilradical is \((t)\), annihilated by \((x,t)\), with length one. Its reduced quotient is \(k[x]_{(x)}\); its maximal-ideal tangent space has dimension two. The three points lie on different exceptional curves and are therefore distinct. This proves both the support and the complete special-fiber scheme structure.

**4. Tangent and formal action.** In the same chart the automorphism in coordinates \((x,t)\) is
\[
(x,t)\longmapsto\left(x(1+t),-\frac{t}{1+t}\right).
\]
Its derivative at the origin is \(\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)\), which specializes to the identity matrix in characteristic two. The nonzero higher-order term \(x\mapsto x+xt\) shows the formal automorphism is not the identity. Were it formally conjugate to its linear part, it would be conjugate to the identity and hence itself the identity, contradiction. This proves non-linearizability.

**5. Point-count corollary.** Over \(\mathbb F_q\) the fixed reduced support is a split \(\mathbb P^1\), with three additional rational isolated points precisely in odd characteristic. Nilpotents contribute no additional field-valued points. The displayed finite-field count follows for every prime-power \(q\).

## Verification

The independently runnable, self-contained chart-and-enumeration verifier `verify_fixed.py` requires Python 3 and SymPy. It checks the involution's algebraic formulas, the exact fixed ideal in local coordinates, its characteristic-two quotient relations, the identity derivative and a complete pointwise enumeration of the blowup and its factor-swap fixed points over \(\mathbb F_p\) for \(p\in\{2,3,5,7,11,13\}\). Running `python3 verify_fixed.py` returned `M05_TRANSPOSITION_WILD_EMBEDDED_FIXED_SCHEME_VERIFIED` and the fixed counts \(3,7,9,11,15,17\) in that order. The proof above, rather than just the finite tests, establishes the integral claim and every extension-field statement.

## Relationship to prior work

Boitrel, *Del Pezzo surfaces of degree 5 over perfect fields* (arXiv:2304.05328, first public 2023-04-11), gives the arithmetic surface models and automorphism groups, but does not calculate this full integral equalizer scheme or its embedded characteristic-two structure. Dolgachev–Martin (arXiv:2303.16170) give the characteristic-free degree-five automorphism-group background within their classification, not these wild fixed-scheme equations. Bauer–Catanese (2020) give equivariant equations and the action on ten exceptional curves, not the specialization of the transposition fixed scheme. Dolgachev–Farb–Looijenga (2018) analyze the Wiman–Edge pencil and special symmetric orbits on this surface, including fixed curves for different conjugacy classes, but their relevant geometric statements do not assert the three embedded special-fiber points of this transposition. These references establish the naturality of the involution, not the new infinitesimal calculation.

## Limitations

The result is for the split integral surface and one transposition conjugacy class (all transpositions are conjugate under the integral marking action). It does not classify the other involution conjugacy class, wild quotient singularities, equivariant deformation spaces or non-split arithmetic forms. Published-search noncoverage is not a proof of absolute historical novelty; an unindexed fixed-locus calculation remains possible. The algebraic verification and the independent mathematical review are distinct: no independent review has been performed.
