# Scientific review: spherical orthogonal sectors

## Correctness

**PASS.** The spherical-join coordinates give independent uniform \(T,U,V\), and the distance to the nearest boundary is the minimum of the four inward great-hyperplane distances. Integrating \(T\) gives the exact two-variable positive-part formula. The inradius follows by maximizing the admissible \(T\) interval. Expansion at its unique maximizer yields a linear-linear endpoint polytope for two distinct pairs of facets, quadratic-linear when one pair merges, and quadratic-quadratic when both merge, with respective powers \(h^3,h^{5/2},h^2\). The stated coefficients follow from elementary positive-part integrals. The moving-aperture statements retain the quadratic corrections on the scale \(\pi-\alpha\asymp\sqrt h\). All limiting rescaled domains are bounded, supporting dominated-convergence and total-variation claims. The unit-sphere, angular-aperture and probability normalization conventions are explicit. The accompanying finite-threshold checker passes, but numerical samples are not relied on as infinite proofs.

## Originality

**PASS, historical priority provisional.** The 2013 spherical-width foundation defines supporting hemispheres and spherical lunes and proves width properties, not random inner-clearance laws. The later spherical constant-width treatment likewise does not evaluate the orthogonal-join inner parallel volume, its three endpoint orders, its critical-window integrals or its Dirichlet marked limit. A Euclidean inner-tube formula for polytopes is not an implication of these statements on the three-sphere; its piecewise-polynomial framework does not imply the curved spherical formulas. The closest published work about orthogonal joins concerns **nearest vertices** rather than **nearest boundary facets**. Previously published spherical-lune results analyze a single lune or its pairwise chord moments, not this two-factor joined geometry. The argument provides direct calculations for the new observables. Search incompleteness leaves a nonzero historical-priority risk from general spherical inner-tube literature; no unconditional uniqueness claim is made.

## Value

**PASS.** The orthogonal join of two sectors is a canonical tractable spherical three-polytope with four facets generically and lower facet counts at hemispheric apertures. The finding quantifies a geometric, rather than arbitrary parameter, transition caused by facet merger: the probability exponent changes twice and the critical aperture scale is precisely the square root of the inradius deficit. The two parameter-crossover integrals and the Dirichlet four-slack shape provide reusable local probabilistic descriptions of spherical polytope clearance. They are not a mere restatement of spherical width or a trivial finite enumeration.

## Sources and remaining limits

Lassak, *Width of spherical convex bodies* (first public 2013-12-06), DOI 10.1007/s00010-013-0237-3, provides the archival geometric vocabulary and classification. Lassak–Musielak, *Spherical bodies of constant width* (2018), DOI 10.1007/s00010-018-0558-3, supplies a nearby later treatment. Koçak–Ratiu, *Inner tube formulas for polytopes* (2010 preprint), arXiv:1008.2040, covers the Euclidean, not spherical, setting. The theorem is restricted to the specified spherical orthogonal join under volume-uniform sampling; it gives pointwise, not uniform, aperture-window limits. Independent mathematical validation has not been performed.
