# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

The quartic has no linear factor and its three irreducible-quadratic remainders are t,t+1,t+1. In the field of size 81, the Euler test x^40=1 holds for exactly the 40 nonzero squares, and −1 is one of them. My separate implementation uses Horner field multiplication, this Euler test (explicitly excluding zero), and one full binary label per center for each support. Seeing all 2^k labels is exactly shattering, including when a center is in the support and gives a negative bit there. Counting rooted supports by translation gives k f_k=81 h_k. Enumeration of every zero-containing support through size five gives h_1…h_5=(1,80,3160,81920,587400). For size six it is safe to discard a sorted five-prefix unless every one of its 32 labels is realized by at least two centers: splitting such labels by the last query is necessary. There are 400 eligible prefixes and 5,063 increasing extensions; none is shattered. Thus all other size-six supports are excluded by the necessary condition, and higher ranks are excluded by heredity (also by the 81-center ceiling). The resulting coefficients equal the stated polynomial. Every shattered five-support is maximal. Taking the zero-containing four-subsets of all shattered normalized five-supports detects every possible five-extension for a normalized quartet; it leaves exactly 240 maximal normalized quartets. All 3,160 normalized triples appear in shattered quartets, so there are no additional lower facets. I classified the bad and maximal quartets separately by traversing the orbits generated by an order-40 square multiplier, Frobenius and all reanchorings. Each group image containing zero is a square/Frobenius image reanchored at one of its members. The normalized bad orbit sizes 40,160,40 and maximal orbit sizes 160,80 have exactly the stated representatives. Counting pointed four-supports translates these to sizes 810,3240,810 and 3240,1620. This is a classification under the specified subgroup, not a claim about its index in the full automorphism group.

## originality

PASS

The same-day Paley-49 result is the closest inspected catalogue work: a complete open-neighborhood census including maximal quartets, but over a different field, and it classifies extremal five-support orbits rather than the bad and maximal quartet orbits here. The published Paley-25 computation is also a different field. The primary quadratic-residue work gives general-field asymptotic bounds and numerical searches in prime fields only, using a stricter zero convention in the relevant numerical discussion. Those results do not settle the F81 open-neighborhood coefficients, rank or five stated subgroup orbits. This judgment credits prior methods and nearby finite classifications rather than claiming novelty for enumerating traces itself.

## value

PASS

The Paley graph at a natural small square-field order tests whether the counting ceiling for neighborhood VC dimension is attained. The exact rank-five result falls short of that ceiling, and the quartet census distinguishes two obstructions: some quartets already miss a trace, whereas others realize every trace but cannot be enlarged while preserving that property. Knowing the orbits and their sizes makes this distinction testable for explicit supports and records information that the rank alone or a saturated count cannot provide. This is a natural exact finite invariant, not merely an arbitrary parameter slice of a known table.
