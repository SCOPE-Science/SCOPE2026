# Three exponential layers and red-wedge clustering in a two-forbidden-colour homogeneous graph

## Finding
In the Fraïssé limit of complete graphs with named edge colours red, green and neutral, forbidding monochromatic red and monochromatic green triangles, name a base of \(2m\) vertices whose red edges form a perfect matching and all other base edges are neutral. For each fixed \(r\ge3\) let \(T_r(m)\) count complete ordered injective \(r\)-types avoiding the base. Then, for some \(\rho_r<25/32\), as \(m\to\infty\),
\[T_r(m)=8^{rm}\left(1+\binom r2(7/8)^m+3\binom r3(25/32)^m+O_r(\rho_r^m)\right).\]
The exact all-arity formula is \(T_r(m)=\sum_H b(H)^m\), summing over all labelled three-coloured complete graphs on \(r\) vertices without red or green monochromatic triangles, with \(b(H)\) the number of admissible colourings of the \(2r\) cross edges to one red matched pair. For a random type from this finite set, conditioned on at least two nonneutral edges among the new variables, asymptotically with probability one there are exactly two red edges sharing one endpoint. At arity two, the red/green/neutral channels count exactly \(56^m,47^m,64^m\).
The only internal colourings attaining the three displayed largest bases are, respectively, the all-neutral graph, one red edge with all other edges neutral, and two red edges sharing one endpoint with all other edges neutral. Thus the second-layer multiplicity is \(\binom r2\) and the third-layer multiplicity is \(3\binom r3\).

## Assumptions and scope
The palette consists of three named symmetric irreflexive binary relations \(R,G,B\), exactly one holding for each pair of distinct vertices. A finite complete edge-coloured graph is admissible if no three distinct vertices form a monochromatic \(R\)-triangle or a monochromatic \(G\)-triangle; \(B\) is neutral. The class has hereditary and joint embedding properties. It has strong amalgamation: over any common induced subgraph, colour every new cross-pair \(B\), which creates no forbidden monochromatic triangle. Hence there is a unique countable homogeneous limit. All finite admissible diagrams embed and their orbits over an individually named finite parameter base equal complete nonalgebraic types. The base of size \(2m\), for integer \(m\ge0\), has exactly \(m\) disjoint red edges; its remaining edges are neutral. For the theorem \(r\ge3\) is fixed; all \(r\) new variables are mutually distinct and outside the base.

## Proof
Fix an admissible internal colouring \(H\) on new labels \(1,\ldots,r\). At a single red-matched old pair \(u,v\), each new label \(i\) has an ordered pair \((c(i,u),c(i,v))\) of colours. Its eight permitted states are all members of the nine ordered pairs of colours except \((R,R)\), which would make a forbidden red triangle with \(uv\). An internal red edge \(ij\) forbids both endpoint colours being \(R\) at either old vertex; an internal green edge forbids both being \(G\); an internal neutral edge imposes no condition. Let \(b(H)\) count the length-\(r\) state words satisfying these constraints. For different old matched pairs the constraints are independent because every old edge between pairs is neutral. The complete extension count for this fixed internal graph is precisely \(b(H)^m\), proving the finite exact formula.

The all-neutral pattern has \(b=8^r\). With precisely one red edge, the two participating new vertices have \(56\) permissible paired states: among the eight states of the first, four contain no red coordinate and admit eight choices for the second, while the other four have one red coordinate and admit six choices, giving \(4\cdot8+4\cdot6=56\). Thus \(b=56\cdot8^{r-2}=(7/8)8^r\). With exactly one green edge, direct inclusion-exclusion gives \(b=(8^2-9-9+1)8^{r-2}=47\cdot8^{r-2}\): the nine excluded configurations for each new point attached red to both endpoints, with one in their intersection. With two red edges incident at one new vertex, independently choose the two leaves after the central state: \(4\cdot8^2+4\cdot6^2=400\), giving \(b=400\cdot8^{r-3}=(25/32)8^r\).

To establish the strict hierarchy, observe that adding a nonneutral internal edge never increases \(b\); it decreases it strictly whenever that edge imposes a genuinely new forbidden pair-state condition. The possible two-edge patterns, up to colour and endpoint incidence, have local factors \(400,324,301\) on three new labels (incident red/red, red/green, green/green), and \(3136,2632,2209\) on four labels (disjoint red/red, red/green, green/green). The direct enumerations follow from the eight permitted per-variable pair-states; for example the first is the displayed four-plus-four central-state sum. After normalization to \(8^r\), every two-edge configuration other than an incident red/red pair is strictly below \(25/32\), with the disjoint red/red bound \(3136/4096=49/64\). A single green edge has ratio \(47/64<25/32\). If an internal pattern contains an incident red/red pair and at least one additional nonneutral edge, assign all other old links neutral except those at a chosen endpoint needed to violate the additional constraint while respecting the two red-edge constraints; this shows a strict decrease. Patterns with three or more nonneutral edges and no incident red/red pair are bounded by a two-edge subpattern below \(25/32\). Hence the finite set of remaining internal patterns for each fixed \(r\) has a largest normalized factor \(\rho_r<25/32\). The internal patterns with red/green monochromatic triangles are excluded entirely by the definition of the age. Summing the finitely many terms proves the expansion and its multiplicities.

For the conditional statement, after discarding zero- or one-nonneutral-edge types, the leading terms are precisely \(3\binom r3\) two-red-edge wedges, all at base \(25/32\) relative to \(8^r\), while all other terms have strictly smaller exponential base. Dividing by the corresponding sum proves the conditional concentration.

The accompanying Python 3 standard-library checker enumerates all edge colour assignments on each finite sample without using the preceding formula. It independently verifies local factors \(64,56,47,400,324,301\), and compares direct complete-diagram enumeration with the factorized formula for \((r,m)=(2,1),(2,2),(3,1)\). Recorded output is provided as `checker_output.txt`. The proof, not the finite enumeration, establishes every \(m\) and every fixed \(r\).

## Relationship to prior work
Henson's 1971 homogeneous forbidden-clique construction establishes the graph-theoretic Fraïssé context; Conant's 2015 free-amalgamation treatment provides general model-theoretic background. Neither supplies this particular three-colour, two-forbidden-triangle matching-base spectrum. Previously identified homogeneous Henson-graph extension work treats single uncoloured edge relations and disjoint cliques; its rare-edge-clustering results are structurally analogous, but do not identify the red/green asymmetry, the \(56/47/64\) factors, or the \(25/32\) red-wedge layer in the present coloured age. The exponential-sum identity is a direct finite-amalgamation decomposition; novelty is asserted for the exact first three layers and conditional red-wedge dominance, not for the abstract decomposition alone.

## Limitations
The tuple arity \(r\) is fixed in the asymptotic statement and the base is specifically a red matching with neutral cross-edges. No growing-arity uniform error bound, arbitrary finite-base formula, or independent mathematical certification is claimed. Hidden equivalent coloured-graph enumerations remain a historical-priority risk.
