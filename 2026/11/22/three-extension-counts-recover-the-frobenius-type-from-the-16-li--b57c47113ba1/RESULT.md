# Three extension counts recover the Frobenius type from the 16 lines and 40 squares of a degree-four del Pezzo surface

## Finding
For every smooth degree-four del Pezzo surface \(X/\mathbb F_q\), in any characteristic, let \(L_X\) be the reduced geometric scheme of its sixteen exceptional curves and let \(Q_X\) be the reduced finite scheme of the forty anticanonical divisors that consist of four distinct exceptional curves with square intersection graph. Both are finite étale over \(\mathbb F_q\).

The sixteen-line Frobenius action alone has twelve possible cycle spectra across the eighteen conjugacy classes of \(W(D_5)\). The forty-square action alone has sixteen possible spectra. The ordered pair of actions has eighteen, one for each Weyl conjugacy class. More strongly, the six integers
\[
\bigl(\#L_X(\mathbb F_{q^n}),\#Q_X(\mathbb F_{q^n})\bigr)_{n=1,2,3}
\]
already distinguish all eighteen classes and determine the complete two finite-étale zeta functions.

The cutoff three is minimal on the full Weyl group: two distinct Weyl conjugacy classes, each of size 160, have line spectrum \(2^2 6^2\) and square spectra \(1^1 3^1 6^6\) versus \(1^1 3^5 6^4\). They give the same count pairs \((0,1)\), \((4,1)\) in degrees one and two, but give \((0,4)\) versus \((0,16)\) in degree three. The claim of minimality is for the Weyl-group classification and does not require realizing both classes over each particular finite field.

## Assumptions and scope
The ground field is any finite field, with no restriction on its characteristic. A degree-four del Pezzo surface is a smooth projective geometrically integral surface with ample anticanonical class and \(K_X^2=4\). Over an algebraic closure it is the blowup of the projective plane at five points in general position, with sixteen geometric exceptional \((-1)\)-curves. An *intersection square* is an unordered set of four such curves whose pairwise intersection graph is a chordless four-cycle; their sum is the anticanonical divisor. We count these divisors, not a choice of ordering, a geometric singular point, or a geometric hyperplane containing the surface.

A Frobenius cycle spectrum \(\{r^{c_r}\}\) means \(c_r\) orbits of length \(r\). The associated finite étale scheme has zeta function \(\prod_r(1-T^r)^{-c_r}\), and its number of points over the extension of degree \(n\) is \(\sum_{r\mid n}r c_r\). The conclusions classify possible Frobenius actions; the theorem does not assert existence of every conjugacy type over every specified \(\mathbb F_q\).

## Proof
Use a geometric blowup marking with divisor basis \(H_0,E_1,\ldots,E_5\), where \(H_0^2=1\), \(E_i^2=-1\), and \(-K_X=3H_0-\sum E_i\). The sixteen exceptional classes are
\[
E_i\ (1\le i\le5),\qquad H_0-E_i-E_j\ (i<j),\qquad 2H_0-\sum E_i.
\]
Their pairwise intersection numbers, computed in this basis, give a five-regular triangle-free graph on sixteen vertices. Fix a vertex and label its five neighbors by the five binary words \(u_i\) of Hamming weight four in \(\mathbb F_2^5\). Each of the remaining ten vertices is uniquely determined by the unordered pair of its neighbors among these five. Label it by the sum of the two coordinate basis words. The resulting map identifies the sixteen vertices with the *even-weight* subgroup
\[
V=\{v\in\mathbb F_2^5:\textstyle\sum_i v_i=0\}
\]
and identifies geometric intersection with binary Hamming distance four.

The graph automorphism group is precisely \(V\rtimes S_5\). Indeed, translations by elements of \(V\) and permutations of the five bits preserve Hamming distance four, giving 16 times 120 automorphisms. Conversely every graph automorphism is determined by the image of one vertex and by the induced permutation of its five neighbors, because every other vertex is uniquely determined by a pair of those neighbors. Its order is therefore at most \(16\cdot120=1920\). This is the standard even signed-permutation representation of \(W(D_5)\).

The forty geometric anticanonical squares are, for all \(1\le i<j\le5\), the four affine cosets of the two-dimensional binary subgroup generated by \(u_i,u_j\):
\[
Q_{i,j,v}=\{v,v+u_i,v+u_j,v+u_i+u_j\},\qquad v\in V/\langle u_i,u_j\rangle.
\]
The four vertices have exactly the four desired graph edges. Direct summation of the exceptional divisor classes for each such square gives \(-K_X\). Conversely every induced four-cycle in the sixteen-vertex graph is one of these cosets: the two independent length-four difference vectors leaving any chosen vertex must be distinct \(u_i,u_j\). Thus there are \(\binom52\cdot4=40\) distinct anticanonical square divisors. The affine action preserves this forty-element set, so both Frobenius permutation actions arise from the same element of \(W(D_5)\).

The remaining classification is a finite, exhaustive group calculation with a small enough search space to be checked directly. The accompanying exact-integer program `verify.py` constructs the sixteen divisor classes and their intersection graph independently of the binary model; enumerates the forty squares independently in both models; enumerates all \(16\cdot5!=1920\) affine Weyl elements; computes their sixteen-line and forty-square permutation cycle spectra; and constructs all conjugacy classes using the four independent even translations and the four adjacent coordinate transpositions. Those generators generate the entire semidirect product, so their conjugacy-connected components are the full conjugacy classes, not sample classes.

The calculation gives eighteen classes, twelve distinct line spectra, sixteen distinct square spectra, and eighteen distinct joint spectra. Using the exact formula for extension fixed points it finds respectively thirteen, seventeen, and eighteen distinguishable signatures from the first one, two, and three extension degrees. For two degrees the unique collision consists of the two 160-element classes stated in the finding. This proves both sufficiency of degree three and minimality of that cutoff on the complete Weyl-group action. The calculation is an exhaustive certificate of a finite group computation, not a numerical extrapolation to infinitely many ground fields.

## Verification
Execute `python3 verify.py` from a directory containing the companion `verify.py` file. The program has only standard-library dependencies; it performs integer divisor intersection checks, the exhaustive group and conjugacy searches, and writes `frobenius_spectra.csv`. Its final success marker is `DEGREE_FOUR_DEL_PEZZO_16_LINES_40_SQUARES_18_CLASSES_SHARP_3_EXTENSIONS_VERIFIED`. The companion CSV lists all eighteen classes, a representative in signed-affine coordinates, class size, line and square cycle types and the six extension counts. The program verifies the multiset of class sizes and that their sum is 1920.

## Relationship to prior work
Bergvall–Gounelas describe the degree-four del Pezzo Weyl-group geometry and the relevant four-component anticanonical configurations in their study of marked del Pezzo moduli and its cohomology. Meroni–Ranestad–Sinn (Proposition 3.3) independently count forty four-singularity hyperplane sections of degree-four del Pezzo surfaces; the geometric count of forty is not claimed new. Banwait–Fité–Loughran analyze del Pezzo Frobenius traces and inverse Galois questions, rather than the joint zeta functions of these two canonical zero-dimensional incidence schemes. In contrast, the claim here is an exact *joint arithmetic classification* and a sharp *inverse theorem for the first three extension counts*, which do not follow directly from the known cardinalities or Picard traces.

## Limitations
This proves a uniform *possible-action* theorem. It does not realize all eighteen Weyl types over every fixed finite field, nor assert a field-by-field optimal cutoff. The underlying geometric configuration and the number forty were known. Novelty is qualified by possible previously unindexed cycle-index or Weyl character tables implying the same exact joint inverse statement; there has been no independent mathematical audit. The computation is a complete finite-group exhaustive verification rather than a formal proof-assistant certificate. It computes the zeta functions of the line and square schemes, not the full zeta function of the del Pezzo surface.
