# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The geometric reduction is exact: hyperelliptic genus-three theta characteristics are the \(28\) unordered branch-point pairs (odd) and the distinguished hyperelliptic theta class plus the \(35\) unordered partitions of eight branch points into equal halves (even). Since the base field has odd characteristic, the corresponding theta-characteristic schemes are finite étale and Frobenius acts by permuting these labels. The two fixed-point formulas count invariant pairs and invariant-or-complemented four-subsets without approximation. The \(22\) partitions of eight exhaust the possible Frobenius types, and the exhaustive certificate independently compares the full orbit traversal with generating-function/Burnside counts. All \(22\) first-three-extension signatures are distinct, while the displayed first-two-extension collisions show sharpness. The proof covers every odd finite field without making a numerical extrapolation.

## Originality — PASS, with priority limitation
Dolgachev's hyperelliptic theta-subset correspondence and symplectic action, Dina–Ionica's genus-three theta framework, and Braden–Disney-Hogg's automorphism-group orbit tables are credited as prior foundations. Braden–Disney-Hogg, arXiv:2404.09890, Table 6 concerns automorphism actions on selected curves; the new claim instead concerns all arithmetic Frobenius types on arbitrary hyperelliptic genus-three curves and the optimal inverse fingerprint from parity-specific extension-field counts. No inspected source supplies this three-extension reconstruction theorem or the exact \(22\)-type comparison. A previously unindexed equivalent finite character table is a remaining priority risk; novelty is not inferred simply from absent search hits.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Recovering the residue degrees of a canonical branch divisor from counts on the two canonical spin schemes is a natural arithmetic reconstruction problem. The three-extension cutoff is optimal over every odd finite field. Separate full parity spectra lose information, while the combined truncated data recover it; this is an exact obstruction-and-reconstruction result, not merely an arbitrary finite list. It has potential use in comparing arithmetic spin and branch invariants, without asserting an improvement over factoring the branch polynomial.
