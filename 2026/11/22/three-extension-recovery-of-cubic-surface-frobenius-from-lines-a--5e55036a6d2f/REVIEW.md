# Scientific review: cubic-surface lines and tritangent descent

## Correctness — PASS

The claim applies to every geometrically smooth cubic surface over every finite field. Its two finite étale configuration schemes have cardinalities \(27\) and \(45\), geometrically described by explicit divisor classes and their anticanonical triples. The exact standalone computation enumerates the full \(51\,840\)-element group and its \(25\) conjugacy classes; independent line-intersection and class-sum tests recover the same tritangent set. The computed sharpness collisions are directly present in the reproduced table, and large-field realization follows from published positive-density results. The theorem does not claim actual realization of each type over a fixed small field.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

The prior \(25\)-class table in Banwait–Fité–Loughran supplies Picard eigenvalues and \(27\)-line cycle types; it does not supply the action on all \(45\) tritangents or state an optimal three-extension recovery theorem. McKean addresses rational line counts; Brundu–Logar–Polli give geometric tritangent data and surface automorphisms in characteristic zero; Loughran–Trepalin classify realizability of Weyl types. Public records on cyclic cubic-surface tritangents give special-family geometry, not the full \(W(E_6)\) arithmetic inverse problem. The result is an exact new finite-configuration calculation contingent on absence of an unindexed equivalent table; that literature-priority risk remains.

## Value — PASS

The joint line/tritangent arithmetic separates all \(25\) Picard Galois types, while lines alone distinguish only \(20\) orbit spectra and tritangents only \(24\). The optimal three-extension cutoff gives a small, computable package of natural arithmetic invariants sufficient for complete Galois type reconstruction. The two sharp collision pairs explain exactly why fewer extensions do not work. This is a natural inverse problem rather than a redundant geometric count.
