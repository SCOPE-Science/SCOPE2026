# Scientific review: three Fourier-mean area phases and facet horns

## Correctness — PASS
For every planar convex polygon and every positive parameter, the proof covers all directions, including coincident projected vertex levels, both normal and nonnormal directions, and the endpoints \(p=2,4\). The projection chord function is continuous and piecewise affine precisely away from edge normals; its derivative-jump representation gives the exact finite fractional-energy formula. Positive mean-square trigonometric amplitudes exclude any hidden cancellation at \(p=4\). The boundary-edge Fourier identity and rescaling isolate each parallel edge, with oscillatory cross-terms negligible at leading order and the remaining edges of lower order. The exponent \(2(p-2)/p<1\) proves finite area. Direct square and triangle identities are checked by the standalone script. The checks sample special geometries, but all-poly proof is analytic; no numerical sample is used to prove a universal quantifier.

## Originality — PASS (provisional)
The motivating 2026 Fourier-mean paper already establishes the \(p=2\) finite-area law and square noncompactness, which are explicitly credited and not claimed as new. The earlier all-polygon \(1<p<2\) facet-cusp finding concerns convexity below the compactness threshold, not \(2<p<4\) area or global \(p=4\) ray divergence. Published cube directional residues address product geometry, and a quartic smooth disk has its own three phases with different exponents. Wuttke derives polygon form factors but not the present Fourier-mean radial/area classification. The current result gives a complete all-polygon area phase and sharp facet-horn coefficients not contained as stated or as direct special cases in those sources. Residual risk: classical polytope Fourier-integrability results might make this a corollary with a different phrasing. One historically relevant article on averaged Fourier decay was available only at abstract level here; no claim of exhaustive historical priority is made.

## Value — PASS
A natural open geometric issue after the introduction of Fourier mean bodies is what an unbounded Fourier mean set looks like and whether its area remains finite. The existing universal result controls area only through the dimension threshold, whereas this theorem identifies a strictly larger, exact finite-area interval for *every polygon*, and the sharp endpoint where every direction blows up. The all-edge-normal coefficient describes the precise long-horn geometry and depends on geometric edge lengths rather than just classifying compactness. The projected-vertex finite formula provides computable values for arbitrary irregular polygons and for coincident projection levels. These constitute a full structural classification, not a new parameter of an isolated shape.

## Residual scientific limitations
The claim is confined to planar convex polygons and fixed \(p\) in the horn asymptotic. It neither addresses higher-dimensional polyhedra nor proves a p-uniform asymptotic near four. The known p=2 area identity is credited as established. Independently audited status has not been assigned.
