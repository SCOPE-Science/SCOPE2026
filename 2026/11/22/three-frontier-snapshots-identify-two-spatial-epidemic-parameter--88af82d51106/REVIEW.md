# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The result applies to any seeded classical trajectory satisfying strict positivity, and uses the exact source-defined observable \(C=R+R_\infty p_{\mathrm R}\). The \(\beta_R\) transfers cancel to yield \(\dot C=\gamma I\), which paired with the frontier equation proves the integral identity. A strictly negative derivative of \(\eta(z)=z/((1+z^2)\arctan z)\), weighted-average ordering on disjoint intervals, and the two uniform endpoint limits establish the sharp bounds and global invertibility for the *reduced observation relation*. All quantifiers and exclusions are stated; numerical tests corroborate rather than replace proof.

## Originality — PASS, literature-bounded

The originating paper derives the model, arctangent saturation and reproduction threshold and performs numerical curve fitting; no finite three-observation inverse or sharp parameter-free interval appears in its model, results, conclusions or appendices. The 2026 Bergström--Favero--Britton identifiability paper treats a different SIR system with unknown reporting/immunity rather than spatial-frontier saturation. The related broader identifiability literature does not imply this exact strict integral-ratio theorem without new analysis. Scientific priority is bounded by searched sources and remains subject to independent review.

## Value — PASS

The paper estimates spatial coefficients using numerical epidemic-curve fitting. This exact result gives a reduced-data strategy when unreached-district and whole-population recovered counts are available, separates the saturation scale from spread intensity, and provides a falsifiable, parameter-free necessary inequality. The sharp endpoint limits also expose why inference becomes weak near linear or fully saturated spreading. This is not merely a normalization check or a routine fixed-parameter simulation.
