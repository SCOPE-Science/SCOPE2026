# Three-gap census of bounded-remainder cylinders in cyclic Sturmian lifts

## Finding
Fix an irrational number \(0<\alpha<1\) and an integer \(m\ge2\). Let \(Y_\alpha\) be the two-sided Sturmian difference shift generated by \(y_i=\lfloor s+(i+1)\alpha\rfloor-\lfloor s+i\alpha\rfloor\), and define the cyclic integral lift
\[
X_{\alpha,m}=\{x\in(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z)^{\mathbb Z}: (x_{i+1}-x_i\bmod m)_{i\in\mathbb Z}\in Y_\alpha\}.
\]
A word \(w\) in this lift is *bounded-remainder* if there exists \(C_w<\infty\) such that, for every \(x\in X_{\alpha,m}\) and integer \(N\ge1\),
\[
\left|\sum_{t=0}^{N-1}{\bf1}_{[w]}(\sigma^t x)-N\mu([w])\right|\le C_w,
\]
where \(\mu\) is the unique invariant probability. Write \(B_{\alpha,m}(n)\) for the number of such length-\(n\) words.

For \(n=1\), \(B_{\alpha,m}(1)=0\). For \(n\ge2\), let \(a\) and \(b\) be the unique indices in \(\{1,\ldots,n-1\}\) minimizing and maximizing \(\{i\alpha\}\), respectively. Define \(E_m(r)={\bf1}_{m\mid\lfloor r\alpha\rfloor}\) for every signed integer \(r\). Then the exact all-length formula is
\[
\boxed{ B_{\alpha,m}(n)=m\big[(n-a)E_m(a)+(n-b)E_m(-b)+(a+b-n)E_m(a-b)\big]. }
\]
In particular, this is a three-integer arithmetic census, although the entire length-\(n\) lift language has \(mn\) words.

For the golden slope \(\alpha=(\sqrt5-1)/2\) and modulus \(m=2\), let \(F_1=F_2=1\) and \(F_{r+1}=F_r+F_{r-1}\). At every \(r\ge4\),
\[
\boxed{
B_{\alpha,2}(F_r)=
\begin{cases}
0,&r\equiv1\pmod3,\\
2F_{r-2},&r\equiv2\pmod3,\\
2F_{r-1},&r\equiv0\pmod3.
\end{cases}}
\]
Thus the density of bounded-remainder cylinders at Fibonacci lengths has precisely the three subsequential limits \(0\), \(\varphi^{-2}\), and \(\varphi^{-1}\), where \(\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2\). These densities refer to the \(2F_r\) distinct length-\(F_r\) cylinders, not to their invariant measures.

## Assumptions and scope
All words are *contiguous*, bilateral-language words. Frequencies are with respect to the unique invariant probability, and the bound above is uniform over every orbit and every positive horizon. At singular intercepts the two possible endpoint codings are both admitted. The circle presentation uses \(\mathbb R/m\mathbb Z\), rotation by \(\alpha\), and the coding \(x_i(t)=\lfloor t+i\alpha\rfloor\bmod m\). The circle has its normalized Haar probability. Irrationality guarantees that the \(mn\) distinct cuts \(j-i\alpha\) for \(0\le i<n\), \(j\in\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z\) are distinct. Each complementary interval has a distinct coding word, so \(p_{X_{\alpha,m}}(n)=mn\).

## Proof
**1. Interval criterion.** The length-\(n\) cylinders correspond one-to-one to the intervals between consecutive cuts \(j-i\alpha\bmod m\); their frequencies are their lengths divided by \(m\). This follows since the initial symbol fixes the integer part of the intercept modulo \(m\), while the \(n-1\) successive increments distinguish the \(n\) familiar Sturmian rotation intervals in each unit cell. The irrational rotation is uniquely ergodic; endpoint codings do not change cylinder frequencies.

Kesten's bounded-remainder interval theorem, applied after dividing the circle by \(m\), says that an interval between cuts has uniformly bounded centered visit counts precisely if its length belongs to \(\alpha\mathbb Z+m\mathbb Z\). The usual statement is an almost-everywhere criterion. Its sufficient direction is actually uniform: an interval of length \(r\alpha\bmod m\) has indicator a finite telescoping sum of translates of the fundamental rotation interval, hence a bounded transfer, including either endpoint convention. The necessity direction already rules out the all-orbit bound. For the finitely many split codings, visits differ from those of a circle endpoint coding by a uniformly finite amount, since one orbit meets a fixed cut at most once. Thus this criterion applies exactly to the stated symbolic definition.

Write the fractional positions \(u_i=\{i\alpha\}\) for \(0\le i<n\). The cut circle consists of \(m\) translated copies of these points, read in reverse orientation. Suppose that the right successor of \(u_i\) is \(u_j\) in circular order, and put \(r=j-i\). Their positive circular gap is \(\delta=\{r\alpha\}\in(0,1)\). The corresponding lifted cylinder interval has length \(\delta\). Its two endpoints lie on the same \(\alpha\)-rotation orbit modulo \(m\) exactly when \(r\alpha-\delta=\lfloor r\alpha\rfloor\) is divisible by \(m\). Consequently each such ordered neighboring pair contributes either all \(m\) lifted bounded-remainder cylinders or none, according to \(E_m(r)\).

**2. A proved three-gap successor lemma.** For \(n\ge3\), let \(u=\{a\alpha\}\) and \(v=1-\{b\alpha\}\). The defining extremal properties imply \(a+b\ge n\): if \(a+b<n\), the fractional part of \((a+b)\alpha\) is either smaller than \(u\) or larger than \(1-v\). They also imply \(u+v<1\), because otherwise the nonzero difference between \(a\) and \(b\) would give a new fractional part smaller than \(u\) or larger than \(1-v\). In fact, the circular successor of index \(i\) is
\[
S(i)=\begin{cases}
i+a,&i+a<n,\\
i-b,&i+a\ge n\text{ and }i\ge b,\\
i+a-b,&i+a\ge n\text{ and }i<b.
\end{cases}
\]
These three possible gaps have lengths \(u\), \(v\), and \(u+v\), respectively. Here is a direct exclusion argument. In the first case, a nearer successor with positive index displacement contradicts minimality of \(u\). A nearer successor at displacement \(-r\), where \(r\le i<b\), would give \(\{r\alpha\}>1-u\), hence \(\{(b-r)\alpha\}<u\), a contradiction. In the second case a nearer successor with negative displacement contradicts maximality of \(\{b\alpha\}\). A nearer successor at positive displacement \(d\le n-1-i<a\) would imply \(\{(a-d)\alpha\}>1-v\), another contradiction. In the third case a nearer positive displacement must satisfy \(d<a\), and again forces \(\{(a-d)\alpha\}>1-v\); a nearer negative displacement \(-r\) has \(r<b\) and forces \(\{(b-r)\alpha\}<u\). Both are impossible. All candidate indices lie in \(\{0,\ldots,n-1\}\), so this proves the successor formula without assuming the desired census. For \(n=2\), the same successor formula holds directly with \(a=b=1\), and the third branch has zero weight.

The successor displacements \(+a\), \(-b\), and \(a-b\) appear respectively \(n-a\), \(n-b\), and \(a+b-n\) times. Combining the orbit criterion from step 1 with these multiplicities gives the boxed exact formula, including the \(n=2\) boundary case. At \(n=1\), every interval has length one, which is not in \(\alpha\mathbb Z+m\mathbb Z\), proving the exceptional assertion for \(m\ge2\).

**3. The golden-slope phase law.** At \(n=F_r\), \(r\ge4\), the nearest positive and negative return indices are \(F_{r-1}\) and \(F_{r-2}\), exchanged according to the parity of \(r\). Thus \(a+b=n\), so the long-gap term vanishes. The classical continued-fraction identities give
\[
\lfloor F_j\alpha\rfloor=F_{j-1}-{\bf1}_{j\text{ even}},\qquad
\lfloor-F_j\alpha\rfloor=-F_{j-1}-{\bf1}_{j\text{ odd}}.
\]
These identities also follow directly from \(F_j\alpha=F_{j-1}+(-1)^{j-1}\varphi^{-j}\). Since \(F_j\) is even if and only if \(3\mid j\), precisely the term producing \(F_{r-2}\) survives when \(r\equiv2\pmod3\), the term producing \(F_{r-1}\) when \(r\equiv0\pmod3\), and neither survives when \(r\equiv1\pmod3\). This proves the exact phase law and, using \(F_{r-1}/F_r\to\varphi^{-1}\) and \(F_{r-2}/F_r\to\varphi^{-2}\), its three asserted accumulation values.

## Relationship to prior work
Kang and Jäger (2026, first public September 1) study minimality and unique ergodicity of permutative lifts, including Fibonacci and noble-means substitutions. Their accessible full text does not supply a census of which individual cylinders are bounded-remainder. Kesten's theorem, revisited via cohomology by Kelly and Sadun, provides the exact interval criterion, and classical three-gap theory identifies neighbor distances. These two ingredients, the return-index successor calculation, and Fibonacci arithmetic are explicitly separated in the proof: no novelty is claimed for Kesten's theorem, the basic three-gap theorem, or the lift's strict ergodicity. The claimed new result is their *all-length arithmetic cylinder census and exact three-phase density law* for this specific finite extension.

## Limitations
This is a self-review rather than independent validation or publication-priority certification. The no-prior-equivalent claim is restricted to the inspected source material, available research records, and accessible historical claims; uncatalogued or inaccessible results could dominate it. The statement treats exact cylinder indicator observables, not arbitrary locally constant observables; the general cohomological setting is already addressed in earlier literature. Only the golden slope/modulus two Fibonacci subsequence receives an explicit modular phase classification; the all-slope/all-modulus formula itself holds at every length.
