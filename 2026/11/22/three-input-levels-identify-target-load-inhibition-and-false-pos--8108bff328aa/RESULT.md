# Three input levels identify target load, inhibition, and false positives in an ideal environmental-DNA assay

## Finding

**Theorem (sharp three-level recovery).** In the Poisson-thinning environmental-DNA assay specified below, the three exact detection probabilities at template-input levels \(1,2,3\) uniquely determine the unknown target intensity \(\theta>0\), inhibitor survival factor \(0<q<1\), and dilution-independent false-positive probability \(0\le f<1\). Two levels, at \(1,2\), do not determine the triple, even with exact probabilities. The three-level reconstruction is explicit, globally unambiguous and locally nonsingular throughout the domain, despite possible nonmonotonicity of detection with template input.

For \(p_j=\Pr(\text{positive at level }j)\) and \(y_j=-\log(1-p_j)\), put
\[
B=4(y_1+y_3)-6y_2,\qquad C=4y_1y_3-3y_2^2.
\]
The unique admissible solution is
\[
a=\frac{B-\sqrt{B^2-4C}}2,\qquad
q=\frac{y_2-a}{2(y_1-a)},\qquad
\theta=\frac{y_1-a}{q},\qquad f=1-e^{-a}.
\]
These expressions apply to triples exactly generated by the model. For arbitrary observed probabilities, the right-hand side characterizes an admissible triple only when the discriminant and all divisions are defined, \(a\ge0\), \(0<q<1\), \(\theta>0\), and all three reconstructed probabilities agree with the observations.

The trend in positive-detection probability has a sharp three-regime classification: it strictly decreases between all successive input levels for \(0<q<1/2\); it increases then decreases for \(1/2<q<2/3\); and it strictly increases for \(2/3<q<1\). At \(q=1/2\), the first two probabilities tie; at \(q=2/3\), the last two tie. In all regimes, three levels remain identifiable.

## Assumptions and scope

Take a homogeneous extract from one ecological sample, aliquoted into independent reaction wells. The three prescribed relative template-input amounts are exactly \(j=1,2,3\), with all other reaction conditions held fixed. For each level \(j\), target marker copies enter a well as a Poisson variable of mean \(j\theta\). Conditional on this count, each marker independently yields a detectable target signal with probability \(q^j\), representing a specified exponential loss of per-copy detection efficiency as inhibitor-bearing input increases. Independently, a background mechanism makes a well falsely positive with the same probability \(f\) at all three levels. The well is called positive if either a target signal or a background event occurs. Here \(\theta>0\), \(q=e^{-\eta}\) with \(\eta>0\), and \(0\le f<1\).

Poisson thinning makes the number of effective target signals Poisson with mean \(j\theta q^j\). Consequently
\[
p_j=1-(1-f)\exp(-j\theta q^j),\qquad
y_j=a+j\theta q^j,\quad a=-\log(1-f)\ge0.
\]
The inputs \(1,2,3\) are *three aliquot-size or concentration levels*, not merely three individual binary assay results. Identifiability concerns the three exact population probabilities, obtainable in principle from repeated independent assays at each level. It is not a claim of exact finite-sample recovery or experimental validity of the exponential-inhibition law.

## Proof

**Exact response.** If \(N_j\sim\operatorname{Pois}(j\theta)\), independent per-marker success probability \(q^j\) gives \(\Pr(\text{zero target signals})=e^{-j\theta q^j}\). Independence of the background event gives \(1-p_j=(1-f)e^{-j\theta q^j}\), hence the displayed log-response formula.

**Algebraic inverse.** For a candidate background log-intensity \(z\), let \(u_j=y_j-z\). If \(u_j=j\theta q^j\), then \((u_2/2)^2=(u_1)(u_3/3)\). Therefore every solution satisfies
\[
3(y_2-z)^2-4(y_1-z)(y_3-z)=0,
\]
or \(z^2-Bz+C=0\). Substituting \(y_j=a+j\theta q^j\) factors the left side as
\[
-(z-a)\bigl(z-a-4\theta q(1-3q+3q^2)\bigr).
\]
The other algebraic root is
\[
a'=a+4\theta q(1-3q+3q^2)>a,
\]
because \(1-3q+3q^2=3(q-1/2)^2+1/4>0\). But
\[
y_1-a'=-3\theta q(2q-1)^2\le0.
\]
For any admissible candidate, \(y_1-z=\theta' q'>0\). Therefore \(a'\) is inadmissible (including the equality case \(q=1/2\)). The true \(a\) is the smaller quadratic root. Once it is known, the first two equations yield exactly the displayed \(q\) and \(\theta\), and \(f=1-e^{-a}\). This proves global uniqueness without assuming that the three response probabilities are monotone.

**Sharp two-level failure.** For any admissible \((a,\theta,q)\), and any sufficiently small \(\varepsilon>0\), let
\[
\widetilde a=a+\varepsilon,\quad
\widetilde q=\frac{y_2-\widetilde a}{2(y_1-\widetilde a)},\quad
\widetilde\theta=\frac{y_1-\widetilde a}{\widetilde q}.
\]
By continuity, choosing \(\varepsilon\) sufficiently small preserves \(0<\widetilde q<1\), \(\widetilde\theta>0\), \(\widetilde a\ge0\), and exactly the first two responses \(y_1,y_2\). Varying \(\varepsilon\) produces infinitely many distinct admissible parameter triples with the same two exact detection probabilities. Thus the bound of three levels is sharp.

**Nonsingularity.** Direct differentiation of \((y_1,y_2,y_3)\) with respect to \((a,\theta,q)\) gives
\[
\det\frac{\partial(y_1,y_2,y_3)}{\partial(a,\theta,q)}
=2\theta q^2(3q^2-3q+1)>0.
\]
The coordinate changes \(a\leftrightarrow f\), \(q\leftrightarrow\eta\), and \(y_j\leftrightarrow p_j\) have nonzero derivatives in the stated domain; the sign of this displayed determinant is for \((a,\theta,q)\), not for an arbitrary ordering of the physical parameters. Thus local identifiability holds throughout the interior, though numerical conditioning deteriorates near \(\theta=0\) or \(q=0\).

**Dose-order regimes.** Since \(y_2-y_1=\theta q(2q-1)\) and \(y_3-y_2=\theta q^2(3q-2)\), and since \(p_j=1-e^{-y_j}\) is strictly increasing in \(y_j\), the stated ordering and boundary ties follow. This completes the proof.

## Verification

The standalone `check_identification.py` evaluates 46,824 exact-rational diagnostic assertions across multiple rational strengths, backgrounds, and inhibitor factors; it checks quadratic roots, inadmissibility of the spurious root, reconstruction, strict Jacobian positivity, trend regimes, and explicit nonuniqueness of two levels. Run with `python3 check_identification.py` using Python 3 and its standard library only. These finite tests do not replace the preceding real-parameter proof.

As a simple exact example, with \(a=0\), \(\theta=1\), and \(q=1/2\), the transformed responses are \((y_1,y_2,y_3)=(1/2,1/2,3/8)\). The extra quadratic root is \(a'=1/2\), which forces \(y_1-a'=0\) and violates \(\theta' q'>0\). The first two response probabilities tie, yet the triple remains uniquely recoverable from all three.

## Relationship to prior work

Wilcox and coauthors (2013) studied detection and specificity in environmental-DNA assays of closely related fish species, using concentration series and repeated PCR tests. Schultz and Lance (2015) developed a Poisson-based sensitivity model for eDNA surveys; they noted that inhibition was not measured in their application and might be represented by a reduction in effective marker input. McKee, Spear, and Pierson (2015) compared dilution and inhibitor-removal treatments empirically. These studies establish the motivation and components of the simplified mathematical model, but the inspected primary full texts of Wilcox and Schultz do not state the present global three-input-level identification theorem or its positivity-excluded second quadratic root. The McKee paper's accessible abstract does not establish that theorem, but its complete text was not available for comparison.

Classical exponential-reconstruction methods, including Prony-type approaches, may provide broad mathematical background, but no checked source established this particular sharp three-level result for a background-offset, inhibited Poisson response. This is a best-knowledge comparison, not a claim of certified historical priority. In particular, results identifying a hidden ecological occupancy chain from temporal covariances address different observations and unknowns; they cannot directly invert the three-dose assay response considered here.

## Limitations

The theorem concerns exact response probabilities, not three binary assay outcomes or guarantees under finite sampling noise. False positives must be independent of and invariant with input level; inhibition must act as the specified exponential per-copy thinning; input levels must be calibrated and in the ratio \(1:2:3\); and separate wells must behave as independent, homogeneous samples. Cross-amplification by other taxa, copy clustering, censoring, calibration uncertainty, and dose-varying false-positive rates can violate the model. At \(\theta=0\), inhibition is not identifiable; the theorem excludes that boundary. As \(q\to0\), inference is ill-conditioned despite interior uniqueness. This is a mechanistic benchmark, not a field-validated protocol. Priority risk remains from inaccessible full text and broader exponential-system identification literature.
