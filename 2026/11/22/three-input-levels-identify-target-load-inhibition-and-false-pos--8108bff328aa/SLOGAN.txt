Under a specified inhibited-Poisson model for environmental-DNA assays with an unknown constant false-positive rate, three exact detection probabilities at template-input levels \(1,2,3\) uniquely recover target intensity, inhibition and background, whereas two levels never suffice in the interior. A quadratic reconstruction has a second algebraic root, but positivity always excludes it, even when the measured detection probabilities are nonmonotone across input levels.
