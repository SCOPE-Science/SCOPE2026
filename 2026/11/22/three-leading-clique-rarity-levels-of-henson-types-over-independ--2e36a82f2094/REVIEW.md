# Scientific review: three leading clique-rarity levels

## Correctness — PASS
The result is a theorem about labelled finite extension diagrams over a named independent parameter set in the countable homogeneous \(K_{q+1}\)-free graph, with \(q\ge3\), \(r\ge q+1\), and arbitrary \(m\ge0\). Henson universality and homogeneity and the graph-type criterion in Conant (2014) justify the correspondence between complete injective types and induced \(K_{q+1}\)-free extensions. An internal \(q\)-clique makes precisely its supersets inadmissible as neighborhoods for each old vertex. The three maximal allowed-neighborhood counts then follow from cardinalities and intersections of Boolean principal upsets. A third distinct \(q\)-clique strictly contributes a new forbidden subset. The proof explicitly establishes the three equality classes, their nonemptiness, the finite exponential remainder, and all conditional limits. The critical-arity formula uses a complete enumeration of clique multiplicities in graphs on \(q+1\) vertices. All five finite test regimes in the accompanying checker passed. The tests are corroborative rather than a proof of unrestricted sizes.

## Originality — PASS, provisional
The full primary sources Henson (1971, Section 2) and Conant (2014, Section 3) provide existence and consistency, not the claimed spectral hierarchy. An indexed Henson higher-arity theorem gives the independent-parameter transfer and rare-edge first two terms only for the triangle-free case; this theorem excludes \(q=2\) and claims instead a richer \(K_q\)-free limiting ensemble with the third leading class for \(q\ge3\). An indexed higher-clique-free rare-edge result concerns repeated maximal old cliques; that parameter geometry produces an asymptotically edgeless new tuple, whereas independent old vertices here leave a nontrivial graph ensemble. Indexed one-type results and the closest checked related parameter results likewise do not imply these two rare-clique conditional concentration statements. Actual semantic searches covered aliases, the exact small coefficient tuple \((41,16,6)\), and clique-neighborhood formulations. Absence of a hit does not certify priority: an unindexed general enumeration or a stronger transferable statement remains possible.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The invariant is a natural uniform law on complete finite-base types, and the problem asks how parameter geometry changes the induced structure of new tuples. The answer supplies three sharp exponential weights and a qualitative selection phenomenon: despite being sampled in a \(K_{q+1}\)-free host, the unconditioned new tuple becomes \(K_q\)-free, while successive rare violations localize first into one \(q\)-clique and then into two nearly coincident cliques. At the smallest arity where the second phenomenon can occur, the **entire** spectrum reduces to three explicit terms. That is structural information beyond a simple count or a routine one-point specialization. It is complementary to previously examined old-clique bases and cannot be substituted for those formulas.
