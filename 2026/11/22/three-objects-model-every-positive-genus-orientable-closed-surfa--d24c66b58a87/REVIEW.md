# Scientific review

## Correctness (PASS)

The category has only three strictly ordered objects, so the composition table is associative without further relations. Exactly one two-simplex contains each private composite edge; removing these pairs gives the stated retained two-complex. All three vertex-link graphs are explicitly checked as single cycles (the first requires the specified oddness of the modulus). The signed sum of the surviving two-simplices is a nonvanishing integral orientation cycle, and the exact Euler characteristic identifies the surface genus. The lower bound follows because a two-object acyclic category has a one-dimensional nerve, whereas a closed orientable surface has nonzero integral second homology. The enclosed checker verifies the independently derived finite cases and is not substituted for the uniform proof.

## Originality (PASS, subject to priority risk)

The full text of Carcacía-Campos's arXiv:2611.09718v1, notably Sections 1.1, 2.3, 3 and 8, defines the invariant, proves dimensional bounds and describes three-object composition tables, but establishes exact values for different families (spheres, bouquets, Moore spaces, projective and lens spaces). The genus computation is not a special case of those values. His crystallization remark concerns general pseudotriangulations, not proof that a three-vertex surface complex arises from a composition table. Tanaka's arXiv:1707.01179v1 treats structural homotopy of acyclic categories and Δ-complexes but does not give the explicit genus family in the inspected sections. Semantic database and published-literature searches checked categorical-surface and torus aliases, composition-table reductions, and potential general coverage. No covering statement was found; unindexed earlier constructions and equivalences remain possible.

## Mathematical value (PASS)

The newly defined object-complexity invariant is evaluated sharply on the entire natural family of positive-genus closed orientable surfaces, all with the same minimum of three objects despite unbounded genus and fundamental-group complexity. This reveals that the genus is stored in morphisms and their composition rather than object count and improves the direct circle-product upper bound of four already in the motivating paper. The explicit model is compatible with the strict nerve constraint, which a generic three-vertex pseudotriangulation need not satisfy. This is an all-genus structural classification, not a finite computation or renaming of a known bound.

## Closest literature and limitations

Carcacía-Campos (2026, arXiv:2611.09718v1) supplies the definition, lower-bound method and three-object table formalism; its sphere and lens-space results do not entail the surface claim. Tanaka (2017, arXiv:1707.01179) covers strong homotopy of acyclic categories, not this evaluated invariant. Classical crystallization gives a nearby but categorically weaker three-vertex description. The statement concerns finite acyclic categories and orientable surfaces, not minimal morphism counts, nonorientable surfaces or finite posets. Priority remains subject to older or incompletely indexed papers; independent expert assessment is not available.
