# Scientific review of three optimal observation clocks for a stable Jordan shear

## Correctness — PASS
The two-event Fisher matrix is exactly the Gram matrix of the rows \(e^{-at}(1,st)\), divided by the common noise variance. The proof first eliminates all designs whose earlier time is positive: a shift raises the normalized squared row trace without changing its determinant, which strictly worsens the smaller eigenvalue, while the scalar prefactor worsens all information; the inverse-trace and determinant criteria admit direct monotonicity formulas. The remaining one-variable D- and A-criteria have unique turning points at dimensionless delays \(1\) and \(c\). For E-optimality, the differentiated characteristic polynomial and the positive derivative of \(G(z)=e^{2z}/z-1/[z(z-1)]\) prove a unique global maximizer for every shear \(s/a>0\). Endpoint clipping, strict ordering, the eigenvalue formula, and asymptotics follow without finite-enumeration assumptions. The embedded script checked six algebraic identities and 117,600 finite criterion comparisons; the infinite-parameter proof is independent of the sample counts.

## Originality — PASS with historical-priority risk
The strongest closely related classical source is Dette, Melas and Pepelyshev (2006), which explicitly studies the same pair of exponential-polynomial regressors for one decay rate, but optimizes freely weighted approximate E-designs: its single-exponential Example 1 fixes the second support by \(e^{-z}=z-1\). The exact-two-event **equal-weight** E-optimum derived here instead depends strictly on the shear ratio and diverges logarithmically with that ratio; the classical free-weight support result therefore neither supplies nor implies this classification. Jost, Sager and Le (2017) prove general finite-support properties for continuous experimental designs with adjustable weights; their examples do not yield the exact two-observation D/A/E comparison. Aspeel and collaborators (2021) optimize a different Kalman prediction-error objective, while Krauss, Lopez and Müller (2022) prove sampled-observability rank conditions, including Jordan blocks, without worst-direction information optimization. The published pinned-Jordan result on three measurement events has mandatory initial and terminal observations and one free third event, and optimizes a determinant rather than two-event A/E criteria. The published distinct-two-exponential result has the same three-event pinned determinant structure but a different generator. None of their inspected full scientific statements implies this two-event three-criterion theorem. Broader exact-design and older exponential-regression literature could nevertheless contain an equivalent formulation; absence from search is not a proof of publication priority.

## Value — PASS
Two scalar measurements are the smallest budget giving full-rank information on the two initial coordinates of an observable planar Jordan chain. Determinant, average inverse variance, and worst-direction information are standard non-equivalent objectives, and their exactly classified optimal times are provably different once the horizon allows them. The A-optimal delay is universal across shear strengths, whereas the E-optimal delay grows with the logarithm of the dimensionless shear and can be much later. This is a structural, quantitative sampling rule and not an arbitrary small parameter computation, a reparameterization of the three-observation determinant theorem, or a claim of a general-matrix result.

## Limitations and closest literature
The model requires fixed known damping and shear, exactly two equal-variance independent Gaussian observation events and the first coordinate as output. A and E information criteria depend on the chosen state coordinates. Unrestricted observation weights, process noise, more than two readings, and arbitrary defective matrices remain outside scope. The nearest historical design reference is Dette–Melas–Pepelyshev (2006), https://doi.org/10.1007/s10463-006-0031-2; dynamic measurement scheduling references are Jost–Sager–Le (2017), https://doi.org/10.3390/pr5010010; Aspeel et al. (2021), https://doi.org/10.1186/s13634-021-00732-8; and Krauss–Lopez–Müller (2022), https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.06451.
