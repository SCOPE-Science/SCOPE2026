# Scientific review: integral homology of cyclic triangular-cylinder quotients

## Correctness: PASS
The statement distinguishes the ordinary geometric orbit space from a vertex-orbit simplicial complex. The action is free because no independent set contains all three colors in any layer; a fixed geometric point would have invariant barycentric support. Thapper's Proposition 3.2 gives exactly one odd-dimensional sphere for two residue classes and a wedge of two even-dimensional spheres for the third, for every integer in the stated range. The rank-two action has characteristic polynomial \(t^2+t+1\) by the Lefschetz fixed-point formula and rational trace \(-1\); its norm vanishes and \(|\det(g-1)|=3\). The cyclic group-cohomology calculations and the two-row integral Serre spectral sequence are explicit in RESULT.md. Multiplicativity and finiteness force the relevant transgressions to be units, leaving no additive extension ambiguity. The integral universal coefficient theorem then gives each claimed torsion group and its exact order.

A standalone enumerator checks the integer chain condition \(\partial^2=0\), orbit freeness, and mod-two, mod-three, and mod-five homology for every \(4\le m\le9\). Its output is retained. These finite checks substantiate the constructions but do not replace the unrestricted spectral-sequence argument. The proof relies on a cited previously established homotopy classification of the covering complexes; no independent proof of that classification is claimed here.

## Originality: PASS, with explicitly bounded uncertainty
The most important possible dominating source is Thapper (arXiv:0812.1165v1), whose Proposition 3.2 already gives all the **nonequivariant** homotopy types. That fact is explicitly cited as an input and not claimed. Chakraborty (arXiv:2611.18495v1) studies Cohen–Macaulayness and Buchsbaum properties for the same graph family, not color-rotation orbit-space integral homology. A previously known result for the identical \(P_m\square K_q\) cyclic-color quotient establishes freeness, universal covering, and fundamental groups, so the first homology here is also not claimed as new. The actual contribution is **all higher integral homology for every \(m\ge4\)**, including unbounded-degree three-torsion and the top-free dichotomy.

Relevant published research-index comparisons also considered cyclic quotients of chessboard complexes, cyclic Hom-complex quotients, and categorical cycle–clique product independence complexes. These concern genuinely different simplicial complexes or actions, and their results do not imply this classification. Dimpi–Singh (arXiv:2303.16484v1) studies general prime-order orbit spaces of three-sphere wedges, with related spectral-sequence methods but different covering-homology ranks and no graph-specific all-length formula. The remaining risk is an unindexed earlier formula or a stronger theorem for free cyclic actions on one/two sphere wedges which directly specializes to this result. Searches cannot establish uniqueness; publication priority is provisional.

## Mathematical value: PASS
These triangular cylinders form a canonical infinite family in current combinatorial topology, and cyclic color permutation is the natural nontrivial free symmetry of their three-vertex layers. The quotient has an exact ladder of \(\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z\) classes through a dimension growing linearly with the path length, despite all covers being integral sphere wedges. Moreover the precise length congruence determines whether top free homology survives. This is an integral invariant that neither the earlier nonequivariant homotopy classification nor the known fundamental group determines. The theorem is a complete natural quotient-homology classification, not an arbitrary finite sample or mere Betti-number recomputation. The standard spectral-sequence method does not by itself identify the input color-action module, the required differential, or the family-specific residue phase transition.
