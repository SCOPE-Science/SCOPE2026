# Three primitive relations among 72 shortest product tori on \(K_{2,3,4}\)

## Finding
Let \(G=K_{2,3,4}\) have vertex classes \(A=\{0,1\}\), \(B=\{2,3,4\}\) and \(C=\{5,6,7,8\}\), and let \(X=\operatorname{UConf}_2(|G|)\) be the collision-free space of unordered pairs of points on its geometric realization. For every unordered pair of vertex-disjoint, unoriented graph triangles, take the integral fundamental class of the torus obtained by moving one particle around each triangle. There are exactly 72 such geometric triangle-pair tori.

**Integral classification.** The 72 natural classes generate
\[
H_2(X;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{69}.
\]
Their integral relation module is free of rank three, with an explicit basis consisting of three signed 36-term relations given in `artifacts/integral_presentation_certificate.json`. An indexed selection of 69 of the 72 classes is an **integral basis** of the entire second homology. Every product torus of *any* two vertex-disjoint simple graph cycles (1,206 such unoriented pairs in total) belongs to this same integral lattice.

**Symmetry of relations.** Under the natural part-preserving group \(S_2\times S_3\times S_4\), the three-dimensional rationalization of the integral relation module is
\[
\operatorname{sgn}_{S_2}\boxtimes\operatorname{sgn}_{S_3}\boxtimes
\bigl(\operatorname{sgn}_{S_4}\otimes\operatorname{Std}_{S_4}\bigr),
\]
where \(\operatorname{Std}_{S_4}\) denotes the three-dimensional zero-sum permutation representation. This is an assertion about the rational representation; no identification with a specific integral standard lattice is claimed.

## Assumptions and scope
The graph is the simple complete tripartite graph with precisely the indicated parts; its geometric edges are not required to be additionally subdivided. Part labels are distinguished. A simple graph cycle has at least three edges and no repeated vertices. Two cycles are disjoint when their vertex sets are disjoint. Each unoriented cycle is listed once, canonically starting at its least vertex with the second vertex smaller than the final vertex. A pair of disjoint cycles is unordered; choosing orientations only fixes an overall sign of its product-torus fundamental class. All homology is ordinary integral singular homology. The symmetric-group action only permutes labels within a part. Primary MSC2020: 55R80.

## Proof
**1. A genuine cubical chain model.** The standard equivariant deleted-product deformation retraction for two points on a graph [1,2] gives a two-dimensional CW model for \(X\). Its cells are: unordered pairs of distinct vertices (36 zero-cells), pairs \((v,e)\) of a vertex and a nonincident edge (182 one-cells), and unordered pairs of vertex-disjoint edges (198 two-cells). Sort graph edges lexicographically and orient each \((a,b)\), \(a<b\), from \(a\) to \(b\). Orient each square by its product factors in ascending edge order. For \(e_i=(a,b)<e_j=(c,d)\), its signed boundary in the one-cell basis is
\[
\partial_2[e_i,e_j]=[(b,e_j)]-[(a,e_j)]+[(c,e_i)]-[(d,e_i)].
\]
For \((v,e_i)\), \(\partial_1[(v,e_i)]=[(v,b)]-[(v,a)]\). The verifier checks \(\partial_1\partial_2=0\) entry by entry and builds all 198 two-boundary columns. There are no three-cells, so \(H_2(X;\mathbb Z)=\ker\partial_2\), automatically torsion-free.

**2. Full enumeration of the geometric generators.** A simple cycle is represented by its canonical sequence of distinct vertices. Exhaustive depth-first enumeration yields 4,395 simple cycles, of respective lengths three through nine in counts 24, 99, 264, 624, 1,152, 1,368 and 864. Among unordered vertex-disjoint pairs there are 1,206, of length types \((3,3),(3,4),(3,5),(3,6),(4,4),(4,5)\) in counts 72, 288, 288, 144, 198 and 216. Expand two edge-oriented cycle chains into square cells with the Koszul sign when swapping their factors. All 1,206 resulting integral two-chains have signed boundary zero. Their individual primitive geometric classes are ordinary product torus fundamental classes.

**3. Rational dimension with an integral certificate.** Exact Gaussian elimination over \(\mathbb F_{101}\) gives \(\operatorname{rank}(\partial_2\bmod101)=129\). Therefore the rank of \(\partial_2\) over \(\mathbb Q\) is at least 129. The certificate identifies 69 triangle-pair tori and 69 square cells for which the 69-by-69 integer coefficient determinant equals \(+1\). Thus there exist 69 rationally independent two-cycles, so \(\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Q}(\partial_2)\le198-69=129\). Equality proves \(\dim_{\mathbb Q}H_2=69\).

The same unimodular minor proves *integral*, not merely rational, generation. Projection of the rational two-cycle space onto the selected 69 square coordinates is injective, because the selected 69 tori are a basis of that space. The selected classes map to a unimodular basis of the projected integral coordinate lattice; hence any integral two-cycle has an integral combination of those classes with identical selected coordinates. Their difference has zero projection and is zero by injectivity. Consequently those 69 tori form an integral basis of \(\ker\partial_2\) and give the asserted integral homology.

**4. Complete relations and the remaining tori.** For the 72 triangle tori, exact integer unit-pivot elimination uses precisely the preceding 69 basis columns and produces exactly three dependent columns. Their three relation vectors each have 36 entries equal to \(\pm1\) and zero elsewhere. Their coefficients on the three dependent-column coordinates form a unit triangular matrix, so these three relations generate the *entire* integral kernel of \(\mathbb Z^{72}\twoheadrightarrow H_2\), not a finite-index subgroup of it. Independently, every one of the 1,206 geometric tori reduces with integer coefficients to zero after subtracting the 69 integral basis tori; no rational denominator is needed. The certificate records the 69 selected torus positions, 69 selected square coordinates and all three complete signed relation vectors.

**5. The relation representation.** Every within-part vertex transposition sends each of the 72 oriented triangle tori to another one, possibly with a minus sign. Apply the six adjacent-transposition generators for \(S_2\times S_3\times S_4\) to the three complete integral relations. The resulting 3-by-3 integer matrices are reproduced in the certificate and checked against the actual 72-dimensional signed permutation action. The generators of \(S_2\) and \(S_3\) act as negative identity. On the \(S_4\) factor the three adjacent transpositions have trace \(-1\), a three-cycle has trace zero and a four-cycle has trace \(+1\); their complete three-dimensional rational character identifies the parity twist of the standard representation. This establishes the displayed symmetry description without asserting an integral lattice splitting.

## Relationship to prior work
Barnett–Farber [1] supplied the deleted-product model and graph-cycle intersection theory; Farber–Hanbury [2] gave nonplanar maturity and edge-addition techniques. These are foundational input, not new results here. The previous exact \(K_{3,3,3}\) two-particle computation [3] establishes a shortest-torus basis and length-type lattice indices for *three parts of equal size three*. It does not state the three integral relations or their action for \(K_{2,3,4}\). The uniform rational representation result [4] explicitly assumes that **all** three part sizes are at least three, so it does not include this two-vertex first part; even if a rational character extended by another argument, it would not supply the integral 72-generator presentation and the exact relation lattice. Previously studied two-particle relations for \(K_{4,4}\) [5] and \(K_6\) [6] concern different graphs and symmetry groups, not a complete tripartite graph with part sizes 2,3,4. The new result treats an unequal-size tripartite boundary directly and computes its integral generator-relations structure. An unindexed equivalent calculation or a stronger general presentation could still subsume this case.

## Limitations
This is an exact classification for the indicated finite graph, not a formula for arbitrary complete multipartite graphs. It concerns only second homology of unordered two-point configurations, and only geometric tori from vertex-disjoint graph cycles. The three relations are not claimed to be minimal in support length (only a full integral basis), and the integral equivariant isomorphism type of the rank-three relation lattice beyond the explicit action matrices is not identified. Computational enumeration is exhaustive for the stated finite graph but does not prove an infinite-family statement. The review is not independent scientific certification.
