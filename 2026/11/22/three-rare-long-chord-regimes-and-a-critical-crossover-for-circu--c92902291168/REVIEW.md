# Scientific review

## Correctness
The result uses the motion-invariant line measure \(d\alpha\,dt\), normalized by the cap perimeter via Cauchy's projection formula. A direct disk-plus-half-plane intersection yields the exact length for every line. The fixed negative-height result is a disk-offset interval integrated over all surviving diameter directions. The fixed positive-height result follows from the unique chord and its two-dimensional, one-sided local wedge; truncated chords cannot contribute to the limiting area away from the wedge boundary. The critical limit uses angle and offset scaled by \(\sqrt\varepsilon\), an explicit bounded limiting region, and dominated convergence. The standalone numerical quadrature confirms these separate mechanisms but is not a proof.

## Originality
The most directly studied retrieved source, Bäsel (2014), computes invariant-line chord laws for regular polygons rather than a clipped circular disk. Gille (2005) considers three-dimensional hemispheres; Coleman's accessible abstract enumerates different convex bodies. Published results on smooth perturbations of disks and higher-dimensional strictly curved diameter pairs do not directly imply this nonsmooth planar clipping crossover. Specific searches for aliases and stronger coverage found no equivalent three-piece critical formula. Gates (1987) and Sulanke (1962) are broader historical chord-distribution sources whose full text could not be obtained; a general density theorem in either might cover individual fixed-shape exponents, and historical priority remains provisional. No independent novelty guarantee is claimed.

## Mathematical value
Crofton chord sampling is a canonical integral-geometric observable. Cutting a disk by a moving line changes its diameter set from a positive-angle family to one boundary-supported pair, then to a unique chord joining corners. The precise change of rare-event exponents, together with a continuous but nonanalytic three-piece crossover at the natural square-root clipping scale, gives a concrete model of degenerate diameter geometry. It is not merely a normalization change or known disk formula substitution.

## Limits
The asymptotics apply for fixed \(h\) or fixed \(\lambda\), not uniform over simultaneous arbitrary parameter regimes. The all-chord integral is exact but unevaluated. Supplementary quadrature is finite evidence. Historical comparisons of inaccessible older broad papers remain an explicitly recorded risk.
