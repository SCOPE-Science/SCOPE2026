The rich-word substitution \(i\mapsto0(i+1)\), \(d-1\mapsto0(d-1)(d-1)\) has exactly \(d+1,d+1,3\) shortest absent scattered subsequences at its first three full-alphabet iterates. More generally, over any alphabet \(L\cup\{a,b\}\), every word \((UaUbUaUbb)^mZ\) with \(m\ge2\), \(\operatorname{alph}(U)=L\), and \(a\notin\operatorname{alph}(Z)\) has precisely \(\{a^{2m+1},ba^{2m},b^2a^{2m-1}\}\) as its shortest absent language.
