# Review

## Correctness — PASS

The claim is fully quantified on the uniform screened four-cycle with \(0<t<1/2\). All 24 permutations fall into three eight-element symmetry orbits. Exact generalized characteristic polynomial identities, a Sylvester positive-definiteness certificate and a strict signed-cubic comparison prove the three-way order of worst-case one-sweep squared-energy factors. The exact ordinary spectra prove the distinct asymptotic comparison. The shipped executable reproduces every symbolic polynomial and the signed identity, with supplementary rational numerical checks. Parameter-limited experiments alone are not used as infinite proofs.

## Originality — PASS (with residual bibliographic risk)

Varga's classical theorem already establishes the relevant spectral-radius optimality, and it is not advanced as new. Dessalew and collaborators (2021) treat refinements and asymptotic relations; Xiao, Xiao and Sun (2019) study complexity of symmetrized orders. A public finite-horizon arrowhead ordering theorem has different graph support, and a public screened-Poisson block theorem optimizes a randomized single-block draw, not all full-sweep deterministic orders. No implication to the exact one-sweep four-cycle three-tier norm was identified in the inspected primary claims and searched records. Harrar (1993) and Kuo–Chan (1990) were available at abstract level only: specialist priority verification remains a genuine risk, not proof of uniqueness.

## Value — PASS

The three-orbit law gives a sharp and nontrivial separation between a widely used two-stage red–black schedule, a three-stage interlaced alternative, and sequential cycle order, using the natural energy metric of SPD linear solvers. Red–black is spectrally tied for fastest convergence yet strictly worst after a complete first sweep, with a sharp almost twofold weak-coupling penalty. The theorem does not predict wall-clock cost, later-horizon optimality, or behavior on larger/weighted cycles.
