# Three-torsion and 58 two-cycles in an order-nine quotient of the rank-three binary symplectic building

## Finding

Consider the rank-three spherical symplectic building \(\mathcal B\) of a six-dimensional symplectic vector space over \(\mathbb F_2\). The norm-one cyclic subgroup \(C_9\) of order nine described below acts simplicially, preserving isotropic-subspace rank. Its **ordinary geometric quotient** \(Y=|\mathcal B|/C_9\) has

\[
\pi_1(Y)\cong H_1(Y;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z,
\qquad H_2(Y;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{58},
\qquad H_i(Y;\mathbb Z)=0\quad(i\ge3).
\]

Moreover \(H_0(Y;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z\), \(H^1(Y;\mathbb Z)=0\), and \(H^2(Y;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{58}\oplus\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z\). The quotient has a natural orbit-cell decomposition with 59 vertices, 315 edges and 315 triangles. The unique order-three subgroup fixes precisely nine isolated rank-two isotropic vertices, falling into three orbits under \(C_9\). All six order-nine group elements have empty fixed-point sets. Thus the nonfree symmetry decreases the expected cyclic fundamental group from order nine to order three.

## Assumptions and scope

Let \(E=\mathbb F_2[t]/(t^6+t+1)\), represented as the field with 64 elements, and let \(F=\mathbb F_8\) be its unique subfield with eight elements. Use the alternating form

\[
B(x,y)=\operatorname{Tr}_{F/\mathbb F_2}(xy^8+x^8y).
\]

The automorphism \(x\mapsto x^8\) is the nontrivial automorphism of the quadratic extension \(E/F\). Write \(a=t^7\), which has multiplicative order nine, and define \(C_9=\langle a\rangle\), acting on \(E\) by multiplication. Since \(a^9=1\), this is a subgroup of the norm-one torus of \(E/F\) and preserves \(B\). The complex \(\mathcal B\) is the **order complex** of the poset of nonzero totally \(B\)-isotropic \(\mathbb F_2\)-linear subspaces of \(E\) of dimensions one, two, or three. A simplex is a proper nested flag, and its dimension is at most two. Quotient means the topological quotient of the geometric realization, **not** an abstract simplicial quotient that discards stabilizer identifications.

## Proof

**1. Symplectic form and torus.** Since \(z\mapsto z+z^8\) has image exactly \(F\), the expression \(B(x,xz)=\operatorname{Tr}_{F/\mathbb F_2}(x^9(z+z^8))\) is not identically zero in \(z\) whenever \(x\ne0\): the field trace pairing on \(F\) is nondegenerate. Hence \(B\) is nondegenerate; it is alternating because the characteristic is two. The chosen polynomial is primitive, so \(t\) has order 63, and \(a=t^7\) has order nine. Since \((ax)^9=x^9\), multiplication by \(a\) is symplectic.

**2. Fixed flags and the nine branch vertices.** If \(g\in C_9\) has order nine, the order of two modulo nine is six. Thus its minimal polynomial on \(E\) over \(\mathbb F_2\) has degree six, and \(E\) has no nonzero proper \(g\)-invariant \(\mathbb F_2\)-subspace. In particular \(g\) fixes no building vertices. Let \(\omega=a^3\), of order three. An \(\omega\)-stable subspace is an \(\mathbb F_4\)-linear space, hence has even dimension over \(\mathbb F_2\); within our rank-one/line/Lagrangian poset, only rank-two subspaces can be fixed. Every such invariant rank-two subspace is \(x\mathbb F_4\) for \(x\ne0\). Since \(\omega^8=\omega^2\) and \(\omega+\omega^2=1\),

\[
B(x,\omega x)=\operatorname{Tr}_{F/\mathbb F_2}(x^9).
\]

Consequently, the \(\omega\)-stable plane \(x\mathbb F_4\) is isotropic precisely if \(\operatorname{Tr}_{F/\mathbb F_2}(x^9)=0\). There are 21 distinct \(\mathbb F_4\)-lines in \(E\), and the norm map \(x\mathbb F_4\mapsto x^9\in F^*\) has fibers of size three. Among the seven nonzero elements of \(F\), exactly three have trace zero. Therefore exactly \(3\cdot3=9\) vertices of the rank-two type are fixed by \(\omega\), and the same by \(\omega^2\). Because the action preserves ranks, a point in the interior of a simplex can be fixed only if each of its rank-distinct vertices is fixed. As no rank-one or rank-three vertex is fixed, the fixed-point set of the order-three subgroup is precisely those nine isolated points. In particular the stabilizers occurring are either trivial or \(C_3\). Since each of the nine fixed-line vertices has orbit length three, there are three branched orbits in the geometric quotient.

**3. Fundamental group.** The Solomon–Tits theorem for a split rank-three group of type \(C_3\) over \(\mathbb F_2\) gives \(|\mathcal B|\simeq\bigvee^{512}S^2\): the standard unipotent subgroup has order \(2^9=512\). Thus the building is path connected and simply connected. Armstrong's orbit-space theorem identifies \(\pi_1(|\mathcal B|/C_9)\) with the quotient of \(C_9\) by the normal subgroup generated by all elements having fixed points. Those elements are exactly \(C_3\), by Step 2. It follows that

\[
\pi_1(Y)\cong C_9/C_3\cong\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z.
\]

**4. Integral homology.** The Euler characteristic of the original building is \(1+512=513\). For a finite group acting cellularly without inversions, the quotient Euler characteristic is the average of the Euler characteristics of the fixed subcomplexes. Here the identity contributes 513, each of the two nonidentity order-three elements contributes nine isolated points, and the remaining six elements contribute zero. Therefore

\[
\chi(Y)=\frac{513+9+9}{9}=59.
\]

The quotient is a connected finite two-dimensional CW complex, hence has no homology above degree two. Its first integral homology is the abelianization of the fundamental group, namely \(\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z\). Its second integral homology is a subgroup of the free abelian group of two-cells, so it is free; the Euler formula therefore gives \(\operatorname{rank}H_2(Y;\mathbb Z)=59-1=58\). The integral cohomology follows from the universal coefficient theorem.

## Relationship to prior work

The Solomon–Tits theorem establishes the homotopy type of the **unquotiented** building, including its sphere count. Armstrong's theorem and the Brown–Higgins groupoid treatment provide the general orbit-space fundamental-group mechanism. Drudge studies the orbits of Singer groups and subgroups on projective flats, but not the isotropic restriction of this specific order-nine symplectic action or the geometric quotient's homotopy invariants. The present claim combines the symplectic norm-one action, its nine fixed isotropic lines, Armstrong's stabilizer quotient and the exact integral Euler/homology computation. None of those general prior theorems alone supplies the fixed-isotropic-line calculation or these numerical invariants.

## Limitations

The result determines the fundamental group and all integral homology/cohomology groups, **not** the complete homotopy equivalence type, cup products, or the full equivariant module structure. The exact cellular calculation is reproducible but does not establish uniqueness relative to unindexed unpublished work. Publication-priority comparison is limited to inspected sources and relevant accessible indices. The literal polynomial model fixes one explicit conjugacy-type representative; no statement about every arbitrary order-nine subgroup action is asserted.
