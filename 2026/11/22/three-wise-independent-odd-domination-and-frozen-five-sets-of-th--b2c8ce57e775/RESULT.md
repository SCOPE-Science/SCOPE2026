# Three-wise independent odd domination and frozen five-sets of the Pappus graph

## Finding

Let \(P\) be the cubic, bipartite Pappus graph on eighteen vertices and let \(M=I+A(P)\) over \(\mathbb F_2\). A set \(S\subseteq V(P)\) is *odd dominating* when each closed neighborhood has odd intersection with \(S\), equivalently \(M\mathbf 1_S=\mathbf 1\). Its complete cardinality enumerator is

\[
  O_P(z)=\sum_{S:\,M\mathbf 1_S=\mathbf 1}z^{|S|}
  =18z^5+92z^9+18z^{13}.
\]

There are exactly \(128\) solutions, forming an affine binary code of dimension seven whose uniform distribution is **three-wise independent**: for any three distinct vertices and any prescribed choice of selected/not selected on them, exactly sixteen solutions realize the choice. Four-wise independence fails already on any closed neighborhood.

The eighteen minimum odd-dominating sets have cardinality five and are precisely
\[
 S_v=N_P(v)\cup\{w:\operatorname{dist}_P(v,w)=4\},\qquad v\in V(P).
\]
For distinct centers \(u,v\), the intersection \(|S_u\cap S_v|\) is respectively \(0,1,2,1\) when \(\operatorname{dist}_P(u,v)=1,2,3,4\). Thus all eighteen minimum sets are isolated under one- or two-vertex simultaneous replacements, while the exact-three-replacement graph is the distance-three graph of \(P\), connected, six-regular, with \(54\) edges and diameter three. No odd-dominating set of \(P\) induces a connected subgraph; this last consequence is included for completeness, not asserted as independent novelty.

## Assumptions and scope

The vertex set is \(\{0,1,\dots,17\}\). Form a cycle \(0-1-\cdots-17-0\), then add the nine chords

```
(0,5) (1,8) (2,13) (3,10) (4,15)
(6,11) (7,14) (9,16) (12,17)
```

These are the standard Pappus graph's chords, also specified by the LCF notation `[5,7,-7,7,-7,-5]^3`. All graph neighborhoods in the odd-domination definition are *closed*. All binary matrix operations use \(\mathbb F_2\); all set counts are for labeled vertices. A simultaneous replacement of \(t\) vertices means deleting and inserting exactly \(t\) vertices, preserving cardinality and feasibility at both ends; no intermediate configurations are required.

## Proof

**1. Metric geometry and minimum size.** Direct breadth-first search on the displayed 27 edges gives, from every vertex, distance-layer sizes \((1,3,6,6,2)\) and intersection array
\[
 \{b_0,b_1,b_2,b_3;c_1,c_2,c_3,c_4\}
 =\{3,2,2,1;1,1,2,3\}.
\]
These facts can also be checked from the neighbor lists generated by `verify_direct.py`. The Pappus graph has girth six. Every selected vertex contributes to at most four closed neighborhoods. As all eighteen closed neighborhoods must contain at least one selected vertex, \(|S|\ge\lceil18/4\rceil=5\).

For each \(v\), the set \(S_v\) selects its three immediate neighbors and both vertices at distance four. At a vertex in layer zero, one sees three chosen neighbors; at a vertex in each of layers one, two, three and four, the closed neighborhood contains exactly one chosen vertex by the intersection numbers and bipartite layer structure. Hence \(S_v\) is odd dominating and \(|S_v|=5\).

Conversely, suppose \(|S|=5\) is odd dominating. Every vertex in \(S\) has an even number of selected neighbors (since its closed-neighborhood selected count is odd). Because \(P\) has girth six, no subgraph on five vertices contains a cycle; a finite forest with all degrees even has no edges. Thus \(S\) is independent. Its five members have fifteen distinct-incidence neighbor contributions to the thirteen vertices outside \(S\). Each outside vertex has one or three selected neighbors. The equation \(15=13+2\) forces exactly one outside vertex \(v\) to have three chosen neighbors, all others one. Thus \(N(v)\subseteq S\). The six vertices at distance three from \(v\) see none of \(N(v)\). Among the two additional selected vertices, one at distance two, three or four can cover at most two, one or three vertices, respectively, in that six-vertex layer (its own closed neighborhood is counted for a distance-three selected vertex). Only two vertices at distance four can cover all six. That layer has exactly two such vertices; hence \(S=S_v\). The same enumeration proves distinctness of the eighteen sets.

**2. Rank, three-wise independence, and the spectrum.** The following eleven original closed-neighborhood rows are linearly independent over \(\mathbb F_2\):

```
0 2 1 3 8 6 5 7 4 9 11
```

The seven linearly independent kernel vectors, in vertex order from index zero to seventeen, are

```
010101101010000000
101101000001100000
011101110001010000
011011010000001000
010111011100000100
110110000100000010
100000101101000001
```

Each vector can be multiplied by the explicitly defined matrix to check it maps to zero; their coordinates in columns \(10,12,13,14,15,16,17\) form an identity matrix. The row and kernel certificates imply \(\operatorname{rank}(M)=11\), so the affine solution space has exactly \(2^7=128\) vectors; it is nonempty because the constructed \(S_v\) solve the equation.

Read the seven kernel vectors as rows. The eighteen columns, encoded as seven-bit integers in vertex order, are

```
62 3d 0e 37 38 1f 45 1c 51 70 01 46 02 04 08 10 20 40
```

All values are hexadecimal. None is zero, and the XOR of any two or any three *distinct* values is nonzero; the `verify_algebra.cpp` file checks all \(18+\binom{18}{2}+\binom{18}{3}\) relevant comparisons. Therefore projection of the kernel onto any three coordinates is surjective, proving exactly \(2^{7-3}=16\) solutions in each of the eight patterns on three prescribed vertices. Four-wise independence fails because each four-element closed neighborhood has fixed odd parity.

Let \(X=|S|\) for a uniformly chosen solution. Three-wise independence gives \(\mathbb E X=9\) and \(\operatorname{Var}X=18/4=9/2\). As \(P\) is cubic, \(M\mathbf 1=\mathbf 0\); complementation pairs solutions of weights summing to eighteen. Bipartiteness, with shores of nine vertices, implies every solution has odd weight: sum the equations for vertices of one shore to obtain \(|S|\equiv9\pmod2\), since each vertex contributes once or three times. The established minimum five and complementation bound the possible weights to \(5,7,9,11,13\). Both extreme coefficients equal eighteen by the minimum-set classification. Write the multiplicities at seven and eleven as \(b\) each. The variance equation gives
\[
 \frac{36\cdot16+2b\cdot4}{128}=\frac92,
\]
so \(b=0\) and the nine-weight multiplicity is \(128-36=92\), proving the polynomial.

**3. Optimal exchange geometry.** Computing the intersections of all eighteen explicitly defined sets gives the distance-wise table

| Distance between centers | Number of unordered pairs | Size of intersection |
|---|---:|---:|
| 1 | 27 | 0 |
| 2 | 54 | 1 |
| 3 | 54 | 2 |
| 4 | 18 | 1 |

This is a finite exhaustive certificate, checked by both implementations from the displayed graph, not an assertion of a general distance-regular theorem. Replacing \(t\) members of a five-set requires intersection size \(5-t\). The maximal intersection for distinct optima is two, achieved precisely at center distance three. Therefore no one- or two-member move is feasible and the three-replacement graph has exactly the distance-three adjacency of \(P\). Its \(54\) edges, degree six, connectivity and diameter three are obtained by exhaustive breadth-first search on the eighteen centers; the complete algorithm is embedded as `verify_direct.py`.

**4. Connectedness boundary.** For every odd dominating set, each selected vertex has even degree in the induced graph. Since \(P\) is cubic, the induced degrees are zero or two. Any connected induced selected subgraph of more than one vertex must be a cycle, necessarily even because \(P\) is bipartite. But every solution has odd cardinality and at least five vertices. Thus no connected solution exists. The general Eulerian observation is pre-existing literature; the exact Pappus-specific code classification is the main claim.

## Verification

`verify_direct.py` independently builds the graph using a cycle and chord list, then exhausts all \(2^{18}=262144\) subsets, all \(\binom{18}{3}=816\) triples, all \(153\) center pairs, and the eighteen breadth-first searches in the three-move reconfiguration graph. `verify_algebra.cpp` builds the same graph via independently coded LCF shifts, row-reduces \(M\), checks nullspace and triple-XOR certificates, and independently enumerates all subsets. With Python 3, run `python3 verify_direct.py`; with a C++17 compiler, run `c++ -std=c++17 -O2 verify_algebra.cpp -o verify_algebra && ./verify_algebra`. Both are standalone, require no third-party libraries or external datasets, and were replayed after extraction from the packaged result.

The proof above is all finite: full enumeration of eighteen-vertex graph configurations is exhaustive, not extrapolation to an infinite family. The rank certificate and minimum-set classification provide independent mathematical explanations of the census.

## Relationship to prior work

Caro and Klostermeyer (2003) define and investigate odd domination and its minimum cardinality; Caro, Klostermeyer and Yuster (2005) study connected odd domination and the general even-degree condition for induced selected subgraphs. Those general facts and the immediate no-connected-solution corollary are not claimed as original. Existing graph-specific censuses already give eighteen ordinary dominating five-sets on Pappus; this minimum count, viewed only as ordinary domination, is not a new conclusion here. Odd domination is a stronger parity condition, and the exact 128-solution affine coset, three-coordinate uniformity and full three-weight spectrum are distinct. Results for generalized Petersen graphs or rook graphs do not imply those Pappus-specific parity-code data. The minimum odd-domination geometry and exchange calculations provide supporting classification rather than a separate priority claim for the bare number eighteen. Direct title, keyword, theorem and implication comparisons of available sources did not identify an earlier statement covering this joint result.

## Limitations

The finding concerns one specified labeled graph; it does not classify odd domination for all cubic graphs, distance-regular graphs or Pappus-type configurations. Source discovery is not comprehensive, so an inaccessible or unindexed earlier code table may overlap. The self-review are not independent scholarly review. The three-move diameter statement refers to the optimal-set exchange graph, not to intermediate paths of suboptimal states.
