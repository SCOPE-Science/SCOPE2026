# Self-review

**Single assessed claim.** The exact threshold-capture mean and Poisson-jump absorption probability for a Brownian process with sign-reversing position multipliers, together with their sharp strict-contraction and endpoint asymptotics. All statements concern the same stopping rule and parameter domain defined in `RESULT.md`.

## Correctness — PASS
For each \(D,r,L>0\) and \(0<q\le1\), a uniform finite-time capture probability ensures a bounded mean and uniqueness of bounded solutions. On the left of \(-L/q\), every reset is an absorbing event and the exponential bounded solution yields an inhomogeneous Robin boundary. On the remaining compact interval the arguments of the pantograph operator remain inside that interval, and explicit convergent odd and even entire-function series span the two initial-value solutions. Matching at the Robin and absorbing boundaries yields the displayed strictly positive denominator and exact laws. Product-coefficient asymptotics and the separate exactly reflective \(q=1\) specialization prove the two rare-jump laws and the singular strong-reset behavior. `verify.py` passed 250 independent recurrence, finite-difference and asymptotic diagnostics; those finite tests support but do not replace the proof. No stationary-process assumption enters the reflective boundary case.

## Originality — PASS, with residual priority risk
Biroli et al., arXiv:2406.08387v1, study **exact point hitting** with negative proportional resets: a jump flying over the target does not absorb, and their full-line backward equation lacks the absorbing reset-payoff interface. The publicly available *A simulation-free resolvent for reflected partial-reset first passage* and *Certified first-passage series for Brownian reflection–rescaling* were read in full at the RESULT level and explicitly retain the point-hit convention. Neither theorem implies the nonzero jump-capture channel or the present one-sided threshold-capture law. Pierce, arXiv:2204.07215v1, uses positive multipliers, while Radice et al., arXiv:2410.05835v1, use discrete additive jumps and different resetting. Actual published-finding corpus source-, parameter-, alias- and equivalence-search queries were run, and accessible same-slot claim records were checked; no covering statement was found. Standard backward-equation methods are not claimed new. Unindexed stronger literature or equivalent formulations remain possible and independent priority adjudication has not occurred.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The distinction between entering a target region by a jump and merely landing on its exact boundary is essential for an intermittent search. The result evaluates that newly enabled capture channel and the mean, and it classifies a singular transport regime: arbitrarily strong strict-contraction resetting makes mean capture time exponentially large, whereas exact reflection leaves a finite \(L^2/(2D)\) mean and a positive limiting jump-capture probability. These are global parameter effects rather than isolated samples. No optimization theorem, stochastic-rescaling generalization, or joint singular scaling window is claimed.
