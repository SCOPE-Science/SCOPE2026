# Toral derivations of one-variable truncated local algebras: parity, ranks, and orbits

## Finding
Let \(k\) be any field and \(N\ge 3\). Set \(A_N=k[t]/(t^N)\). Classify the ordinary \(k\)-linear derivations \(D:A_N\to A_N\) satisfying the composition identity \(D^2=D\).

If \(\operatorname{char}(k)\ne2\), the zero map is the only such derivation. In characteristic two, in which \(h=\lfloor N/2\rfloor\), all solutions are the zero map and precisely the maps
\[
D_f(g)=f(t)g'(t)\pmod{t^N},\qquad
f(t)=t+\sum_{j=\delta}^{h-\varepsilon}a_jt^{2j},
\]
where \(\delta=0\) for even \(N\) and \(\delta=1\) for odd \(N\), and \(\varepsilon=1\) for even \(N\) and \(\varepsilon=0\) for odd \(N\). Thus the index range is \(0\le j\le h-1\) for \(N=2h\) and \(1\le j\le h\) for \(N=2h+1\). In either parity the nonzero solutions form an affine \(h\)-space (on their reduced parameter locus), and each has \(k\)-linear rank exactly \(h\).

For \(k=\mathbb F_q\), the rank-enumerator is \(1+q^hX^h\) in characteristic two and \(1\) otherwise. Under conjugation by \(\operatorname{Aut}_k(A_N)\), odd-length characteristic-two algebras have exactly two orbits (zero and nonzero); even-length ones have exactly three (zero, the nonzero derivations preserving the maximal ideal, and those not preserving it). For \(N=2h\), the respective orbit sizes are \(1\), \(q^{h-1}\), and \((q-1)q^{h-1}\); for \(N=2h+1\), they are \(1\) and \(q^h\).

## Assumptions and scope
All derivations are ordinary Leibniz derivations \(D(ab)=D(a)b+aD(b)\), not weighted or Hasse–Schmidt operators. The expression \(D^2\) is composition, not the pointwise square. The theorem holds for all fields, finite or infinite, with \(N\ge3\); finite-field formulas require a prime-power \(q\). By the reduced solution locus we mean the reduced subscheme in the affine coefficient space of derivations; no assertion about reducedness or smoothness of the unreduced defining scheme is made. Conjugacy is by unital \(k\)-algebra automorphisms.

## Proof
**1. Parameterize every derivation.** A derivation is determined by \(f=D(t)\), with \(D(t^i)=it^{i-1}f\). The relation \(D(t^N)=0\) is equivalent to \(N t^{N-1}f=0\). Thus \(f\) may have a constant term precisely when the characteristic divides \(N\). Each permitted \(f\) defines a derivation \(f\,d/dt\).

**2. Exclude nonzero idempotent derivations outside characteristic two.** In any commutative algebra, direct application of the Leibniz rule twice gives
\[
(D^2-D)(xy)=(D^2-D)(x)y+x(D^2-D)(y)+2D(x)D(y).
\]
When \(D^2=D\) and \(2\ne0\), taking \(x=y=t\) forces \(f^2=0\). For nonzero \(f\in k[t]/(t^N)\), let \(v(f)\) be its least nonzero degree. Then \(f^2=0\) implies \(v(f)\ge\lceil N/2\rceil\ge2\). But \(D^2(t)=ff'\) has valuation at least \(2v(f)-1>v(f)\), or vanishes. It cannot equal \(D(t)=f\). Hence \(D=0\).

**3. Characteristic two.** Here \(D^2\) is itself a derivation, because the cross term \(2D(x)D(y)\) vanishes. Consequently \(D^2=D\) is equivalent to its value on \(t\), namely
\[
 f(f'-1)=0\quad\text{in }k[t]/(t^N).
\]
If \(f\ne0\) has positive valuation at least two, then \(ff'\) has strictly larger valuation than \(f\), so it is impossible. If \(f\) is a unit, then \(f'=1\). If \(v(f)=1\), its annihilator is the ideal generated by \(t^{N-1}\); therefore \(f'-1\in(t^{N-1})\). Since in characteristic two a formal derivative has only even-degree terms and degree at most \(N-2\), this forces \(f'=1\) as well. Thus every nonzero solution has \(f'=1\): it is \(t\) plus an arbitrary even polynomial. The relation on \(t^N\) requires zero constant term for odd \(N\) and imposes no restriction for even \(N\), giving exactly the displayed normal forms. Conversely these satisfy both the truncation condition and \(ff'=f\), hence they are idempotent derivations.

**4. Rank.** For all admissible nonzero \(f\), \(f'=1\), so the coefficient of \(t\) in \(f\) is one. The derivation kills every even power, while \(D(t^{2i+1})=t^{2i}f\) for each \(2i+1<N\). These \(h\) images have pairwise distinct least odd-degree terms unless \(f\) has nonzero constant term. In the latter case their least even degrees are distinct instead. Hence they are independent and \(\operatorname{rank}D=h\). Their number over \(\mathbb F_q\) is \(q^h\). The normal forms over every extension field show that the reduced nonzero locus is \(\mathbb A^h\), disjoint from the isolated zero point.

**5. Automorphism orbits.** Write \(a=f(0)\). Because \(f'=1\), \(s=f-a\) has zero constant term and linear coefficient one; substitution \(t\mapsto s\) defines a unital algebra automorphism of \(A_N\). Moreover \(D(s)=D(f)=ff'=f=s+a\). Thus in coordinate \(s\) the derivation has the normal form \((s+a)d/ds\). If \(a\ne0\), replace \(s\) by \(s/a\) to normalize the constant to one. The invariant distinction is whether \(D\) preserves the unique maximal ideal \((t)\), equivalently whether \(a=0\). For odd \(N\), only \(a=0\) occurs. For even \(N\), both cases occur with the stated orbit sizes, since exactly one of the \(h\) free even coefficients is \(a\). The zero derivation is a separate orbit. This completes the proof.

## Verification
The standalone `check_idempotent_derivations.py` checks the original Leibniz identity on all monomial pairs, the original composition identity \(D^2=D\) as a matrix equation, the proposed normal forms, and each matrix rank. It exhausts all permitted derivations for \(\mathbb F_2\) at \(3\le N\le9\), \(\mathbb F_4\) at \(3\le N\le6\), \(\mathbb F_3\) at \(3\le N\le5\), and \(\mathbb F_5\) at \(3\le N\le4\). The checks passed. Enumeration verifies these finite cases only; the preceding symbolic argument proves the theorem for every field and \(N\ge3\).

## Relationship to prior work
Qiu–Zheng–Dan (first public preprint in 2023) develop free quasi-idempotent differential Rota–Baxter algebras and establish Gröbner–Shirshov bases over characteristic zero; their differential operator has a potentially nonzero *weight* in its Leibniz rule. The present theorem classifies ordinary, weight-zero Leibniz derivations under the separate composition identity \(D^2=D\) in arbitrary characteristic and on a specified finite local algebra. Hoffmann–Kowalski study integrating restricted/iterative derivations into Hasse–Schmidt derivations, primarily over fields; that does not furnish the coefficient normal forms and finite-ring orbit multiplicities here. Premet studies restricted derivation geometry for prime characteristic greater than two on different truncated polynomial algebras, and explains the toral viewpoint. Related existing finite-field censuses for square-zero derivations on \(k[t]/(t^N)\) concern \(D^2=0\), not this nonzero idempotent locus. Related toral-derivation counts for null-filiform Leibniz algebras involve a different nonassociative multiplication and do not logically imply the present associative local-ring classification.

## Limitations
This theorem does not classify derivations with \(D^p=D\) for odd primes beyond the special idempotence condition, nor the nonreduced scheme structure of the equation locus. Some published literature and historical records are inaccessible; absence of an exact match in the inspected record does not prove publication priority. The zero and affine components are statements about the reduced solution locus only.
