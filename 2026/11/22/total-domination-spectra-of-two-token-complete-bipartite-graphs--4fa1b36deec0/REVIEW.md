# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The token graph has exactly the two claimed bipartition classes; selected cross states and selected same-shore states must each separately dominate the opposite class. The cross requirement is at most one empty row/column, and the same-shore requirement is an edge cover of at least one whole shore. These facts prove the complete polynomial by inclusion-exclusion. The critical lemma for minimality is that a deletion-minimal edge cover has star components; the analogous cross incidence classification and formal labelled-star exponential formula are proved in `RESULT.md`. The closed lower, upper and minimum-multiplicity formulas follow from explicit extremizers. Exact independent enumerations in `verify.py` corroborate the low-order cases. Risks: numerical experiments cannot substitute for the general structural proof; no evidence is supplied for host families beyond complete bipartite graphs.

**Originality — PASS (provisional historical priority).** The motivating papers arXiv:2610.31557 and arXiv:2611.15124 determine **ordinary** domination numbers, not the full total-domination polynomial or inclusionwise minimal total-domination spectrum. arXiv:2610.31557 gives a more general ordinary-dominating-set characterization, which is relevant but does not directly state the open-neighbourhood polynomial or the star-forest minimality classification. published-finding corpus searches found adjacent records on ordinary domination polynomials of two-token bicliques and on total domination for different graph products, none with an equivalent host-and-invariant statement. Risks: a published or unindexed general set-enumerator could subsume the formulas; priority is not independently certified.

**Value — PASS.** This completes two natural exact combinatorial counting invariants across an infinite standard graph family whose ordinary domination theory is under active study. It identifies all minimum total-dominating-set counts, the entire inclusion-minimal cardinality spectrum and a sharp upper total-domination number that grows substantially beyond the minimum. This is more than a change of notation or a single small finite table; the star-forest structural lemma explains the spectrum. Limits: the result does not determine total domination on arbitrary token graphs or any reconfiguration distance.
