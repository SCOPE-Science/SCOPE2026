# Scientific review

## Correctness

PASS. The endpoint-indexed integral decomposition of invariant two-paths produces oriented triangles, differences of long squares, and differences of backtracks. Consequently the regular/nonregular two-boundary quotient is generated by edge backtracks. Incidence differences identify the edge generators in each connected component, triangle boundaries kill the class, and the arc-sum homomorphism proves every surviving component class has infinite order. Naturality follows on chains. The finite exact-field checker corroborates 1103 cases; it does not prove the infinite statement or certify integral torsion, which is handled by the proof.

## Originality

PASS with priority risk. This is not the discovery of regularization, a new generating set, or a two-vertex example: Chaplin (2022), Props. 3.15/3.17 and Rem. 3.18, and earlier GLMY sources give those. The contribution is a global integral and functorial classification of the regularization kernel over **all** finite symmetric orientations, with triangle-free component generators and explicit persistence transitions. Close primary full-text comparisons and research-record searches found the ingredients but no statement dominating the global natural kernel theorem. Because this deduction uses known generators, an equivalent existing formulation is the main residual risk.

## Mathematical value

PASS. The theorem determines a natural relative invariant for an unrestricted family, not an arbitrary small instance. It identifies the exact obstruction to replacing nonregular with regular path homology: one free class for each nontrivial triangle-free component, with sharp death upon triangle formation, and provides coherent graph-filtration maps. This makes convention changes in degree-one network topology quantitatively transparent. It does not assert a splitting or novel higher-homology classification.

## Closest literature and limitations

Muranov–Muranova (arXiv:2611.15308v1, first public 2026-11-11) studies path complexes with concatenation, functorial relations and variants; its results motivate the setting but do not classify this kernel. Chaplin (DOI:10.1007/s41468-022-00108-3), §3.3 and Example 3.21, contains directly applicable generating-set ingredients and selected examples. The GLMY original path-homology work and the motif paper (DOI:10.1007/s41109-021-00441-z) were also compared. Only finite symmetric orientations and degree one are covered; noncanonical splitting and complete priority certainty are not claimed.
