# Scientific review: paired width and geometric brightness of ellipsoids

## Correctness — PASS

The proof explicitly fixes both observables in a shared Haar-uniform direction. The support-function identity gives squared half-width, the complementary-minor formula gives squared area divided by \(\pi^2\), and the normalized Gaussian representation gives the exact \(\operatorname{Dirichlet}(1/2,1/2,1/2)\) law of coordinate squares. The affine two-dimensional Jacobian is the nonzero Vandermonde of the three squared semiaxes. This proves the density, normalization, and reconstruction map. Spherical fourth moments independently prove the exact strict-negative squared-measurement covariance. The equal-axis cases are derived as singular beta/point-mass laws. Symbolic determinant identities and 900,000 independent Gaussian-normal samples over three axis configurations support, but do not replace, this proof.

## Originality — PASS, provisional historical priority

The full available text of Kim–Yaskin–Zvavitch's 2015 *Distribution functions of sections and projections of convex bodies* treats separate one-dimensional marginal area distributions, not a jointly sampled random width and projection area. Muinonen–Lumme's 2015 ellipsoid geometry and photometry supply analytic formulas, but the material inspected does not present the Haar-pushforward triangular density or eight-point inverse. The full text of the 2026 study *Endpoint tomography from paired sections and shadows of triaxial ellipsoids* treats the one-dimensional product of precisely the two quadratic observables; knowing that product law does not give their full joint law or directional fibers. Abas's 2026 broad random-width-and-brightness preprint focuses in its accessible abstract on polyhedral marginal densities and finite direction-system reconstruction; its full 92-page text was not accessible for a decisive line-by-line comparison. A general bivariate quadratic-form distribution may yield this special case by substitution; the review accordingly makes no claim of absolute historical priority or a fundamentally new integration method.

## Mathematical value — PASS

A natural two-measurement experiment in classical geometric tomography is completely classified: the joint support has exactly three vertices, the density is explicit and integrably singular at each side, and a **single paired measurement** recovers all principal direction squares, hence the viewing normal up to exactly the inherent reflection symmetries. The population law itself recovers all semiaxes from its convex-support vertices and exhibits a sharp rank reduction from triaxial to spheroidal to spherical cases. The exact negative squared-observable covariance quantifies mutual dependence rather than merely reporting separate marginals. This conditional inverse information is inaccessible from either isolated distribution or the previously studied product of the two measurements. It is useful as an exact ground-truth model for paired shape inversion, not an arbitrary slice chosen to make a simple calculation.

## Closest literature and limitations

The formulas for support width, ellipsoid projection area, and spherical Dirichlet squares are classical, and no novelty is claimed for them individually. An ellipsoid's unsquared width/brightness covariance, measurement noise, other convex bodies, and orientation recovery beyond sign symmetries are outside the claim. The uninspected full text of arXiv:2609.26202, and possible general multivariate quadratic-form density literature, remain residual priority risks. The mathematically elementary affine-pushforward proof limits the interpretation of the result to an exact natural classification, not a general tomography theorem.
