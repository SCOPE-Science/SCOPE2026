# Triple closed-neighborhood domination in the Clebsch graph: three geometric optimum types and a sharp two-replacement threshold

## Finding
Consider the standard **five-regular** Clebsch graph \(G\), not its ten-regular complement. The minimum number of selected vertices that appear at least three times in each closed neighborhood is exactly ten. There are \(312\) optimal selections. Their six omitted vertices induce exactly one of \(3K_2\), \(C_5\sqcup K_1\), and \(C_6\), in respective multiplicities \(40,192,80\). These three types also form three orbits under the natural order-\(1920\) affine-coordinate group.

Define \(\mathcal R_t\) to have the optimal selections as vertices; two are adjacent if one can replace between one and \(t\) selected vertices simultaneously, keeping the resulting set optimal. Then \(\mathcal R_1\) has no edges; \(\mathcal R_2\) is connected, has exactly \(5,220\) edges and has radius and diameter both \(3\). Its degree distribution is \(120\) vertices of degree \(39\), and \(192\) of degree \(30\). This establishes a sharp two-replacement mobility threshold. The complete triple-domination polynomial is
\[
312z^{10}+1872z^{11}+1580z^{12}+560z^{13}+120z^{14}+16z^{15}+z^{16}.
\]

## Assumptions and scope
Let \(V=\{0,1,\ldots,15\}\), representing four-bit binary vectors. Connect \(a,b\) exactly when \(a\oplus b\in\{1,2,4,8,15\}\). Here \(\oplus\) denotes bitwise XOR. The set \(D\subseteq V\) is *triple dominating* when \(|N[v]\cap D|\ge3\) for all \(v\in V\), with closed neighborhood \(N[v]=\{v\}\cup\{v\oplus 1,v\oplus 2,v\oplus4,v\oplus8,v\oplus15\}\). Labels are retained; no quotient is taken for cardinality counts.

The equivalent sixteen-vertex realization consists of the even-parity binary five-bit words, two adjacent when their Hamming distance is four. Its affine-coordinate group is generated by all sixteen even-parity translations and the \(120\) permutations of the five coordinates. In this work an orbit means an orbit under that explicitly defined group. The asserted result is finite, for this graph alone, and is not a universal graph-family formula.

## Proof
**Exact finite enumeration.** For any candidate \(D\), create its sixteen-bit incidence mask \(m\). The necessary-and-sufficient test is
\[
\operatorname{popcount}(m\mathbin{\&}b_v)\ge3\qquad(v=0,\ldots,15),
\]
where \(b_v=\sum_{a\in\{0,1,2,4,8,15\}}2^{v\oplus a}\). This test has no heuristic search or truncation: `verify_cayley3.py` evaluates every one of the \(2^{16}=65536\) masks and groups all passing masks by cardinality. The output polynomial stated above has zero coefficients in all cardinalities below ten, and coefficient \(312\) at ten. This completely proves minimality and the entire polynomial for this finite graph.

**Geometric description.** Write \(R=V\setminus D\). Since every closed neighborhood has six points, the triple-domination condition is equivalent to \(|R\cap N[v]|\le3\) for every \(v\). Independently, `verify_even3.py` enumerates all \(\binom{16}{6}=8008\) six-element omissions in the even-parity five-word graph and finds exactly \(312\) feasible ones. The induced degree sequences are \((1,1,1,1,1,1)\) for 40 sets, \((0,2,2,2,2,2)\) for 192 sets, and \((2,2,2,2,2,2)\) for 80 sets. The graph has no triangles, so these correspond respectively to \(3K_2\), \(C_5\sqcup K_1\), and \(C_6\). Exhaustion also verifies the converse: these are all induced six-vertex subsets of their respective types in \(G\). Thus the classification is if-and-only-if, not just a partition of feasible sets.

**Orbit certificate.** For each of the \(120\) five-coordinate permutations and sixteen even translations, the checker transforms each feasible six-set by the explicit binary-coordinate action. It computes each orbit of the resulting action and verifies that the three exhaustive orbit sizes are \(40,80,192\). Because the action maps the five-bit Hamming-four graph to itself, each transformed subset is a valid vertex subset; the program checks orbit closure and that all \(312\) states are exhausted.

**Sharp reconfiguration.** A simultaneous replacement of at most \(t\) selected vertices transforms \(D\) to \(D'\) precisely when \(|D\cap D'|\ge10-t\). The first checker constructs the entire \(312\)-state graph from this exact predicate at \(t=1,2,3\). For \(t=1\), it checks zero edges and 312 singleton components. For \(t=2\), it checks 5,220 undirected edges, degree multiset \(\{39^{(120)},30^{(192)}\}\), one component, and all 312 breadth-first-search eccentricities equal to three. The second checker rebuilds the graph from six-hole intersections \(|R\cap R'|\ge4\) using an unrelated even-word representation; it independently verifies these same figures. These finite exhaustive checks establish connectivity and exact diameter, not merely experimental examples.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify_cayley3.py` and `python3 verify_even3.py` with standard Python 3; no external dependencies or data are needed. Compare their standard output against `cayley3_output.json` and `even3_output.json`. The scripts employ distinct graph models and enumeration orders, verify the two replacement graphs, and independently check the optimal shape classification. The first also enumerates all subsets for the complete polynomial; the second separately excludes seven-hole subsets by exhaustive testing of all \(\binom{16}{7}=11440\) cases. All computations are exact integer arithmetic. A graph finite-enumeration certificate does not imply a theorem about arbitrary strongly regular graphs.

## Relationship to prior work
Cockayne and Thomason (2008) define \(k\)-tuple domination by requiring at least \(k\) selected vertices in each closed neighborhood and prove probabilistic general upper bounds; their complete four-page article contains no Clebsch-graph optimum classification. Standard descriptions of the five-regular Clebsch graph give the strongly regular parameters \((16,5,0,2)\) and the folded-hypercube construction but not the triple domination count or exchange geometry. Henning and Kazemi (2010) discuss the *open-neighborhood* \(k\)-tuple **total** domination predicate, which differs from the present closed-neighborhood predicate; their introduction distinguishes the two. Prior results on identifying codes, metric resolving sets, and locating domination of this graph also impose different selection conditions. The present result does not claim these earlier definitions or graph properties as new.

## Limitations
The classification concerns only the labeled 16-vertex five-regular Clebsch graph; the natural affine-coordinate group was explicitly specified but the complete automorphism group is not required. The checks are exhaustive finite proofs, not an infinite-family argument. Historical novelty is provisional: an unindexed table or alternate use of the term three-fold covering might contain a coincident count. Independent human review has not been performed.
