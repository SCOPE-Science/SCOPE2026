# Tripotent–square-zero fibers of irreducible cubic matrices in characteristic two

## Finding

**Theorem.** Let \(K\) be an arbitrary field of characteristic \(2\). Let \(A\in M_3(K)\) have irreducible characteristic polynomial
\[
f_A(X)=X^3+aX^2+bX+c\in K[X].
\]
Write \(\mathcal D(A)\) for the set of ordered pairs \((T,N)\) satisfying \(A=T+N\), \(T^3=T\), and \(N^2=0\), with no commutation hypothesis.

If \(a\ne0\), then \(\mathcal D(A)=\varnothing\). If \(a=0\), there is a canonical bijection
\[
\mathbb P(K^3)\longrightarrow\mathcal D(A).
\]
For each line \(Ku\), choose any \(u\ne0\), and let \(v:K^3\to K\) be the unique functional specified by
\[
v(u)=0,\quad v(Au)=b+1,\quad v(A^2u)=c.
\]
Then \(N=uv\) (the rank-one operator \(w\mapsto u\,v(w)\)) and \(T=A+N\). Replacing \(u\) by a nonzero scalar multiple does not change \(N\) or \(T\). This construction yields every decomposition exactly once.

In particular, for each even prime power \(q\),
\[
|\mathcal D(A)|=\begin{cases}q^2+q+1,&\operatorname{tr}(A)=0,\\ 0,&\operatorname{tr}(A)\ne0.\end{cases}
\]
In the nonempty case, the action by simultaneous conjugation of the centralizer of \(A\) is transitive, and the scalar-quotient centralizer \(\mathbb F_{q^3}^{\times}/\mathbb F_q^{\times}\) acts sharply transitively.

## Assumptions and scope

The characteristic is exactly \(2\), the matrix size is exactly \(3\), and \(f_A\) is irreducible over \(K\). No finiteness of \(K\) is needed for the classification. The cardinality and Singer-group assertions require finite \(K\). The theorem concerns tripotent summands, not diagonalizable or idempotent summands. The summands need not commute.

## Proof

**1. Necessity of the trace condition.** Since \(f_A\) is irreducible of degree three, \(A\) and \(A+I\) are both invertible. Every square-zero \(3\times3\) matrix has rank at most one and trace zero. If \(A=T+N\) and \(T^3=T\), then over an algebraic closure every eigenvalue of \(T\) is zero or one. Its trace is therefore either zero or one (as an element of \(K\)), and equals \(\operatorname{tr}(A)=a\).

If \(a=1\), the algebraic multiplicity of eigenvalue one for \(T\) is either one or three. In the first case, zero is a simple root of \(X^3-X=X(X+1)^2\), so the zero-primary part is semisimple and \(\operatorname{rank}T=1\). Thus \(\operatorname{rank}(T+N)\le2\), contradicting invertibility of \(A\). In the second case \(T\) is invertible, and \(T^3=T\) gives \(T^2=I\); hence \((T+I)^2=0\), so \(\operatorname{rank}(A+I)=\operatorname{rank}((T+I)+N)\le2\), contradicting invertibility of \(A+I\). Therefore \(a\ne0\) is impossible (values other than zero or one were already ruled out).

**2. Restriction on the tripotent characteristic polynomial.** Suppose \(a=0\). The number of eigenvalues equal to one, counted with algebraic multiplicities, must be even. If it is zero, the minimal polynomial divides both \(X^3\) and \(X(X+1)^2\), hence divides \(X\), and \(T=0\). Then \(A=N\) is square-zero, impossible for irreducible \(f_A\). Hence exactly two eigenvalues are one and the third is zero, so
\[
\chi_T(X)=X(X+1)^2=X^3+X.
\]
Conversely, every \(3\times3\) matrix with this characteristic polynomial satisfies \(T^3=T\) by Cayley–Hamilton.

**3. Exact classification of perturbations.** Every nonzero square-zero \(3\times3\) matrix has rank one, so write \(N=uv\) for \(u\in K^3\setminus\{0\}\) and a nonzero functional \(v\) with \(v(u)=0\). The adjugate identity and Cayley–Hamilton give, when \(a=0\),
\[
\operatorname{adj}(XI-A)=X^2I+XA+A^2+bI.
\]
Consequently the rank-one determinant lemma, with subtraction equal to addition in characteristic two, yields
\[
\chi_{A+uv}(X)=\chi_A(X)+v(u)X^2+v(Au)X+v(A^2u)+b\,v(u).
\]
Under \(v(u)=0\), requiring \(\chi_{A+uv}(X)=X^3+X\) is equivalent to
\[
v(Au)=b+1,\qquad v(A^2u)=c.
\]
For every \(u\ne0\), the vectors \(u,Au,A^2u\) form a basis: otherwise the cyclic \(K[X]\)-module generated by \(u\) would have nonzero annihilator of degree at most two, impossible because \(f_A\) is irreducible. Thus the three prescribed values determine exactly one \(v\). Moreover \(c\ne0\), so \(v\ne0\), and \(N=uv\) has rank one. It is square-zero because \(v(u)=0\). The required characteristic polynomial and Cayley–Hamilton imply that \(T=A+uv\) is tripotent.

For a scalar \(s\in K^\times\), replacing \(u\) with \(su\) changes \(v\) to \(s^{-1}v\), so \(uv\) is unchanged. Conversely, every admissible \(N\) has a unique image line \(\operatorname{im}N=Ku\), and the uniqueness of the prescribed \(v\) recovers it. This gives the bijection.

**4. Finite-field count and symmetry.** If \(|K|=q\), the number of lines in \(K^3\) is \((q^3-1)/(q-1)=q^2+q+1\). For irreducible \(f_A\), the centralizer algebra \(K[A]\) is the field \(\mathbb F_{q^3}\), and its invertible elements act on \(K^3\) by multiplication in that field. Their action on lines is transitive and has stabilizer exactly \(\mathbb F_q^\times\). By the equivariance of the defining equations for \(v\), this is precisely the conjugation action on \(\mathcal D(A)\). The scalar-quotient action is therefore free and transitive. \(\square\)

## Verification

A standalone arithmetic checker `verify_cubic_fibers.py` exhaustively evaluates every rank-one square-zero perturbation of each irreducible cubic companion matrix over \(\mathbb F_2\) and \(\mathbb F_4\). It finds exactly seven decompositions for the unique trace-zero irreducible cubic over \(\mathbb F_2\), and exactly twenty-one for each of the five trace-zero irreducible cubics over \(\mathbb F_4\); all other irreducible cubics have none. The checker inspects 42 and 6300 rank-one candidates, respectively. Run `python3 verify_cubic_fibers.py`; success terminates with `IRREDUCIBLE_CUBIC_TRIPOTENT_FIBERS_OK`. The finite checks corroborate, but do not replace, the all-field proof.

## Relationship to prior work

Danchev, García and Gómez Lozano (2026, first public version April 16) prove broad diagonalizable-plus-square-zero results in characteristic two and study nonderogatory cubic matrices, but do not enumerate the tripotent decomposition fiber or its centralizer orbits. Their earlier 2024 paper, Remark 2.1, already constructs **one** square-zero perturbation of any nonderogatory matrix with a prescribed equal-trace characteristic polynomial. That existing existence statement is not claimed as new here; the new assertion is the exact fiber description, its size in all even finite fields, and the sharply transitive centralizer action.

The later Phuc–Danchev preprint (first posted November 18, 2026) proves a cubic obstruction and an existence/nonexistence criterion in dimension three over \(\mathbb F_2\). Its binary-field criterion agrees with the existence part here, but it does not provide the complete decomposition count, projective parametrization over arbitrary characteristic-two fields, or the Singer action. This later paper is used for comparison, not as the eligibility-defining source.

## Limitations

No statement is made for reducible cubics, larger matrix sizes, or characteristic different from two. The decomposition is generally not into commuting summands and does not require diagonalizability of the tripotent matrix. The proof uses irreducibility essentially. No independent external review has yet been completed. Although searches located no exact enumerative equivalent, an implicit formulation in the older matrix-perturbation literature remains a historical-priority risk.
