# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The projective gradient equations are reduced rigorously to a critical-point ideal with parameter eliminant \((t^6+t^3+1)(t^6-3t^3+1)\). The two factors are distinct, squarefree and disjoint from the endpoint parameters. A direct critical-point formula gives two points over each of twelve roots. The rank-three local Hessian and cubic transverse coordinate prove both germs are \(A_2\), not merely experimentally identified singularities. The nonzero parameter derivative yields local discriminant order two per point, so the complete degree-48 discriminant has exponent four at every root. Irreducibility over \(\mathbb Q\), the cyclotomic sextet and the real embedding of the other sextet prove the arithmetic orbit partition. `verify.py` reproduces exact polynomial identities. No finite enumeration has been used as a proof of completeness.

## Originality — PASS, with priority risk

Li Bassi, arXiv:2611.14747v1, gives the fourfold, the pencil and its transversality criterion, but does not evaluate the singular parameter divisor or the local/Galois classification. The general projective-duality literature and Marquand's involution classification do not state these explicit invariants. Exact polynomial/alias searches and comparisons against available mathematical research records yielded no dominating result. Unindexed historical studies of Fermat cubic duals and Eckardt pencils remain a residual priority risk; absence from search results is not a uniqueness certificate.

## Value — PASS

The precisely twelve exceptional fibers are the natural missing boundary of the smooth Prym/Eckardt family singled out by the initiating construction. Their paired \(A_2\) types, fourth-power discriminant and nontrivial arithmetic split explain why this special smooth cubic fourfold degenerates differently from a general pencil and supply exact data for future compactification or monodromy calculations. The result is a complete natural classification for the specified model, not a numerical exercise or an arbitrary subfamily.
