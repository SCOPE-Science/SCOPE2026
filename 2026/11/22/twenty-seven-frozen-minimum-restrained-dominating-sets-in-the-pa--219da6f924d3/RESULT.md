# Twenty-seven frozen minimum restrained dominating sets in the Pappus graph

## Finding
Let \(G\) denote the eighteen-vertex Pappus point–line incidence graph. An ordinary dominating set \(S\) requires a neighbor in \(S\) for every vertex outside \(S\). A restrained dominating set requires that each outside vertex additionally have a neighbor outside \(S\). The ordinary domination number is \(\gamma(G)=5\), attained by exactly 18 sets. The restrained domination number is \(\gamma_r(G)=6\), attained by exactly 207 sets.

Under the full automorphism group of order 216, these 207 optimum sets lie in five orbits of sizes \(9,18,18,54,108\), respectively. Exactly 27 optimum sets induce three disjoint edges. These 27 sets are the isolated vertices of both optimum-preserving one-vertex token-jump and adjacent-vertex token-slide reconfiguration graphs. The remaining 180 optimum sets form a single connected component in both graphs. The jump graph has 432 edges, with 108 vertices of degree four and 72 of degree six in the main component; its diameter and radius are six. The slide graph has 324 edges, with 108 vertices of degree two and 72 of degree six in its main component; its diameter and radius are eight.

The complete restrained-dominating-set polynomial is
\[
R_G(z)=207z^6+1242z^7+3429z^8+5040z^9+4671z^{10}+3294z^{11}+1809z^{12}+756z^{13}+297z^{14}+54z^{15}+27z^{16}+z^{18}.
\]
In particular, there are no restrained dominating sets of size seventeen.

## Assumptions and scope
Write the nine point-vertices as \(p_{x,y}\) with \(x,y\in\mathbb F_3\), and the nine line-vertices as \(\ell_{d,a}\), for \(d\in\{x,y,s\}\) and \(a\in\mathbb F_3\). There is an edge from \(p_{x,y}\) to \(\ell_{d,a}\) exactly when \(a=x\) for \(d=x\), \(a=y\) for \(d=y\), or \(a=x+y\) for \(d=s\). This is the standard \((9_3)\) Pappus incidence graph, a simple cubic bipartite graph with eighteen vertices and 27 edges.

For certificate indexing, number points \(p_{x,y}\) by \(3x+y\), and number the lines \(\ell_{x,a},\ell_{y,a},\ell_{s,a}\) by \(9+a,12+a,15+a\), respectively. A set is encoded by the integer \(\sum_{i\in S}2^i\); this encoding belongs to the reproducibility certificate rather than the mathematical definition. A token jump replaces any one selected vertex by any unselected vertex. A token slide additionally requires the two exchanged vertices to be adjacent. Intermediate configurations must remain minimum restrained dominating sets.

## Proof
**The ordinary/restraint gap.** Let a five-vertex dominating set contain \(a\) points and \(b\) lines. Since each chosen point can dominate at most three unchosen lines and conversely, \(a+3b\ge9\) and \(b+3a\ge9\). Thus \(a+b\ge5\), and when \(a+b=5\), its shore sizes are \((2,3)\) or \((3,2)\). If two points are chosen, they must dominate all six unchosen lines, so their three incident-line pencils are disjoint. Equivalently, the two points have different values of each of \(x,y,x+y\); their difference is \((t,t)\) with \(t\ne0\). The chosen three lines are forced to be exactly the lines not incident with either chosen point, namely the third line in each of the three selected directions. These three lines meet at the third point of the omitted affine direction. That point is unselected and has all three neighbors selected. Consequently no five-set can be restrained dominating. There are three lines of three points in the omitted direction, hence nine unordered two-point choices; point–line duality yields nine more ordinary minimum dominating sets. This proves the 18-count and \(\gamma_r(G)\ge6\). Configurations of size below five are excluded by the same two inequalities.

**Exhaustive optimum classification.** The second part is an exact finite enumeration over all \(2^{18}\) subsets, with two independently implemented exhaustive certificates. In the Python checker, the adjacency masks \(N(v)\) are formed from the above incidence rules, and each subset \(S\) is accepted if and only if every outside vertex \(v\) satisfies both \(N(v)\cap S\ne\varnothing\) and \(N(v)\cap (V\setminus S)\ne\varnothing\). The C++17 checker separately generates the 18-vertex graph and enumerates all subsets by a binary recursive inclusion/exclusion tree, independently testing the two neighbor conditions. Both count 207 feasible six-subsets and zero feasible smaller subsets, establishing \(\gamma_r(G)=6\).

The Python checker constructs all 108 affine point-line automorphisms fixing the chosen three directions, composes them with an explicit incidence-reversing duality, and verifies that the resulting 216 permutations are distinct adjacency automorphisms. The group order is also consistent with the established full automorphism order for the Pappus graph. Five disjoint orbits cover all 207 optima. Representatives, recorded as integer masks in the stated indexing, are \(3682,3591,38150,5040,6020\), with respective orbit sizes \(9,18,18,54,108\); their numbers of induced edges are \(3,3,0,0,1\). Each of the first two types induces exactly three disjoint edges, giving all 27 such optima.

**Reconfiguration.** From each of the 207 feasible masks, generate each unordered one-out/one-in replacement, retain it only if the new mask also occurs in the complete feasible six-subset list, and for sliding retain only replacements along incidence edges. Exhaustive adjacency construction gives the degree and edge totals stated above. Breadth-first search from every one of the 207 states gives exactly 27 singleton components and one 180-vertex component for each move rule; every vertex in the 180-component has eccentricity six for jumps and eight for slides. Direct comparison with the orbit representatives shows that the 27 singleton states are precisely the two three-edge induced-matching orbits. Thus both the frozen-state classification and the sharp diameters follow.

## Verification
The package includes standalone standard-library Python and C++17 programs, `verify_affine.py` and `verify_cpp.cpp`. Execute `python3 verify_affine.py` and `g++ -O2 -std=c++17 -Wall -Wextra -pedantic verify_cpp.cpp -o verify_cpp && ./verify_cpp`. Each constructs the incidence graph independently, checks all relevant subsets and reconfiguration edges, and terminates with `PASS` only after its assertions succeed. The Python checker also independently constructs and checks every automorphism used for the five orbit certificates. The C++17 checker verifies the full restrained-set cardinality polynomial and both component and eccentricity counts. Programs were executed against the final package sources; finite exhaustive certificates here cover the complete \(2^{18}\)-state domain, not a sample.

## Relationship to prior work
Poghosyan and Zverovich (first public preprint January 2009) discuss general restrained-domination bounds and its definition. Brešar and Henning (first public preprint March 2024) establish the sharp \(2n/5\) universal upper bound for cubic graphs; for the eighteen-vertex Pappus graph that result allows an optimum of at most seven but does not imply the exact six-vertex classification. Neither inspected source contains the stated Pappus optimizer enumeration or its two move graphs. Indexed results for semitotal domination and locating-total domination of the Pappus graph concern different selected-set feasibility predicates, so their optimum counts and reconfiguration graphs do not imply the restrained result. Published exact tables and equivalent definitions were sought by the recorded searches, without a matching covering statement. A negative search is not a guarantee of historical priority.

## Limitations
The assertions establish a finite, exact statement only for the defined Pappus incidence graph; they do not generalize to all cubic graphs or affine incidence graphs. The orbit count uses a group construction independently checked to contain 216 distinct automorphisms and the known Pappus group order. Unindexed or inaccessible publications may contain earlier overlapping computations. This work has undergone self-review only; no independent scholarly audit has been conducted.
