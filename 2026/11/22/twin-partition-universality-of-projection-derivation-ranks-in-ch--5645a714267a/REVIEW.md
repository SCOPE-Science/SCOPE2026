# Self-review

**Correctness — PASS.** The square and mixed-basis derivation identities force symmetric twin blocks with zero sums in characteristic two. Self-adjoint idempotents biject with nondegenerate subspaces of the alternating hyperplane (or its one-dimensional-radical quotient), giving an even-rank classification. Symplectic transitivity and a counted family of graph lifts establish the exact finite-field polynomial. Finite independent equation-enumeration and extension-field tests agree; they are not used as an infinite proof.

**Originality — PASS relative to inspected evidence.** Reis–Cadavid (arXiv:2006.11934, Theorem 3.1) already proves the derivation block structure for arbitrary characteristic, which is foundational prior coverage and not claimed as original here. Cadavid–Rodiño Montoya–Rodriguez (arXiv:1807.02493) treats derivations in characteristic zero. Neither inspected statement classifies the composition-idempotent stratum or gives this all-graph rank polynomial. The closest related indexed work concerns square-zero (rather than idempotent) derivations of a different evolution algebra, or idempotent derivations of Lie algebras on graph data. Publication priority beyond the inspected records remains uncertain.

**Value — PASS.** The invariant is an exact, natural nonlinear stratum in a known derivation space, for all connected graph evolution algebras in characteristic two, not an arbitrary small example. It produces a symplectic counting formula, a complete sharp even-rank spectrum, and a universality statement insensitive to edges beyond the twin partition. These are useful for distinguishing projection-derivation behavior from unrestricted derivation spaces and from idempotent algebra elements.

**Limitations.** Simple connected graphs with at least two vertices and characteristic-two coefficient fields only; other characteristics and disconnected graphs are not covered. Computation checks finite examples, not the entire family. No independent audit or proof of absolute literature priority is asserted.
