# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

On distinct basis vectors the Leibniz identity equates scalar multiples of two nonzero 0–1 neighborhood vectors. For different supports the two scalars vanish, whereas identical supports give symmetric off-diagonal entries in characteristic two. Thus D is block-diagonal on open twin classes. The square identities give D(e_i^2)=0; each neighborhood is a union of whole twin classes, their block supports are disjoint, and every class occurs in some neighborhood. Hence each block kills its all-ones vector. These basis identities are also sufficient by bilinearity. The self-adjoint idempotents for the nondegenerate dot product on a block are in bijection with their nondegenerate images via projection along the orthogonal complement. Killing the ones vector places the image in H=1^perp, where the dot product is alternating because B(x,x)=(sum x_i)^2. In odd block size H is symplectic; in even size its radical is the line of ones and the quotient is symplectic, each with symplectic dimension 2h in the relevant space. Nondegenerate subspaces of H have zero intersection with the radical; each rank-2r quotient subspace has exactly q^(2r) distinct complements to the radical in its inverse image, counted by differences of linear sections. The symplectic group is transitive on the nondegenerate rank-2r subspaces with stabilizer Sp(2r) times Sp(2h−2r); dividing group orders gives q^(2r(h−r)) times the Gaussian binomial with base q^2. This yields the parity-dependent coefficient and a product over independently selected blocks. Symplectic bases and linear sections also give all even ranks over arbitrary characteristic-two fields, including the cases a=1,2 with h=0; the sums of the block rank intervals have no omitted even rank. Fresh row-span enumeration in F2 through size six corroborates the small coefficients independently of the supplied matrix enumeration; it is not the arbitrary-dimensional proof.

PASS

The general derivation-space normal form and its dependence on the twin partition are already essentially supplied by Reis–Cadavid. Symplectic subspace counts are standard. The distinct conclusion is the exact rank census for the nonlinear idempotence constraint in this specific derivation space, including the even-class radical-lift factor and the complete product for arbitrary open twin sizes; not the derivation-space block classification or a new counting theorem about symplectic spaces alone.
