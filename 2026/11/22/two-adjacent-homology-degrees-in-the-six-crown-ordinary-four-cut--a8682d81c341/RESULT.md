# Two adjacent homology degrees in the six-crown ordinary four-cut complex

## Finding
Let \(G=K_{6,6}\setminus\{a_i b_i:1\le i\le6\}\) be the *six-crown graph*, and let \(K=\Delta_4(G)\) be its ordinary four-cut complex. Then the **combinatorial Alexander dual** has integral homotopy type
\[
K^{\vee}\simeq S^4\vee\bigvee^{66}S^3.
\]
Consequently
\[
\widetilde H_i(K;\mathbb Z)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&i=5,\\
\mathbb Z^{66},&i=6,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
All facets of \(K\) have dimension seven. Thus the two non-top homology degrees make \(K\) nonshellable and non-Cohen--Macaulay over every field.

## Assumptions and scope
Label the graph vertices \(0,\ldots,11\), with shores \(A=\{0,\ldots,5\}\) and \(B=\{6,\ldots,11\}\). The cross-edge \(\{i,6+j\}\) exists exactly when \(i\ne j\), with \(0\le i,j\le5\). There are no within-shore edges. Let \(\mathcal D\) consist of the four-vertex subsets inducing disconnected subgraphs. By definition, \(K\) is generated by \(V\setminus Q\) for \(Q\in\mathcal D\); its combinatorial Alexander dual is
\[
X=K^{\vee}=\{S\subseteq V:\text{no }Q\in\mathcal D\text{ is contained in }S\}.
\]
The assertion is only for this 12-vertex graph and this cutoff; no all-crown formula or claim about equivariant homotopy is made.

## Proof
**Disconnected quadruples.** A four-set is disconnected exactly in three mutually exclusive cases: it lies entirely in one shore (\(2\binom64=30\) choices); it has three vertices on one shore and one opposite vertex, the latter's matched vertex being one of the three (\(2\cdot6\binom52=120\) choices); or it contains two vertices on each shore, paired by their identical indices (\(\binom62=15\) choices). Thus \(|\mathcal D|=165\). Independently enumerating all \(\binom{12}{4}\) four-sets and testing induced connectivity confirms this classification.

**The dual's exact face poset.** A nonempty set is a face of \(X\) if and only if it avoids every one of these 165 forbidden quadruples. The resulting 768 nonempty faces have dimension-by-dimension counts
\[
(f_0,f_1,f_2,f_3,f_4,f_5)=(12,66,220,330,120,20).
\]
All 66 pairs and 220 triples occur, so the two-skeleton is that of the full 11-simplex. In particular, \(X\) is simply connected.

**Finite acyclic matching and integral boundary.** Encode a face as its 12-bit characteristic vector. Process the vertices in the fixed order
\[
(3,2,4,7,1,11,9,0,5,6,10,8).
\]
At each vertex, among still-unmatched nonempty faces, pair a face not containing that vertex to its one-vertex extension whenever both remain. This yields exactly 350 pairs and the critical-face counts
\[
(c_0,c_1,c_2,c_3,c_4,c_5)=(1,0,0,66,1,0).
\]
The enclosed verifier orients each cover relation from the larger face to the smaller, except that matching edges are reversed. An exhaustive topological sort visits all 768 nodes of the resulting 2,832-edge directed graph, proving that the matching is acyclic, not merely that a sampling found no cycles.

The integral Morse boundary from the unique critical four-cell to the 66 critical three-cells is computed with signed incidence coefficients. For each three-face \(t\), recursively replace a matched lower three-face by the other facets of its partnered four-face with coefficients \(-[u:f]/[u:t]\), where \([u:t]=\pm1\); critical three-faces map to their basis vectors, whereas three-faces paired with two-faces map to zero. Starting from the oriented boundary of the critical four-cell, exact integer reduction leaves the *zero* coefficient vector in \(\mathbb Z^{66}\). Because the matching has no critical one- or two-cells, its Morse CW complex has one vertex, 66 three-cells, and one four-cell attached to their wedge. The attaching map lies in \(\pi_3(\bigvee^{66}S^3)\cong\mathbb Z^{66}\), where Hurewicz identifies its class with this zero degree vector. The attachment is therefore nullhomotopic and \(X\simeq\bigvee^{66}S^3\vee S^4\) *integrally*.

**Passage to the original cut complex.** For a complex on 12 labeled vertices, combinatorial Alexander duality gives \(\widetilde H_i(K;\mathbb Z)\cong\widetilde H^{9-i}(K^\vee;\mathbb Z)\). The dual is a torsion-free wedge of spheres of dimensions three and four, so universal coefficients yield precisely the stated homology in degrees six and five. All generating disconnected four-sets have size four, hence \(K\) is pure of dimension \(12-4-1=7\). Any pure shellable seven-complex has reduced homology only possibly in degree seven; similarly Cohen--Macaulayness requires lower reduced homology to vanish over the field. Here it does not.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py` with Python 3.9 or later; this is a standard-library-only reproducible check. The source regenerates the forbidden sets and all dual faces from connectivity, constructs the complete matching, checks acyclicity on the *entire* oriented Hasse graph, computes signed integral critical boundary coefficients and checks independent mod-two boundary-matrix ranks. The recorded output appears in `verification_output.txt`. The ranks are \((11,55,165,99,20)\) in boundary degrees one through five and corroborate the homology ranks; the integral proof rests on signed Morse reduction, not on mod-two information alone.

## Relationship to prior work
Bayer et al., *Topology of Cut Complexes of Graphs* (first arXiv submission 26 April 2023), define ordinary cut complexes and prove results for complete bipartite graphs, cycles, and **Cartesian** clique prisms. The complete-bipartite benchmark gives \(\Delta_4(K_{6,6})\simeq\bigvee^{100}S^4\); deleting a perfect matching instead yields the two adjacent integral homology groups above. Their clique prism \(K_6\square K_2\) has edges within shores and is **not** this bipartite crown \(K_6\times K_2\) (categorical/tensor product). The sequel studies disjoint unions, squared paths, grids and equivariant examples, without an identified crown four-cut theorem. Existing indexed work on the **three-cut** crown complexes determines a different cut parameter. A separately recorded computation for the five-crown four-cut complex gives a single sphere degree; it does not imply this mixed-dimensional six-crown conclusion.

## Limitations
This is a finite exact classification, not an asymptotic formula or a theorem for all crowns. The homotopy equivalence is supplied for the Alexander dual \(K^\vee\), and only integral homology (not the full homotopy type) is asserted for \(K\). The exact matching is machine-reproducible but has not received an independent external audit. Equivalence to an unindexed prior calculation cannot be categorically excluded.
