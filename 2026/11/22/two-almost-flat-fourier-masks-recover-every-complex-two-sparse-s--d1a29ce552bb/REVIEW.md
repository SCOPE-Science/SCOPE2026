# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

The claim is an exact, all-even-length statement about a pair of known diagonal masks. The discrete inverse Fourier transform of intensity supplies cyclic autocorrelations. For distinct non-antipodal support indices, the complex ratio of off-zero autocorrelations uniquely identifies an ordered support pair because signed binary-power differences are distinct and all phase differences are contained in an interval shorter than a period. The two distinct zero-lag weights then identify each squared amplitude, and the off-zero complex coefficient identifies the relative phase. For antipodal supports, strictly disjoint zero-lag ratio intervals identify the site pair, while the two independent real projections \(2\operatorname{Re}z\) and \(2\operatorname{Re}(qz)\), \(\operatorname{Im}q\ne0\), identify its relative phase. The one-mask lower bound already follows from ambiguities among one-sparse signals. No finite computation is used as an infinite proof. The delivered checker confirms 2,952 example reconstructions, all support types, and the critical arithmetic code identities.

## Mathematical value — PASS

For even cyclic grids, antipodal signal pairs produce a self-inverse Fourier correlation lag, so every real diagonal mask sees only the same real quadrature and fails to distinguish conjugate relatives. The construction uses arbitrarily small **both** amplitude and phase changes, thereby simultaneously coding the unknown antipodal support and recovering its missing phase quadrature. It yields a complete, exactly sharp minimal-mask theorem for the entire two-sparse class, including the smallest even grid, rather than a generic-position or dimension-asymptotic assertion. It explains which very weak form of complex modulation repairs a natural parity obstruction. Stability is explicitly not claimed; the result's interest is algebraic identifiability.
