# Two- and three-sphere wedges in the eight-cut complex of the truncated tetrahedron

## Finding
Let \(T\) be the twelve-vertex graph of the truncated tetrahedron described below, and let \(\Delta_8(T)\) be the *ordinary* eight-cut complex. There is an integral homotopy equivalence
\[
\Delta_8(T)\simeq\bigvee^{6}S^{2}\ \vee\ \bigvee^{109}S^{3}.
\]
In particular, the reduced integral homology groups are \(\widetilde H_2\cong\mathbb Z^6\), \(\widetilde H_3\cong\mathbb Z^{109}\), and zero in every other degree. The complex is pure of dimension three but neither shellable nor Cohen–Macaulay over any field.

## Assumptions and scope
Write \(V=\{(a,b):a,b\in\{0,1,2,3\},\ a\ne b\}\). Two distinct members \((a,b),(c,d)\) are adjacent if and only if \(a=c\) or \((a,b)=(d,c)\). This is the standard truncated-tetrahedral graph, with twelve vertices, eighteen edges and vertex degree three. Order the vertices lexicographically, starting at index zero; the vertex sequence is \((0,1),(0,2),(0,3),(1,0),(1,2),(1,3),(2,0),(2,1),(2,3),(3,0),(3,1),(3,2)\).

For a finite simple graph \(G\) on \(n\) vertices, the ordinary \(k\)-cut complex is generated by all subsets \(V(G)\setminus S\), where \(|S|=k\) and \(G[S]\) is disconnected. Here \(n=12\) and \(k=8\); hence every generating facet contains exactly four vertices. All homotopy statements concern the geometric realization of this abstract simplicial complex, with integral coefficients unless stated otherwise.

## Proof
**Exhaustive facet characterization.** For each of the \(\binom{12}{8}=495\) eight-element vertex sets, evaluate connectedness in the induced graph using reachability restricted to that vertex set. Exactly 252 are disconnected, and their 252 distinct complements give the tetrahedral facets. Taking all nonempty subsets of those facets yields the complete face vector \((f_0,f_1,f_2,f_3)=(12,66,204,252)\). The enumeration and all signed matrices are generated, rather than stored as unverified numerical tables, by the standalone `CHECK.py`.

**Simple connectivity and the second boundary.** All \(\binom{12}{2}=66\) edges occur, while only sixteen of the \(\binom{12}{3}=220\) triangles are absent. For each absent triangle \(\{u,v,w\}\), the exhaustive checker constructs another vertex \(z\) such that \(\{u,v,z\},\{u,w,z\},\{v,w,z\}\) are all present triangles. Thus the edge loop bounding every absent triangle is null-homotopic through an explicit three-triangle cone. The complete two-skeleton of the eleven-simplex is simply connected, and these cone fillings show that deleting the sixteen triangle interiors does not introduce fundamental-group generators. Consequently \(\pi_1(\Delta_8(T))=0\). Moreover, the boundaries of the present triangles generate the full integral cycle lattice of the complete one-skeleton, so the integral second chain boundary has rank \(66-12+1=55\), with \(H_1=0\).

**Integral third boundary.** Orient each face by increasing vertex index. The signed simplicial boundary \(\partial_3:C_3\to C_2\) has shape \(204\times252\) and precisely 1008 initial nonzero entries, each equal to \(1\) or \(-1\). The supplied checker performs integral unit-pivot elimination by invertible row and column operations. It eliminates 143 unit pivots and leaves a zero residual matrix, and no intermediate coefficient exceeds one in absolute value. Therefore the Smith normal form of \(\partial_3\) has exactly 143 diagonal ones and otherwise zeros. The code separately checks \(\partial_2\partial_3=0\) over \(\mathbb Z\) and recomputes the rank of \(\partial_3\) as 143 over \(\mathbb F_2\). Because the image of \(\partial_3\) is primitive in \(C_2\), it is also primitive in \(\ker\partial_2\). Thus
\[
\widetilde H_2\cong\mathbb Z^{204-55-143}=\mathbb Z^6,\qquad
\widetilde H_3\cong\mathbb Z^{252-143}=\mathbb Z^{109},
\]
without integral torsion. No higher chains exist.

**Homotopy type.** The two-skeleton is a simply connected two-dimensional CW complex, and by the Hurewicz and Whitehead theorems it is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of \(204-55=149\) two-spheres. Under this equivalence, the 252 three-cell attaching maps correspond, via \(\pi_2\cong H_2\), to an integral matrix with primitive rank 143. A change of integral basis puts the attaching maps into 143 degree-one kills and 109 null attachments. Consequently the resulting space is homotopy equivalent to six two-spheres and 109 three-spheres wedged at a point. Since the original complex is pure three-dimensional but has nonzero degree-two reduced homology over every field, neither shellability nor Cohen–Macaulayness is possible.

## Verification
Run `python3 CHECK.py` with a standard Python 3 interpreter; no external packages, data sets, websites or saved intermediate paths are required. The included `CHECK_OUTPUT.txt` is the captured successful run. The checker constructs the graph from ordered pairs, enumerates all 495 eight-subsets, reconstructs every face, supplies witnesses for the sixteen missing triangular loops, verifies \(\partial_2\partial_3=0\), performs the 143 integral unit eliminations, and independently checks the mod-two third-boundary rank. The proof of the infinite homotopy statement for this fixed finite complex relies on these exhaustive finite steps and standard topological theorems, not on extrapolation from random samples.

## Relationship to prior work
Bayer, Denker, Jelić Milutinović, Rowlands, Sundaram and Xue's *Topology of Cut Complexes of Graphs* introduces ordinary \(k\)-cut complexes, treats several graph families, and gives a face-lattice criterion (Theorem 6.1). Its classification table does not include the truncated-tetrahedral graph; the criterion requiring a complete next-to-top skeleton does not apply here because sixteen triangles are missing. Their sequel treats disjoint unions, squared paths and grids, not this graph. A published three-cut result for graphs with triangles and no four-cycles includes the truncated tetrahedron at \(k=3\), but its statement concerns a different complex and does not determine the ordinary eight-cut homotopy type. This result concerns the terminal small-dimensional regime, not the earlier three-cut or four-cut regimes.

## Limitations
This establishes the nonequivariant integral homotopy type of one specific ordinary cut complex. It does not determine the full \(k\)-cut filtration, symmetry characters, equivariant stable homotopy, or the topology for all truncated polyhedral graphs. The novelty assessment is limited to the inspected and indexed literature, and equivalent unindexed work may exist. No independent audit has been performed.
