# Scientific review: two-chain ordered-pair types

**Correctness — PASS.** The finite-extension criterion was tested independently by constructing full transitive closures (2,367 decisions). Unary types are parametrised by at most two cut positions per named chain. In two-variable extensions, the feasibility predicates depend only on four cut coordinates per named chain and endpoint flags; stratification by their weak order gives bidegree-at-most-four polynomiality on positive lengths. Full enumeration on a five-by-five positive grid, five points on each coordinate axis and the empty base therefore proves the closed formulas for all nonnegative lengths. Seven outside-grid tests provide additional independent consistency checks. The finite checks are not treated as proof without the degree lemma.

**Originality — PASS, with priority risk.** The primary 2011 random-poset reducts paper concerns automorphism groups and definability, not named two-chain pair enumeration. The published chain-only pair-type formula agrees with the one-empty-chain boundary but leaves all mixed coefficients unconstrained. The published one-type lower bound singles out unions of at most two chains as an extremal class but does not imply the new two-point identity. The published complete height-two pair spectrum treats a different base. Searches under pair-orbit, ideal/filter, two-chain, and bivariate-spectrum descriptions returned no covering result. An equivalent older interval-count or four-cut summation might be unindexed; the current priority assessment is not independent.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The disjoint-two-chain family is the exact equality boundary in a previously established unary-type extremal theorem. The new two-point classification reveals a rigid law \(I=N(2N+1)/3\) that fails on general width-two bases, as well as the uniform one-half limiting comparable-type proportion for arbitrarily imbalanced chain lengths. Those properties are not determined by generic unary enumeration or by the earlier chain-only result.

**Limitations.** Nothing is claimed for non-disjoint width-two posets, higher arities, or random samples of vertices. This is an all-size result whose generality relies essentially on the weak-order interpolation lemma. Literature equivalence outside the inspected records remains possible.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
