# Scientific review: two Narayana residue sections

## Correctness

**PASS.** The exact matrices \(M^7=I+2B\) and \(M^{14}=I+4C\) were reconstructed, and the parity table identifies precisely \(j=2,3\) as having an odd first-coordinate linear coefficient at both dyadic lifting levels. The binomial expansion then proves the exact difference valuation for all nonnegative distinct parameters, not just tested ranges. Counting equal-size parity cosets proves bijectivity; the same difference law and seven-term mod-two word prove least period. The included checker verifies arithmetic through power \(2^{12}\) and direct integer difference valuations at lower levels.

## Originality

**PASS, with residual historical risk.** Full proofs were compared for the previously published zero-count theorem, valuation classification, and complete state-cycle spectrum for the same recurrence. The first specifies one fiber (zero) and its lift; the second specifies only powers of two dividing values; and the third specifies three-coordinate periods. None determines two full complementary scalar parity transversals or the uniqueness of the two index classes under a global scaled isometry. Also inspected were the primary 2015 modular-sequence study, the older morphism paper, and the OEIS period table. A stronger unindexed general theorem might cover the statement; this review is not a universal priority certificate.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** The two progressions give a canonical exact solution to every congruence \(a_n\equiv y\pmod{2^k}\) with \(n\) restricted to the appropriate one of two index classes, for all precisions. The sharp metric identity and the two-out-of-seven classification explain why these representatives exist and why the other five sections cannot have the same property. This goes beyond period and divisibility data and supplies a structural modular inversion invariant of a classical cubic recurrence.
