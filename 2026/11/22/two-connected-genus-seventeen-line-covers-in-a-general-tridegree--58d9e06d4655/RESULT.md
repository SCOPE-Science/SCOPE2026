# Two connected genus-seventeen line covers in a general tridegree-\((2,2,2)\) Fano threefold

## Finding
Let \(R\subset (\mathbf P^1)^3\) be a general smooth divisor of multidegree \((2,2,2)\) over \(\mathbf C\), let \(\pi:X\longrightarrow(\mathbf P^1)^3\) be the branched double cover, and let \(f:X\to\mathbf P^1\) be projection through the first factor. There are exactly \(24\) singular fibers of \(f\), each a degree-four del Pezzo surface with precisely one ordinary double point. Over the smooth-fiber locus the relative scheme of anticanonical lines is finite étale of degree \(16\); the normalization of its closure has exactly TWO connected components \(C_u,C_v\), both smooth curves of genus \(17\) and degree \(8\) over the base. Each factors as a connected étale double cover \(C_i\to B_i\) of a smooth genus-nine discriminant curve \(B_i\subset \mathbf P^1\times\mathbf P^1\) of bidegree \((4,4)\), followed by a degree-four projection \(B_i\to\mathbf P^1\). Each \(B_i\) is simply ramified at exactly \(24\) points with pairwise distinct images, and both have the SAME set of \(24\) branch values. Thus \(C_i\to\mathbf P^1\) has \(48\) simple ramification points in \(24\) pairs; the local monodromy around each singular fiber is a product of two disjoint transpositions in each eight-line system.

## Assumptions and scope
All varieties are over \(\mathbf C\); general means a point of a nonempty Zariski-open subset of the coefficient space \(\mathbf P H^0((\mathbf P^1)^3,\mathcal O(2,2,2))\). Let \(t,u,v\) denote the three factor coordinates, with the usual binary homogeneous coordinates on each factor. On the base \(S=\mathbf P^1_t\times\mathbf P^1_u\), write the branch equation as \(F=a(t,u)v_0^2+b(t,u)v_0v_1+c(t,u)v_1^2\), where \(a,b,c\in H^0(S,\mathcal O(2,2))\). The \(v\)-ruling discriminant is \(B_v=\{b^2-4ac=0\}\subset S\); interchange \(u,v\) to define \(B_u\). The 'line scheme' is the reduced relative Hilbert scheme of curves of anticanonical degree one over smooth del Pezzo fibers; its closure means closure of this generic finite scheme in the relative Hilbert scheme, not every vertical component supported on a singular fiber. The stated normalization is taken after reduction.

## Proof
**1. Tangent ruling fibers give all sixteen lines.** A smooth fiber \(X_t\to\mathbf P^1_u\times\mathbf P^1_v\) is a double cover branched over a smooth \((2,2)\) elliptic curve. Its anticanonical bundle is the pullback of \(\mathcal O(1,1)\). If \(L\subset X_t\) is an anticanonical line, then its image has \(\mathcal O(1,1)\)-degree one; it must be a ruling fiber and \(L\) maps isomorphically onto it. Such a fiber has a line component precisely when its quadratic branch restriction is a perfect square, in which case the pullback splits into two rational curves with anticanonical degree one, and by adjunction each is a \((-1)\)-curve. Each projection of the smooth \((2,2)\) elliptic branch curve has degree two and four simple branch points by Riemann–Hurwitz. Therefore each ruling supplies eight lines, for a total of sixteen, with the two systems separated globally by the fixed ambient rulings.

**2. Smooth genus-nine tangency curves.** Relative to \(S\), the tangency condition is \(D=b^2-4ac=0\), a divisor of type \((4,4)\). The nonempty generic locus where both ruling discriminants are smooth and their projections to the \(t\)-line have simple, distinct critical values is established by the exact finite-field open-condition witness in `verify.py`; it tests every projective affine chart and proves a nonzero integer certificate survives in characteristic zero. Adjunction gives \(g(B_i)=(4-1)(4-1)=9\), and each \(B_i\to\mathbf P^1_t\) has degree four. Riemann–Hurwitz gives total ramification \(2g(B_i)-2+2\cdot4=24\), all simple with distinct images on this generic locus. In fact the critical-value polynomials for the two rulings coincide, as verified in the explicit witness; intrinsically both detect singular members of the genus-one family \(R_t\subset\mathbf P^1\times\mathbf P^1\): a smooth \((2,2)\) genus-one fiber has four distinct branch points in either double-cover projection, whereas a general nodal fiber coalesces one pair. Consequently the two sets of critical values agree.

**3. The component-splitting double cover is connected.** On \(B=B_v\), the rank-one binary quadratic \(F\) defines a morphism \(r:B\to\mathbf P^1\), sending it to its unique square-root linear form up to scale (the inverse of the Veronese embedding of the conic of rank-one quadratic forms). The coefficient map \(B\to\mathbf P^2\) comes from \(\mathcal O_B(2,2)\), and the Veronese has degree two. Thus \(r^*\mathcal O_{\mathbf P^1}(2)\cong\mathcal O_B(2,2)\), so
\[
\eta=\mathcal O_B(1,1)\otimes r^*\mathcal O_{\mathbf P^1}(-1)
\]
is a two-torsion line bundle. Choosing one of the two line components above each tangent ruling fiber is exactly choosing a square root of the nonzero scalar multiplying the square linear form. These choices form the étale double cover associated with \(\eta\).

It is NONTRIVIAL. Let \(Z\subset S\) be the common zero scheme of \(a,b\); for general coefficients it is eight distinct points, with \(c\ne0\) at each point. All eight lie on \(B\), and they are exactly the zero divisor of the pulled-back coordinate of the square-root linear form: \(r^*\mathcal O(1)\cong\mathcal O_B(Z)\). Hence \(\eta\cong\mathcal O_B(1,1)(-Z)\). If \(\eta\) were trivial, a nonzero section of \(\mathcal O_B(1,1)\) would vanish on \(Z\). But the restriction map \(H^0(S,\mathcal O(1,1))\to H^0(B,\mathcal O_B(1,1))\) is an isomorphism, by the restriction sequence with \(\mathcal O_S(-3,-3)\) and its vanishing \(H^0=H^1=0\). Similarly, the Koszul resolution for the transverse \((2,2)\) complete intersection \(Z=V(a,b)\), twisted by \(\mathcal O(1,1)\), implies \(H^0(S,\mathcal I_Z(1,1))=0\). Contradiction. Thus \(\eta\) is nontrivial, and the étale double cover \(C_v\to B_v\) is connected. Its genus is \(g(C_v)=2g(B_v)-1=17\). The same argument gives connected \(C_u\) of genus seventeen.

**4. Degenerations and monodromy.** A smooth branch divisor \(R\) of tridegree \((2,2,2)\) is a K3 surface by adjunction and cohomology of the ambient product; its topological Euler characteristic is \(24\). (Equivalently, its Chern-number calculation gives \(c_2(R)=24\).) The generic smoothness and squarefreeness in Step 2 imply its fibration over \(\mathbf P^1_t\) has exactly 24 fibers, each with one ordinary node. In the double cover \(X_t\) a branch node has local equation \(w^2=xy\) after analytic coordinates, so each corresponding del Pezzo surface has a unique \(A_1\) singularity. Over a simple critical value of \(B_i\to\mathbf P^1\), its four-sheeted monodromy is one transposition. The connected étale double cover \(C_i\to B_i\) lifts it to TWO disjoint transpositions among its eight sheets, because it is étale over the ramification point. There are 24 such values with two ramification points per value, totaling 48, consistent with Riemann–Hurwitz for \(C_i\to\mathbf P^1\): \(2\cdot17-2=-16+48\).

## Verification
`verify.py` is a standalone deterministic SymPy checker in characteristic \(101\). Using all \(27\) coefficients drawn by `random.Random(130052)`, it checks smoothness of the tridegree \((2,2,2)\) branch surface in all eight affine charts; smoothness of each bidegree \((4,4)\) discriminant and transversality of the associated eight-point coefficient base in all four charts; and degree \(24\), squarefreeness, and equality of the two critical-value polynomials. The checker prints `PASS`. This certifies the necessary nonempty generic-open conditions and arithmetic, not the general theorem by finite enumeration. The global claims follow from Steps 1–4.

## Relationship to prior work
Dvorsky–Kaliman–Zaidenberg (2026) prove algebraic ellipticity of the same Mori–Mukai 3-1 double covers and identify the three degree-four del Pezzo fibrations, without analyzing the two connected relative line curves or their genus and branching. Chachay (2026) studies counts of the sixteen lines on individual smooth degree-four del Pezzo surfaces, but not the relative covers across a moving family. Lehmann–Tanimoto (2019/2024, Example 10.7) construct an étale double cover of a degree-four discriminant cover in a **different** conic-bundle-based del Pezzo fibration; its discriminant map has twelve, rather than twenty-four, branch points, so its results do not compute the curves in this family. The connectedness proof here is a new calculation for the tridegree-two branch family. Targeted database and literature searches found no equivalent complete statement; this is a qualified originality judgment, not proof of historical priority.

## Limitations
The theorem is for a general smooth branch equation over \(\mathbf C\), not every smooth branch. The claim concerns the normalized closure of lines on smooth fibers and does not describe all additional isolated or embedded features of the relative Hilbert scheme above special singular fibers. It does not identify the entire global monodromy groups, the Jacobians of the two genus-seventeen curves, or their moduli. Prior unindexed literature can contain specialized calculations despite the targeted comparisons. Independent validation has not been performed.
