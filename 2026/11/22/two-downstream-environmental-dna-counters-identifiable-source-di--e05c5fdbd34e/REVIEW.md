Related public mathematical results on Gaussian-plume location and two-exponential sampling address different parameter targets and do not imply this claim. A broader statistical optimal-design theorem could still subsume the asymptotic; independent priority review and some inaccessible source texts remain residual risks.

## Value — PASS

Source location is a documented unresolved practical motivation for eDNA observation, and distinct DNA states suggest that a single loss rate can be an inadequate calibration benchmark. The result precisely characterizes what two fixed downstream stations can learn about a source when released quantity is unknown, distinguishes calibration from absence of identifiability, and gives a scale-independent far-source spacing law with a sharp accelerated information-loss exponent. It is an exact, natural design boundary rather than an arbitrary parameter slice. Application requires measured attenuation constants and stable transport; real rivers may instead have nonmonotone concentration plumes and extra-Poisson noise.
