# Mathematical review

## Correctness
The proof reconstructs \(M^7=I+2B\) and \(M^{14}=I+4C\) with explicit integer matrices, verifies the scalar oddness of \(eBM^rv\) and \(eCM^rv\) for both indices \(r=3,4\), and uses exact squaring to prove the two-sided index-difference valuation. This supports the all-modulus parity-chart bijections, the sharp scalar period, and the two singleton output balls. The accompanying checker separately recomputes the integer matrices, exhausts all moduli through \(2^{14}\) and checks 1,920 high-precision increment identities. The finite tests corroborate the proof and are not its logical basis.

## Originality
Kuloğlu–Özkan–Shannon (2022), Theorems 2.1 and 2.5, derive periodicity and period lifting, not first-coordinate preimage multiplicities. Franzone–Harrington (2024), Section 3, give specific target-index residue tables for other arithmetic applications, not universal powers-of-two coverage. The nearest publicly accessible generalized Narayana state-cycle classification determines periods of three-coordinate states, and the Narayana binary valuation classification determines divisibility shells: neither implication yields the stated exact **value** maps of the two index branches or the singleton classes \(2,7\pmod8\). Analogous three-adic index isometries do not imply this two-adic formula, as their matrix residues and target sectors differ. An unindexed prior equivalent or stronger fiber theorem remains a genuine priority risk; novelty is assessed under the inspected sources, not guaranteed exhaustively.

## Value
The question of which residues a classical recurrence represents modulo arbitrary powers, and how frequently its values collide, is natural in modular recurrence arithmetic and in index-set constructions used in the motivating 2024 paper. An explicit all-power covering theorem with two reversible parity selectors and a positive-proportion singleton-output obstruction is a structural result, not a sample of a finite table. The exact minimal fiber and full least period establish sharpness. A complete spectrum for the remaining output classes is not proved.
