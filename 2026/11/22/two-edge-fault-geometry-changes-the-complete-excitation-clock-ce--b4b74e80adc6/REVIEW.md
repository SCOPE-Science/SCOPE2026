The local Greenberg–Hastings update is explicitly defined with synchronous 0/1/2 states. Each graph has a universal-twin core: the future of every core color class depends only on which colors are present and the marked endpoint states. A representative with one vertex per present color therefore has exactly the same return-time and preperiod as any larger state with the same signature. The 189- and 567-signature exhaustive transition checks enumerate every representative and give the ten rows of support-size coefficients shown in `RESULT.md`. The computational certificate is finite and exhaustive for the fixed signature spaces, not an extrapolation from ring sizes.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
The claim compares the two isomorphism types of **exactly two missing edges** in a clique, including all first-entry populations and eventual periods. The closest published Greenberg–Hastings result on cocktail-party graphs classifies *perfect matching* deletions and includes only the exceptional four-site two-edge case; its graph family does not contain either asserted \(n\ge5\) two-edge family. The complete split-graph excitation census includes a single-edge deletion of a clique, but two *adjacent* deletions yield a non-split induced three-vertex path of nonedges and are not covered. The matching-deletion study of three-color cyclic dynamics treats a **different** update rule. Gravner–Lyu–Sivakoff (2016) and Doba (2018) give strong qualitative oscillation criteria and rate formulas for general graphs, but not this all-order ten-cell census or the adjacent-versus-disjoint comparison. Actual published-finding corpus queries for two-edge adjacency, exact entry times and graph isomorphism aliases returned no directly covering result. The risk of unindexed historical enumerations or equivalent derivations remains; search absence does not establish absolute priority.

## Value — PASS
For the *minimal* nonisomorphic ways to delete two edges from a complete network, the result fully determines all temporal layers and exact orbit populations at every order. Merely knowing from general theory whether a state ultimately oscillates does not determine the four-round relaxation histogram. It is structurally surprising that **adding two edges' incidence to each other** simultaneously creates two additional primitive three-cycles *and* enlarges the extinction basin by six initial states, while changing the final-delay oscillatory count from eight to two. The comparison isolates a natural geometry-of-faults effect with no artificial graph parameters and yields the complete finite-size response, not a small isolated numerical observation. Individual nonzero orbit basin sizes remain outside scope.
