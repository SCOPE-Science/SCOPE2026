# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The analytic inverse coordinate reduction is valid globally because \(a>-1\) makes the factor \(A(y)\) positive. Differentiating the one-dimensional inverse equation yields the exact fold threshold and a complete derivative sign chart. Two simultaneous first/second derivative degeneracies are ordinary Whitney cusps, and a nonzero cubic jet gives the sharp \(3/2\) fold-opening constant. Properness and degree are separately proved. The included standard-library checker passes exact rational identities, two exact cusp factorizations, a three-preimage example and twelve numerical opening tests; finite checks do not substitute for the analytic universal proof.

## Originality — PASS within inspected evidence
The full accessible scientific text of Zhang et al. (Chinese Physics B 31, 030503, 2022; DOI 10.1088/1674-1056/ac4025) develops the same map, fixed points and local multipliers, then numerical attractor simulations; it does not establish the present global inverse partition and cusp coefficient. General critical-curve methods are classical, and the separate Bischi–Gardini–Mira polynomial \(Z_1\)–\(Z_3\) examples and Tikjha–Muni rational \(Z_0\)–\(Z_2\) map cover different objects. General Whitney fold/cusp existence is not claimed as new. An unindexed equivalent result remains possible, and some related older/newer texts were available only in part.

## Value — PASS
A complete inverse-preimage census is naturally relevant to planar noninvertible dynamics and potential basin topology. The theorem gives the maximal central one-sheeted target strip, exact two-cusp critical-value boundary and full three-sheet tongues for the published rational family, with quantitatively sharp cusp scaling. This is a full structural classification of a natural invariant feature, not a small isolated parameter calculation.

## Limits
Only \(a>-1\) and \(c\ne0\) are treated. No forward attractor, stable-cycle or basin-bifurcation theorem follows from the inverse geometry alone. The online-manuscript date has conflicting publisher metadata; the earliest public claim used is the dated December 5, 2021 publisher record.
