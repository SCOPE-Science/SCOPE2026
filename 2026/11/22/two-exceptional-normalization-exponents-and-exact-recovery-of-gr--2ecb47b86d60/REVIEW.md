# Scientific review: normalization-dependent identifiability in quadratic contagion

**Correctness — PASS.** The theorem is a finite-dimensional rank statement with a complete analytic proof. Hypergeometric sampling gives the displayed two-moment generator, with the rates at one and two infectives reconstructing both moments for every \(N\ge3\). The column curves have strictly nonzero curvature for every real \(\eta\notin\{1,2\}\), establishing exact rank on arbitrary distinct support points, while elementary identities establish the two exceptional ranks. Two experiment rows \(t\) and \(1/t\) have an injective three-point moment map. Both rational ambiguity examples and 2,501 exact-rational computational checks support the formula. Computations are corroborative; the infinite-parameter claim follows from the explicit strict-curvature proof.

**Originality — PASS, within inspected literature.** Muslim (arXiv:2608.04943, 2026) establishes the moment reduction and a general matrix-rank test; those are expressly credited. The new precise claim evaluates the rank for *all* normalization exponents in the quadratic focal-update model, identifies two exceptional exponents and sharp support cutoffs, and proves that two individually nonidentifying exposure protocols jointly identify any three-size law. Its extra arguments use generalized-power strict curvature and a joint arithmetic/harmonic inverse moment map. The source's general criterion does not by itself tabulate the exceptional partition or paired-protocol recovery. St-Onge et al. study random hypergraphs with an approximate-master-equation closure, not the finite exact annealed generator; general compartmental identifiability work has a different inverse problem. Searches found no exact equivalent theorem; this is not proof of universal historical novelty.

**Value — PASS.** Per-partner normalization and squared within-group prevalence are natural, explicitly motivated exposure choices, not arbitrary isolated exponents. The result reveals a qualitative identifiability loss at each of those choices and an exact two-experiment repair, with a sharp minimal number of group sizes. It tells whether perfectly observed epidemic transition data contain enough information to distinguish contact-group distributions before any statistical fitting is attempted. This is a structural inference boundary rather than a routine calculation of one epidemic threshold.

**Limits and risks.** Parameters and candidate support are presumed known; inference from sparse noisy trajectories is not addressed. The classification is quadratic only and requires exchangeable, freshly sampled groups. A related full-text analysis of persistent hypergraphs has different dynamics. Historical novelty still needs independent audit; literature search coverage is incomplete.
