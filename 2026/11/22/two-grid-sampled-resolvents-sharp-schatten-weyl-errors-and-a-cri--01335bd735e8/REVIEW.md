# Review

## Correctness — PASS
The exact scalar identity and positivity follow from exponential-function algebra. The full spectral proof establishes the compactness and trace-class properties, passes from the Weyl counting law to scaled spectral measures with explicit small/large-tail control, proves the sharp ordered singular-value tail and weak critical limit, and derives the convergent nuclear zeta coefficient. The finite diagnostic checker tests limiting cases and numerical sampling but is not used as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS (with bibliographic risk)
The motivating manuscript states the sampled-resolvent formula, dyadic structure, trapezoid correction, Romberg convergence on smooth vectors, and operator-norm sharpness, but does not state any positive two-grid trace-class result, spectrum-counting Schatten threshold or coefficient. Its section 5 and discussion were inspected beyond the abstract. The earlier dyadic spectral-zeta manuscript was recoverable only by abstract and its possible overlap is a residual risk. The principal named published-finding corpus near neighbors study different operators (Cesàro–Mellin defects, Rhaly matrices and abstract Romberg quadrature); their statements do not imply the present two-grid Weyl asymptotics. A broader existing functional-calculus trace theorem could subsume the integral step; priority has not been independently certified.

## Mathematical value — PASS
This quantitative result responds to the explicit short-time operator-norm sampling problem by identifying which spectral ideals can resolve the error. The fact that the two-grid approximant is nuclear for every mesh while its error can never be nuclear in spectral dimension at least one is a meaningful boundary. The exact Mellin coefficient, zero-free positive filter, and critical weak-ideal constant give reproducible precision for standard elliptic generators, rather than merely a routine first-order scalar estimate.

## Limits
The setting is self-adjoint compact-resolvent generators with a one-term Weyl law, and does not treat arbitrary semigroups or stochastic sampling. Only self-review were performed; there is no independent external audit.
