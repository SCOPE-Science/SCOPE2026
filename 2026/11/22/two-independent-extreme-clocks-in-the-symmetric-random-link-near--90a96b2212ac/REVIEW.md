# Mathematical assessment

**Correctness (PASS).** For every prescribed matching, the threshold event forces all incident exponential clocks past the threshold; the residual ranking probability is the previously established product of reciprocal-pair factors. This gives the exact factorial moment (3). The isolated-vertex factorial moment (4) counts distinct incident edges once. In the mixed calculation, conditioning on a fixed isolated vertex set leaves complementary edges independent, and the ordered marked-pair waiting-time increments have rates \(j(2m-2j-1)\). Their total expectation is \(O(1/n)\), whereas the gap between the relevant two thresholds is of order \(\log n/n\). The overlap configurations are negligible after separately bounding the other isolated vertices' edges. Hence all mixed factorial moments converge to those of independent Poisson variables, proving the two-Gumbel joint law. The finite checker verified 4123 deterministic assertions. Continuous atomless weights are handled by the hazard transform. Risks: the result applies only for fixed threshold offsets and iid shared edge weights; finite tests are not infinite proofs.

**Originality (PASS, subject to historical priority).** Cooper–Frieze (1995) establish the model, counts and connectivity, not the joint high-weight clocks. The published exact reciprocal-component law already supplies the complete unmarked matching product, and the threshold-marked version is directly recovered by memorylessness: neither is asserted as independently original. Gupta–Iyer (arXiv first posted 2006-04-27) treat spatial random geometric nearest-neighbor extremes, not a complete graph with independently weighted edges or a joint reciprocal/isolation theorem. The genuinely separate step is the mixed-factorial-moment decoupling and resulting joint extreme law. Searches for late isolated-vertex mergers, largest reciprocal weights and joint extreme clocks did not identify a statement implying this result. Residual risk: a general continuous Erdős–Rényi graph-process theorem could cover the same pair of extreme clocks without using reciprocal-neighbor terminology; absence from the inspected sources is not a universal novelty proof.

**Value (PASS).** In a symmetric random-link nearest-neighbor network, a vertex's worst nearest-edge quality and the worst reciprocal-pair quality are two operationally distinct extremes. Their typical transformed levels have leading scales \(\log n/n\) and \(\log n/(2n)\), respectively. The independence theorem permits simultaneous asymptotic tail calibration for those two extremes from one random edge array; it cannot be inferred from two marginal Gumbel limits alone. This is an interpretable stochastic-network distinction rather than a reparameterized expectation or a small numerical improvement.
