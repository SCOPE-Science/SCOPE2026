# Scientific review: two nested critical windows for near-brightest circular-cone shadows

## Correctness — PASS
The exact Cauchy-projection formula reduces normalized brightness to a function of one Haar-uniform axial cosine. The prior deterministic global-maximizer classification supplies a unique polar maximum below the aspect threshold and a unique nondegenerate oblique maximizing latitude above it. At the tie the maxima are disjoint. Direct differentiation establishes strictly positive oblique curvature \(\kappa\), the envelope slope \(d\), and the second derivative \(q\). The polar brightness is exactly \(\pi z\), so its contribution is an exact interval length; the analytic Morse lemma gives the symmetric oblique interval's square-root length and an \(O(t^{3/2})\) remainder. The two-window expansions follow from \(m(k)-\pi=d(k-k_c)+q(k-k_c)^2/2+O((k-k_c)^3)\). Conditional total variation is a consequence of convergence of the scaled interval endpoints with constant original latitude density. The full proof establishes the asserted quantifiers, strict inequalities, and inner threshold boundary. The independent numerical formula implementation passes; numbers alone are not an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS (provisional historical priority)
Vickers–Brown (2001) and Pennell–Deignan (1989) precede the area formula, and the publicly available global brightness-direction theorem determines the aspect threshold and maximizing directions. None of their inspected accessible treatments states the \(\varepsilon\)-scale probability crossover, the nested \(\varepsilon^2\)-scale window, or the conditional polar–oblique mixture. The pre-existing implicit full distribution makes these formulas in principle derivable, and the inaccessible Pennell–Deignan complete paper remains a priority risk; this is not a claim that the known shadow PDF is new. Targeted searches and material primary-source readings support a narrower, non-implied joint-parameter theorem rather than a title-only comparison.

## Value — PASS
The deterministic optimizer switch is a natural point where fixed-aspect probability asymptotics fail to be uniform. The explicit outer and inner windows quantify how two disjoint geometric maximizing sets compete at distinct orders, including the otherwise undetermined case where their first-order area differences cancel. The conditional viewing-direction limits encode the geometry of rare near-optimal observations. This is a complete local phase-transition description in a standard integral-geometric model, not a routine change of coordinates for an individual fixed-aspect CDF.
