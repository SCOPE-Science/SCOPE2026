For a reversible diffusion with diffusivity \(D(x)\propto(1+x)^{-\delta}\), a formally inadmissible inverse mean-first-passage profile can appear valid until an enormous observation distance. Two near-critical routes to failure have the same exact Gumbel-type slope-deficit profile, but their sharp visibility horizons grow respectively on single-exponential and double-exponential distance scales. The shifted \(2+\delta\) admissibility ceiling and universal far-field clipping identify the two boundaries and their physical meaning.
