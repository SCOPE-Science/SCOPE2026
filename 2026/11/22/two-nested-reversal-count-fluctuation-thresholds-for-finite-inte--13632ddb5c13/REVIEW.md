# Scientific review: two nested reversal-count thresholds

## Correctness — PASS

The claim concerns the number of Poisson direction reversals before the first hit of either absorbing endpoint, conditional on the endpoint reached. The outgoing-velocity transfer boundary problem is well-defined and has a unique solution because there is a positive uniformly bounded chance of reaching the next endpoint without another reversal. The matrix-square identity, exact probability generating functions, normalized series through second order, and mean/variance differences have been reconstructed. The derivative numerators used for strict spatial monotonicity are positive, and the variance at the unique mean crossover has the explicit negative rational value shown in RESULT.md. The two asymptotic threshold expansions follow from their rational implicit equations. The independent transfer-matrix calculations and symbolic checker passed; finite diagnostics are not substituted for the analytic proof.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)

The nearest primary sources treat two-boundary splitting laws and boundary first-passage times (Guéneau–Touzo, Angelani, Park–Singh–Fai), or one-sided first passages conditioned on a reversal count at a **fixed observation time** (Cinque). Their inspected statements do not give the two-sided conditional reversal-count mean/variance spatial ordering, the all-rate variance crossover, the \(\sqrt3\) onset of the mean crossover, their strict nesting, or their \(12/\ell^3\) separation. The closest indexed research results compare first-exit **time** variance in sticky or three-velocity particles or exit polarity marks in a different process. Rate-tilting of known first-exit transforms may implicitly yield the generating functions, so novelty is claimed for the global two-threshold classification, not for the classical splitting law or telegraph resolvent. A stronger unindexed marked-first-passage result remains a publication-priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The physical object is a finite-speed particle's reorientation burden before escape, a natural event count distinct from travel time. Conditioning on escape side creates two measurable risk rankings. The result proves that the variance ranking always changes once with starting location, even when the mean ranking does not, and that strong tumbling produces a nonempty region with opposing mean and variance rankings. The exact onset and asymptotic separation quantify the range in which these operational assessments disagree. The result is a complete continuum-parameter classification, not an isolated parameter check, and has no claim about arbitrary switching rules or reactive endpoints.
