# Self-review

## Correctness (PASS)
Both equations were matched to the two published characteristic-seven constructions. Their binary-quartic discriminant factorizations were checked symbolically modulo seven, including the degree-two infinity factors; factor irreducibility and the two additional quadratic square specializations were checked exactly. The Kodaira fiber types follow from distinct quadratic roots and simple discriminant zeros on smooth K3 total spaces. Exhaustive evaluation over all 2,500 base pairs in \(\mathbb P^1(\mathbb F_{49})^2\) gave counts \(3040\) and \(3320\), and two different presentations of the extension field independently gave the same results. Distinct finite-field point counts prove nonisomorphism over the base field. The two source papers prove smoothness of the input models; no broader unverified surface classification is used.

## Originality (PASS, with residual risk)
Auel--Engel--Kedlaya--Shaikh already prove that the fibers over every rational base parameter have nonsplit type \(I_2\) (Proposition 8.1); Brandhorst--Takada already give their own coefficient matrix and the pointlessness of its K3 surface. Those previously established facts are not counted as new. The new comparison identifies both **complete** residual discriminants, their distinct Frobenius degrees and singular-fiber types, and unequal quadratic-extension point counts of the two smooth K3 surfaces. Searches for these exact equations, counts, fiber spectra and comparison with the primary full-text sections found no explicit or dominating claim. A construction equation implies a computable result in principle, but its sources do not establish this cross-example classification. Unindexed computational tables or unpublished calculations remain a genuine historical-priority risk.

## Value (PASS)
The recently settled boundary for existence of pointless K3 surfaces leaves the arithmetic geometry of explicit examples underdetermined. A precise comparison of independently published models at the same extremal field size distinguishes them already over \(\mathbb F_{49}\) and classifies *every* bad fiber of their natural genus-one pencils, rather than simply rechecking lack of \(\mathbb F_7\)-points. The extra fibers differ fundamentally: two degree-four orbits of nodes in one example versus two degree-two orbits of split reducible fibers in the other. These complete natural invariants are useful for arithmetic and geometric follow-up and are not ad hoc coefficient slices.

## Scientific limitations
No full zeta function, geometric isomorphism classification, or Néron--Severi lattice is asserted. Input surface smoothness is sourced to the two primary papers. Priority outside accessible literature remains provisional.
