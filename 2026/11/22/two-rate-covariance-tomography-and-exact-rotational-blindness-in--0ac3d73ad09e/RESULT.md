# Two-rate covariance tomography and exact rotational blindness in planar resetting

## Finding

**Two-rate inverse theorem and sharp blindness alternative.** Consider a two-dimensional Ornstein–Uhlenbeck diffusion, with a general stable real drift matrix and independent, strictly positive bath noises, that is completely reset to the origin at a known positive Poisson rate. Let its stationary covariance matrices at two distinct resetting rates \(0<r_1<r_2\) be \(C_1,C_2\). Then the drift and both bath diffusivities are **uniquely reconstructible if and only if** the observable scalar
\[
 h=\bigl((C_1-C_2)^{-1}C_1\bigr)_{22}-\bigl((C_1-C_2)^{-1}C_1\bigr)_{11}
\]
is nonzero. When \(h=0\), every physically realizable pair of observations admits an open one-parameter family of physically admissible drift/diffusivity pairs; hence uniqueness fails maximally along this singular branch. An explicit reconstruction and a necessary-and-sufficient admissibility test are given below.

Moreover, the failure can persist under **every positive resetting rate and even observation of every complete stationary one-time position distribution**. For arbitrary \(\kappa>0\), positive diagonal \(D\), and \(\omega\in\mathbb R\), the different drifts \(K_\omega=\kappa I+\omega JD^{-1}\), with \(J=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\), all give the same stationary covariance \(C_r=2D/(2\kappa+r)\) and the same stationary position distribution at every \(r>0\), while their irreversible rotational dynamics differs.

## Assumptions and scope

The unreset process in \(\mathbb R^2\) obeys the Itô equation
\[
 \mathrm dX_t=-KX_t\,\mathrm dt+\sqrt{2D}\,\mathrm dW_t,
\]
where \(K\) is a real \(2\times2\) matrix all of whose eigenvalues have strictly positive real parts, \(D=\operatorname{diag}(d_1,d_2)\) with \(d_1,d_2>0\), and \(W_t\) is standard two-dimensional Brownian motion. Independent Poisson events of intensity \(r>0\) instantaneously send both coordinates to zero. Every stationary mean is zero. The **only inverse observations** are both full symmetric matrices \(C_1=\operatorname{Cov}_{r_1}(X)\) and \(C_2=\operatorname{Cov}_{r_2}(X)\), with the rates known and the same unknown \(K,D\) at both rates. Cross-coordinate covariances must be measured; only two marginal variances do not meet the assumptions.

The admissible domain of arbitrary measured pairs consists of real symmetric matrices for which the algorithm below finds a stable real \(K\) and a strictly positive diagonal \(D\). A pair generated by the model is automatically positive definite, and \(\Delta=C_1-C_2\) is strictly positive definite. The following formulas use matrix indices \(1,2\) and standard inverses of strictly positive definite matrices.

Set
\[
 \Delta=C_1-C_2,\quad Q=r_2C_2-r_1C_1,\quad J=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
 K_0=\tfrac12 Q\Delta^{-1},
\]
and define
\[
 D_0=\tfrac12\bigl(K_0C_1+C_1K_0^{\mathsf T}+r_1C_1\bigr),\quad
 h=(\Delta^{-1}C_1)_{22}-(\Delta^{-1}C_1)_{11},\quad
 d_0=(D_0)_{12}.
\]
Every candidate is parameterized without loss by
\[
 K_\omega=K_0+\omega J\Delta^{-1},\qquad
 D_\omega=\tfrac12\bigl(K_\omega C_1+C_1K_\omega^{\mathsf T}+r_1C_1\bigr),\qquad \omega\in\mathbb R.
\]
Its off-diagonal bath entry satisfies \((D_\omega)_{12}=d_0+\omega h/2\). Hence:

1. If \(h\ne0\), set \(\omega_*=-2d_0/h\). The data are realizable **exactly when** \(K_{\omega_*}\) has \(\operatorname{tr}K_{\omega_*}>0\) and \(\det K_{\omega_*}>0\), and both diagonal entries of \(D_{\omega_*}\) are strictly positive. In that case this is the unique realizing pair.
2. If \(h=0\) and \(d_0\ne0\), the observations are not realizable with independent baths.
3. If \(h=d_0=0\), the complete realization set is all \((K_\omega,D_\omega)\) satisfying the same strict stability and positivity conditions. It is an open subset of the real \(\omega\)-line, so any nonempty realization set contains an interval; uniqueness is impossible.

For arbitrary proposed data, additionally require \(C_1,C_2\) and \(\Delta\) strictly positive definite before applying the test. Such positivity is necessary for the model; sufficiency is as in the three cases above.

## Proof

**1. Covariance equation and strict ordering.** The Poisson-reset generator acts on the matrix function \(X X^{\mathsf T}\) by adding the reset contribution \(-rXX^{\mathsf T}\). Itô's formula at stationarity gives
\[
 KC_r+C_rK^{\mathsf T}+rC_r=2D.
\]
Equivalently, if \(M(t)\) is the covariance at unreset age \(t\), initialized at zero,
\[
 M(t)=2\int_0^t e^{-Ku}De^{-K^{\mathsf T}u}\,\mathrm du,
 \qquad C_r=\int_0^\infty re^{-rt}M(t)\,\mathrm dt
 =2\int_0^\infty e^{-ru}e^{-Ku}De^{-K^{\mathsf T}u}\,\mathrm du.
\]
For every nonzero real vector \(v\), the integrand \(v^{\mathsf T}e^{-Ku}De^{-K^{\mathsf T}u}v\) is strictly positive at every finite \(u\). Since \(r_2>r_1>0\), multiplication by \(e^{-r_1u}-e^{-r_2u}>0\) for \(u>0\) shows \(v^{\mathsf T}(C_1-C_2)v>0\). Thus \(C_1,C_2,\Delta\) are all positive definite.

**2. Exact inverse fiber.** Subtraction of the two stationary covariance equations yields
\[
 K\Delta+\Delta K^{\mathsf T}=r_2C_2-r_1C_1=Q.
\]
Because \(Q\) is symmetric, this is equivalent to \(K\Delta=Q/2+\Omega\) for some real skew-symmetric \(\Omega\). Every real skew-symmetric \(2\times2\) matrix is \(\omega J\), so the most general candidate is exactly \(K_\omega\). Substituting into the first covariance equation forces precisely \(D_\omega\); the second automatically follows by subtraction. This parameterization therefore includes every possible realization.

**3. The diagonal-bath constraint.** Put \(M=\Delta^{-1}C_1\). By symmetry of \(C_1,\Delta^{-1}\),
\[
 2(D_\omega-D_0)=\omega\bigl(JM-M^{\mathsf T}J\bigr),
\]
whose \((1,2)\) entry is \(\omega(M_{22}-M_{11})=\omega h\). Independent noises require \(d_0+\omega h/2=0\), proving the three alternatives. For real \(2\times2\) matrices \(K\), both eigenvalues have positive real part exactly when \(\operatorname{tr}K>0\) and \(\det K>0\). Positivity of each diagonal bath entry is likewise necessary and sufficient. Under those conditions, the Lyapunov equation has a **unique** stationary covariance because the positive-stable drift (and any positive reset rate) make its homogeneous matrix evolution decay. Since the proposed \(C_1,C_2\) obey both equations, they are exactly the realized covariances. The strict inequalities make the realization set open in \(\omega\) whenever it is nonempty, proving the claimed nonidentifiability.

**4. The exceptional branch is genuine and the identifiable branch is generic.** If \(K=\operatorname{diag}(k_1,k_2)\) and \(D\) is positive diagonal, then \((C_r)_{ii}=2d_i/(2k_i+r)\) and direct substitution gives
\[
 h=\frac{2(k_2-k_1)}{r_2-r_1}.
\]
Thus \(h\ne0\) holds on a nonempty open, and indeed algebraically generic, set of physical parameters. The exceptional equation is not an identity imposed by the model.

**5. An all-rate, all-position-marginal blind family.** Fix \(\kappa>0\), any strictly positive diagonal \(D\), and real \(\omega\). The drift \(K_\omega=\kappa I+\omega JD^{-1}\) satisfies
\[
 K_\omega D+DK_\omega^{\mathsf T}=2\kappa D,
 \qquad \operatorname{tr}K_\omega=2\kappa,
 \qquad \det K_\omega=\kappa^2+\frac{\omega^2}{d_1d_2}>0.
\]
The unique unreset covariance evolution started at zero is therefore
\[
 M_\omega(t)=\frac{1-e^{-2\kappa t}}{\kappa}D,
\]
independent of \(\omega\). The unreset position at each age is a centered Gaussian with this covariance, so its **entire age-specific probability law**, not just its second moment, is independent of \(\omega\). The exponential-age stationary mixture at each resetting rate inherits this blindness. Its covariance integrates to \(C_r=2D/(2\kappa+r)\) for every \(r>0\). Conversely, \(\omega\ne0\) generates a nonzero rotational Onsager component, because \(K_\omega(D/\kappa)-(D/\kappa)K_\omega^{\mathsf T}=2\omega J/\kappa\), unlike \(\omega=0\). Thus different physical irreversible dynamics are observationally identical even under a complete one-time rate scan.

## Verification

An independent exact-rational implementation of the covariance Lyapunov solve and inverse reconstruction is supplied as `checker.py` (requires Python 3 and SymPy). Running `python checker.py` prints `PASS exact-rational scenarios: 144` and recovers the illustrative asymmetric drift and independent baths
\[
 K=\begin{pmatrix}2&1/2\\-1/3&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 D=\operatorname{diag}(1,3),\qquad r_1=1,\quad r_2=2,
\]
with \(h=-80760/72181\) and precisely matching covariance matrices. Further exact-rational tests verify the all-rate-blind family for unequal and equal bath strengths. The program tests finite examples only; the quantified theorem is supported by the analytic proof above, not by extrapolation from computations.

## Relationship to prior work

Majumdar, Schehr, Viot and Oshanin (first preprint November 4, 2026) established stationary distributions, covariances and currents for a harmonic Brownian gyrator under fixed-point and circular Poisson resetting. Their **forward** analysis motivates the experimental inverse question; those formulas and the renewal mixture itself are prior work. Godrèche and Luck (2018) established the general irreversible Ornstein–Uhlenbeck Sylvester/Onsager framework and illustrated how stationary laws can conceal irreversibility, independently of resetting. The result here does **not** claim these frameworks or the basic Lyapunov/renewal identities as new. The claimed additional mathematical content is a sharp **two-reset-rate inverse classification** with independent diagonal baths, the complete nongeneric affine fiber and attainability test, plus an explicit strongly nonidentifiable family across all resetting rates and full one-time marginals. Monti et al. (2026) developed stable matrix parameterizations and likelihood methods for unrestricted Ornstein–Uhlenbeck drift, not the same two-rate stationary resetting inverse.

A semantic search of published mathematical finding summaries and targeted literature searches did not identify a theorem with the same two-rate reconstruction/degeneracy assertions. An equivalent could be implicit in generic matrix inverse-system-identification literature; title and abstract searches do not establish absolute novelty. No claim is made about identifiability from time-dependent correlations or experimental noisy data.

## Limitations

The results are for exactly two dimensions, known independent Poisson reset rates, full resetting to the origin, stable linear drift and strictly positive **diagonal** noise covariance. They do not imply that stationary covariances suffice if bath noise has an unknown off-diagonal component, or if only marginal variances rather than the full covariance are measured. At \(h=0\) physical parameter recovery cannot be made unique even using both matrices; the explicit all-rate example shows much stronger one-time observational blindness for a special nonreciprocal subfamily. No noise-robust stability estimate is claimed near \(h=0\), where division by a small \(h\) can amplify measurement errors. The exact-rational checker does not independently certify the continuous-time theorem. Research-priority conclusions are conditional on the investigated sources and would benefit from independent literature assessment.
