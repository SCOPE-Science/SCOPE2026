For a positive-stable planar drift, the reset generator gives the stationary Lyapunov equations \(KC_i+C_iK^{\mathsf T}+r_iC_i=2D\). Subtraction gives \(K\Delta+\Delta K^{\mathsf T}=Q\). Strict positivity of \(\Delta\) follows by integrating a strictly positive quadratic form with the strictly positive weight difference of the two reset-rate exponentials. The general solution is exactly \(K_\omega=(Q/2+\omega J)\Delta^{-1}\); the bath's off-diagonal constraint is \(d_0+\omega h/2=0\). This proves the dichotomy and, with strict trace/determinant and bath-positivity conditions, the necessary-and-sufficient realizability classification. The inverse's sufficiency is guaranteed by uniqueness of the stable Lyapunov solution, not merely by the two algebraic residuals being zero.

The distinct full-marginal blindness family satisfies \(K_\omega D+DK_\omega^{\mathsf T}=2\kappa D\), giving an exact Gaussian age law independent of \(\omega\); Poisson age mixing preserves this property for every reset rate. These steps are reconstructed in the proof and checked for boundary quantifiers. The standalone exact-rational checker passed 144 examples including asymmetric drifts, unequal positive bath diffusivities and blind branches. The finite test suite is supporting evidence, not an infinite proof.

## Value — PASS

The unknown planar drift has four real parameters and the two independent baths add two, precisely matching the six scalar entries of two measured symmetric covariances. The theorem goes beyond dimension counting: it supplies a complete, constructive, observable necessary-and-sufficient identifiability test; explicitly describes every admissible parameter when identification fails; and exhibits a continuum of physically stable but dynamically distinct models whose **entire reset-rate family of stationary position laws** is identical. This is a motivated inverse-diagnostics boundary for nonequilibrium and reset-driven diffusion rather than a tuned small numerical example. The blind-family result explains why steady-state tomography can conceal rotational nonequilibrium dynamics even when reset rate can be changed freely.
