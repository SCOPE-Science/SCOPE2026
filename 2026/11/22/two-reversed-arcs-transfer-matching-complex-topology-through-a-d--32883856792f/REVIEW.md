# Scientific review

**Correctness (PASS).** Every forward-only multipath is exactly a matching of the remaining bipartite graph; a single reversed arc creates no cycle; both reversed arcs create a cycle only when the two cross arcs are selected. This exhaustive characterization yields an exact union of two cones and an edge-cone. The cofiber has the homotopy type of three suspensions of the matching complex on the four-vertex complement, including the augmented empty case. A standalone face and mod-two chain checker passed 1,301 finite graph tests; the proof covers all finite simple bipartite graphs and all integer coefficients. The explicit fifth-homology three-torsion example uses the established integral theorem of Jonsson rather than a field extrapolation.

**Originality (PASS against inspected evidence).** Caputi–Collari–Di Trani–Smith, arXiv:2208.04656 (full text, definitions and alternating/tournament/bipartite sections), provides the foundational identification for unmodified alternating orientations but not this sharp two-arc cycle threshold. Caputi–Collari–Di Trani, arXiv:2110.11206 (full available text, multipath examples), likewise does not cover the two-reversal cofiber. The full available original published result on one source–sink feedback arc concerns Hamiltonian path top classes, not the all-degree homotopy transfer here. The oriented-matchings paper arXiv:2203.03476 studies a different construction. Targeted scientific-record searches returned only those neighboring topics or fully bidirected bipartite multipath problems. Residual risk: a differently indexed, equivalent orientation-change theorem could exist.

**Value (PASS).** The result gives a universal, sharp four-cycle criterion and an all-degree integral homotopy transfer mechanism, not merely a small-graph example. It explains how a two-arc perturbation of an acyclic orientation creates arbitrarily complicated matching-complex homology, including three-primary torsion already known to exist in a natural chessboard complex. It also identifies the precise necessity of two cross edges.

**Limitations.** Exactly two disjoint reversed edges. No assertion for adjacent/multiple reversals or induced group actions; finite tests are not an integral proof, and the Kneser/chessboard torsion input is not new. Independent validation was not performed.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
