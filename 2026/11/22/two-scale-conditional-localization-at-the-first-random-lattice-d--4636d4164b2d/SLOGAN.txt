At the first radius where a randomly translated disk can cover three or four square-lattice sites, the disk center has two different conditional spatial scales: triangle coverage produces a uniform parabolic-cap law after square-root/linear anisotropic rescaling, whereas four-site coverage produces a uniform triangle law after linear/linear rescaling. Their sharp total-variation errors are respectively \((3\sqrt2/4)\sqrt\varepsilon+O(\varepsilon)\) and \((4/3)\varepsilon+O(\varepsilon^2)\), revealing location information absent from previously known hull-area probabilities.
