# Review: Two-scale Euclidean chord spectra of two-hub graphical zonotopes

## Correctness — PASS
The claim fixes the canonical Euclidean embedding, exact unit generator normalization, distinct-vertex uniform law, and all integer ranges. The proof derives the acyclic word model from the absence of oppositely oriented mixed columns; direct coordinate subtraction establishes the exact squared distance; inclusion-exclusion yields the nine-kernel polynomial with the correct diagonal subtraction and factor of two. All exponent integrality and boundary cases \(n\ge2\) are accounted for. The same-branch limit uses the explicit covariance eigenvalues \(8/3,8/9\) of the difference vector, plus an independent law-of-large-numbers leaf contribution \(4/3\); the opposite-branch variance is \(16/81\). Exceptional words have exponentially small mass. The verifier independently enumerates all pairs for dimensions 2–6 and performs convolution and aggregate-moment checks through dimension 12. This supports, but does not replace, the all-dimensional algebraic and probabilistic arguments.

## Originality — PASS, provisional priority
The closest published research records for the **same** polytope are *Exact radial shells and a Gaussian vertex-radius law for biclique graphical zonotopes* (`fb52cfc91ae5`) and *Exact vertex covariance and a six-leaf eigenaxis transition for two-hub graphical zonotopes* (`cf349f53c764`). Their full RESULT texts were inspected. The first proves a one-point norm spectrum and one-point radial CLT; the second determines a second-moment tensor. Neither determines the two-point joint geometry, the complete pair-distance polynomial, or the conditional near/far limits. The published complete-multipartite circumsphere theorem (`2fc427ff213b`) already implies the diametral pair claim; that portion is expressly credited, not presented as novel. The two primary graphical-zonotope papers, arXiv:2111.12422 and arXiv:2201.06896, were inspected in their accessible full HTML renderings at the pertinent vertex, shuffle, and deformation sections; the full HTML of arXiv:2303.09021 was also inspected at its orientation enumeration results. These do not state a Euclidean pair-distance law. Other accessible published geometry claims were compared for the same objects, metric and logical coverage. Broader unindexed orientation correlation formulas could still imply the result; historical priority remains provisional.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The invariant is a natural complete Euclidean chord census, not a selected ad hoc distance or a routine extremal number. The two-hub graph is the first complete-bipartite extension beyond the star: its two coherent orientation branches cause a geometric separation that is invisible in a graph-only vertex count and cannot be recovered from its already known radial histogram or covariance. The result provides an exact polynomial-time coefficient algorithm for a family with exponentially many vertices, a shifted non-Gaussian near-chord law, a distinct far-chord Gaussian law, and an asymptotic half/half separation between squared distances of orders \(n\) and \(n^2\). The near/far dichotomy is a meaningful structural boundary for metric statistics on graphical zonotopes.

## Scope and residual risks
Canonical unit-weight \(K_{2,n}\) zonotopes only; no result is asserted for other graph families, edge weights, volume sampling, or Gaussian normal-cone sampling. Priority could be affected by a differently formulated existing joint sign-matrix enumerator or by unpublished work. The coefficient convolution is not claimed to be a closed rational generating function in dimension. Finite checking alone does not establish the limit theorems.
