Gaussian averaging in a rapidly switching harmonic trap loses relative accuracy first at \(x\asymp q^{1/4}\) with a strict quartic exponential correction, then has a non-Gaussian large-deviation rate at \(x=\sqrt q\,y\), with an explicit unique Gaussian-variance saddle and positive prefactor. Rare displacements reveal the weak hidden trap with probability increasing from \(1/2\) toward \(1\), and the rate function identifies both confining stiffnesses even though the central Gaussian law sees only their sum.
