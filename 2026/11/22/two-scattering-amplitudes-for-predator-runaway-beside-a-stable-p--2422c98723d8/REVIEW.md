## Originality — PASS, within inspected evidence

The original full-text Hong–Zhang article (https://doi.org/10.3390/math11061399), including its model, equilibrium stability section, Neimark–Sacker derivation, numerical examples, and conclusion, provides no invariant escape wedge or positive finite pair of escape amplitudes. Its local fixed-point results imply neither a global competing escape set nor the sharp \(e^{sn}\) and \(e^{e^{sn}}\) hierarchy. The closest broad conceptual predecessor, Parshad et al. (https://arxiv.org/html/2208.00153v1, Sections 2.3–2.4), already proves continuous-time predator explosion in a high-food no-refuge model; its real-time ODE conclusion is not the discrete recurrence or its precise limit constants, nor does its parameter regime provide a simultaneous attracting prey-only equilibrium. The 2017 Ghosh–Sahoo–Poria model (https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chaos.2017.01.010) is continuous-time and studies equilibrium and limit-cycle dynamics. Targeted semantic checks found related but non-covering discrete extinction results in other predator-prey maps. Related full text and available abstracts were read only to the material extent stated in the structured record; database non-retrieval alone was not treated as proof of novelty. Residual risk: an earlier unindexed paper could contain an equivalent discrete asymptotic or a general asymptotic theorem with sufficient hypotheses.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The exact empirical-time survival amplitude \(L\) and the renormalized prey amplitude \(K\) distinguish infinitely many high-predator histories inside a phase region where the prey-only equilibrium remains attracting from other initial conditions. A qualitative statement that predators can grow without bound was already known in a related differential equation, but the simultaneous coexistence in this *discrete* map, the open trapping region, and the two sharply normalized asymptotic clocks answer a specific global dynamics question left by the source's local bifurcation study. The \(n\) correction in the prey exponent, alongside its initial-state-dependent superexponential coefficient and finite residual amplitude, would be lost in coarse logarithmic growth estimates. The theorem does not purport to give a global basin boundary or all possible invariant sets; uncontrolled predator densities are a limitation of the model rather than an ecological forecast.
