# Review of two sharp Euclidean chord shells in alternating type-C Gale-interval polytopes

## Correctness

PASS. The proof specifies the exact type-C Gale upper interval and its native Euclidean coordinates. Suffix inequalities reduce to a two-walk barrier, and all diameter pairs are classified by forced two-step blocks. The next shell has a single agreeing coordinate; excluding agreement at one and using the five-state path adjacency recurrences gives the exact independent odd/even-position sums. The pair enumeration verifies all cases through dimension twelve without replacing the unbounded argument.

## Originality

PASS, subject to historical-priority uncertainty. Chen et al. establish the general interval and its suffix model but do not state top Euclidean distance counts. Eur et al. develop general polytope algebra and volume/lattice-point counts rather than the native chord metric. The closest identified metric theorem treats only linked toric snake intervals of the form \([S,S\cup\{n\}]\); for \(r\ge2\), the interval \([\varnothing,\{2,4,\ldots,2r\}]\) does not lie in that family. Searches for complement pairs, near-antipodal distance distributions and type-C alternating Gale intervals found no covering theorem. Unindexed equivalent treatments are possible.

## Mathematical value

PASS. The two highest shells are canonical metric statistics of a genuinely non-snake infinite family of feasible polytopes. Their exact enumeration yields a complete diametral graph and a sharp exponential separation between its extremal and near-extremal pair populations; the five-state defect method is reusable for longer outer shells. This is a natural structural classification, not an arbitrary dimension-specific table.

## Limits

The theorem does not determine lower distance shells or metric invariants after a nonorthogonal affine coordinate change. The finite checker is calibration only; there is no independent mathematical audit. Literature coverage is bounded by identified, accessible sources.
