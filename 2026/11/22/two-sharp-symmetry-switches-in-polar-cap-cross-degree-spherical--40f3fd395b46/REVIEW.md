# Review of the polar-cap cross-degree leakage theorem

## Correctness — PASS
The exact operator is the cross projection from harmonic degree three to harmonic degree two through one polar cap, not the full Slepian operator. The stabilizer decomposition into three matched transverse orders and one unmatched source order is exhaustive. Independent uniform-sphere moments give all six normalization constants. Direct conditional integrations give three rational polynomials times a common positive beta-moment factor. The dominant-sector comparisons reduce to one strictly monotone ratio and an upward quadratic with two roots. At the monotone crossing, a separately factored positive algebraic defect proves the third sector is strictly below; hence there are exactly two global top-mode transitions, not just two candidate crossings. The complete dimension/multiplicity count, high-dimensional limits and thin-cap condition constant follow. All required parameter boundaries and equalities are explicit. `verify.py` reports 308 exact, finite-inequality and independent numerical quadrature checks; the proof, rather than these tests, establishes all dimensions.

## Originality — PASS, best-knowledge
The classical 2004 polar-cap Slepian matrix already contains cross-degree integrals for spherical harmonics on the two-sphere, so the matrix and harmonic separation are not new. The 2021 scalar/vector/tensor framework extends such methods; this result claims neither framework. The closest 2026 published two-adjacent-degree theorem ranks hemispherical modes across degree pairs, but fixes the cap cutoff at zero; it does not provide variable-aperture degree-two-to-three singular values, the dimension-uniform pair of threshold equations, their strict ordering, or the high-dimensional boundary. Direct statement-and-implication comparisons, not title matching, found no covering result among the inspected sources and retrieved mathematical records. Residual risk: a specialized cap harmonic table or uncatalogued treatment might give the same low-degree transitions; independent publication-priority assessment remains open.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Adjacent-degree leakage is a concrete obstacle in localized spherical spectral analysis, including separation of quadrupole and octupole components. The degree pair \((2,3)\) is the first adjacent positive-degree pair with three matched transverse orders, making the three-sector classification natural rather than an arbitrary degree slice. The sharp, all-dimensional phase diagram identifies which representation sector dominates that leakage at every one-cap aperture, with unexpectedly two switches, explicit transition multiplicities, a universal high-dimensional cutoff scale, and an exact thin-cap conditioning exponent and coefficient. These are motivated spectral facts, not arbitrary polynomial evaluations or a restatement of the Slepian block-diagonalization. The result is a focused exact classification, not a claim about arbitrary bands or a general new concentration theory.

## Closest literature and limits
Simons, Dahlen and Wieczorek (2004), Section 5.1, provide the generic cap harmonic coupling matrix in dimension three; Michel, Plattner and Seibert (2021), Sections 3.2 and 4, provide scalar/vector/tensor Slepian construction; the 2026 *Sharp sectoral concentration and complete eigenvalue ordering for two adjacent spherical degrees* covers the hemispherical slice. Those methods are compatible with the present proof, but their explicitly stated conclusions do not subsume its two-aperture-transition classification. The work does not solve arbitrary-degree coupling or nonpolar observation geometries. Verification computations are finite, and originality is necessarily provisional without an independent priority check.
