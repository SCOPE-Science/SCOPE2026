# Review of finite-star heat-trajectory variation sensitivity

## Correctness — PASS
The derivation is finite-dimensional and exact: the star's edge-difference evolution is generated by \(-(I+J)\), with \(J^2=kJ\). Exponentiation and differentiation give the entrywise transfer matrix, whose common absolute column sum is the induced real \(\ell^1\) norm because every edge-difference vector is realizable. A one-leaf indicator attains equality. The cusp/critical-time classification follows from the sign of a single exponential, and the strict degree ordering from an explicit positive derivative of the log peak height. Exact rational comparisons at degrees eight and nine establish the threshold. The packaged standard-library checker independently compares vertex heat evolution and edge derivatives and passes all stated finite tests. No finite test stands in for the arbitrary-degree proof.

## Originality — PASS, qualified by priority risk
Keller–Lenz, *Dirichlet forms and stochastic completeness of graphs and subgraphs* (arXiv:0904.2985, PDF introduction and framework), gives the abstract finite/infinite graph semigroup setting, not a sharp star temporal-BV derivative. Chen–Coulhon–Hua, *Riesz transforms for bounded Laplacians on graphs* (arXiv:1708.05476, PDF pp. 1–6), proves \(\ell^p\) heat-gradient bounds for \(1<p\le2\), not the sharp endpoint derivative norm. The indexed *Exact transient Lipschitz amplification for complete-bipartite heat semigroups* concerns \(\|P_t\|_{\mathrm{Lip}}\), not \(\|\partial_tP_t\|_{BV}\); operator-norm differentiation is not valid in general. Related exact star and complete-bipartite heat-evolution theorems already supply sharp ordinary heat-semigroup norms for these graphs; their scalar evolution norms do not determine the BV norm of the time derivative or the distinct late-peak threshold. The indexed weighted-tree paper classifies whether \(\|P_t\|_{BV}\le1\) at all times; it does not imply the precise temporal-derivative peak profile. The indexed friendship-graph sensitivity result uses a different graph and bounded-input norm. Semantic searches for star heat, edge variation, temporal derivative, peak times and aliases did not find the present claim. The full published indexed files could not be accessed here, so implicit priority remains possible and independent verification remains necessary.

## Value — PASS
This exact nontrivial endpoint-response classification separates smoothing of an input from sensitivity of its motion: although \(P_t\) is Markovian, its variation-rate operator develops a secondary rebound only at four or more leaves and a *superunit* rebound only at nine or more. Both degree cutoffs are sharp, attained, and proved in one all-size formula, and the second peak approaches two. This is an exact stability diagnostic for the canonical branching diffusion, rather than a single small-case numerical observation, arbitrary parameter slice, or merely re-evaluation of the heat semigroup norm.

## Residual limitations
The statement assumes positive unit spoke conductances, unit vertex masses, real signals and ordinary edge variation. It does not extend to nonuniform stars or general trees.
