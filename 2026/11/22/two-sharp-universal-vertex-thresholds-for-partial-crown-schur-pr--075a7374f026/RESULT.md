# Two sharp universal-vertex thresholds for partial-crown Schur projections

## Finding

Let \(k\ge2\) and let \(r,s\ge0\) be integers. Start with the crown incidence matrix \(J_k-I_k\), then adjoin \(r\) rows and \(s\) columns containing only ones. Equivalently, \(B_{k;r,s}\) is the \((k+r)\times(k+s)\) zero–one matrix whose only zeros are \((i,i)\) for \(1\le i\le k\). Denote the norm of entrywise multiplication by this mask on complex operator-norm matrices by
\[
\nu_{k;r,s}=\sup_{\|X\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le1}\|B_{k;r,s}\circ X\|_{\mathrm{op}}.
\]
The **complete sharp norm formula** is
\[
\boxed{\nu_{k;r,s}=\begin{cases}
2-2/k,&r=s=0,\\[2pt]
k/\sqrt{2k-1},&rs=0,\ r+s>0,\ 2\le k\le3,\\[2pt]
2-2/k,&rs=0,\ r+s>0,\ k\ge4,\\[2pt]
\dfrac{k-1+2k\sqrt{k+2}}{4k-1},&r,s>0,\ 2\le k\le4,\\[6pt]
2-2/k,&r,s>0,\ k\ge5.
\end{cases}}
\]
Consequently, **one** universal shore increases the crown Schur norm only for \(k=2,3\), while **both** shores increase it exactly for \(k=2,3,4\). Once an additional row or column exists, duplicating that kind of universal vertex does not further increase the norm. In particular, the last strict one-sided increase occurs at \(k=3\), and the last strict two-sided increase occurs at \(k=4\):
\[
\nu_{3;1,0}=\frac3{\sqrt5}>\frac43,\qquad
\nu_{4;1,1}=\frac{3+8\sqrt6}{15}>\frac32.
\]
The \(k=2\) values \(2/\sqrt3\) and \(9/7\) are previously known small Schur norms, not new priority claims. The contribution is the complete all-parameter universal-vertex classification and its distinct stabilization thresholds.

## Assumptions and scope

All operator spaces and matrices are complex. The mask acts from \(B(\mathbb C^{k+s},\mathbb C^{k+r})\) to itself with the spectral operator norm; \(\|\cdot\|_{S_1}\) denotes the (unnormalized) nuclear/trace norm. Integers \(r,s\) may be zero or arbitrarily large. A universal row is an all-one row, and a universal column an all-one column, including their mutual intersection.

For a nonnegative probability vector \(p\), write \(D_p=\operatorname{diag}(\sqrt{p_i})\), and define the weighted trace-norm functional
\[
F_M(p,q)=\|D_p M D_q\|_{S_1}.
\]
The word “optimal” below refers to maximizing this functional, not to a unique vector of weights. The theorem makes no assertion of uniqueness.

## Proof

**1. Trace-dual variational formula and concavity.** For every finite complex matrix \(M\),
\[
\|S_M\|=\max_{p\in\Delta_m,\,q\in\Delta_n} F_M(p,q),
\]
where \(\Delta_m\) and \(\Delta_n\) are probability simplexes. Indeed, by duality the norm of the Schur map on bounded operators equals that of the same Schur map on trace class; the trace-class unit ball is generated by rank-one contractions \(uv^*\), for unit vectors \(u,v\). Diagonal phases on the left and right do not change singular values, so \(\|M\circ(uv^*)\|_{S_1}=F_M(|u|^2,|v|^2)\). Conversely, every pair of probability vectors is obtained from such a pair of unit vectors.

A key fact is that \((p,q)\mapsto F_M(p,q)\) is **jointly concave**. For strictly positive \(p,q\), the trace-norm factorization identity gives
\[
F_M(p,q)=\frac12\inf_{M_{ij}=\langle y_i,z_j\rangle}
\left(\sum_i p_i\|y_i\|^2+\sum_j q_j\|z_j\|^2\right),
\]
where vectors are taken in an arbitrary finite-dimensional Hilbert space. This follows by applying the singular-value decomposition to \(D_pMD_q\) and undoing the positive diagonal scalings. An infimum of affine functions is concave; continuity handles vanishing weights. This weighted-nuclear-norm symmetry principle is standard in Schur theory (e.g. Levene 2026, Lemmas 2.3 and 2.5), not claimed as new.

**2. Universal-vertex duplication invariance.** Suppose two rows of \(M\) are identical. Their corresponding rows of \(D_p M D_q\) are proportional, with factors \(\sqrt{p_i}\) and \(\sqrt{p_j}\). A unitary rotation in this row pair replaces them by a single row weighted by \(\sqrt{p_i+p_j}\) and one zero row, leaving all singular values unchanged. The same argument applies to identical columns. Combining all newly adjoined rows/columns reduces every case to \((r,s)\in\{(0,0),(1,0),(0,1),(1,1)\}\). Both the upper bound and its attainment survive this reduction, since the aggregate weights can be split among duplicated vertices. Thus only the presence of each universal shore matters.

**3. Symmetry reduction of the weighted optimization.** Simultaneously permuting the first \(k\) old rows and the first \(k\) old columns leaves the mask fixed. Concavity from Step 1 lets us average any maximizer over this permutation group. Hence a maximizer exists whose weights are constant separately on the first \(k\) rows and first \(k\) columns. If there is one extra row on one side and none on the other, the old columns have weights \(1/k\), while the old rows have common weights \(x/k\), with the extra row having weight \(1-x\), for some \(0\le x\le1\). The transposed case is identical. When there is one extra row and one extra column, the square mask is symmetric. Interchanging row/column weight vectors leaves the weighted nuclear norm unchanged; their average is also maximizing by concavity. We may therefore use the **same** weight on both shores: \(x/k\) on each of the \(k\) old vertices and \(1-x\) on each extra vertex.

**4. No universal vertices.** For \(r=s=0\), uniform weights give \((J_k-I_k)/k\). Its singular values are \((k-1)/k\) once and \(1/k\) exactly \(k-1\) times; its trace norm is \(2-2/k\). Simultaneous-permutation averaging proves this value is the global maximum, not merely a witness.

**5. One universal shore.** With the weights from Step 3, the \(k+1\) by \(k\) weighted mask has \(k-1\) singular values equal to \(\sqrt{x}/k\) and one additional singular value \(\sqrt{1-(2k-1)x/k^2}\). Its trace norm is therefore
\[
f_k(x)=\frac{k-1}{k}\sqrt{x}+\sqrt{1-\frac{2k-1}{k^2}x},\qquad 0\le x\le1.
\]
Both summands are concave and the first is strictly concave on the interior. Direct differentiation gives its sole positive stationary point at
\[
x_{\mathrm{one}}=\frac{(k-1)^2}{2k-1}.
\]
This lies in \((0,1)\) exactly when \(k=2,3\). Evaluating the stationary value gives \(f_k(x_{\mathrm{one}})=k/\sqrt{2k-1}\). For \(k\ge4\), \(f_k\) increases to the endpoint \(x=1\), yielding \(f_k(1)=2-2/k\). The exact endpoint derivative has sign \(k^2-4k+2\), changing between \(k=3\) and \(k=4\).

**6. Both universal shores.** With symmetric weights from Step 3, the weighted \((k+1)\times(k+1)\) matrix is real symmetric. On the zero-sum subspace of the first \(k\) coordinates it has eigenvalue \(-x/k\) with multiplicity \(k-1\). Its remaining two-by-two block, on the constant old-coordinate vector and new coordinate, equals
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
(k-1)x/k &\sqrt{x(1-x)}\\
\sqrt{x(1-x)}&1-x
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The determinant is \(-x(1-x)/k\), so the two eigenvalues have opposite signs in the interior. Their combined absolute values equal the discriminant square root. Therefore the complete trace norm is
\[
h_k(x)=\frac{k-1}{k}x+\sqrt{1+\frac{2x}{k}-\frac{4k-1}{k^2}x^2}.
\]
The radicand is positive on \([0,1]\), and \(h_k\) is strictly concave. Its unique unconstrained stationary point is
\[
x_{\mathrm{both}}=\frac{k}{4k-1}\left(1+\frac{2(k-1)}{\sqrt{k+2}}\right),
\]
with exact value \(h_k(x_{\mathrm{both}})=(k-1+2k\sqrt{k+2})/(4k-1)\). This point is in \((0,1)\) precisely for \(k=2,3,4\). For all \(k\ge5\), the derivative at the right endpoint is nonnegative and the function increases to \(h_k(1)=2-2/k\). Explicitly,
\[
h_k'(1)=\frac{k^2-5k+2}{k(k-1)},
\]
negative for \(k=2,3,4\) and positive for \(k\ge5\). This establishes the claimed branches and the exact two threshold positions.

**7. Attainment.** At the maximizers above, let \(U\) be the partial isometry from the polar decomposition of \(D_p B_{k;r,s}D_q\). Trace/operator-norm duality supplies a contraction \(X\) with \(\operatorname{Tr}((D_p B D_q)^*X)=\|D_p B D_q\|_{S_1}\). It yields an input attaining the Schur-map norm through the rank-one trace-class dual witness. For all larger \(r,s\), distribute each extra aggregate mass over its duplicated rows or columns; Step 2 preserves the singular values. All branches are exact and attained.

## Verification

The standalone `checks/verify_partial_crown.py` checks the branch formulas against the singular-value reductions for a range of integer core sizes, verifies the derivative branch switches, tests duplication invariance and computes independent numerical maximizations of the full weighted trace-norm functional on representative rectangular matrices. Its reported diagnostic count and requirements are in its output. These finite checks are supplementary; the all-integer result follows from Steps 1–7, not from numerical optimization, sampling, or solver termination.

## Relationship to prior work

Levene's 2026 *A half-line of Schur idempotent norms* motivates the study of crown incidence masks \(J_k-I_k\), but its paper's main construction hangs rooted trees on a core to produce intervals of Schur-idempotent norms. Its core computation gives \(\|J_k-I_k\|_\bullet=2-2/k\), which is credited. It does not state the present universal-row/column norm classification, whose new phenomena occur already at core sizes three and four.

Levene's 2014 *Norms of idempotent Schur multipliers*, Proposition 5.1(8), gives the \(3\times3\) norm \(9/7\), which equals \(\nu_{2;1,1}\) up to row/column permutations; Remark 5.3 also subsumes the \(k=2\) one-shore value \(2/\sqrt3\). Neither result supplies the all-core classification here. Davidson–Donsig's general Schur norm/factorization methods, and later matrix-factorization approaches, contain the general variational machinery but not the displayed phase diagram as an identified theorem.

Published results for signed partially flipped diagonal masks \(J_n-2\operatorname{diag}(1^k,0^{n-k})\) have a **different** symbol and stabilization index. Their norm does not determine that of the present zero–one deleted-matching mask \(J_n-\operatorname{diag}(1^k,0^{n-k})\), since taking half of a signed Schur map plus the identity does not preserve its norm. Likewise, the Schur norm for radius-one star masks is a different support pattern, although the small three-vertex example overlaps. The present result is a single complete classification, not a restatement of these distinct formulas.

## Limitations

Only deletion of one matching inside an otherwise complete bipartite incidence mask is considered. Arbitrary deleted-edge sets, weighted masks, infinite graphs with infinitely many independently missing edges and other Schatten-norm objectives lie outside the theorem. The result does not assert uniqueness of maximizing weight vectors and does not identify the smallest core size for any broader graph operation. The classical variational principle and low-dimensional norm values are explicitly credited. An older implicit specialization of general Schur-factorization/graph-pattern results may exist; originality has not undergone independent bibliographic certification, and independent mathematical audit has not been performed.
