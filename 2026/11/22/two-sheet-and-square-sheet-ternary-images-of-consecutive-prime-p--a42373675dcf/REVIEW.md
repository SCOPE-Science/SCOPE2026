# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

For every \(s\ge1\), the binomial coefficient valuation bound gives the exact scaled isometry on each of the two regular ternary input classes. Centering the third class at \(-1/2\) gives a scaled square times a unit function with a unit square root. The resulting coordinate change is itself a 3-adic isometry, so the entire critical image, not only a subset, is attained. The two images are disjoint modulo nine. Direct square-root fiber counting yields the closed image and exact multiplicity laws. The independent standard-library checker verified all target fibers, rather than only image sizes, in 34 complete finite rings. Its finite coverage is not used to infer the general theorem.

The closest 2023 primary source is the modular-Waring work of Arias, Borja and Anaya, which permits independent coordinates in sums/differences of powers; its lifting conclusions do not preserve the consecutive-input constraint. The earlier Arias–Borja–Rubio polynomial-image methods have the same independent-variable focus, principally on squares. Patel–Siksek's consecutive-like-powers theorem concerns integral perfect-power Diophantine solutions, not local representation fibers. Published analogous ternary binomial and dyadic adjacent-sum results have different objects; their conclusions do not imply the present all-parameter ternary difference classification. ## Mathematical value — PASS

The natural local-solubility problem for consecutive equal powers has a nontrivial critical-point phase: two regular sheets coexist with a scaled quadratic sheet, with sharply different fiber sizes. The theorem resolves all levels and exponents in the family, including the exact Haar measure and conditional multiplicities, and can serve as a precise local obstruction for adjacent-power Diophantine equations. Its claim is confined to the all-\(3^s\) family and does not assert broader Waring or Diophantine results.
