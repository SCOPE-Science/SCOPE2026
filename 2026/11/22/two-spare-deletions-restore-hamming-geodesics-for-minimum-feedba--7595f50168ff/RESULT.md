# Two spare deletions restore Hamming geodesics for minimum feedback sets of the Heawood graph

## Finding

Let \(H\) be the incidence graph of the Fano plane, with seven point-vertices, seven line-vertices, and edges joining incident point-line pairs. Let \(\mathcal F_b(H)\) be the **token-addition/removal (TAR) reconfiguration graph**: its vertices are all feedback vertex sets \(S\subseteq V(H)\) with \(|S|\le b\), and two such sets are adjacent precisely when their symmetric difference has one vertex. A feedback vertex set (FVS) is a vertex set whose deletion leaves an induced forest. All sets and configurations are unlabelled subsets of the fourteen distinguishable graph vertices. Let \(\mathcal M\) be the collection of minimum FVSs; its previously known cardinality is \(70\), and every member has size \(4\).

**Theorem.** The sharp TAR capacity allowing every optimum to communicate is \(b=6\). At \(b=5\), the graph \(\mathcal F_5(H)\) consists of fourteen connected components of order \(51\) and \(168\) isolated vertices, and has \(882\) vertices and \(700\) edges in total. Each nontrivial component has one five-vertex configuration at its centre, five minimum configurations adjacent to it, and nine additional pendant five-vertex configurations attached to each minimum configuration. At \(b=6\), the full graph \(\mathcal F_6(H)\) is connected, with \(3136\) vertices and \(8008\) edges. For **every** \(S,T\in\mathcal M\), the shortest TAR distance at this first connected capacity is exactly

\[
 d_{\mathcal F_6(H)}(S,T)=|S\triangle T|=8-2|S\cap T|.
\]

In fact, a shortest route can always be chosen to delete only vertices of \(S\setminus T\) and add only vertices of \(T\setminus S\). The graph \(\mathcal F_6(H)\) has radius \(10\) and diameter \(12\); every feasible state of size \(j\in\{4,5,6\}\) has eccentricity \(j+6\). The numbers of *unordered distinct minimum-pair* distances \(2,4,6,8\), respectively, are \(140,630,980,665\).

Thus preserving optimum feasibility under addition/removal requires exactly **two spare deletion places**, but at that capacity every pair of optimal solutions admits a detour-free shortest route. This optimum-geodesic property does **not** extend to all larger feasible sets: among all unordered pairs in \(\mathcal F_6(H)\), exactly \(382466\) pairs have distance two above their symmetric-difference lower bound and \(840\) pairs have distance four above it; all other pairs attain that lower bound.

## Assumptions and scope

The Fano plane is the projective plane of order two. One explicit model has point set \(\{1,2,\ldots,7\}\), viewed as nonzero three-bit vectors, and lines \(\{a,b,a\oplus b\}\) for distinct nonzero three-bit vectors \(a,b\). The associated bipartite graph \(H\) has fourteen vertices and twenty-one edges. The only allowed TAR moves add or delete a single **vertex of the deletion set**, never swap two vertices in one move. The cap \(b\) applies to **every intermediate** deletion set. The theorem concerns this one canonical graph, not all finite projective planes or cubic graphs.

## Proof

**Minimum size and geometric kernels.** The Heawood graph is cubic. If an FVS \(S\) has \(k\) vertices, induces \(e(S)\) edges, and its complement has \(c\) tree components, edge counting yields

\[
21-3k+e(S)=14-k-c,\qquad c=2k-7-e(S).
\]

Thus no FVS has size at most three. For \(k=4\), every FVS is independent and its ten-vertex complement is a tree. The known geometric classification can be checked directly: for each Fano line \(\ell\), let \(B_\ell\) consist of the four point-vertices outside \(\ell\), together with the line-vertex \(\ell\). Dually, for each Fano point \(p\), let \(B_p\) consist of the four line-vertices not passing through \(p\), together with the point-vertex \(p\). Each of these fourteen kernels has size five; the five four-subsets of each kernel are exactly the seventy minimum FVSs, with no repetitions. To independently establish this premise, both supplied verifiers test all possible four-subsets for acyclicity and compare the resulting set family to these fourteen kernels. This prerequisite classification and its count are **prior work**, not the discovery asserted here.

**The sharp disconnected budget.** Since no three-vertex FVS exists, the graph at capacity four has seventy isolated vertices. For budget five, each four-vertex optimum has exactly ten adjacent five-vertex supersets, since the FVS property is upward closed. Two distinct optimum sets lie together under a five-vertex superset precisely when they differ in one vertex. The kernel description shows that this happens exactly for two four-subsets of the same \(B_\ell\) or \(B_p\): different kernels intersect in at most two vertices, while optimum sets in one kernel intersect in three. Hence one kernel gives one common five-vertex centre, five four-vertex optima, and nine other size-five supersets per optimum. The latter supersets cannot be shared by two optima; otherwise those optima would be a second shared pair and their union would equal the kernel. This produces fourteen disjoint \(51\)-state components, each with \(50\) edges. The complete FVS enumeration counts \(812\) five-vertex sets, of which \(14(1+5\cdot9)=644\) belong to those components; the other \(168\) five-vertex FVSs contain no minimum set and are isolated when the cap is five. Thus there are \(14\cdot51+168=882\) states and \(14\cdot50=700\) edges.

**Connectedness and the exact optimum metric at budget six.** Every TAR path from \(S\) to \(T\) changes each vertex in \(S\triangle T\) at least once, so its length is at least \(|S\triangle T|\). The second supplied verifier independently constructs the Fano incidence graph from the cyclic difference set \(\{0,1,3\}\) modulo seven, enumerates the seventy optima, and checks *all* \(\binom{70}{2}=2415\) distinct optimum pairs using only monotone states of the form \((S\setminus X)\cup Y\), with \(X\subseteq S\setminus T\) and \(Y\subseteq T\setminus S\). A state is admitted only if deleting it leaves a forest and it has cardinality between four and six. Its explicit breadth-first search always reaches \(T\). Because each permitted step strictly reduces \(|U\triangle T|\), the witnessed paths have length exactly \(|S\triangle T|\); this is a complete finite proof for all optimum pairs, not sampling or inference from a partial search.

For the entire TAR graph at cap six, the first verifier independently enumerates all \(2^{14}=16384\) vertex subsets, tests foresthood using union-find, retains precisely the \(70+812+2254=3136\) feasible subsets of size at most six, and constructs every legal one-element addition/removal edge. Complete graph traversal finds one connected component and \(8008\) edges. Running breadth-first search from **every** one of its \(3136\) states determines each eccentricity, all pairwise distances, the stated radius and diameter, and the stated excess-distance counts. The second verifier, based on the different cyclic line model and a recursive DFS cycle test, independently checks the \(3136\)-state connectivity and the optimum geodesics. Each enumeration uses exact integer/set operations and exhaustive finite loops; the full source code below specifies all cases and is executable as ordinary Python 3.

## Verification

Place `verify_exhaustive.py` and `verify_independent.py` in the same directory and run `python3 verify_exhaustive.py` and `python3 verify_independent.py` with Python 3 and no additional dependencies. Both programs terminate with explicit assertions if a claim fails. The exhaustive checker returns the complete degree-of-feasibility counts, capacity-five components, capacity-six edge count, exact eccentricities by state size, optimum-pair Hamming equality and full distance/excess histograms. The independent checker reconstructs the graph from a different seven-line model, verifies all geometric kernels and components, and explicitly finds monotone optimal paths for all \(2415\) distinct minimum pairs. Both completed successfully on the stated files; there is no untested parameter range because the theorem is finite and specifies all fourteen graph vertices. The source files, not an external log, are the replayable certificate.

## Relationship to prior work

The earlier *All seventy minimum feedback-vertex sets of the Heawood graph* proves the optimum size, complete two-orbit Fano classification and seventy-set count. These are used as prior premises and independently checked here; the current theorem instead concerns the reconfiguration spaces with one and two spare deletion positions, their component and metric structure, and the exact optimum-pair geodesic condition. The kernel classification immediately yields the fourteen nontrivial budget-five components, so this portion is a transparent consequence of earlier geometry; it does **not** account for the 168 isolated size-five states, full budget-six connectivity, or the budget-six shortest-path and eccentricity results.

Bousquet, Hommelsheim, Kobayashi, Mühlenthaler and Suzuki establish a general fixed-cardinality FVS exchange theorem for non-minimum solutions of subcubic graphs (Theorem 4 in the 2023 paper). It supports *some* connectivity of five-vertex FVSs after exchange operations, and gives relevant context for a two-place TAR buffer, but does not yield this graph's exact capacity-five component census, full budget-six distance metric, monotone optimum geodesics or eccentricity trichotomy. Their exchange step replaces one vertex at once; the present TAR step adds **or** deletes just one. The full publicly accessible introduction and theorem statements were inspected; the complete publisher text was not accessible, and an attempted authorized institutional retrieval could not proceed, so undiscovered special-case treatment remains a priority risk. The 2019 decycling formula supplies general background but no paths between deletion sets. Records on other small cubic graphs concern different underlying graphs or different reconfiguration rules.

## Limitations

The metric theorem is specific to the fourteen-vertex Heawood graph. Its certificate is exhaustive finite computation complemented by the analytic capacity-five component derivation, not a theorem for every cubic graph or every projective incidence graph. The result does not claim that arbitrary feasible budget-six pairs have Hamming shortest paths; the displayed excess counts expressly show otherwise. Earlier equivalent special-case claims under different terminology, and unreadable portions of the closest 2023 publisher article, remain possible. The supplied programs are transparent complete finite certificates, but they are not a formal proof-assistant development.
