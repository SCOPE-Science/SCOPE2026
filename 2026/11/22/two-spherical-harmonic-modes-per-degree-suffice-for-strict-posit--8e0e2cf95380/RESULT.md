# Two spherical harmonic modes per degree suffice for strict positive definiteness

## Finding
Write a point on the unit two-sphere as \(x=(x_1,x_2,x_3)\), put \(u(x)=x_1+i x_2\) and \(v(x)=x_3\), and define
\[
K(x,y)=\frac{1+v(x)v(y)}{1-\tfrac12u(x)\overline{u(y)}}.
\]
**Theorem.** This rational kernel is jointly real analytic and Hermitian strictly positive definite on \(S^2\). In the spherical-harmonic degree-\(\ell\) subspace, the integral operator associated with \(K\) has exactly one positive spectral direction at \(\ell=0\) and exactly two at every \(\ell\ge1\), with all remaining directions zero. Thus only \(1+2L\) of the \((L+1)^2\) harmonics of degrees at most \(L\) are active, a fraction tending to zero. The real-valued kernel \(K_{\mathrm R}=\operatorname{Re} K\) is also strictly positive definite and has exactly four active real harmonics at each degree \(\ell\ge2\), three at degree one, and one at degree zero; its cumulative harmonic density likewise tends to zero. Neither kernel is universal in \(C(S^2)\). Within the two-series family in the proof, deleting either entire feature series destroys strict positive definiteness.

## Assumptions and scope
The sphere \(S^2=\{x\in\mathbb R^3:|x|=1\}\) carries its ordinary surface-area measure. Strict positive definiteness means that for **every** integer \(N\ge1\), **every** set of pairwise distinct sites \(x_1,\ldots,x_N\in S^2\), and **every** nonzero coefficient vector \(c\in\mathbb C^N\), the Gram quadratic form \(\sum_{i,j}\overline{c_i}K(x_i,x_j)c_j\) is strictly positive. An active degree-\(\ell\) harmonic means a strictly positive eigenvalue of the sphere integral operator restricted to that eigenspace of the spherical Laplacian. Universality means uniform density of the associated reproducing-kernel space in \(C(S^2)\), with complex-valued functions for \(K\), and real-valued functions for \(K_{\mathrm R}\). No claim is made that two is the minimum possible number of harmonics at each degree among *all* strictly positive definite spherical kernels.

## Proof
**Positive feature decomposition.** Since \(|u(x)|\le1\), a uniformly and absolutely convergent geometric series gives
\[
K(x,y)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{-k}\big[u(x)^k\overline{u(y)^k}+v(x)u(x)^k\overline{v(y)u(y)^k}\big].
\]
Its terms are rank-one positive semidefinite kernels. The denominator has absolute value at least \(1/2\), proving smoothness and joint real analyticity as well.

**Finite-site strictness.** If a finite-site Gram quadratic form is zero, nonnegativity of every summand yields, after conjugating the coefficients if necessary,
\[
\sum_{i=1}^N c_i u(x_i)^k=0,\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^N c_i v(x_i)u(x_i)^k=0\quad(k=0,1,2,\ldots).
\]
List the distinct projected values as \(w_1,\ldots,w_r\), where \(w_j=u(x_i)\) for a nonempty fiber. Set \(A_j=\sum_{u(x_i)=w_j}c_i\) and \(B_j=\sum_{u(x_i)=w_j}c_iv(x_i)\). Taking just \(k=0,\ldots,r-1\), both moment systems are Vandermonde systems in the *distinct complex numbers* \(w_j\), hence \(A_j=B_j=0\) for all \(j\). Each fiber of \(u:S^2\to\overline{\mathbb D}\) consists of at most the two points \((\operatorname{Re}w_j,\operatorname{Im}w_j,\pm\sqrt{1-|w_j|^2})\). A singleton fiber has its coefficient zero by \(A_j=0\); a two-point fiber has unequal real heights, so its two coefficients vanish by the invertible system \(A_j=B_j=0\). Thus \(c=0\), contradiction. This establishes complex Hermitian strict positive definiteness at every finite set, without relying on finite numerics.

**Exact harmonic sparsity.** The polynomial \((x_1+i x_2)^k\) is homogeneous and harmonic of degree \(k\) in \(\mathbb R^3\), since \((\partial_{x_1}^2+\partial_{x_2}^2)u^k=0\). The polynomial \(x_3(x_1+i x_2)^k\) is likewise homogeneous and harmonic of degree \(k+1\). Therefore the *only* feature vectors in degree \(\ell\ge1\) are \(u^\ell\) and \(v u^{\ell-1}\); their different azimuthal frequencies \(\ell\) and \(\ell-1\) make them orthogonal and nonzero in \(L^2(S^2)\). Uniform convergence permits termwise integration, and positive rank-one coefficients give precisely two strictly positive eigenvalues on this two-dimensional span, with zero operator on its orthogonal complement. At degree zero only the constant feature occurs. The full degree-\(\ell\) harmonic subspace has dimension \(2\ell+1\), so the count through \(L\) is \(1+2L\) versus \((L+1)^2\).

**Real covariance and non-universality.** Write \(K_{\mathrm R}=\operatorname{Re}K\). Its Gram matrix is real symmetric. For every real vector its quadratic form equals that of \(K\), which is strictly positive; for any complex vector use its real and imaginary parts separately. Thus \(K_{\mathrm R}\) is strictly positive definite. The real and imaginary parts of each non-real complex feature form two real harmonic directions. At degree \(\ell\ge2\) there are four such directions; degree one has \(\operatorname{Re}u\), \(\operatorname{Im}u\), and \(v\), and degree zero has the constant. Their total count through \(L\ge1\) is \(4L\), still \(o(L^2)\). Let \(h(x)=3v(x)^2-1\). The nonzero signed measure \(h(x)\,d\sigma(x)\) annihilates **all** feature functions: integration against each nonconstant \(u^k\) or \(v u^k\) vanishes by azimuthal integration; the constant and \(v\) terms vanish by the zero mean and oddness of \(h\). Consequently it annihilates the uniform closure of either kernel's feature span, while its integral against \(h\) is positive. Neither kernel has a \(C(S^2)\)-dense reproducing-kernel space. This is consistent with strictly positive finite Gram matrices because the witness measure is not finitely atomic.

**Two-series obstruction.** If all \(v u^k\) features are removed, the north and south poles, which have the same \(u=0\), have identical feature vectors and give a singular two-point Gram matrix. If all \(u^k\) features are removed, every equatorial point \(v=0\) has zero feature vector and zero self-kernel. Thus neither entire feature series can simply be deleted while retaining strictness in this construction.

## Verification
All claims above are derived analytically for arbitrary finite site sets and all harmonic degrees. The optional numerical program `check.py` checks positive minimal Gram eigenvalues of the complex and real kernels at 84 randomly chosen finite configurations of 2–15 sites (168 eigenvalue diagnostics), and the exact harmonic-count identities for 49 cutoffs (98 checks), totaling 266 finite consistency diagnostics with a fixed seed. It prints the observed minimum eigenvalue. These tests are **not** proofs of strict positivity over infinitely many configurations and are not used in the Vandermonde proof. `check.py` requires Python 3 and NumPy. The rational formula and proof have no external numerical dependencies.

## Relationship to prior work
Guella and Jäger, arXiv:2205.07396 (first public 15 May 2022), characterize strict positive definiteness of spherical harmonic diagonal kernels by annihilation of finite atomic measures (their Theorem 1) and develop asymptotic sufficient criteria (their Theorem 4). The finite-atomic characterization is **acknowledged as prior**; the present claim is the explicit rational two-ladder construction, the exact two-per-degree harmonic occupancy, and its separation of finite-atomic strictness from universality. At the north pole, the two chosen degree-\(\ell\) features vanish for every \(\ell\ge2\), so the normalization requirement that underlies their Theorem 4's near-complete asymptotic criterion cannot establish this example. Buhmann and Jäger, arXiv:2105.02586, give general sufficient conditions for axially symmetric and convolutional kernels, including strong positivity of all azimuthal blocks; their published statements do not supply the present two-ladder sparse closed form. The published-finding corpus semantic comparisons listed in the accompanying review concerned existing isotropic Poisson kernels and spherical harmonic sampling results, not a more general implication of the present result.

## Limitations
This is an explicit **complex-valued non-isotropic** example and its real part, not a characterization of all sparse strict-positive spectral supports and not a claim of optimal two-mode sparsity among arbitrary kernels. The reproducing-kernel spaces are not universal. The algebraic proof is complete, but historical novelty remains a best-knowledge assessment: the 2020 two-sphere non-isotropic kernel paper by Jäger was located in abstract and preprint form but its full original text was not inspected in this comparison. Independent mathematical review has not been performed.
