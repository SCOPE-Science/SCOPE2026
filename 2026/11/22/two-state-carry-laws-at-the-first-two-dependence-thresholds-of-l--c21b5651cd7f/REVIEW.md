# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS

For the exact finite moment statement, expanding all powers gives a zero-frequency digit sum. Modular division produces uniquely determined integer carries. With integer \(p\ge4\) and \(q\ge p-1\), the strict inequality \(p+1<2q\) prevents any carry beyond \(-1,0,1\). Reflection symmetry folds the three-state walk into the asserted two-state transfer, including arbitrary positive nonconstant amplitudes. Positivity of explicit top Fourier coefficients proves strict finite-length ordering. Cayley–Hamilton and the positive-matrix Perron theorem give the uniform-amplitude recurrence and rate; coefficient expansions give the small-amplitude constants. Exact-rational replay of independently convolved Laurent coefficients passed 220 checks. The exhaustive-in-parameter conclusion comes from the proof, not from these finite tests.

## Originality — PASS (best knowledge)

Bonami–Latała–Nayar–Tkocz (2018), Lemma 7, already proves the qualitative failure of independent-model moments for geometric base equal to an even integer moment order; Lemma 11 establishes high-base factorization. These statements do not supply the exact adjacent-base \(2\times2\) transfer for all integer orders at least four, all lengths and varying amplitudes, nor its strict base ordering and sharp Perron exponents. Fraser (2024), Section 3.1, describes Fourier spectra and dimensions of Riesz measures; it does not give the asserted finite-product moment law. Nearby work on optimizing lacunary Fourier polynomials concerns additive sums rather than multiplicative Riesz densities. A general carry-automaton or transfer-operator theorem could subsume the explicit formula and remains the principal publication-priority risk; lack of a discovered exact match is not a uniqueness proof.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The first two base ratios below the independence threshold are analytically different, with distinct exact growth rates and even distinct orders of onset as the modulation amplitude tends to zero. The result upgrades the qualitative dependence mechanism in a motivating published theorem to exact finite-length formulas and the sharp asymptotic rate, including the inhomogeneous amplitude case, without requiring a paper-sized downstream theorem. These are natural quantities for quantifying dependence and Rényi-type moments of finite lacunary density approximants. The examples at fourth moment give explicit, independently checkable irrational growth constants.

## Scientific limits and comparisons

The work does not treat exponents outside the specified integers, shifted phases, smaller bases, or arbitrary weighted sums of Riesz products. Classic general transfer-operator literature may contain a broader formulation. Independent mathematical review has not been performed.
