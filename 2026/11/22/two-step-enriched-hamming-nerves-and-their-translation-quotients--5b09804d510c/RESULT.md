# Two-step enriched Hamming nerves and their translation quotients

## Finding

Let \(d\ge1\), \(q\ge2\) be integers, and write \(G=H(d,q)=K_q^{\square d}\). Give its vertex set \((\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z)^d\) the Hamming metric. The inclusive filtered enriched nerve at length two is the simplicial set \(N_2(G)\), whose \(p\)-simplices are ordered tuples \((v_0,\ldots,v_p)\) such that
\[
\sum_{j=1}^{p} d_G(v_{j-1},v_j)\le2.
\]
A simplex is degenerate precisely when it is obtained by inserting a consecutive repetition. Set
\[
 n=q^d,\qquad s=d(q-1),\qquad t=\binom d2(q-1)^2,\qquad
 k=1-s+\binom{d+1}{2}(q-1)^2.
\]
Let \(X=|N_2(G)|\). Coordinatewise addition makes \(T=(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z)^d\) act on the simplicial set. All assertions below hold for every such \(d,q\):
\[
X\simeq\bigvee^{nk-1}S^2,
\qquad H_2(X;\mathbb Q)\cong\mathbb Q[T]^{k-1}\oplus I_{\mathbb Q}(T).
\]
The actual geometric action on \(X\) is free. Thus \(Y=X/T\) is a connected two-dimensional CW complex with
\[
\pi_1(Y)\cong T,\quad H_1(Y;\mathbb Z)\cong(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z)^d,\quad H_2(Y;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{k-1},
\]
with \(H_0(Y;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z\) and no other homology. A nonidentity translation has trace \(-1\) on \(H_2(X;\mathbb Q)\); the identity has trace \(nk-1\).

## Assumptions and scope

The construction is the *ordered simplicial-set* enriched nerve of a finite, symmetric graph metric. In particular the oriented edges \((u,v)\) and \((v,u)\) are distinct one-cells, and the backtracking tuple \((u,v,u)\) is a nondegenerate two-cell. It is **not** the unordered diameter-threshold Vietoris–Rips complex. The cyclic group \(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z\) is used only to label the alphabet and define its regular translations; \(q\) need not be prime or a prime power. All homology with unspecified coefficients is singular integral homology of the geometric realization. The quotient is a geometric quotient of the complete realization, not an invariant subcomplex or an invariant-chain approximation.

## Proof

**1. A graph presentation for the two-step enriched nerve.** For any finite connected simple graph \(G\), all nondegenerate simplices of \(N_2(G)\) are in dimensions zero, one, or two: any nondegenerate successive step costs at least one. The one-cells are the ordered pairs of distinct vertices at distance one or two; the two-cells are precisely the ordered length-two walks \((u,v,w)\) with \(u\sim v\sim w\), including \(u=w\). The alternating boundary is
\[
\partial(u,v,w)=(v,w)-(u,w)+(u,v),
\]
where \((u,u)\) is a degenerate face and contributes zero. The two-cell \((u,v,u)\) makes the reversed edges \((u,v)\) and \((v,u)\) homotopy inverses. If \(d_G(u,w)=2\), its intermediate-vertex two-cell identifies the direct one-cell \((u,w)\) with \((u,v)(v,w)\). Each graph triangle is filled by the two-cell for its successive two-step walk. If \(u,w\) have two distinct common neighbors \(v,v'\), the two different identifications of \((u,w)\) equate the two length-two paths and fill the corresponding four-cycle. Conversely, the listed two-cell relations follow from graph backtracks, triangles and four-cycles. By cellular van Kampen, \(\pi_1(|N_2(G)|)\) is therefore the edge-path fundamental group of \(G\), divided by the normal closure of its triangle and four-cycle loops. This is a presentation, not a claim that all graphs become simply connected.

**2. Hamming loops are generated by the specified small cycles.** Each graph edge in \(H(d,q)\) changes exactly one coordinate. Consecutive changes in different coordinates can be interchanged by traversing the evident coordinate square. Repeating this local interchange rearranges the coordinate changes in any closed edge walk into contiguous blocks with the same coordinate. Because the complete walk closes, the net path of each coordinate block is a closed walk in its complete one-coordinate factor \(K_q\). That walk contracts by triangle fillings (and backtrack cancellations). It follows that \(X\) is simply connected, even for \(q=2\), where there are no distinct-vertex triangles.

**3. Cell counts and the integral homotopy type.** Each vertex has \(s=d(q-1)\) distance-one neighbors and \(t=\binom d2(q-1)^2\) distance-two neighbors. The nondegenerate simplex counts are
\[
 c_0=n,\qquad c_1=n(s+t),\qquad c_2=ns^2,
\]
because the middle vertex of any two-cell can be chosen arbitrarily and its two neighbors independently. Hence
\[
\chi(X)=n(1-s-t+s^2)=nk.
\]
As \(X\) is a connected simply connected finite two-dimensional CW complex, \(H_1(X;\mathbb Z)=0\); its \(H_2\) is a subgroup of the free cellular \(C_2\), so it has no torsion, and its rank is \(\chi(X)-1=nk-1\). The degree-two Hurewicz theorem represents a free integral basis by maps of two-spheres. Their wedge induces an isomorphism on all integral homology groups and, by the simply connected Whitehead theorem, is a homotopy equivalence. This proves the asserted sphere wedge without extrapolation from finite examples.

**4. Freeness and quotient fundamental group.** An element of \(T\) acts by simultaneously translating every vertex of an ordered tuple. The nonidentity elements move every vertex. They therefore stabilize no nondegenerate simplex (the first vertex would have to be fixed). Every point of the geometric realization belongs to the interior of a unique nondegenerate simplex, so no nonidentity element fixes a geometric point. Thus \(X\to Y=X/T\) is a regular covering with deck group \(T\). Since \(X\) is simply connected, \(\pi_1(Y)\cong T\). The quotient has \(c_i/n\) cells in degree \(i\), giving \(\chi(Y)=k\). Its first homology is the abelianization of \(T\), namely \(T\) itself, and is finite. Its second homology is free because it is the kernel of the cellular boundary \(C_2(Y;\mathbb Z)\to C_1(Y;\mathbb Z)\). Hence Euler characteristic determines \(\operatorname{rank}H_2(Y;\mathbb Z)=k-1\); there is no homology above dimension two.

**5. Complete rational translation character.** Over the rationals, only \(H_0(X)\cong\mathbb Q\) and \(H_2(X)\cong\mathbb Q^{nk-1}\) survive. Any nonidentity translation permutes each finite set of nondegenerate cells without fixed cells, so its cellular alternating Lefschetz number is zero. The identity acts trivially on \(H_0\); therefore every nonidentity translation has trace \(-1\) on \(H_2\), and the identity trace is \(nk-1\). This character is precisely \(k\) copies of the regular character less the trivial character. Maschke semisimplicity over \(\mathbb Q\) gives the displayed representation \(\mathbb Q[T]^{k-1}\oplus I_{\mathbb Q}(T)\).

## Verification

The included `check_nerve.py` constructs every nondegenerate simplex and its signed boundary for the six profiles \((d,q)=(1,2),(1,3),(2,2),(2,3),(3,2),(3,3)\). It checks \(\partial_1\partial_2=0\) and reduces the degree-two boundary independently modulo the primes \(2,3,5,1000003\). All tested ranks agree and match the formula; the five-cycle is an explicit nonsimply-connected control. The separately implemented `check_quotients.py` constructs the translation-orbit cellular boundary using integral coefficients and computes exact Smith normal forms for seven profiles, including \((1,4)\). Every test gives precisely \(d\) invariant factors equal to \(q\) and all other nonzero invariant factors equal to one, confirming the formula for quotient first and second homology in those finite profiles. The printed outputs are included as separate text files. These calculations check the conventions and sample cases; the uniform theorem follows from the proof above.

## Relationship to prior work

Otter introduced the length-filtered enriched nerve as the simplicial-set foundation of blurred magnitude homology, and Kuribayashi studied its homotopy-level interleavings and magnitude-path spectral sequence. Their results establish the framework, not the above two-parameter integral quotient calculation. Pre-existing Hamming-graph Vietoris–Rips results, including full group actions, concern unordered simplicial complexes constrained by pairwise diameter rather than the sum of successive ordered distances. Related computations for complete bipartite two-step nerves concern a different graph family or different quotient groups; the one-dimensional, two-letter specialization is a known boundary phenomenon rather than the source of novelty. The graph presentation in Step 1 uses familiar triangle/square path relations; the substantive contribution is the uniform identification for Hamming products and the resulting exact canonical quotient homology and rational character.

## Limitations

The classification is only at inclusive length two, for uniform-alphabet Hamming graphs, and for their full coordinate-translation group. It does not describe the persistence maps at other length thresholds, the integral \(\mathbb Z[T]\)-lattice of second homology, the quotient's full homotopy type, or its cup-product structure. Although targeted source comparisons found no covering statement, priority cannot be guaranteed against unindexed or unexamined publications. The mathematical verification is a self-review; no independent audit has been performed.
