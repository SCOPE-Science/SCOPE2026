# Two-torsion in the rotation quotient of octagonal noncrossing matchings

## Finding
For the complete noncrossing-matching complex \(K\) of the convex octagon (including boundary edges), the ordinary geometric orbit spaces under rotations and full dihedral symmetries satisfy
\[
|K|/C_8\simeq M(\mathbb Z/2,1)\vee S^2,
\qquad |K|/D_8\simeq *.
\]
Here the Moore space \(M(\mathbb Z/2,1)\) is obtained from a circle by attaching one two-cell with degree-two map. Thus the rotation quotient has \(\pi_1\cong\mathbb Z/2\), \(H_1\cong\mathbb Z/2\), and \(H_2\cong\mathbb Z\), while the dihedral quotient is contractible.

## Assumptions and scope
Label the corners of a strictly convex octagon by \(0,\ldots,7\) in cyclic order. Every unordered pair of corners is an eligible edge, including the eight boundary sides. A face of \(K\) is a finite set of eligible edges such that no two meet at a corner and no two cross in their relative interiors. Thus \(K\) is the noncrossing matching complex denoted \(NXM_8\) in Jonsson's terminology. The cyclic group is generated by \(i\mapsto i+1\pmod8\); the dihedral group also contains \(i\mapsto-i\pmod8\). All topological quotients are quotients of the **geometric realization** by the indicated finite group, with stabilizers retained. They are not naive simplicial orbit complexes.

## Proof
A finite group action on the face poset induces an action without inversions on its order complex (the barycentric subdivision), because vertices in a chain have different cardinalities. The orbit cells of strict chains therefore form a regular CW structure for the genuine geometric quotient. Enumerating all nonempty noncrossing matching faces gives face numbers \((28,140,140,14)\) in dimensions zero through three. Orbit enumeration of strict chains gives cell counts \((45,169,168,42)\) for the rotation quotient and \((35,112,105,27)\) for the dihedral quotient.

For the full dihedral action, the explicit free-face procedure in `verify_nxm8.py` makes \(139\) successive elementary collapses. At every step it checks that the proposed free face has a unique remaining incident coface and that this coface has no surviving higher coface. Exactly one vertex remains. Therefore the geometric dihedral quotient is contractible; no inference is made merely from vanishing homology.

For rotations, the same procedure makes \(149\) elementary collapses, leaving a two-dimensional regular CW complex with \((20,62,44)\) cells in dimensions zero, one, two. Its one-skeleton is connected. Contracting the explicitly constructed nineteen-edge spanning tree (a homotopy equivalence) leaves one vertex, \(43\) loop edges and \(44\) two-cells. The code reconstructs the attaching word of every two-cell from the oriented barycentric triangles. It then carries out \(42\) elementary generator–relator eliminations: a loop generator is eliminated only when it occurs exactly once with exponent \(+1\) or \(-1\) in the boundary word of a chosen two-cell. Cyclic conjugation and cancellation of attaching words are homotopies; eliminating such a loop and two-cell is a homotopy equivalence of two-dimensional CW complexes, with substitution in every remaining attaching word. The final presentation has **one** loop and **two** two-cells, with attaching words \(a^{-2}\) and the empty word, respectively. Their CW complex is \(M(\mathbb Z/2,1)\vee S^2\). No higher-dimensional cells survive, so this proves the asserted homotopy equivalence, not just a computation of fundamental group or homology.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify_nxm8.py` in the directory containing the embedded standalone file. It uses the Python standard library only, enumerates all faces and strict chains, builds the actual group-orbit cell structure, checks all free-face decisions, calculates the spanning tree and the two-cell attaching words, and independently replays each generator elimination. It asserts the displayed cell counts and the final words, and prints `VERIFY_OK`. As boundary controls the code also computes the rotational/dihedral quotient homotopy types for polygon sizes \(5\), \(6\), and \(7\): respectively \((S^1,*)\), \((S^1,*)\), and \((\bigvee^3 S^1,S^1)\). For at most four corners the complex is at most one-dimensional, so a geometric quotient cannot have integral first-homology torsion. Consequently the octagon is the first size exhibiting the rotational torsion described here.

## Relationship to prior work
Jonsson's *Simplicial Complexes of Graphs*, Section 16.3, treats precisely the underlying complexes \(NXM_n\) and proves semi-nonevasiveness, homological depth bounds and Euler-characteristic formulas (Theorems 16.7–16.12); it does not state this orbit-space decomposition. Armstrong, Rhoades and Williams (2013), Section 5, discuss rotation on **perfect** noncrossing matchings viewed as a finite set, not geometric quotients of the simplicial complex of all partial noncrossing matchings. McConville–Mühle (2022) study a different noncrossing matching complex on two ordered alphabets with unmatched-letter symbols; its objects and group action do not identify with this octagonal chord complex. Database matches concerning graph matching complexes, cyclic nonmatching complexes, and chessboard complexes involve different simplicial objects and do not imply either octagonal quotient result. These comparisons establish a bounded literature assessment, not a guarantee of first publication.

## Limitations
The classification is specific to the full one-step rotation and full dihedral group on an eight-corner polygon; it does not address every subgroup or larger polygons. Its correctness depends on exact finite orbit enumeration and the explicit elementary homotopy moves reproduced by the attached verifier; no higher-dimensional or infinite-family inference is drawn. Publication priority remains vulnerable to unindexed equivalents or different formulations of polygonal matching quotients. No independent audit has been performed.
