# Homotopy type of the rotation quotient of octagonal noncrossing matchings

## Finding

Let \(K\) be the simplicial complex of all noncrossing partial matchings on the eight cyclically ordered vertices of a strictly convex octagon, including its boundary sides as possible matching edges. For the action of the cyclic group \(C_8\) generated by advancing one vertex in the cyclic order, the **ordinary geometric orbit space** satisfies
\[
 |K|/C_8\simeq (S^1\cup_{\deg 2}e^2)\vee S^2
 = M(\mathbb Z/2,1)\vee S^2.
\]
The contribution is the homotopy decomposition, not the previously computed homology or fundamental group. Contractibility of the full dihedral quotient is also known [1].

## Definitions and scope

Label the vertices by \(0,\ldots,7\) in cyclic order. The vertices of \(K\) are all unordered pairs of distinct labels. A simplex is a set of such chords with disjoint endpoints and disjoint relative interiors. The group acts by permutations of the labels, so the action is defined even when the Euclidean realization of the polygon is not regular. The quotient is taken after geometric realization; it is not a homotopy quotient or a complex on chord orbits.

## Proof

Enumerating the matching faces by excluding shared endpoints and alternating endpoint pairs gives face numbers \((28,140,140,14)\). The simplices of the barycentric subdivision are strict chains of nonempty faces. Since the group preserves face sizes, each size occurs at most once in a given chain. If two points of a closed chain simplex have the same image under the quotient map, their nonzero barycentric coordinates must have supports with the same size sequence. The supports coincide and the group element must fix every supporting vertex, so the two points coincide. Consequently the flag orbits are the cells of a regular CW structure on the geometric quotient; the boundary facets of a flag are obtained by deleting one chain member. Enumerating the orbits gives \((45,169,168,42)\) cells.

The exact collapse algorithm in `verify_nxm8.py` makes 149 elementary collapses, each time deleting a facet and its unique remaining immediate coface, after checking that the coface is maximal. In a regular cell structure of this form, the pair can be removed by an elementary deformation across the closed simplex representing the coface. The remaining complex has \(20\) vertices, \(62\) edges and \(44\) triangles, with no higher cells. Its one-skeleton is connected. Contracting a spanning tree of 19 edges, a homotopy equivalence, yields a single vertex, 43 loops and 44 two-cells. Each triangular boundary gives a cyclic word in the oriented loop edges by walking its boundary and discarding tree edges.

In the two-complex, free reduction and cyclic conjugation of a word correspond to homotopies of the attaching map. If a loop edge occurs once with exponent \(\pm1\) in a chosen word, the corresponding disk homotopes that edge, relative to its endpoints, to the path given by the rest of the word (inverting as dictated by orientation). Replace each occurrence of that edge in the other attaching words by the resulting path. This changes those attaching maps only by homotopy in the complex containing the chosen disk. The edge and chosen disk can now be removed: before the other disks are attached their union with the remaining graph deformation retracts onto that graph, by pushing across the disk from its single traversal of the edge. No higher cells have to be tracked in these moves. The verifier makes 42 such eliminations and finds one loop \(a\) and two attaching words, \(a^{-2}\) and the empty word. Thus one disk attaches by a map of degree two up to orientation and the other has constant attaching map. This is the asserted wedge.

## Reproduction

The standard-library program `verify_nxm8.py` rebuilds the face and orbit lists, verifies the free-face steps, and computes the cyclic attaching words. Run `python3 verify_nxm8.py`; the octagonal rotational line displays the 149 collapses, the residual cell counts and the two final words, and the run ends with `VERIFY_OK`. The program also computes small polygon controls and the known octagonal dihedral quotient; those outputs are not additional novelty claims.

## Prior work and limitations

[1] studies the **same** octagonal chord complex and the same ordinary geometric quotients. It already establishes \(\pi_1(|K|/C_8)\cong\mathbb Z/2\), integral \(H_1\cong\mathbb Z/2\) and \(H_2\cong\mathbb Z\), the nonzero mod-two cup square, and contractibility of \(|K|/D_8\). Its integral reduction is a chain calculation; its rotation presentation computes the fundamental group using the two-skeleton of a complex with 42 three-cells. Neither step identifies the homotopy type of the whole rotational quotient. Here the actual elementary collapses remove all the three-cells before the attaching maps are simplified. This yields a homotopy model in which the spherical summand is realized, rather than just an abstract free homology class.

Jonsson [2, §16.3] studies the underlying noncrossing matching complexes. The rotation studied in [3, §5.1] is an action on the finite set of *perfect* noncrossing matchings. The shuffle-word complex in [4, §3] uses two ordered alphabets and a different face condition. The result here is specific to the ordinary rotational quotient for the octagon, not a theorem about larger polygons or other subgroups. Comparison with available sources does not settle publication priority over an unlocated equivalent result.
