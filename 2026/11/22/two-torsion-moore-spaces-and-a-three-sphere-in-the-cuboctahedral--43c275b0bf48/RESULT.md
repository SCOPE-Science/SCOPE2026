# Two torsion Moore spaces and a three-sphere in the cuboctahedral matching quotient

## Finding
Let \(C=L(Q_3)\) be the cuboctahedral graph, and \(K=\operatorname{Match}(C)\) its ordinary matching complex. The geometric orbit space under the full 48-element automorphism group of \(C\) has integral homotopy type \[|K|/\operatorname{Aut}(C)\simeq M(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z,2)\vee M(\mathbb Z/6\mathbb Z,2)\vee S^3.\] Here \(M(A,2)\) denotes a Moore space with sole nonzero reduced integral homology \(A\) in degree two. In particular, \(\pi_1=0\), \(\widetilde H_2\cong\mathbb Z/2\oplus\mathbb Z/6\), \(\widetilde H_3\cong\mathbb Z\), and other reduced homology is zero.

## Assumptions and scope
Write cube vertices as three-bit integers \(0,\ldots,7\), adjacent when their exclusive-or has a single nonzero bit. Enumerate the twelve cube edges lexicographically. The cuboctahedral graph \(C=L(Q_3)\) has those twelve cube edges as vertices and twenty-four edges, themselves enumerated lexicographically; two vertices of \(C\) are adjacent exactly when their corresponding cube edges intersect. The matching complex \(K\) has a \(q\)-simplex for each collection of \(q+1\) pairwise vertex-disjoint edges of \(C\). This concerns **all matchings**, not only perfect matchings or the matching complex of the cube. Its full graph automorphism group has 48 elements, induced by the bit flips and coordinate permutations of the cube. The normal form \(M(\mathbb Z/m,2)=S^2\cup_{\deg m}e^3\) fixes our Moore-space convention.

## Proof
**Quotient cell construction.** Enumerate all \(2648\) nonempty matchings. The counts by cardinality one through six are \((24,204,744,1116,528,32)\). Take the order complex of the nonempty face poset (barycentric subdivision), quotient its strict flags by the 48 coordinate-permutation/bit-flip automorphisms, and orient each representative flag by increasing face size. Each flag orbit is a genuine cell and its boundary is the alternating sum of deleted-flag orbits. Because group elements preserve face sizes, stabilizers cannot permute the vertices within a strict flag. In particular, the quotient has a regular simplicial cell structure; there are no inverted simplices. The cell counts in dimensions zero through five are \((73,843,2983,4253,2520,480)\).

**Exact homology.** Directly check the boundary-incidence matrices and their chain-complex identities. An exhaustive sequence of 5117 free-face collapses gives a smaller cellular chain complex with cell counts \((38,229,421,230,0,0)\); every deletion removes a cell and its unique available higher-dimensional coface, with no coface above that dimension. Over \(\mathbb F_{101}\), the first three boundary ranks on that reduced complex are \(37,192,229\). The zero boundary composition and the upper bound \(\operatorname{rank}(\partial_2)\le229-37=192\) show that the middle rank is exactly 192 over \(\mathbb Q\). The integral third boundary (421 rows, 230 columns) undergoes 227 exact unimodular unit pivots, leaving a 194-by-3 integer matrix. Its entries have gcd 2; all two-by-two minors have gcd 12, and the three remaining columns have rank two. The resulting full Smith invariant factors of the third boundary are 227 units, 2, 6, and one zero column. Consequently its rank is 229, and its cokernel has torsion \(\mathbb Z/2\oplus\mathbb Z/6\). Since \(\operatorname{im}\partial_2\) is free abelian, the torsion of this cokernel agrees with that of \(H_2\). Dimension and rank counting give \(\widetilde H_2\cong\mathbb Z/2\oplus\mathbb Z/6\), \(\widetilde H_3\cong\mathbb Z\), \(H_1\) finite and all higher reduced homology zero.

**Simple connectivity.** The original \(K\) has 24 vertices, 204 edges and 744 triangles. A spanning-tree presentation of its fundamental group has 181 generators; exact Tietze substitutions eliminate all 181, leaving only trivial relators. By Armstrong's orbit-space theorem, the fundamental group of \(|K|/G\) is \(G\) modulo the normal subgroup generated by its elements having fixed points. Three independent bit flips and the two adjacent coordinate transpositions generate \(G\), and each preserves at least one nonempty matching, hence fixes the corresponding barycenter of \(|K|\). Therefore \(\pi_1(|K|/G)=0\), so the possible finite \(H_1\) is zero.

**Homotopy type.** The quotient is a simply connected finite three-dimensional CW complex after the collapses. Hurewicz identifies its \(\pi_2\) with \(H_2\), providing maps from the two Moore spaces realizing the two torsion generators. The Whitehead exact sequence for a simply connected three-dimensional CW complex makes \(\pi_3\to H_3\) surjective, supplying a map from \(S^3\) realizing the free third-homology generator. Their wedge map induces an isomorphism on every integral homology group; the homological Whitehead theorem for simply connected CW complexes makes it a homotopy equivalence. This completes the classification.

## Relationship to prior work
The classical theory of matching complexes (Björner–Lovász–Vrećica–Živaljević) and equivariant topology of independence complexes (Engström) motivate the object. The exactly matching previously published integral nonequivariant calculation establishes \(K\simeq\bigvee^{57}S^3\), but it does **not** classify the symmetry quotient. The full-octahedral representation calculation states that the orbit has rational homology \(\widetilde H_3(-;\mathbb Q)=\mathbb Q\) and otherwise rationally acyclic, explicitly declining an integral or homotopy classification. The present theorem identifies two invisible degree-two torsion summands and upgrades the rational sphere statement to a complete integral homotopy classification. No priority claim is made beyond the checked sources.

## Limitations
The statement covers the ordinary geometric quotient by the entire 48-element automorphism group, not the rotational subgroup, all subgroups, or arbitrary line graphs. It is a computationally reproducible finite classification, not an assertion of a uniform theorem for graph families. The checker uses exact arithmetic and exhaustive enumeration; proof of the global homotopy equivalence additionally uses standard orbit-space, Hurewicz and Whitehead theorems. Some possible additional published sources were not available in full, so an equivalent unindexed theorem is not logically excluded. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
