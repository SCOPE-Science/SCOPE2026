# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The central deterministic covering lemma is proved using the extreme right and left endpoints and two independently witnessed types of spanning intervals. The union bound yields \(2nb^2+2e^{-nb}\), valid for \(b<1/2\), and the chosen \(b=2\log n/n\) is admissible for \(n\ge9\). The universal-vertex and complete-graph probabilities are established classical evidence and direct rank-matching enumeration, respectively. The diameter equivalence follows from earliest finishing and latest starting intervals. The standalone verifier exhausts every pairing up to seven intervals, without treating enumeration as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
The known exact \(2/3\) universal-vertex identity is explicitly credited to Justicz–Scheinerman–Winkler. Diaconis–Holmes–Janson discusses random interval graphs and restates that identity; its accessible full text does not present a domination-number or diameter theorem. Searches for the two-vertex connected/total domination law, the two/three diameter distribution, and their possible equivalent formulations produced no directly covering result. The original 1990 primary full text was inaccessible; exact historic priority is a residual risk, not a claim of exhaustive novelty.

## Value — PASS
The ordinary, total and connected domination numbers are natural global graph invariants; the result shows that two overlapping sampled vertices suffice with an explicit finite-size error, despite large disjoint interval packings. The full two-point diameter limit combines a deterministic interval-graph equivalence and a high-probability geometric coverage argument, giving a precise structural law not supplied by universal-vertex probability alone.

## Limits
The error bound may not be optimal; dependence on identical atomless endpoint laws is essential to the quantitative calculation. Neither independent audit nor independent mathematical validation has been performed.
