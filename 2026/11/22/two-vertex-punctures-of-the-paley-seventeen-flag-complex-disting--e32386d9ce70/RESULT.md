# Two-vertex punctures of the Paley-seventeen flag complex distinguish adjacent from nonadjacent pairs

## Finding
Let \(P_{17}\) denote the graph on \(\mathbb F_{17}\) with \(x\sim y\) exactly when \(y-x\) is a nonzero square, and put \(K=\operatorname{Cl}(P_{17})\). For every unordered pair of distinct vertices \(\{u,v\}\), consider the induced deletion complex \(D_{u,v}=K[\mathbb F_{17}\setminus\{u,v\}]\). Then
\[
D_{u,v}\simeq
\begin{cases}
\bigvee^{8}S^{2},&u\sim v,\\
\bigvee^{6}S^{2},&u\not\sim v.
\end{cases}
\]
The natural inclusion \(D_{u,v}\hookrightarrow K\) injects second **integral** homology as a primitive direct summand. The respective cokernel ranks are \(8\) and \(10\). Thus two adjacent deleted vertices leave exactly two more independent two-dimensional cycles than two nonadjacent deleted vertices. Every other reduced integral homology group of either punctured complex is zero.

## Assumptions and scope
The vertices are precisely the seventeen residues \(0,\ldots,16\); the quadratic residue set is \(Q=\{1,2,4,8,9,13,15,16\}\). Graphs are simple, and the clique complex includes every complete vertex subset. The homotopy statements are nonequivariant and integral, not claims about quotient spaces. Deletions retain all edges and triangles between surviving vertices; they do **not** recompute a distance metric. The assertion concerns every unordered pair of distinct vertices, divided into the edge and nonedge orbits of the natural affine action. The pre-existing ambient result \(K\simeq\bigvee^{16}S^2\) is included only for comparison; the package also checks it directly.

## Proof
**1. The graph and orbit reduction.** The nonzero squares are exactly \(Q\). Its multiplicative group acts on the additive group by \(x\mapsto ax+b\), with \(a\in Q\), \(b\in\mathbb F_{17}\). These \(136\) maps preserve adjacency. If the difference \(v-u\) belongs to \(Q\), translation followed by multiplication with its inverse sends \((u,v)\) to \((0,1)\). If the difference is outside \(Q\), multiplication with \(3/(v-u)\) (a quotient of two nonsquares, hence a square) sends it to \((0,3)\). Since \(3\notin Q\), the two representatives exhaust all \(136\) unordered pairs, \(68\) in each class.

**2. Finite Morse certificates of both punctured complexes.** Every clique has size at most three; this is verified by testing all four-subsets directly. The adjacency representative \(D_{0,1}\) has face vector \((15,53,47)\); the nonadjacency representative \(D_{0,3}\) has \((15,52,44)\). The file `verify_paley17_pair.py` contains an explicit list of fourteen spanning-tree edges for each representative. From each tree, match every nonroot vertex with its unique parent tree edge. Initialize all tree edges as resolved; then repeatedly choose the lexicographically least triangle containing precisely one unresolved edge, match that triangle to the edge and mark the edge resolved. In the adjacent case this produces \(14+39=53\) matched pairs and leaves one critical vertex and eight critical triangles. In the nonadjacent case it gives \(14+38=52\) matched pairs and leaves one vertex and six critical triangles. Each pair is a codimension-one incidence; no face is reused. Exhaustively orienting every Hasse cover upward on matched incidences and downward otherwise gives directed acyclic graphs on all \(115\) and \(111\) nonempty faces, with \(247\) and \(236\) checked cover incidences, respectively. The verifier implements a complete topological-sort check of acyclicity, rather than sampling matchings. Acyclic discrete-Morse theory therefore provides a CW model with exactly one zero-cell and respectively eight or six two-cells, and no one-cells or higher cells. The two-cell maps are necessarily constant into the zero-skeleton, proving both wedge-of-spheres homotopy equivalences over the integers.

**3. Primitive integral inclusions.** The single-vertex deletion \(D_0=K[\mathbb F_{17}\setminus\{0\}]\) has a separately checked complete acyclic Morse matching with one vertex and eleven two-dimensional critical cells, so \(D_0\simeq\bigvee^{11}S^2\). The same construction on the full complex verifies the known \(K\simeq\bigvee^{16}S^2\). Their face vectors are \((16,60,56)\) and \((17,68,68)\), with \(60\) and \(68\) checked pairs. The link of a second deleted vertex in the once-punctured complex is a connected graph: it has \((7,9)\) vertices and edges for the adjacent case, or \((8,12)\) for the nonadjacent case. Thus their first integral homology ranks are respectively \(3\) and \(5\), and all other reduced homology is zero. For any vertex \(w\) of a simplicial complex \(A\), the pair \((A,A-w)\) has relative homology \(H_i(A,A-w;\mathbb Z)\cong\widetilde H_{i-1}(\operatorname{lk}_A(w);\mathbb Z)\). Applying the integral long exact sequence first to the second deletion and then to the first, and using the vanishing first homology of all deletion complexes established above, gives exact sequences
\[
0\to\mathbb Z^8\to\mathbb Z^{11}\to\mathbb Z^3\to0,
\qquad
0\to\mathbb Z^6\to\mathbb Z^{11}\to\mathbb Z^5\to0,
\]
and then \(0\to\mathbb Z^{11}\to\mathbb Z^{16}\to\mathbb Z^5\to0\). All cokernels are free abelian, so these sequences split as abelian groups. Composing the injections gives the claimed primitive, split integral embeddings with cokernel ranks \(8\) and \(10\). The inclusion is the actual induced simplicial inclusion, not just an abstract isomorphism of ranks.

## Verification
Using only Python 3 and its standard library, execute `python3 verify_paley17_pair.py` and compare its one-line, sorted-key JSON output byte-for-byte with `expected_output.json`. The verifier independently rebuilds the graph and each simplicial face set; checks the two explicit spanning trees, the deterministic full matchings, all face incidences and directed-Hasse acyclicity; verifies both link graph ranks; and exhaustively checks all \(136\) affine maps and the mapping of every unordered vertex pair into one of the two canonical orbits. It also independently checks the one-point and full-complex matchings required for the inclusion argument. The recorded expected output is included, but only a fresh replay constitutes a verification check. The proof uses the finite exact certificate plus standard discrete-Morse and relative homology theorems; no finite experimental sequence is extrapolated to a larger graph.

## Relationship to prior work
Rinaldo and Romeo (first public version July 16, 2018) already observed that the Paley-seventeen independence complex is vertex decomposable. By self-complementarity, the unpunctured clique complex has the corresponding property; this is not a new assertion. Cioabă, Guo, Ji and Mim (August 2025) study spectra and first cohomology of strongly regular graph clique complexes, while Cioabă and Mim (June 25, 2026) develop general first-homology vanishing results. Previously published direct computations determine the unpunctured seventeen-vertex clique complex and its affine homology character. None of these inspected results states the **complete two-vertex induced-deletion homotopy profile**, distinguished by adjacency, or its integral inclusion ranks. In particular, vertex-decomposability of the full complex and knowledge of its Euler characteristic do not by themselves establish that *both* two-point deletion orbit types have vanishing fundamental group and free homology in one degree. The exact deletion certificates supply this extra input. Clique and independence descriptions are identified under the graph's self-complementarity; they are not treated as distinct discoveries.

## Limitations
This is an exact classification for two deleted vertices of the seventeen-point Paley graph. It does not determine larger deletion profiles, equivariant homotopy types, group-module splittings or higher Paley orders. Published vertex-decomposability and ambient homotopy results are not reclaimed. Literature priority cannot be certified exhaustive; no independent mathematical validation has been carried out.
