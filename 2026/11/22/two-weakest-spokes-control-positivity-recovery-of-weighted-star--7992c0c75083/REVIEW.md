# Scientific review: weighted-star biharmonic resolvent positivity

## Correctness — PASS
The single theorem is about \((I+hL^2)^{-1}\) for every positive weighting of a finite star with at least two leaves. Symmetric spectral calculus converts the resolvent into the real part of \((I+i\sqrt h L)^{-1}\). A direct Schur complement gives the leaf-to-leaf entry phases, and positivity reduces to whether the least-conductance pair has crossed the angle \(\pi/2\). The explicit rational scalar \(F_w\) is strictly decreasing because all differences \((w_k-w_1)(w_k-w_2)\) are nonnegative. It has opposite infinite-end signs, giving exactly one positive root. The bounds and all their equality/limit statements follow from elementary pointwise estimates. The identity at \(h=0\) is handled separately. A dependency-free numerical and exact-rational checker adds 387 finite tests; it is corroboration, not an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual historical risk
Gregorio–Mugnolo (2017, arXiv:1712.07370), in the inspected discrete-graph Sections 2 and 6 and the surrounding full-text search, treat the continuous-time semigroup and eventual positivity and mention minimal-degree semigroup transitions. Their statements do not establish a weighted-star backward-Euler resolvent cutoff or identify the two least-conductive leaves as its controlling pair. Fontan–Altafini (2021, arXiv:2109.14587) study signed Laplacian pseudoinverses and eventual exponential positivity, not the rational biharmonic map at arbitrary positive steps. Relevant published graph-step classifications for Crank–Nicolson, Gauss–Legendre, and cycles have different scalar propagators or graph families. Their parameterized conclusions do not imply this root formula. Searches in those literatures and in accessible mathematical-result records found no equivalent or dominating result. No search establishes absolute historical uniqueness; specialist or unindexed treatments may exist.

## Value — PASS
The existence of a positive recovery horizon for a fixed finite connected graph follows qualitatively from its simple zero Laplacian eigenvalue, but that fact does not locate the first nonnegative step, nor exclude later negative windows. This result gives a complete sharp classification for every positively weighted star, reduces a matrix-sign problem to the two weakest leaves and a monotone one-dimensional equation, and determines exact attained and limiting bounds. The large-conductance contrast has a sharp factor of \(m-1\) at fixed two weakest spokes, a robustness feature not implied by unweighted examples or general eventual-positivity theory. The same threshold exactly controls whether the smoothing map is an \(\ell^\infty\)-contraction.

## Limits
The result is not a general weighted-tree theorem and does not describe the complete operator-norm transient profile, or individual numbers of sign changes for noncontrolling pairs. Upper extremality for three or more leaves is limiting, not attained by finite conductances. The historical-priority comparison is best-knowledge, not a guarantee of publication novelty.
