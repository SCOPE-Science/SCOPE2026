# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

The domain is every integer \(m\ge2\), all positive real weights \(\alpha,\beta\), and the complex Fermat–Ceva blow-up. The two Bézout inequalities \(3A+mB\le3md\) and \(2B\le3d\), plus exact treatment of the two excluded line families, prove the full weighted infimum with no asymptotic assumption. Three regimes of weights give the claimed sharp bound and strict off-wall minimizer list. On the wall, the degree-\(2m\) forms have no base points after a single blow-up at each of the \(m^2+3\) marked points; their local leading forms are checked explicitly. The general fiber is smooth and geometrically connected because exceptional curves over the grid are sections, and adjunction gives genus \((m-1)(m-2)/2\). The standalone exact checker passed for all \(m=2,\ldots,40\); it is corroboration, not the infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, with priority risk

The closest primary statements in Pokora (arXiv:1711.09364, Example 3.4), Janasz–Pokora (arXiv:2004.04043, Example 1.3), and Dumnicki et al. (arXiv:2611.03797, introduction and Proposition 2.1) treat **equal weights**, particularly the known value \(1/(m+1)\). They do not state the full two-weight nef threshold, the switch to coordinate sides at \(\beta=m\alpha\), or the uniqueness of volume saturation at that ratio. The old Ceva/multinet framework concerns pencils and is credited rather than claimed as new. Searches for weighted Fermat–Ceva Seshadri constants and associated nef isotropic walls found no equivalent result, but absence of indexed results is not a uniqueness proof. The main residual risk is an unindexed geometric treatment of this exact weighted nef slice.

## Mathematical value — PASS

This is an exact two-parameter local-positivity classification for an infinite sequence of blow-ups with rapidly growing Picard number, not a re-evaluation of the known equal-weight constant. The wall distinguishes two geometrically different families of optimal curves; it is the **only** weight ratio attaining the numerical volume bound, where the nef boundary is isotropic and yields a concrete genus-\((m-1)(m-2)/2\) fibration with \(m^2\) sections. The weighted problem is intrinsic because the singular locus splits into triple and high-multiplicity vertex strata. The full nef cone and the other singular fibers remain unclassified.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
