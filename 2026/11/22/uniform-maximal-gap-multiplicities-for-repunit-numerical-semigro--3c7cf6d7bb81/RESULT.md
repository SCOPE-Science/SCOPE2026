# Uniform maximal-gap multiplicities for repunit numerical semigroups

## Finding

Let \(b,n\ge2\) be integers and put \(R_j=(b^j-1)/(b-1)\). Consider the *ordinary repunit numerical semigroup*
\[
S_{b,n}=\langle R_n,R_{n+1},\ldots,R_{2n-1}\rangle\subseteq\mathbb Z_{\ge0}.
\]
A *gap block* is a maximal interval of consecutive positive integers outside \(S_{b,n}\). If \(H_{b,n}(\ell)\) is the number of such blocks of length \(\ell\), then the entire gap-block-length generating polynomial is
\[
\boxed{\sum_{\ell\ge1} H_{b,n}(\ell)z^\ell
 =b z^{n-1}\left(\sum_{v=0}^{b-1}z^v\right)
 \prod_{j=2}^{n-1}\left(\sum_{v=0}^{b-1}z^{vR_j}\right).}
\]
The empty product for \(n=2\) is one. **Every nonzero coefficient of this polynomial is exactly \(b\).** Equivalently, the possible lengths are precisely the \(b^{n-1}\) distinct integers
\[
\ell=(n-1)+v_1+\sum_{j=2}^{n-1}v_jR_j,
\qquad v_1,\ldots,v_{n-1}\in\{0,\ldots,b-1\},
\]
and **each occurs in exactly \(b\) different maximal gap blocks**. There are \(b^n\) gap blocks in total. The shortest block has length \(n-1\), the unique largest *length* is \(R_n-1\), and that length itself occurs \(b\) times. (There is no assertion that the longest block is unique.)

For illustration, the lengths for \((b,n)=(2,4)\) are \(3,4,6,7,10,11,13,14\), each twice. For \((b,n)=(3,3)\), they are \(2,3,4,6,7,8,10,11,12\), each three times.

## Assumptions and scope

All parameters are integers with \(b,n\ge2\). The numerical semigroup is generated by the \(n\) *consecutive repunits* starting at length \(n\), not by repunits translated by an additive shift and not by the two-generator semigroup alone. Membership, gap blocks, and lengths are in the nonnegative integers; zero belongs to the semigroup. All assertions are exact for arbitrarily large \(b\) and \(n\).

## Proof

**1. Apéry representatives.** Write \(a=R_n\). For each residue \(0\le r<a\), let \(w_r\) be the smallest element of \(S_{b,n}\) congruent to \(r\pmod a\). The explicit Apéry formula proved as Lemma 3.3 in Liu–Xin–Ye–Yin (2023), applied with \(d=1\), \(k=n-1\), and \(a=R_n\), states
\[
w_r=a q_n(r)+r,\qquad
q_n(r)=\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}b^j c_j(r),
\]
where \(r=\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}c_j(r)R_j\) uses the greedy decomposition into denominations \(R_1,\ldots,R_{n-1}\). The source proves optimality of that greedy system (Lemma 3.1) and the Apéry formula under \(a\ge n-2\), which holds here. The identity \(R_{n+j}=b^jR_n+R_j\) verifies the specialization. The Apéry property therefore gives for every integer \(k\ge0\)
\[
ka+r\in S_{b,n}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad k\ge q_n(r).
\]

**2. Exact staircase recursion.** For \(n=2\) the residue interval is \(0\le r\le b\) and \(q_2(r)=br\). For \(n\ge3\), use \(R_n=bR_{n-1}+1\). All \(0\le r<R_n\) have the unique form \(r=tR_{n-1}+u\), with \(0\le t\le b-1\), \(0\le u<R_{n-1}\), *or* the last residue \(r=bR_{n-1}\). Greedy coin change first chooses \(t\) copies of \(R_{n-1}\) and the lower greedy decomposition of \(u\). Thus
\[
q_n(tR_{n-1}+u)=tb^{n-1}+q_{n-1}(u),\qquad
q_n(bR_{n-1})=b^n.
\]
By induction, \(q_{n-1}(0)=0\), \(q_{n-1}(R_{n-1}-1)=b^{n-1}\), and every consecutive difference of \(q_{n-1}\) is either zero or \(b\). At each junction between the \(t\)-blocks, and at the final residue, the differences are zero because the preceding block ends at \((t+1)b^{n-1}\). Consequently \(q_n\) is nondecreasing, \(q_n(0)=0\), \(q_n(a-1)=b^n\), and each positive difference is precisely \(b\).

**3. Jump-location recurrence.** Let \(E_{b,n}\) be the set of residues \(r\in\{1,\ldots,a-1\}\) at which \(q_n(r)-q_n(r-1)=b\). The preceding recursion gives
\[
E_{b,2}=\{1,\ldots,b\},\qquad
E_{b,n}=\bigcup_{t=0}^{b-1}\bigl(tR_{n-1}+E_{b,n-1}\bigr)\quad(n\ge3).
\]
Each union is disjoint: \(E_{b,n-1}\) lies strictly inside \([0,R_{n-1})\), and the displayed translates occupy disjoint intervals. Hence \(|E_{b,n}|=b^{n-1}\) and
\[
E_{b,n}=\left\{u+\sum_{j=2}^{n-1}t_jR_j:\quad1\le u\le b,\quad0\le t_j\le b-1\right\},
\]
with all representations distinct.

**4. Turning jumps into maximal missing blocks.** Fix an integer \(k\ge0\). By Step 1, among integers in the block \([ka,(k+1)a-1]\), those outside the semigroup have precisely the residues satisfying \(q_n(r)>k\). Since \(q_n\) is nondecreasing and \(q_n(0)=0\), those residues form either an empty set or one terminal suffix beginning at some \(r\ge1\). Its length is \(a-r\). A jump of height \(b\) at \(r\) means
\[
q_n(r-1)\le k<q_n(r)
\]
for exactly \(b\) consecutive nonnegative integer values of \(k\). Thus this jump produces exactly \(b\) terminal suffixes of length \(a-r\). Distinct blocks \([ka,(k+1)a-1]\) cannot join across their boundary, since \(a(k+1)\in S_{b,n}\). Every gap block arises from a jump. Therefore the complete histogram is precisely \(H_{b,n}(a-r)=b\) for \(r\in E_{b,n}\), and zero otherwise.

**5. Positive-exponent factorization and endpoints.** Because
\[
a-b-(b-1)\sum_{j=2}^{n-1}R_j=n-1,
\]
replacing \(u\) by \(b-v_1\) and each \(t_j\) by \(b-1-v_j\) rewrites each length \(a-r\) uniquely as \((n-1)+v_1+\sum_{j=2}^{n-1}v_jR_j\). Summing its monomial proves the polynomial identity. The minimum occurs at all \(v_j=0\) and is \(n-1\). The maximum occurs at \(r=1\), hence \(a-1\). There are \(b^{n-1}\) distinct lengths, each with multiplicity \(b\), proving the final count.

As a consistency check, summing lengths recovers the known genus:
\[
\sum_{\ell\ge1}\ell H_{b,n}(\ell)
=\frac{b^n}{2}\left(n-1+\frac{b^n-b}{b-1}\right).
\]
This is a check against the established Apéry formulas, not a claim of a new genus formula. \(\square\)

## Verification

The accompanying `CHECKER.py` independently constructs semigroup membership by forward dynamic programming and Apéry representatives by shortest paths on residue classes, then compares both with the theorem. Across 26 integer parameter pairs (maximum multiplicity \(511\)), it checked 8,585 maximal gap blocks. It verified all lengths, uniform multiplicities, minimum and maximum lengths, and genus. The computations are **finite corroboration only**; the theorem for every \(b,n\ge2\) follows from the Apéry formula and the proof above. Reproduce with `python CHECKER.py` using a standard Python 3 interpreter.

## Relationship to prior work

The fundamental repunit numerical semigroups and their embedding dimension, Frobenius number, type, and genus were studied by Rosales–Branco–Torrão (2015/2016). The full Apéry and greedy-denomination formula used here is explicitly documented by Liu–Xin–Ye–Yin (2023), Lemmas 3.1 and 3.3 and Section 4. The generalized family of Branco–Colaço–Ojeda (2022/2023) also develops Apéry invariants. These antecedents supply the mathematical starting point and should receive credit; in particular, the new polynomial can be **derived** from their information after the additional staircase-jump analysis. The new claim is the uniform maximal-gap-block multiplicity and exact factorized length spectrum, not those existing Apéry, Frobenius, or genus results. Related published research on isolated gaps in a *different unit-shift* semigroup and on two-generator run frequencies does not imply this all-length distribution for the ordinary \(n\)-generator repunit family.

## Limitations

This is a statement only for ordinary consecutive-repunit numerical semigroups with all \(n\) indicated generators. It is not an extension to arbitrarily translated, thinned, or shifted semigroups. The 2015/2016 original Rosales–Branco–Torrão journal full text was inaccessible at the time of this comparison; the accessible abstract and metadata describe embedding dimension, Frobenius number, type, and genus. Its unstated or implicit treatment of the gap-block histogram therefore remains a publication-priority risk. The result is not independently audited, and computational agreement does not substitute for proof.
