# Review of the exact odd-prime Pascal-column fiber law

## Correctness — PASS

The quantifiers cover every odd prime \(p\), precision \(h\ge1\), and target \(y\bmod p^h\). Factorization cancels the unique denominator factor of \(p\) and yields \(p-1\) regular isometries. In the singular branch, the symmetric falling factorial about its midpoint reduces modulo \(p\) to a square, and a convergent square-root coordinate change produces an exact \(p\)-adic quadratic normal form. Counting solutions of that square congruence, with the correct factor of \(p\) from lifting, proves every fiber. The period is established independently by an upper congruence and a sharp difference witness. The included exact-integer checker validates all \(12\) combinations with \(p\in\{3,5,7,11\}\), \(1\le h\le3\); these finite computations are not an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS on inspected evidence

Fiorot–Gilblas–Tonolo (arXiv:2307.02366, first public 2023-07-05) prove general period results for modular binomial sequences, notably Proposition 3.4, without the full targetwise residue multiplicity. Sun–Zhang (arXiv:0812.3089, first public 2008-12-16) prove surjectivity, and their lifting proof gives one regular branch; those are not new claims here. The targetwise extra fibers are instead governed by a *uniform family of quadratic critical branches*, yielding the explicit exact count at all moduli. A published fifth-column \(2\)-adic fiber law concerns one different fixed lower index, not the all-odd-prime \(p+1\) statement. Focused indexed mathematical searches and the inspected primary material did not identify the complete formula. Possible implicit deductions or unindexed prior formulations remain a genuine priority risk.

## Value — PASS

Counting inverse fibers is the natural refinement of the previously solved existence/universality question for fixed Pascal columns. The family \(p+1\) is the first lower index beyond the prime, where the residue-class branch at the origin becomes singular and introduces square-root concentration. The result determines *all* local multiplicities uniformly in both prime and precision, revealing an exact two-mechanism distribution rather than a routine special case. The source literature's periods and general value-surjectivity alone do not quantify these local biases.

## Limits and closest material

The theorem excludes \(p=2\) and gives no blanket all-column result. The 2008 Sun–Zhang paper is a strong source for the regular branches, and independent priority examination may show a weighted interpretation of its method. The 2023 Fiorot–Gilblas–Tonolo paper is the initial public literature source for the steady modular-binomial-period question used here; the earlier Sun–Zhang work is cited as supporting and limiting coverage.
