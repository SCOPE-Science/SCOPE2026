# Uniform two-sphere wedges for geodesic general-position complexes of three-row categorical products

## Finding
For every integer \(n\geq3\), let \(G_n=K_3\times K_n\) denote the **categorical** (tensor/direct) product of two complete graphs, with vertex set \([3]\times[n]\) and adjacency between \((r,a)\) and \((s,b)\) exactly when \(r\neq s\) and \(a\neq b\). Let \(\operatorname{GP}(G_n)\) be the simplicial complex of all vertex sets containing no three distinct vertices on any graph-geodesic. Then the **integral homotopy type**, not merely its homology, is
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{GP}(K_3\times K_n)\simeq \bigvee^{\frac{2n^3-3n^2-3n+4}{2}}S^2\qquad(n\geq3).}
\]
In particular the first four sphere counts are \(11,36,82,155\) at \(n=3,4,5,6\). For every prime \(q\geq5\), the unitary Cayley graph on \(\mathbb Z/(3q)\mathbb Z\) is \(K_3\times K_q\) by the Chinese remainder theorem, so its general-position complex has exactly the above homotopy type with \(n=q\). The result supplies a uniform two-dimensional homotopy spine despite full row facets of dimension \(n-1\).

## Assumptions and scope
Graphs are finite, simple, undirected, connected, and unweighted. A graph geodesic is a shortest path in the ordinary unweighted edge metric. Faces include the empty set; geometric realization is taken in the standard simplicial sense. The symbol \(\times\) denotes **categorical** product, not Cartesian product. The restriction \(n\geq3\) guarantees that all distinct nonadjacent grid cells have a common neighbor and hence that \(G_n\) has diameter two. The theorem is non-equivariant and says nothing about products with a factor of size two or three or more product factors.

## Proof
**1. Complete face classification.** The graph \(G_n\) has diameter two: a pair of distinct cells in a common row or column has a common neighbor because both dimensions have at least three labels. In any diameter-two graph, a triple violates geodesic general position precisely when the induced triple has exactly two edges, forming an induced path on three vertices. Thus general-position sets are precisely sets inducing a disjoint union of cliques.

We record all maximal types, including their faces. A clique in \(G_n\) has pairwise distinct row and column coordinates, hence has size at most three. An independent set with at least two cells lies in one row or one column: two cells sharing a row force any third nonneighbor of both into that row, and the column argument is symmetric. A clique of size three cannot have another disjoint component, because no cell is nonadjacent to all three pairwise distinct rows and columns. If a set contains an edge but is not a clique, the only possible additional nonneighbors of both endpoints are the two opposite corners of their \(2\times2\) coordinate rectangle. Those opposite corners form an edge. Consequently every face lies in a full row simplex, a full column simplex, a four-vertex \(2\times2\) rectangle simplex, or a three-vertex transversal (one cell in each row and a different column). Conversely every subset of each listed simplex induces a disjoint union of cliques, so is a face. This yields a **complete** decomposition into these types, not just a list of examples.

**2. Elementary collapses to dimension two.** In each of the three row simplices, designate column \(0\) (replace \([n]\) with \(\{0,\ldots,n-1\}\) for this step). For each subset \(S\) of a row omitting its designated vertex and of cardinality at least three, remove the pair of faces \((S,S\cup\{(r,0)\})\), in descending order of \(|S|\). The first face is free when removed: no other facet type contains three cells in that row; all strictly larger row cofaces have already been removed; and its sole remaining immediate coface is the second face of this pair. Every row retains only vertices, edges, and triangles containing its designated vertex. If \(n=3\), this row-reduction stage is empty and all row simplices are already two-dimensional.

Next, for each two-row/two-column rectangle, remove its four-vertex simplex together with the triangle obtained by deleting its lower-right corner. This triangle spans exactly those two rows and columns and belongs to no other rectangle, full row, full column, or transversal; it is therefore a free face. The pairs for different rectangles do not interact. After all these elementary collapses, no face has more than three vertices: every row was reduced to dimension two, columns have at most three vertices, transversal cliques have at most three, and every rectangle tetrahedron was removed. Thus the original complex simplicially collapses to a connected two-dimensional simplicial complex \(X_n\).

**3. Simple connectivity via explicit five-triangle fillings.** The one-skeleton is the complete graph on \(3n\) vertices, because every two-element subset is in general position. Hence every edge loop is a product of triangular loops. Every triangle already present as a two-face of the original complex bounds its own simplex. Any triangle *missing from the original complex* has two cells in the same row and the third cell in another row and in a column distinct from both previous columns, or the transposed column configuration. For the row case, write \(A=(r,a)\), \(B=(r,b)\), \(C=(s,c)\), where \(r\neq s\) and \(a,b,c\) are pairwise distinct, and put \(X=(s,a)\), \(Y=(s,b)\). The five triangular faces
\[
[ABX],\quad[BXY],\quad[BYC],\quad[XYC],\quad[AXC]
\]
form a triangulated disk whose boundary is the missing loop \(A\!\to B\!\to C\!\to A\). Each displayed face is valid **in the original complex** by the complete classification (four lie in \(2\times2\) rectangles and one in a row). Some of these disk faces may be removed by the elementary collapses in step 2; the disk is used here before those collapses. The case of a repeated column follows by transposition. Therefore **every** triangular edge loop contracts in the original complex. Since every loop in the complete one-skeleton is a product of triangular loops, the original complex is simply connected. Elementary collapses preserve homotopy type, so \(\pi_1(X_n)=0\).

**4. Euler characteristic and the sphere rank.** Partition the nonempty faces without overlap into (a) row-only subsets; (b) column-only subsets of size at least two; (c) partial-permutation sets of size two or three; and (d) three- and four-element proper/full rectangle sets not counted above. The alternating Euler contributions are, respectively,
\[
3,\qquad -2n,\qquad -6\binom n2+n(n-1)(n-2),\qquad 9\binom n2.
\]
Hence
\[
\chi(X_n)=\chi(\operatorname{GP}(G_n))=3-2n+3\binom n2+n(n-1)(n-2),
\]
so \(\chi(X_n)-1=(2n^3-3n^2-3n+4)/2\). Since \(X_n\) is a finite connected simply connected two-complex, its integral second homology is free of rank \(\chi(X_n)-1\), its positive-degree homology otherwise vanishes, and the Hurewicz map \(\pi_2(X_n)\to H_2(X_n;\mathbb Z)\) is an isomorphism. A wedge of the indicated number of two-spheres maps to \(X_n\) by representatives of an integral homology basis. This map is a homology equivalence between simply connected CW complexes, hence a homotopy equivalence by Whitehead's theorem. Elementary collapses carry the equivalence back to the original complex. The theorem follows for every integer \(n\geq3\).

## Verification
The standalone file `gp_tensor_verify.py` uses two independent finite descriptions: it enumerates faces from the four shape families, and, for \(3\le n\le6\), separately builds the graph, computes all shortest-path distances by breadth-first search, and tests every one of its \(2^{3n}\) vertex subsets against geodesic triples. The two face sets agree exactly. For \(3\le n\le8\), the verifier also replays every claimed elementary collapse and checks that each lower face has a unique surviving coface; constructs the five-triangle fillings for **every** missing triangle; computes Euler characteristic; and independently reduces all simplicial boundary maps over \(\mathbb F_2\). These finite checks agree with the stated two-sphere ranks \(11,36,82,155,261,406\). The output is in `verification_output.txt`. The all-parameter statement is proved by the symbolic arguments above, not extrapolated from those checks.

Run from a directory containing both files with `python3 gp_tensor_verify.py`; no third-party modules are required.

## Relationship to prior work
Klavžar and Yero (2019, Proposition 3.3) determined the **maximum cardinality** of a general-position set for categorical products \(K_p\times K_q\), not their simplicial homotopy types. The 2026 complete general-position-polynomial classification for these same categorical products already identifies the four face families and counts them; that entire combinatorial face classification is **prior work** and is used explicitly in step 1. It does not state, prove, or supply as an immediate counting corollary the uniform free-face reductions, simply connected two-dimensional spine, or integral two-sphere wedge equivalence established here. The paper of Hamed-Labbafian and Tavakoli (first published 27 August 2026) studies general- and edge-general-position **numbers** on unitary Cayley graphs, including two-factor products, but not their full simplicial homotopy type. Rather (2026) studies complete multipartite general-position polynomials, a different graph construction. No assertion is made that combinatorial counting formulas, extremal numbers, or the cluster-graph criterion are original. Searches against named products, general-position complexes, cluster complexes, sphere multiplicities, and published structured findings located no stronger result implying this topological statement. This is a best-knowledge priority assessment, not a uniqueness certificate.

## Limitations
The scope is exactly \(K_3\times K_n\) for \(n\ge3\); no claim is made for \(K_2\times K_n\), arbitrary \(K_m\times K_n\) with \(m\ge4\), higher graph products, weighted geodesics, or automorphism-equivariant homotopy. The symbolic collapse is a non-equivariant construction. The standalone computations cover only stated finite ranges and do not prove the infinite theorem. Unindexed or inaccessible equivalent literature remains a residual originality risk. Independent mathematical validation has not been performed.
