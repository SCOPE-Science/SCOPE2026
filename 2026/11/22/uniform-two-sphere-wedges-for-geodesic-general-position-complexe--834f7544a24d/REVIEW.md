# Scientific review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The domain is all integers \(n\ge3\). The four prior-known face families are exhaustive; row-only free-face pairs and each rectangle's unique three-corner face give genuine elementary collapses to dimension two. Every missing triangle of the complete one-skeleton admits a specified five-triangle nullhomotopy disk (or its transpose). This proves simple connectivity, so the integral Euler rank and the Hurewicz–Whitehead argument prove the **homotopy type** rather than just mod-two homology. The standalone finite checker independently verifies actual graph geodesics at widths 3–6, free-face collapse conditions and mod-two homology at widths 3–8. Finitary checking does not replace the uniform proof.

## Originality
**PASS, best-knowledge priority only.** The most relevant published comparison, *General-position polynomials of categorical products of complete graphs and an infinite nonunimodal family* (2026), explicitly classifies and counts the same four face types; those descriptions are not new. Its complete result was inspected and does **not** provide a two-dimensional collapse, a simple connectivity proof, an integral homotopy equivalence, or a classification of the sphere summands. Klavžar–Yero (2019, Proposition 3.3) gives only the maximum general-position size. Hamed-Labbafian–Tavakoli (27 August 2026) gives extremal general- and edge-general-position numbers of unitary Cayley graphs; Rather (2026) concerns multipartite graphs. Result comparisons were made for categorical/direct/tensor products, cluster-graph complexes, sphere counts, and Cayley graph aliases. An unindexed equivalent cannot be excluded; independent priority audit is absent.

## Mathematical value
**PASS.** The three-row direct product is the first nonbipartite tensor-product row size and yields the arithmetically natural \(\mathbb Z/(3q)\mathbb Z\) unitary Cayley family. Although the simplicial dimension grows with \(n\), the complex collapses to a two-dimensional simply connected spine and has a cubic number of integral sphere summands. This is a motivated full infinite-family topological classification, not a repackaging of a face polynomial: it supplies the homotopy-group and torsion-free consequences that the enumerator does not establish.

## Limitations
No assertion for a two-row product, higher row count, other products, symmetry-equivariant homotopy, or arbitrary Cayley rings; finite checker outputs are supportive but not infinite certificates. Other unindexed results remain a priority risk, and no independent mathematical validation has occurred.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
