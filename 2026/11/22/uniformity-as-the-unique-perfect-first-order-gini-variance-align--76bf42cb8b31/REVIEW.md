# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS
For iid non-atomic, nondegenerate laws with finite fourth moment, the order-two Hoeffding decomposition reduces the limiting correlation to the correlation of the two positive-variance first projections. Equality gives an affine identity on the support; differentiating along the perfect support yields an affine CDF, and support-gap exclusion makes the law uniformly distributed on a finite interval. The converse holds because the projections are identical up to translation and scale. For uniform observations, the overlapping-pair enumeration gives the stated full finite-sample covariance matrix. The Bernoulli-polynomial identity establishes the Watson sample-by-sample transform, and the Fourier CLT plus a summable expected tail proves the centered second-order limit. ## Originality — PASS, with residual priority risk
Hoeffding's general theory supplies the first-projection machinery, not the characterization. Gerstenberger and Vogel (arXiv:1405.5027v1, sections 2–3, full accessible HTML) give the estimators' influence functions and report equal asymptotic efficiency under the uniform law; they do not state that the correlation between the Gini and sample-variance estimators tends to one *if and only if* the law is uniform among all atomless laws. Fortiana and Grané (JRSSB 65, 2003, text of the complete paper) relate Gini mean difference to maximal rank-correlation and empirical variance, but their different goodness-of-fit statistic does not identify the present exact Gini-minus-variance Watson contrast. Čiginas and Pumputis (arXiv:1406.2275, full accessible article) instead address finite-population sampling without replacement. Watson's null limit and the statistic itself are classical. Comparison of these statements, including their hypotheses and implications, did not reveal a dominating result; nevertheless an older unlocated equivalence cannot be ruled out. An older 2004 variance-estimation paper and Watson's 1961 article were accessible only in part or via bibliographic records.

## Value — PASS
The complete non-atomic equality classification is a natural extreme-case question for two familiar scale estimators with almost identical uniform asymptotic influence. The exact finite-sample correlation quantifies their finite divergence, while the exact Watson identity identifies the next-order discrepancy with a known and reproducibly calibrated goodness-of-fit statistic. This connects a natural equality boundary to a practical distributional diagnostic without asserting that the classical Watson test or its limiting distribution is newly discovered.
