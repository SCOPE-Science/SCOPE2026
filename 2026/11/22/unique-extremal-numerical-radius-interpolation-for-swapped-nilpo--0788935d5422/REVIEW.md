# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The scalar compression \((F_{21}-F_{12})/2\) gives the exact Schwarz--Pick lower bound. An explicit polynomial family achieves the critical distance; disk precomposition gives every larger distance. At equality, scalar Schwarz--Pick rigidity pins down the off-diagonal difference. Two saturated numerical-radius states force zero trace and real boundary quotients for the remaining entries, and Fourier coefficient matching reduces them to quadratic polynomials fixed by both node values. The proof treats all bounded holomorphic \(2\times2\) matrices. The included diagnostic independently tests explicit identities and interior contraction but is not a substitute for the proof.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)

The motivating paper explicitly contains the unit-amplitude examples and an extremal rational interpolant, so these are prior facts. Its complete Sections 2 and 4 and examples were inspected: the paper forces one scalar entry combination but does not explicitly state or prove uniqueness of all matrix entries, nor a one-parameter classification of existence, uniqueness and multiplicity for arbitrary amplitudes. Related three-point uniqueness research for scalar functions on Euclidean balls has different source and target spaces. Published-record searches for full-matrix uniqueness, swapped nilpotent interpolation and boundary rigidity, together with accessible earlier claims, identified no equivalent or stronger claim. General interpolation geometry might still imply the result implicitly; the evidence supports a qualified originality assessment rather than absolute uniqueness of discovery.

## Mathematical value — PASS

A concrete two-point counterexample in a new numerical-radius interpolation theory is promoted to a complete sharp existence-versus-rigidity transition for a natural amplitude family. The distinctive result is **full matrix uniqueness** at the boundary even though the operator norm is not controlled by the usual Pick condition; the proof exposes a boundary-face/Fourier mechanism unavailable from the scalar Schwarz--Pick computation alone. Above the same threshold there are infinitely many strict solutions. The result is limited in dimension, but it is a complete and structurally motivated classification, not a numerical table or a routine parameter substitution.
