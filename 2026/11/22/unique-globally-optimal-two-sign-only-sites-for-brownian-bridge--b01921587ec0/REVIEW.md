# Scientific review: two binary Brownian-bridge measurements

## Correctness
PASS. The observation-sign sigma-field has four atoms, and Gaussian global sign symmetry makes the true Bayes conditional mean exactly linear in the two sign bits. The covariance and arcsine identities yield the exact loss. The two-variable reduction is algebraic; for correlations at least one-third its midpoint derivative forces reflection symmetry. Below one-third, a monotone-correlation bound and strictly signed polynomial certificate prove a sharp exclusion. The remaining one-dimensional objective has one maximizer by a strictly decreasing scalar quotient. The coincident and endpoint cases have strictly worse information. A standalone script reproduces independent Gaussian-quadrant checks and all nontrivial factorization certificates; finite checks are corroboration, not the proof.

## Originality
PASS on the inspected sources, subject to residual historical-priority risk. Pronzato--Zhigljavsky (2018) analyze optimal real-valued kernel quadrature, Foster--Lyons--Oberhauser (2019) analyze polynomial Brownian path information, and Guo--Kostina (2019) analyze causal rate-constrained Wiener path reconstruction. Fesl--Koller--Utschick (2022) provide general one-bit Gaussian conditional-mean and arcsine-covariance technology, but do not derive the stated global time-placement optimizer for the Brownian-bridge integral. A publicly available theorem on two i.i.d. Brownian-bridge quadrature sites uses randomly selected full-valued observations and a different optimization criterion. Another on noisy binary location queries concerns a uniform location, not a Gaussian bridge. Full theorem/proof text of both directly relevant public results was inspected, alongside the mathematical material available from the three primary papers. No inspected theorem implies the two-bit sign-only global placement. This does not establish exhaustive scholarly priority.

## Value
PASS. A two-bit fixed-location bridge integral estimate is a natural and highly constrained Gaussian sensing/quantization benchmark. The exact global design is not the two-point full-value design by assumption, and proving reflection symmetry globally requires a nontrivial low-correlation exclusion inequality rather than a local stationary calculation. The result quantifies the irreducible effect of sign compression: the best two signs explain about 63.8% of integral variance, versus 47.7% for one sign and 88.9% for two optimally placed full-valued samples. It provides a precise target for quantized sensing design, without claiming broader binary encoding optimality.

## Closest sources and limits
Full-value Brownian-bridge fixed or random-node design, arXiv:1808.10722v1, arXiv:1904.06998v1, and the public i.i.d. two-node kernel-quadrature theorem are the closest comparators. Guo--Kostina's rate-limited causal Wiener reconstruction and the public noisy location-query theorem do not address the same target/sampling model. Residual risk: an older specialized treatment of two-bit Gaussian-process sensor placement could cover or imply this result. The present claim does not treat bit errors, adjustable thresholds, adaptive sensors or other covariance families. Every reported numerical constant is tied to the exact root equation and the included verifier.
