# Self-review

**Correctness: PASS.** The two-by-two spectrum is obtained from the exact trace and determinant, not a numerical approximation. The independent bound \(m\le e^{-2ah}\) rules out the entire interval following the first half-turn once a left-neighbor improvement is proved. The derivative sign reduces exactly to the unique zero of \(F_\alpha\); all signs and the three cases for the coefficient \(K\) have been checked. The limiting expansion is justified by a simple-root argument and uniform Taylor bounds. The diagnostic code cross-checks finite cases but does not substitute for the universal proof.

**Originality: PASS, with bibliographic risk.** The semigroup-frame paper of Bailey–Gallardo-Gutiérrez–Partington (arXiv:2611.13867v1, Sections 1–3 and main theorems) characterizes general frame generators rather than optimizing these two sampling times. Gwizdek–Kużel–Majdak (arXiv:2611.20029v1, Introduction and Section 3) treat abstract discrete dynamical frames, Stein equations and defects, not this all-damping scalar extremal delay. Three-time oscillator sampling determinant optimization, two-time diffusion sampling, and two-mode discrete actuator condition optimization are not implication-equivalent; their samples, objectives or operators differ. The two-time diffusion result does display the same number \(0.120707519697\ldots\); no novelty is claimed for that standalone constant. Broad optimal-design searches found no exact covering statement. A classical control-theory formulation could remain uncatalogued.

**Mathematical value: PASS.** The exact sample-time design problem is intrinsic to stable dynamical frames and worst-direction observability. Its sharp answer is global despite infinitely many possible oscillatory alias peaks, and it proves uniqueness and the nonlinear dependence on damping. The transition from a near-orthogonal half-period at small damping to a finite damping-scale delay at strong damping gives a usable prescription beyond local Taylor heuristics. The theorem is deliberately restricted to two equally weighted measurements; extrapolations to more samples are not claimed.
