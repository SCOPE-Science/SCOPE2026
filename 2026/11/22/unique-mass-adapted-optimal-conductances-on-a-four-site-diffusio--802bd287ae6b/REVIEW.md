# Scientific assessment: four-site mass-adapted edge conditioning

## Correctness — PASS

The final claim is a simultaneous all-parameter inequality and equality classification for the generalized Laplacian of a four-vertex path with masses \((1,t,t,1)\), \(t>0\), and independently positive edge weights. Both trial vectors in RESULT.md are orthogonal to the constant mode in the mass inner product. Their squared edge differences are identical, namely \((s^2,4,s^2)\), while their mass norms are \(4s(s+1)\) and \(4s(s-1)\), with \(s=\sqrt{1+t}\). The min--max principle therefore proves the sharp condition-number lower bound for **arbitrary** edge weights, not merely reflection-symmetric weights. An explicit fourth eigenbasis verifies attainment and eigenvalue ordering; equality in the lower Rayleigh bound forces both endpoint weights to agree and the middle weight to equal \((1+t)/2\) times either endpoint weight. All substitutions were independently reconstructed symbolically, with the included checker also running 155 numerical consistency tests. The analytic proof is self-contained; no finite test is used as an infinite certificate.

Residual correctness risk: the theorem is confined to strictly positive conductances and the displayed mass pattern; extension to nonnegative boundary weights or other node-mass profiles is not established.

## Originality — PASS on the inspected evidence, with a publication-priority qualification

Kempton, Herrmann and di Bernardo (arXiv:1505.07279v1; inspected introduction and problem formulation) optimize \(\lambda_n/\lambda_2\) for ordinary Laplacians using constrained distributed adaptation. Balan, Begué, Clark and Okoudjou (arXiv:1609.02233v1; inspected full available Section 3) give an edge-reweighting and condition-number framework on the ordinary mean-zero graph Laplacian. Neither text contains the mass-dependent four-site optimizer or the identical-Dirichlet-energy certificate. Boyd, Diaconis, Sun and Xiao (American Mathematical Monthly 2006, entire five-page paper inspected) prove optimal mixing for nonnegative symmetric stochastic paths with the uniform stationary measure; that is not the unconstrained generalized eigenvalue-ratio problem with heterogeneous node masses. Jafarizadeh and collaborators (IEEE TNSE 2021, path calculation in Section VI-H.3 inspected) solve small ordinary-Laplacian path instances, including five vertices, rather than the proposed mass-parametric generalized problem. Xu and Jabbari (2026, open-access Section 3.4.3, equation (30)) discuss simultaneous node/edge weighted Laplacian conditioning but leave that broader optimization beyond their article; their reported four-robot example is a different graph and gives numerical rather than the present closed mass-parametric path solution. Indexed mathematical records on graph spectral designs, mass-weighted networks and spring allocation were compared by **object, normalization and objective**, not title alone. No exact or stronger result covering the mass-parametric theorem was established.

Residual originality risk: the full text of Jafarizadeh and collaborators' Automatica (2020) work could not be inspected; the available abstract and later citations describe nonuniform couplings but not heterogeneous vertex masses. Other undiscovered or unindexed equivalent generalized-eigenvalue results remain possible. This is a qualified best-knowledge originality assessment, not proof of absolute publication priority.

## Value — PASS

The four-site path is the smallest reflection-symmetric chain with a separately tunable central edge. Heterogeneous internal mass/capacitance is an intrinsic, physically motivated departure from the ordinary-Laplacian model. The exact answer establishes a nonuniform, unique, globally optimal conductance profile for every \(t>0\), with a clean structural balancing law: the middle edge is smaller than the outer edges precisely for \(t<1\) and larger precisely for \(t>1\). Its certificate works against arbitrary asymmetric candidate edge weights, giving a reusable min--max proof pattern for spectral design. The result does not pretend to characterize longer chains, general stationary distributions, or multiple performance objectives; its value is the complete natural four-site classification and sharp mass-dependence.
