# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The Bayes estimator follows from Gaussian second-moment conditioning, with its exact captured variance obtained from the Gaussian fourth moment. For the zero-start Ornstein–Uhlenbeck covariance, direct integration yields \(H(x)=\int K(t,x)^2dt\). The critical cancellation \(H'=V'D\) and identity \(G_\eta'=(V+\eta)D'\) reduce global optimality to a strict, single change of sign at the interval midpoint. The boundary inequalities give exactly one global root; implicit differentiation proves strictly increasing optimal time with noise, and the high-noise limiting root follows by division by noise variance. The included checker confirms algebraic steps and independent numerical quadratures. **Risk:** numerical cancellation at extreme damping is a computational issue only; the theorem relies on the analytic sign proof and excludes \(\lambda=0\) except as a separately stated limit.

## Originality — PASS, subject to historical priority
Full primary text was compared by mathematical object and implication. Folia–Rattray (2017) provides linear integrated Gaussian-state filtering and parameter inference, not a quadratic-energy one-snapshot optimum. Sikolya–Baran (2018) concerns stationary complex-OU pointwise IMSPE/entropy and is not a theorem about the nonlinear functional. Nickelsen–Touchette (2022) studies path-integrated OU moments' distributions and large deviations, not design of noisy conditional observations. The historical one-site linear Ornstein–Uhlenbeck integral classification and stationary-OU quadratic path-energy two-site results do not imply this theorem: the chaos order, initial distribution and decision constraints differ. Searches for the exact assertion and its alternative descriptions identified no covering result; absence from a search is not proof of novelty. **Risk:** an unindexed earlier treatment or a general Gaussian second-chaos design principle could imply this special statement; Palamarchuk (2020) was accessible only by abstract, which discusses upper functions rather than optimal observation timing.

## Value — PASS
Accumulated squared state is a standard energy or state-cost functional in stochastic monitoring and linear-quadratic control. The result simultaneously determines the exact optimal single measurement, posterior risk, uniqueness, direction of the noise-induced placement shift, and a finite high-noise location for every damping strength; these are genuinely target-dependent and do not follow from the linear-integral optimal sensor. In the normalized example \(\lambda=1\), an optimal noiseless snapshot reduces risk by approximately \(30.52\%\) relative to the midpoint. The qualification is that the theorem is a natural **one-snapshot** exact classification, not a multi-sensor or adaptive-control optimality statement.
