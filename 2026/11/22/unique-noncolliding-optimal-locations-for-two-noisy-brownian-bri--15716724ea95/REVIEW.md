# Scientific review: two noisy Brownian-bridge quadrature sites

**Correctness: PASS.** The Gaussian bridge covariance yields the exact conditional integral variance as a positive-definite two-by-two inverse. All pairs, including repeated and endpoint sites, are covered. For fixed separation, the derivative sign in the center offset cannot produce an interior local maximum; endpoint sites are strictly dominated. The centered objective has a single maximizer given by the displayed radical, giving an exact global risk and unique unordered pair for all positive noise levels. Five symbolic identities and 765 rational-grid comparisons replayed from the standalone verifier corroborate the algebra without being used as a continuum proof.

**Originality: PASS, qualified.** Santin (arXiv:2210.15959v1, Theorem 5) proves optimal *noiseless* sites for a supremum interpolation power-function norm; the positive differential-operator parameter in that theorem is not observation noise. Briol et al. (arXiv:1706.03369v1) optimize random-node sampling laws. Cai et al. (arXiv:2202.10615v1) provide noisy Bayesian quadrature error rates rather than this exact design. Karvonen et al. (DOI:10.1007/s11222-019-09901-0) analyze signs/magnitudes of weights, and Ji–Müller (arXiv:1604.05375v1) give general Gaussian noisy functional-data design machinery. The closest published Brownian-bridge two-node result concerns i.i.d. **noiseless random sites**, not a deterministic noisy pair. The global two-site formula and strict noncollision were not found in the inspected claims. Equivalent classical optimal-design formulations remain an explicit priority risk; this is not independent originality certification.

**Value: PASS.** Two noisy observations are a meaningful fixed computational/sensor budget. The theorem gives a complete noise-to-location calibration for a canonical first-order pinned Sobolev integration kernel, establishes exactly when separated observations beat both replication and boundary sites, and quantifies how the optimal pair shrinks towards the midpoint at high noise. It does not claim a faster numerical algorithm, broader kernel universality or extension to more sites.

**Limitations and closest literature.** The result assumes a standard Brownian bridge, Gaussian independent additive noise of common known variance, two deterministic nonadaptive sites, and the posterior variance of the integral. The central mathematical risk is older continuous-time c-optimal/Gaussian-response design work stated in different terminology. Symbolic checks do not certify mathematical novelty or independently verify this review.
