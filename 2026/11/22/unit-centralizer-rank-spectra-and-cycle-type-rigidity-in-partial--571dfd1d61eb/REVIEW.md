# Assessment of the unit-centralizer rank-spectrum theorem

**Correctness.** The classification of equivariant partial matchings divides every cycle orbit into singleton, unique antipodal self-matching (even length), or an equivariant pairing with a distinct orbit of equal length. This proves the labeled-cycle factorization and the exact rank support. The normalized reversed polynomial has a first defect coefficient of \(m_\ell s_\ell^2/\ell\) at degree \(\ell\), permitting a triangular reconstruction. Direct diagram multiplication checks every pair of diagrams and permutation units through \(n=4\); explicit cycle-type representatives are checked for \(n=5\), and rational coefficient recovery through \(n=6\). These checks are finite corroborations; the all-dimensional proof is combinatorial.

**Originality.** Archival diagram-monoid structure in Dolinka–East–Gray (arXiv:1512.02279) covers Green classes, ranks of ideals, generators and idempotents, not this unit-centralizer polynomial or the cycle-type inverse theorem. Halverson–Jacobson (arXiv:1808.08118, §5.3) enumerates conjugation-fixed *vertically symmetric* rook-Brauer diagrams as an ingredient in irreducible character calculations, not every diagram fixed by a given permutation, and does not provide the rigidity theorem. Araújo et al. (arXiv:2301.04252, §5) concern natural semigroup conjugacy, which is distinct from unit-conjugation orbit counts. Related centralizer-spectrum rigidity for nilpotent partial bijections is in a different monoid and does not imply the present formula. The residual priority risk is an equivalent cycle-index calculation for diagonally invariant partial matchings not indexed under semigroup terminology.

**Value.** Complete centralizer rank polynomials for every permutation class solve a canonical all-parameter question in a natural diagram monoid; the invertibility of the map from a permutation's cycle type to its rank spectrum is a nontrivial structure-recovery result, and the rank-orbit Burnside formula provides all finite unit-conjugacy orbit counts. The result is not merely an identity for a small numerical sequence, nor is it an arbitrary slice of the monoid.

**Boundaries.** Unit centralizers are not general diagram centralizers, and the orbit formula concerns unit conjugacy rather than the broader semigroup-conjugacy relation. Historical priority remains open to independent verification.
