# Universal commuting-retraction graphs of two-generated nilpotent Lie algebras

## Finding

Let \(L\) be any finite-dimensional nilpotent Lie algebra over a finite field \(k=\mathbb F_q\), with \(n=\dim_k L\ge3\) and \(\dim_k(L/[L,L])=2\). A *retraction* means a \(k\)-linear Lie endomorphism \(e:L\to L\) with \(e^2=e\), where the square denotes composition. Define the simple commuting-retraction graph \(\Gamma(L)\) to have as vertices all retractions other than zero and the identity, with two **distinct** vertices adjacent exactly when they commute under composition.

**Universal graph theorem.** Put \(b=q^{n-2}\) and \(m=q(q+1)/2\). Then
\[
\boxed{\Gamma(L)\cong\bigsqcup_{j=1}^{m}K_{b,b}.}
\]
In particular, the entire graph depends only on the field size and the vector-space dimension, **not** on the Lie bracket or nilpotency class. Every proper nonzero retraction has ordinary linear rank one, with exactly \(q(q+1)q^{n-2}\) such maps. Thus the rank polynomial is
\[
\boxed{R_L(T)=1+q(q+1)q^{n-2}T+T^n.}
\]
The clique polynomial counting cliques including the empty clique and the two trivial retractions is
\[
\boxed{C_L(T)=(1+T)^2(1+2mbT+mb^2T^2).}
\]
Every inclusion-maximal commuting family consists of exactly four maps \(\{0,e,f,\operatorname{id}\}\) with \(ef=fe=0\), and the number of such maximal families is \(mb^2\). With independent uniformly chosen retractions (including zero and identity), the probability of commuting is
\[
\boxed{\frac{(b+5)\,2mb+4}{(2mb+2)^2},}
\]
and tends to \(1/[q(q+1)]\) for fixed \(q\) as \(n\to\infty\) through admissible dimensions.

**Inverse and blindness consequences.** From an abstract unlabeled graph \(\Gamma(L)\), its number of components \(m\) determines \(q\) uniquely, and its component size \(2b\) then determines \(n\). Nevertheless it does not determine the Lie-isomorphism type or nilpotency class. For example, two five-dimensional Lie algebras over the same field, the free two-generated class-three algebra \([x,y]=z,[x,z]=u,[y,z]=v\) and the standard filiform class-four algebra \([x,y]=z,[x,z]=u,[x,u]=v\), have the same graph though distinct nilpotency classes.

## Assumptions and scope

All Lie brackets are alternating, including over characteristic two. The algebra is finite dimensional, nilpotent, and satisfies \(\dim L/[L,L]=2\), equivalently it is minimally generated by two elements. All endomorphisms are ordinary Lie homomorphisms, not arbitrary linear projections or generalized pre-endomorphisms. Graph vertices are labeled endomorphisms; the graph is simple, so diagonal pairs are excluded as edges. We do not assume that \(L\) is free, filiform, graded, or of nilpotency class two. We require \(n\ge3\) to exclude the abelian two-dimensional case. The formula holds for all prime powers \(q\), including characteristic two.

## Proof

**1. A nilpotent generation lemma.** Let \(A\) be any finite-dimensional nilpotent Lie algebra and \(S\subseteq A\) a Lie subalgebra with \(A=S+[A,A]\). Then \(S=A\). Indeed, induction on the lower-central filtration \(\gamma_1(A)=A\), \(\gamma_{j+1}(A)=[A,\gamma_j(A)]\) shows \(A=S+\gamma_j(A)\) for all \(j\): modulo \(\gamma_{j+1}(A)\), write both entries of any bracket using \(S\) plus \(\gamma_j(A)\); the bracket of two entries of \(S\) lies in \(S\), and every other contribution lies in \(\gamma_{j+1}(A)\). Nilpotence eventually forces \(A=S\). Equivalently, a set generates a finite-dimensional nilpotent Lie algebra if and only if its image spans the abelianization. This is the classical Frattini/derived-quotient principle.

**2. Only ranks zero, one, and full occur.** Let \(e^2=e\) be a Lie endomorphism, and set \(H=e(L)\). Its commutator ideal is \([H,H]=e([L,L])\). Also
\[
H\cap[L,L]=e([L,L])=[H,H],
\]
because if \(h\in H\cap[L,L]\), then \(e(h)=h\). Therefore the abelianization \(H/[H,H]\) embeds into \(V=L/[L,L]\), with image \(\operatorname{im}\bar e\), where \(\bar e\) is the induced projection on \(V\). Put \(r=\operatorname{rank}\bar e\in\{0,1,2\}\).

If \(r=0\), then \(H=[H,H]\). Nilpotence of \(H\) gives \(H=0\), hence \(e=0\). If \(r=1\), then the nilpotent Lie algebra \(H\) has one-dimensional abelianization and is generated by one element by Step 1; all alternating brackets of that generator with itself vanish, hence \(\dim H=1\). If \(r=2\), then \(H+[L,L]=L\), so Step 1 applied to \(S=H\) gives \(H=L\). A surjective idempotent endomorphism is the identity. This proves that every nontrivial retraction has rank one and that \(\bar e\) has rank one.

**3. Explicit lifting and exact fibers.** Let \(\pi:L\to V\) be the quotient. Every rank-one Lie endomorphism has the form
\[
e_{u,\lambda}(x)=\lambda(\pi x)u,
\quad u\in L\setminus\{0\},\quad\lambda\in V^*\setminus\{0\}.
\]
Conversely any such map is a Lie homomorphism because it annihilates all brackets and its image is abelian. It is idempotent precisely when \(\lambda(\pi u)=1\). The pairs \((u,\lambda)\) and \((cu,c^{-1}\lambda)\) describe the same map for every \(c\in k^\times\), and these are the only redundancies.

A rank-one projection \(P\) of the two-dimensional space \(V\) is uniquely determined by distinct projective lines \(U=\operatorname{im}P\) and \(W=\ker P\), yielding \(q(q+1)\) choices. For a fixed \(P\), choose \(v\in U\setminus\{0\}\) and the unique functional \(\lambda\) satisfying \(\lambda(v)=1\) and \(\ker\lambda=W\). Its lifting retractions are exactly \(e_{u,\lambda}\) with \(\pi u=v\), and the fiber has cardinality \(|[L,L]|=q^{n-2}=b\). Distinct lifts are distinct maps. Thus there are exactly \(2mb\) proper nonzero retractions.

**4. Commutation criterion.** Let \(e,f\) be two nontrivial retractions, inducing rank-one projections \(P,Q\) on \(V\). If they commute, \(P,Q\) commute. Two commuting rank-one idempotents on a two-dimensional vector space are equal or complementary: in a basis adapted to the image and kernel of \(P\), the rank-one projection \(Q\) must be \(P\) or \(I-P\).

If \(P=Q\), their normalized functionals coincide; direct evaluation gives \(ef=e\) and \(fe=f\), so commutation forces \(e=f\). If \(Q=I-P\), the image line of \(Q\) is the kernel line of \(P\). Hence the defining functional for \(e\) annihilates the lift's abelianization of \(f(L)\), giving \(ef=0\), and symmetrically \(fe=0\). Therefore two **distinct** nontrivial retractions commute if and only if their quotient projections are complementary. Complementation \(P\mapsto I-P\) is a fixed-point-free involution on the \(q(q+1)\) rank-one projections, partitioning them into \(m\) unordered pairs. Since each projection has \(b\) lifts, each pair gives exactly one \(K_{b,b}\) component, proving the graph theorem.

**5. Counting consequences.** The only other idempotents are zero and identity, both central under composition with every endomorphism. The ordinary rank polynomial is immediate from Step 2. The clique polynomial of one disjoint union of \(m\) complete bipartite graphs of part size \(b\) is \(1+2mbT+mb^2T^2\); adjoining the two universal vertices multiplies it by \((1+T)^2\). Every maximal clique is these two universal vertices together with one edge, and those four maps close to a four-element Boolean meet monoid.

There are \(2mb+2\) retractions in total. Their ordered commuting pairs, allowing equality, consist of \(2mb+2\) diagonal pairs, \(2mb^2\) pairs between distinct proper nontrivial retractions, and \(4(2mb)+2\) ordered pairs involving one or both universal idempotents beyond the diagonal. The total simplifies to \(2mb^2+5(2mb)+4=(b+5)2mb+4\). Dividing by \((2mb+2)^2\) proves the probability and its limit. Finally \(q\mapsto q(q+1)/2\) is strictly increasing, so the number of components recovers \(q\), and \(b=q^{n-2}\) then recovers \(n\). The two displayed five-dimensional algebras have different lower-central lengths but the same \(q,n\), proving the blindness statement.

## Verification

The supplied standalone program `verify_nilpotent_lie_graph.py` builds Lie endomorphisms independently by enumerating images of two generators, recursively deducing images of basic iterated brackets, then checking every basis bracket and the composition-idempotence equation. It constructs the entire commutation graph by multiplying the resulting endomorphism matrices, without assuming the graph theorem. It exhaustively verifies the result for both prime fields of sizes two and three and for the following Lie algebras: standard filiform algebras of dimensions three, four and five; the free rank-two, class-three Lie algebra of dimension five.

## Relationship to prior work

Ebrahim and Facchini, *Idempotent Pre-Endomorphisms of Algebras* (arXiv:2304.05079v1), motivate the splitting viewpoint of idempotent operators; their pre-endomorphism framework differs from the ordinary Lie-homomorphism category used here and does not give the universal graph. Classical Frattini/derived-quotient generation and two-dimensional linear projection theory are used as known facts.

The published *Clique polynomials and maximal retraction families in free class-two Lie algebras* [2] gives a much stronger commuting-family count on **free class-two** Lie algebras, and contains the three-dimensional Heisenberg instance of the present formulas. The present theorem expressly does not re-claim that instance; it extends the structure and exact graph to **all** finite-dimensional two-generated nilpotent Lie algebras, of arbitrary nilpotency class and arbitrary quotient relations. The published *Retraction orbits and finite-field rank spectra of free two-step nilpotent Lie algebras* [3] similarly determines single-retraction counts in the free class-two case but not the present all-quotient universality. A published graph theorem for the rank-two exterior associative algebra [4] yields a superficially similar disjoint-union graph, but its algebra and homomorphism category are different; neither theorem logically implies the other. An analogous rank-one commuting-retraction census for finite groups of prime exponent has the same two-generator quotient-projection graph and counting mechanism. This precedent is not claimed as new; a group theorem does not cover arbitrary nilpotent Lie algebras, especially characteristic-p Lie algebras of nilpotency class at least p, beyond the usual Lazard transfer range. The current graph universality is established by an ordinary Lie proof without appealing to group correspondence. These distinctions concern the scope of mathematical statements, not just titles or matching numerical parameters.

## Limitations

The proof requires nilpotence and two-dimensional abelianization; it does not apply to general solvable Lie algebras, arbitrary two-dimensional quotient extensions, or algebras with at least three minimal generators. The graph does not recover the isomorphism type or nilpotency class. The exact prior-history search cannot exclude an equivalent but unindexed theorem under semidirect decompositions or retract semilattices. The 1997 paper on retracts of free Lie algebras is a remaining full-text priority uncertainty; its freely visible description is not evidence of a direct all-quotient commutation-graph formula. No independent external mathematical audit is claimed.
