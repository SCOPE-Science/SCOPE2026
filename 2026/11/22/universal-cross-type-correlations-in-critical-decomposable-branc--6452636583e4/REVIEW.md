# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS

Finite second moments give martingale orthogonality, the conditional-variance decomposition, and growth bounds for mixed offspring innovations. The unique maximal-length path in the triangular offspring matrix identifies the sole leading type-one innovation contribution; the Beta integral yields every coefficient with an explicit \(O(n^{i+j-1})\) residual. Variance positivity gives correlations, and the Jacobi moment Gram formula gives strict positive definiteness and the determinant. The exact-rational reproducibility checker tested 18 concrete branching models and nine moment matrices; these finite tests are corroboration, not a proof of arbitrary-distribution limits.

## Originality — PASS against inspected literature

The closest primary work is Barczy--Bezdány, arXiv:2611.02069v1: its Theorem 3.1 gives a functional weak limit and its Appendix B gives general moment recursions, but the inspected paper does not state the explicit cross-type covariance asymptotics, normalized universal correlations, exact all-type determinant, or deep-type profile. Foster--Ney (1978) gives an earlier Laplace-transform description of one-time weak limits; Quine (1970) treats general multitype moment recursions. The new covariance result is derived from the known framework, not an independent functional convergence theorem. The highly relevant Foster--Ney original could not be read in full; priority beyond available evidence remains a nonzero risk. Accessible indexed mathematical findings about branching and correlation were searched for equivalent and dominating statements, with no identified covering result.

## Value — PASS

This closes an operational gap between a weak population limit and the finite-generation second moments used to quantify dependence: every growing type has a nondegenerate variance, every pair has an explicit positive leading covariance and \(O(n^{-1})\) correlation error, and the entire type-dependence matrix is a single parameter-free strictly positive-definite moment kernel. The full determinant and the limiting deep-type profile quantify multitype dependence without specifying reproduction laws. These are motivated structural features, not a mere restatement of the weak limit.

## Limitations and closest sources

The theorem requires fixed type count, positive adjacent transfers, positive first-type immigrant mean and reproduction variance, and finite second moments. There is no joint high-type population limit.
