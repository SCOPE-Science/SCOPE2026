# Scientific review — universal endpoint amplification in lattice biharmonic smoothing

## Correctness — PASS
The claim is restricted to the nearest-neighbor lattice resolvent and periodic-cycle resolvents. The proof derives the sharp \(\ell^\infty\) operator norm from the absolutely summable convolution kernel. A rescaled-multiplier \(L^1\) limit yields the continuum fourth-order kernel; a separate Parseval-derivative estimate and Cauchy–Schwarz argument establish **uniform absolute-mass tightness**, which is necessary to interchange the norm and scaling limit. Exact integration of the negative lobes yields the displayed coefficient. Absolute summability also justifies periodization, and the fixed-cycle spectral gap proves the opposite-order limit. The residue-based `verify.py` replay passed 452 finite assertions; finite checks are supplementary, not a substitute for the analytic uniform tail proof. No claim is made for arbitrary graph boundaries or a two-parameter uniform limit.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
McElroy's infinite-filter coefficient formulas (2006/2008) and Cornea-Madeira's finite HP-weight formulas (2017) are strong predecessors. The basic oscillatory kernel, negative weights and ordinary diagonalizations are explicitly excluded from the new claim. Yamada's 2020 article examines the mHP spectral representation, with a cycle-graph Fourier description in its appendix, but does not state an exact \(\ell^\infty\) strong-regularization amplification limit or the noncommutation of the iterated finite-cycle limits. The public findings *Only first-difference Whittaker–Henderson smoothing is universally range-preserving* (2026) and *Parity-split positivity thresholds for periodic biharmonic smoothing* (2026) address range preservation and the different coupled regime \(\lambda\asymp n^4\), respectively. The claims compared are not implication-equivalent: none of these stated results evaluates the lattice **absolute impulse-response mass** in the \(\lambda\to\infty\) limit and contrasts it with fixed-cycle strong smoothing. The searches and full-text inspections do not prove global historical priority. Equivalent-kernel terminology in older signal-processing work remains the principal residual risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The sharp absolute-mass coefficient is a dimensionless worst-case input-noise robustness budget, not merely a filter coefficient or numerical table. The strict excess of about 14 percent survives arbitrarily strong smoothing if domain size tends to infinity first. The reversed limit equals one, establishing a concrete, noncommuting domain-size/regularization mechanism. This distinguishes contractivity in energy norms from maximum-coordinate amplification in a commonly used second-difference smoother and identifies a substantive boundary for extrapolating finite-sample intuition to spatially extended signals.
