In a root-doubling recursive tree, the two daughter subtrees repeatedly become identical, yet their size difference exhibits a universal high-moment intermittency law: for every fixed \(r\ge1\), its \(2r\)-th absolute moment is asymptotically \(2^r(2r-1)!!\,\mathrm B(2^r-r,r+1)\) times the \(r\)-th moment of their combined size. Both moments grow exponentially at the exact rate \(r+2^r-1\), exposing rare large imbalance fluctuations that are invisible in first-order subtree-size statistics.
