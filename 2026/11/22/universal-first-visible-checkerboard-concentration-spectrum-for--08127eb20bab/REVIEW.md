# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The theorem is proved for all integers \(q\ge1\) and \(d\ge2q\) and all stated orthogonally invariant measures. The sign character of the region can pair two polynomial parity spaces only if their symmetric difference is the full \(2q\)-set. At degree at most \(q\), equality forces complementary squarefree degree-\(q\) monomials, giving exactly \(\binom{2q}{q}/2\) two-by-two blocks. Uniform-sphere Dirichlet moments yield the explicit normalized coupling \((2/\pi)^q\), with the radial moment canceling. The same blocks lie in the pure spherical harmonics. Independent Gram-matrix reconstruction tests the formula without imposing the block structure. The proof, not the finite tests, establishes the infinite family.

## Originality — PASS with residual priority risk

Full-text examination of Michel–Plattner–Seibert (arXiv:2103.14650v1), sections on general Slepian regions, cap constructions, and the matrix eigenproblem, finds a general framework rather than the stated first-visible checkerboard spectral classification. Bates–Khalid–Kennedy (arXiv:1608.05479) treats efficient arbitrary-region computation, not this evaluated universal spectrum. Searches for spherical checkerboard concentration, coordinate sign products, parity-masked polynomials, and their aliases found nearest published work on hemispherical spectral pairing and Boolean-cube sampling, but neither implies this exact universal \((2/\pi)^q\) shift and complementary-pair multiplicity. Such searches are not proofs of historical priority; unindexed equivalent facts remain a risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Parity-checkerboard observation regions are canonical unions of orthants, and the first degree at which a band sees their geometry is a natural sampling-design invariant. The theorem gives the exact onset, a complete sharp spectrum with explicit extremizers, universal recovery constants, and radial-law robustness simultaneously for every dimension and every checkerboard order, including a pure-harmonic version. It is not a numerical special case or merely a restatement of a general concentration eigenproblem. Higher-band spectra remain open under the present analysis.
