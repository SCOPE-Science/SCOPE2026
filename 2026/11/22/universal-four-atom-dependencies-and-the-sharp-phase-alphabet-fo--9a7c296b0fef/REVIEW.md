## Originality — PASS under bounded comparison

Pfander, *Gabor frames in finite dimensions*, Theorem 11, explicitly constructs one universally dependent Klein-group quartet. Malikiosis, *Spark deficient Gabor frames*, Section 3, proves nonexistence of full spark over noncyclic groups. Krahmer–Pfander–Rashkov provide finite-group short-time Fourier uncertainty bounds, not a complete Klein-group Gabor minor census. Lawrence–Pfander–Walnut's theorem establishes full spark for prime cyclic systems, not this noncyclic matroid. These imply that at least one failure exists, but they do not by themselves imply the symplectic *if and only if* condition, exact multiplicity 240, simultaneous integer and flat witness construction, or the minimal phase alphabet seven. Related indexed findings on finite Gabor phase alphabets concern cyclic systems or Gabor intensity-operator lifting rather than four-vector Klein-group dependence. Claim-level and alias searches did not return a covering theorem or exact table. Residual risk: an unindexed or differently phrased computation may already contain an equivalent complete four-dimensional matroid or phase threshold; no claim of a proof of absolute priority is made.

## Value — PASS

For a canonical finite noncyclic time-frequency system, full spark is structurally impossible. Counting *exactly which* four-atom measurement selections remain invertible is a natural finite robustness invariant: the theorem gives 1580 achievable independent selections and 240 unavoidable failures, with a short symplectic rule recognizing all failures. The seven-phase construction is the smallest quantized flat-window alphabet achieving every attainable independent quartet, and it retains independence of every triple. This is useful in exact finite frame/erasure design beyond a known binary impossibility example. No numerical conditioning or general noncyclic-group extension is asserted.
