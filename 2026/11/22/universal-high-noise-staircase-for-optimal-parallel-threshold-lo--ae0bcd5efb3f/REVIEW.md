# Scientific assessment

## Correctness: PASS
The claim is a fixed-budget, high-noise asymptotic over the **unrestricted deterministic nonadaptive threshold cube**. The exact conditional-likelihood representation reduces the Bayes risk to a finite sum with a uniformly positive denominator for all bit contrasts below one. The Walsh expansion and its uniform Lipschitz-in-threshold remainder justify comparing a clustered design with the repeated-midpoint design to order six. The second-order coefficient uniquely forces every global optimizer toward the midpoint. The fourth-order coefficient's pairwise threshold-distance term bounds all optimal offsets on the squared-contrast scale. At that scale, sorted thresholds reduce to a completed-square finite-dimensional optimization, with a single sorted maximizer and the stated sharp gain coefficient. The proof explicitly handles coincidences, endpoints, existence, sorting, and arbitrary fixed query count. The exact-rational standalone verifier passes 40 nontrivial checks across one through five queries. Those checks support the formulas but are not offered as a substitute for the all-fixed-budget argument.

## Originality: PASS (historical priority qualified)
The closest retrieved published result about two **parallel** noisy thresholds optimizes a symmetric two-location design; its all-contrast two-query formula is consistent with the two-query limiting case here but cannot imply the unrestricted arbitrary fixed-budget staircase or the budget-dependent sixth-order coefficient. A separate related record treats two **adaptive** noisy thresholds after a fixed midpoint first question, so its information and decision constraints are different. Variani et al. analyze asymptotic dyadic-bit repetition, not optimal locations of an unrestricted fixed number of threshold queries in the high-noise limit. Zhou and Hero address resolution-exceedance criteria and coding-scale query asymptotics, rather than minimum posterior squared error at fixed budget and weak signal. The high-noise all-budget optimizer and its sharp coefficient were not found in these inspected texts or the actual mathematical search results. The searches do **not** establish exhaustive priority; the most material residual risk is an unlocated equivalent high-noise Bayesian-design expansion.

## Value: PASS
For a fixed-cost parallel localization experiment, a natural implementation repeats a median query when responses are very unreliable; the theorem proves that this is asymptotically suboptimal for **every** fixed budget of at least three questions, quantifies the precise optimum separation, and supplies the exact sixth-order attainable risk gain. It also distinguishes the two different limits of query count and response reliability: the optimum collapses to a single point, but maintains a nonzero squared-contrast spacing with a universal arithmetic progression, independent of query count except for the number of sites. This is a structural global-design result rather than a reparametrization of a two-query calculation. The improvement is necessarily small at high noise; its precise scaling matters when deciding whether repeated measurements can be improved by deterministic threshold staggering. The scope does not include general adaptive policies, growing budgets, or general priors.
