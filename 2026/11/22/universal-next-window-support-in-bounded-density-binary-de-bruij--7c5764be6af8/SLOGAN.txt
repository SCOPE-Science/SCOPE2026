For binary de Bruijn cycles covering exactly the length-\(n\) words of weight at most \(k\), every locally feasible next-window occurs in some cycle except two unavoidable patterns and, precisely when \(k\ge2\) divides \(n\), two more evenly spaced patterns. A necklace-joining bridge classification explains this exact two-to-four jump for every \(1\le k\le n\).
