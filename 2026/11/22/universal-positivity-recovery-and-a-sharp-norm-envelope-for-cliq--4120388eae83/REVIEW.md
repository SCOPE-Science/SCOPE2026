# Scientific review of the clique-cone semigroup theorem

## Correctness — PASS
The proof constructs all mutually orthogonal spectral projections of the unweighted clique cone, derives the apex, cross-clique and within-clique entries, and uses strict increase of the generator to identify the smallest off-diagonal entry. Differentiating the cross entry proves the all-time positivity dichotomy and uniqueness of the recovery zero. Row-sum stochasticity plus negative-mass counting gives the exact norm bound and its topology-dependent equality condition. The expansion around \(\psi(N)=N\psi(1)\), the limit at large fractional exponent and the strict network-level peak are justified by Taylor expansion, derivative signs and compactness. Exact spectral-projector checks and numerical complete-matrix comparisons replay successfully from `verify.py`. The finite checks do not replace the infinite-parameter analytical proof.

## Originality — PASS, with stated priority risk
The 2023 clique-coalescence article and Kooij's generalized windmill analysis provide spectral antecedents, but do not establish a positivity-recovery time for nonconvex graph semigroups. The closest published 2026 clique-windmill result computes the same three leaf-entry formulas and establishes a criterion for globally **convex** nonnegative filters; it does not cover the locally concave near-origin filters \(e^{-t x^\alpha}\) for \(\alpha>1\) or classify the whole increasing-generator semigroup family. A published complete-multipartite fractional-heat result includes the star subfamily and is explicitly credited. The new claim combines a necessary-and-sufficient two-rate test for arbitrary strictly increasing generators, a graph-partition-independent recovery horizon, and an attained worst-case induced-norm envelope over every heterogeneous clique decomposition. These are implication comparisons, not merely title comparisons. An unindexed theorem with a stronger structural classification would affect historical priority.

## Mathematical value — PASS
For fixed network size, heterogeneous clique communities can have very different internal spectra, yet the exact sign-restoration horizon depends only on two spectral rates. The criterion identifies precisely when a self-adjoint Markov-row-sum semigroup ceases to be positive at early times, and its norm envelope identifies which community structures cause worst possible transient amplification. This gives a structural classification over all increasing spectral generators rather than one tailored exponent or graph instance. The endpoint test can be used for high-order graph diffusion, spectral regularization and non-Bernstein generators without recomputing internal eigenvalues. The known star-specific facts and shared projector algebra are excluded from the claimed contribution.

## Closest literature and limitations
Compared full available texts of the published heterogeneous clique-cone convex-filter result, the complete-multipartite fractional-heat result, and the rational Gauss–Legendre windmill result; inspected the 2023 source on coalescence spectra, the 2019 generalized windmill article and the 2021 full open-access treatment of fractional graph diffusion. The 2018 eventual-positivity source was examined through its publication information and abstract, with a later full-text exposition of the qualitative theory. Some earlier fractional-dynamics manuscripts and unindexed specialized operator-positivity literature were not available in full, leaving nonzero priority risk. The theorem is limited to unweighted cone-over-cliques graphs, increasing real spectral generators and induced counting norms. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
