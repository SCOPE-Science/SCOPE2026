# Scientific assessment: refresh-phase survival fingerprint

## Correctness — PASS

The exact killed-walk sine representation and independent integrated-rate time change yield the finite-time formula for every starting site and phase. Small-frequency spectral scaling gives the completed-period survival amplitude and the first within-period ratio. Uniformity in phase follows from monotonicity of the survival contrast and the continuous limiting profile, without claiming an unproved uniform second-order expansion. Crucially, the spectral weight can change sign: positivity of the fractional cusp coefficient is proved separately using the fractional Bernstein integral and strict decrease of the unit-rate first-passage survival. The finite left derivative follows from a Laplace-derivative domination, while the right derivative is infinite from the positive fractional cusp. The phase minimum and identifiability are strict single-variable inequalities. The packaged checker passed 193 finite assertions across several independent rate-law transforms and initial sites. The test cases are not substitutes for the all-parameter proofs.

## Originality — PASS, with priority qualification

The initiating paper by Levit and Reuveni, arXiv:2609.26925v1, already provides the exact survival spectral integral, the leading \(t^{-1/(2\alpha)}\) tail, and the mean first-passage threshold; none is claimed as new. The 2024 Arutkin–Reuveni work establishes finite-mean jump-rate redraw-time discontinuities in a bounded-domain first-passage density, but neither a heavy-tail fractional survival cusp nor the universal normalized within-period phase asymptotic. Closely relevant published mathematical work on the same model studies the large-distance weak limit, finite-size mean, bounded-rate survival penalty and stationary-phase extreme displacement, not the present fixed-distance, large-time phase fingerprint. A previously published two-time shared-clock persistence theorem addresses a different observable, not this contrast. The proof of the uniform subleading ratio and the positivity/sharp decay of its fractional cusp require additional arguments beyond these stated findings. Possible implicit coverage by a more general semigroup or Tauberian result is a live, nontrivial priority risk; database search results are not a uniqueness certificate.

## Value — PASS

The new object is the *normalized phase-resolved survival* at a deterministic rate redraw, motivated by known first-passage density non-smoothness and by the recently observed heavy-tail mean-time transition. Its universal interior phase dip distinguishes infinite-mean rate distributions from the finite-mean boundary at the first relative order, and strictly identifies the heavy-tail index from any fixed interior phase in the ideal limit. The theorem additionally classifies the one-sided non-differentiability at every redraw and its normalized decay with the number of periods. These are structural, experimentally interpretable observables of an established transport model, not an arbitrary finite table or a mere parameter replacement. It does not establish finite-sample noise robustness or extension to random refresh times.

## Boundaries and remaining risks

Only a deterministic refresh grid, symmetric nearest-neighbor motion, a fixed integer starting site and the stated Laplace regular-variation law are covered. No joint limit in the cusp displacement and number of periods is proved. Full priority is not established; a general inverse-stable-subordination theorem might encompass the phase ratio after additional specialization. Independent scientific validation is outstanding.
