# Mathematical review

## Correctness

PASS. The published coefficient valuation identity reduces the problem to centered valuations of blocks of consecutive integers. A direct divisibility-indicator calculation gives the two-point kernel; at each dyadic modulus, the overlap of two cyclic arcs has its smallest size when the shift is their length. This proves the inequality at every scale. A dyadic rescaling identity fixes the sharp ratio at \(-1/2\), and the two largest relevant dyadic moduli pin down precisely the two equality residues. The convergence and strict upper bound are justified by square-integrable geometric valuation tails. A standalone exact rational checker independently tested 39,769 assertions; these tests do not replace the unrestricted proof.

## Originality

PASS, with residual priority risk. Boros–Moll–Shallit (2003) addressed coefficient valuation questions, and Amdeberhan–Manna–Moll (2007) established the underlying one-point valuation identity and block decomposition. Their inspected mathematical text does not present the signed all-shift covariance kernel, the universal sharp correlation floor, or the complete equality-lag criterion. Public mathematical-index comparisons for shifted factorial-product valuations, carry processes, and dyadic autocorrelations did not identify an implying result. The claim is not the known one-point formula restated: it requires proving the joint extremality of two congruent arcs at every power of two. Unindexed digit-sum increment and factorial-cocycle literature remains a substantive historical-priority risk.

## Value

PASS. The theorem gives a complete and sharp dependence invariant, for every index of a classical coefficient family, rather than a new presentation of individual valuations. Its universal \(-1/2\) lower correlation and the exact dyadic equality shifts identify a rigid negative-dependence phenomenon not visible in the known block-constancy result; the full rational covariance formula also computes arbitrary lags.

## Scientific limitations

The result does not determine higher joint moments, and priority is not independently certified. The checker is finite and is ancillary to the proof.
