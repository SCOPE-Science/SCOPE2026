# Scientific review of the prescribed-commutator fiber theorem

## Correctness — PASS

The claim fixes a finite commutative local ring with \(\mathfrak m^2=0\), \(d=\dim_{\mathbb F_q}\mathfrak m\ge2\), and Heisenberg rank \(h\ge1\). Direct group multiplication makes the commutator exactly the alternating dot product. The analytic proof uses the entire additive character group of the **additive abelian group** of \(R\), not a nonexistent generating character of a non-Frobenius coefficient ring. The crucial ideal \(I_\chi\) is proved to have exactly three possible cardinalities \(t,v,v/q\), with character multiplicities \(1,q-1,t-q\). Restriction to \(\mathfrak m\) genuinely produces an \(\mathbb F_q\)-linear functional, which gives the hyperplane dimension \(d-1\). Fourier orthogonality then yields all three output levels, their multiplicities, positivity, normalization, and the limiting commutativity probability. The normalization counts exactly \(t^{4h}\) horizontal quadruples and \(t^{4h+2}\) ordered group pairs. Replaying `check.py` independently from the packaged path confirms every prescribed target for seven finite-ring models in rank one and for ranks two and three via exact convolution. No general statement is inferred from the finite experiments alone.

## Originality — PASS, bounded priority claim

This finding does **not** claim novelty for determinants over finite fields, length-two chain rings or finite principal ideal rings; the 2016 Choosuwan–Jitman–Udomkavanich paper already handles fixed determinant targets over every principal ideal ring. Its Sections 3–4 require chain/principal hypotheses, while its Section 5 explicitly identifies general finite local rings as a future counting problem. The 2012 Prasad–Singla–Spallone paper contains the strongest matching **nonprincipal ring hypothesis**, but Section 10 studies individual-matrix similarity orbits, not prescribed determinants or group word fibers; its stated Corollary 10.4 implies neither the three fiber sizes nor the group-commuting asymptotic. Schein–Voll's 2014 Heisenberg treatment determines normal-subgroup zeta functions of DVR groups. Three closely related 2026 mathematical records were read in full: the chain-ring commuting-graph classification, chain-ring Heisenberg centralizer lattices, and chain-ring zero-determinant Fourier spectrum. They address distinct invariants and exclude nonprincipal embedding dimension at least two. A further 2026 exact square-zero matrix census over nonprincipal rings studies the polynomial equation \(X^2=0\), not determinant fibers; a square-zero-radical Ramanujan matrix rank paper studies a distinct operator. The indexed searches for synonyms, scalar equivalence, determinant counts, character annihilators and stronger coverage did not identify a statement implying the present formulas. **Historical priority is not certified**: a broader published character-sum theorem could imply this special case, and publication databases do not give a complete novelty guarantee.

## Value — PASS

Prescribed Heisenberg commutators are a natural group word-map invariant refining the zero-output commuting probability. The theorem solves all output fibers, for every symplectic rank, on the first genuinely nonprincipal family of local coefficient rings. The three strict levels distinguish the zero element, nonzero radical, and units; the principal ring \(\mathbb Z/8\mathbb Z\) instead has four determinant fiber sizes and zero-target multiplicity \(736\) rather than \(832\); their sizes and target multiplicities are explicit, and they remain meaningful when the base ring has no generating character. The exact saturation limit \(q^{-4h}\) gives a structural phenomenon for growing embedding dimension rather than only a small parameter count. The older principal-ring determinant paper identifies extension to general local rings as a motivated mathematical problem. The result is **not** claimed as a full local-ring or higher-nilpotency classification.
