# Scientific review

## Correctness

PASS. Reduction to a full-row-rank top coefficient is reversible. The row-lifting lemma relies only on transitivity of the general linear group's right action on surjective row maps and existence of square roots of invertible matrices over the stated field. Rank-two bottom data admit an invertible square completion by the nonzero-product-of-determinant-polynomials argument. Rank-zero data are completed diagonally. Rank-one data have precisely three parabolic conjugacy forms. The characteristic nilpotent form has an explicit three-vector square root, and the specified leading coefficients of the two crucial residual minors do not vanish. All target matrices, coefficient Jordan types and matrix orders \(n\ge7\) are covered. The 120 sampled exact rational checks corroborate, not replace, the proof.

## Originality

PASS on the accessible evidence, with historical priority risk. The closest primary source is Saini, arXiv:2611.19228v1, whose Question 6.1 asks for this high-dimensional nullity-two setting; its earlier theorems settle only \(n=5,6\) and a distinct large-exponent range. Saini–Singh, arXiv:2604.27592v1, treats all dimensions for nullity at most one and the small orders \(n=3,4\). Its statements do not imply all \(n\ge7\) for exponent two and nullity two. Panja–Saini–Singh (2025) treats order two. Claim-level searches of related published records and accessible prior findings found no covering statement. Unindexed equivalents remain possible.

## Mathematical value

PASS. The theorem answers the entire exponent-two part, not an artificially selected matrix example, of a concretely stated current question about coefficient-weighted matrix Waring maps. The construction addresses every rank-\(n-2\) matrix coefficient uniformly and overcomes the nontrivial nilpotent rank-one quotient case through a length-three square-root chain. It does not solve the remaining higher-exponent cases.
