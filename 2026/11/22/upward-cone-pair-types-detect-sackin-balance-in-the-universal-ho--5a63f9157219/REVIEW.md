# Review of the upward-cone Sackin-channel theorem

## Correctness
PASS. The hypotheses identify the complete binary-branching semilinear order. A nonempty antichain yields a rooted binary tree through the first-order definable relation \(C\); upward one-types correspond to nonempty clades. For two cone points, laminar clade pairs determine the complete relation-channel diagram, with two orientations only when the clades coincide. Homogeneity of \((\le,C)\) guarantees that these finite diagrams are the complete types. The ancestor-pair total is \(2S+1\) by an explicit full-binary-tree induction. Every case, including one leaf and the root-stem edge, is covered. A standalone enumeration checks full finite diagrams for all 48 shapes with at most eight leaves. The proof, not bounded enumeration, supports the universal claim.

## Originality
PASS under available evidence, with residual priority risk. Actual published-finding corpus searches for universal homogeneous binary trees, semilinear-order marked antichain types, clade ancestor counts, and the Sackin index found related but non-covering published results. The 2014 original paper's Sections 2 and 3.1 give structure and homogeneity rather than the claimed marked upward-cone pair histogram. The 2021 Sackin article gives the tree-index extrema without model-theoretic types; those extrema are expressly credited. Published full-text comparisons on homogeneous meet-trees and 4-branching leaf \(D\)-sets count other tuple orbit objects. No checked source implies the exact cone-channel identity, though an unindexed equivalent may exist.

## Value
PASS. The upward closure of a named antichain is a canonical definable region of a semilinear order. The count demonstrates a sharp and natural information hierarchy: its unary and aggregate binary orbit counts are shape-blind, but the comparability channels recover a familiar tree-balance invariant with exact attainable endpoints and the first distinguishing base size. This is a structural bridge between logical types and phylogenetic tree balance, not merely a recomputation of the classical Sackin bounds. It does not recover the full topology or claim a general all-base spectrum.

## Scientific limits
The logical count is restricted to two distinct upward-cone points and a finite named antichain. The result relies on the established homogeneity and rooted triple representation. Priority is not certified by negative searches. Classical external path length extrema are credited to earlier work.
