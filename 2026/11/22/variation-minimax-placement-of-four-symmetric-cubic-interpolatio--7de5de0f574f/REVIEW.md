# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
For all \(0<a<1\) and all real four-vectors, the increment decomposition reduces the precise operator norm to three single-step interpolants. Their derivatives are quadratic, their two roots lie strictly in the interval, and their integrated absolute derivatives give \(A(a)\) and \(B(a)\). These positive functions vary monotonically in opposite directions, proving a unique global symmetric-grid minimax. `verify.py` checks the critical polynomial identities and supplies exact rational enclosures for the crossing and optimal value. A 4,000-case random polynomial integration test is supplemental only. The result makes no claims about asymmetric grids.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
The closest fully read public theorem, *Optimal symmetric four-node cubic interpolation for monotone range control* (https://github.com/published-finding corpus/2026/tree/main/2026/10/26/SCOPE-optimal-symmetric-four-node-cubic-interpolation-for-monotone-ran--7f9a4d5c7ee7), uses the same step polynomials but optimizes the largest pointwise range escape for bounded nondecreasing samples. That bound alone does not determine the integral of the absolute derivative, and its optimizer is about \(0.4458209225\), rather than the present \(0.48013692628\ldots\). The uniform-grid range result (https://github.com/published-finding corpus/2026/tree/main/2026/10/4/SCOPE-sharp-monotone-range-defect-of-uniform-four-point-cubic-lagrange--1fad48a6c445) is still narrower in both nodes and interval. Gunzburger–Teckentrup (arXiv:1407.3291) and Berrut–Trefethen (DOI 10.1137/S0036144502417715) treat classical uniform Lebesgue and interpolation stability objectives. A Prestin (1993) BV-interpolation full-text comparison was unavailable; its accessible abstract and opening sections study Jacobi-node weighted-\(L^p\) interpolation error. Shared cubic algebra and potentially unindexed priority are genuine residual risks, not claims of exclusive discovery.

## Value — PASS
A natural approximation-stability objective is to avoid creating oscillation, measured in total variation rather than merely peak range. The result exactly solves the symmetric four-node design and gives distinct, attained outer/central step mechanisms. Relative to equally spaced nodes, optimal placement reduces the sharp amplification from about \(2.00685\) to \(1.42359\); even the four Chebyshev–Lobatto nodes are not optimal for this metric. This is a complete, motivated finite-degree classification, with no speculative higher-degree claims.

## Scope and residual risks
Only symmetric four-point real cubic Lagrange interpolation, whole-interval variation, and fixed interpolation knots are proved. The exact unrestricted four-node optimum and higher degrees are open in this package. Published priority was compared by statement and implication to closest accessible records, but no independent scholarly novelty certification has taken place.
