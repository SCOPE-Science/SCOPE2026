# Self-review

**Correctness — PASS.** The theorem is for real scalars, all integers \(n\ge3\), and all uniform positive rim weights \(\gamma>0\). Each generator is recovered, including its exact length, from an exposed zonotope edge. Linear dependencies of incidence generators identify wheel triangles; for \(n\ge4\), their edge incidence numbers distinguish spokes from rim edges. For \(n=3\), unequal circuit coefficient ratios distinguish the all-rim triangle unless \(\gamma=1\), while the balanced tetrahedral action accounts for precisely the remaining symmetries. The proof contains both the upper-group and the attainment arguments. Exact-rational finite enumerations check six parameter pairs and 5,840 assertions; they are ancillary, not infinite proofs.

**Originality — PASS, with residual bibliographic risk.** The motivating continuous Sobolev rigidity result (Hamad, arXiv:2611.09577v1, Theorem 1.1), the general Lipschitz-free-graph classification (Cúth, Doucha and Titkos, 2024, Section 6), and graphical-zonotope deformation work (Padrol, Pilaud and Poullot, 2023, Sections 1–2) concern different normed spaces or different zonotope invariants. The nearest searched mathematical records address graph variation with an \(\ell_\infty\) vertex term, weighted wheel energy projections, or isometry moduli of cubical hyperplanes, rather than this additive \(\ell_1\) vertex and cycle-gradient norm. The exact symmetry group and unique weight/dimension enhancement were not implied by the inspected statements. A potentially unindexed equivalent theorem on graphic zonotopes or matroid automorphisms remains a risk; unsuccessful search is not proof of absolute novelty.

**Value — PASS.** Classifying all linear isometries of a natural discrete first-order Sobolev norm identifies exactly which spatial and sign symmetries are intrinsic and when an additional reanchoring group appears. It gives a complete group classification across every cycle size and every positive uniform gradient weight, with a sharp isolated critical parameter, rather than an arbitrary finite calculation. The dimensional exception matters for discretized function-space symmetry and illustrates how equality of vertex and gradient penalties changes the geometry of the norm.

**Limitations.** Only real surjective linear self-isometries are classified. No claim is made for nonuniform rim weights, arbitrary graphs, complex scalars, or continuous function spaces. A direct generalization of existing matroid symmetry theory could implicitly contain the classification; independent validation has not occurred.
