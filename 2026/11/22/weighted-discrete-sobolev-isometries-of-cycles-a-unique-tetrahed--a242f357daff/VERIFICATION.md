# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

The norm is the support function of the sum of the 2n segments generated by e_i and γ(e_i−e_(i+1)). No two generator lines coincide for n≥3. A functional vanishing on one line, not the others, exposes a translate of precisely that segment; all exposed edges arise this way. Thus the transpose of an isometry permutes the signed generator vectors exactly, not just their lines. Equivalently the jumps of the gradient at generic points of the nonsmooth hyperplanes recover the same vectors up to sign. Incidence triples are dependent precisely on triangles (one can remove leaves in a forest, using the deleted hub row being the negative sum of retained rows). For n≥4 the only triangles have one rim edge and two spokes; spokes meet two triangles and rim edges meet one. Preserving these sets forces a signed coordinate permutation whose signs agree by adjacency and whose permutation is dihedral. For K₄ the unique all-rim triangle at unequal weight has equal-magnitude circuit coefficients, the other triangles having ratios 1:1:1/γ; exact vectors are needed to distinguish them. This again forces the signed coordinate group. At n=3, γ=1, all six absolute differences of four potentials appear with equal coefficients, so all reanchored permutations are isometries. They realize all permutations of the four triangles, and each edge is the intersection of a unique pair of triangles. The kernel of the triangle permutation fixes the lines of the spokes, so it is diagonal with signs forced equal by the rim vectors. The 24 distinct triangle permutations times the 2 kernel elements give 48, with the global sign independent of the reanchoring subgroup. The displayed R_σ convention reverses the order of permutations under composition, which does not alter the group or its direct product structure.

## originality

PASS

Graphical zonotopes, Whitney's cycle-matroid theorem and the balanced complete-graph permutahedron are prior ingredients. The nearest path-norm result even uses the same weighted-generator and triangle-circuit method. The supported distinct claim is the full cycle classification, including identification of the only weight and dimension at which the weighted representation lifts the tetrahedral group, not the method or the bare automorphisms of a wheel or a permutahedron.

## value

PASS

The additive vertex-and-edge norm is a natural discrete first-order norm on a periodic domain. The problem asks which ordinary spatial and sign symmetries are intrinsic and when a linear reanchoring mixes the roles of mass and gradient. The answer is an exact classification across all dimensions and positive uniform weights, with a sharp parameter exception, not a finite-table recomputation. The fan/path result and bare matroid symmetries do not answer this weighted periodic question.
