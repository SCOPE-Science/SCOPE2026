# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

Hermitian polarity is nondegenerate and sends an isotropic point to its distinct tangent. The orthogonal plane to an isotropic point has that line as radical and anisotropic one-dimensional quotient; the orthogonal plane to an external point is a nondegenerate Hermitian plane, with q+1 isotropic points by the norm map. Thus all external quadratic-field points are ordinary crossings and there are no other intersections. Frobenius fixes the duals of the q+1 points on the subfield conic (odd characteristic) or subfield line (even characteristic); q² external subfield points are crossings. In odd characteristic the dual points lie on a nonsingular conic, so every pair gives a distinct rational intersection and no third rational line passes there. In even characteristic the pencil center is [1:1:1], external because 1+1+1=1; the full pencil covers the rational plane and has a unique line through any other point. The normalization is the disjoint union of the lines with the indicated descent. For odd extensions subtract q(q+1) rational preimages over crossings and add q² crossing images, including those with no rational branch. For even extensions subtract qE. Substituting these counts into the logarithmic definition gives the displayed exponents and coverages at m=1,2. The finite q=2,3 examples are not used as proof of generality.
