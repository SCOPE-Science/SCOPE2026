# Zonal labelings of antiprisms: a three-periodic universal group and sharp strong-group census

## Finding

Consider the standard planar \(n\)-antiprism graph \(A_n\), for every integer \(n\ge4\), and any abelian group \(\Gamma\). A \(\Gamma\)-zonal labeling assigns a nonzero element of \(\Gamma\) to each vertex so the sum at every facial boundary is zero. All solutions of the facial equations, including solutions that assign zero, are uniquely determined by two group elements \(a,b\) when \(3\mid n\), and are identically zero when \(3\nmid n\). Explicitly, in the divisible case the top labels are the periodic triple \((a,b,-a-b)\) and the bottom labels are its two-step cyclic shift. These exhaust the solutions without any finiteness hypothesis on \(\Gamma\).

Consequently, \(A_n\) is \(\Gamma\)-zonal if and only if \(3\mid n\) and \(\Gamma\) has at least three elements. It has a *generative* \(\Gamma\)-zonal labeling precisely when \(3\mid n\), \(\Gamma\) is generated by at most two elements and has at least three elements.

For a finite abelian group of order \(q\), the number of zonal labelings on the **specified graph with its distinguished vertices** is
\[
L_n(\Gamma)=\begin{cases}0,&3\nmid n,\\(q-1)(q-2),&3\mid n.\end{cases}
\]
For \(3\mid n\), the number of orbits of these labelings under the **full graph automorphism group**, with palette elements fixed (no group automorphisms allowed), is
\[
O_n(\Gamma)=\frac{q^2-3|\Gamma[2]|+2|\Gamma[3]|}{6},\qquad \Gamma[d]:=\{g\in\Gamma:dg=0\}.
\]

Every vertex has degree four. Write \(T=\Gamma[4]\), \(t=|\Gamma[2]|\), \(r=|2T|\), for finite \(\Gamma\). The number of **strong** zonal labelings, meaning every vertex label has additive order exactly four, is
\[
S_n(\Gamma)=\begin{cases}0,&3\nmid n,\\t^2(r-1)(r-2),&3\mid n.\end{cases}
\]
Such a labeling exists for an arbitrary abelian group exactly when \(3\mid n\) and \(\Gamma\) contains \((\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z)^2\). Every strong labeling has six distinct permutations of its periodic triple, and therefore the number of strong-labeling automorphism orbits for finite \(\Gamma\) is \(S_n(\Gamma)/6\). A labeling is simultaneously strong and generative if and only if \(3\mid n\) and \(\Gamma\cong(\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z)^2\); then exactly \(96\) such labelings exist, forming \(16\) automorphism orbits.

The integer facial vertex-incidence matrix has Smith normal form with \(2n\) nonzero invariant factors all equal to one when \(3\nmid n\); when \(3\mid n\), it has \(2n-2\) nonzero invariant factors all equal to one, and two zero columns. Equivalently, the universal abelian solution group is respectively \(0\) or \(\mathbb Z^2\), with **no torsion**.

For example, for \(n=6\) and \(\Gamma=(\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z)^2\), there are \(210\) ordinary labelings in \(41\) graph-automorphism orbits, including \(96\) strong and generative labelings in \(16\) orbits. For \(n=4,5,7,8\), there are none, for every abelian group.

## Assumptions and scope

Here \(n\ge4\) throughout. The graph \(A_n\) has top vertices \(t_i\) and bottom vertices \(b_i\), indexed by \(i\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\). Its two cap faces are \((t_0,\ldots,t_{n-1})\) and \((b_0,\ldots,b_{n-1})\); its \(2n\) triangular faces are \((t_i,t_{i+1},b_i)\) and \((b_i,b_{i+1},t_{i+1})\). All face sums use each boundary vertex once. A strong labeling requires *exact* order four, not merely order dividing four. A generative labeling's set of labels generates the entire additive group, including groups that are not finite. Counts refer to labelled vertices, while orbit counts identify colorings only by graph automorphisms and never by automorphisms of \(\Gamma\). The octahedral \(3\)-antiprism is excluded because its graph automorphism group is exceptionally larger.

## Proof

**1. Integer and abelian-group solution space.** Write \(x_i=\ell(t_i)\) and \(y_i=\ell(b_i)\), initially allowing zero. The first triangular equation gives \(y_i=-x_i-x_{i+1}\). Substitution in the second triangular equation gives
\[
(-x_i-x_{i+1})+(-x_{i+1}-x_{i+2})+x_{i+1}=0,
\]
so \(x_i+x_{i+1}+x_{i+2}=0\). Subtracting the adjacent such relations gives \(x_{i+3}=x_i\), and using the three-term relation gives \(y_i=x_{i+2}\).

If \(3\mid n\), choose \(a=x_0\), \(b=x_1\), and set \(c=-a-b=x_2\). Both cyclic consistency and both cap-face equations hold because each cap contains \(n/3\) complete zero-sum triples. Conversely, every solution must arise this way. If \(3\nmid n\), cyclic consistency forces \(x_i=w\) for all \(i\); the triangular equation gives \(3w=0\), and the top cap gives \(nw=0\). As \(\gcd(n,3)=1\), Bézout's identity forces \(w=0\), hence every \(y_i=0\). This works for **all** abelian groups, without assuming cancellation by three. Applied to the free abelian group on the vertices modulo face relations, it gives the claimed torsion-free universal group, and hence the Smith factors.

**2. Nonzero and generative solutions.** With \(3\mid n\), the three distinct positions carry \(a,b,c=-a-b\). Their labels are all nonzero exactly when \(a\ne0\), \(b\ne0\), and \(b\ne-a\). For a finite group of size \(q\), there are \((q-1)(q-2)\) ordered pairs. For arbitrary \(\Gamma\), such a pair exists exactly when \(|\Gamma|\ge3\). Since \(c\in\langle a,b\rangle\), generativity is equivalent to \(\langle a,b\rangle=\Gamma\). If \(\Gamma\) needs two generators, any generating pair must have \(a,b,a+b\ne0\), unless it is cyclic. If \(\Gamma\) is cyclic of order at least three, or infinite cyclic, take \(a=b\) equal to a generator; this pair satisfies the nonzero condition. Thus the stated generative criterion is necessary and sufficient.

**3. Strong labels.** All three elements have order four precisely when \(a,b,c\) have order four. This is equivalent to \(2a\) and \(2b\) being **distinct nonzero** elements of the elementary two-group \(2T\): nonzero doubles force \(a,b\) to have order four, and \(2c=-2a-2b\ne0\) forces \(c\) to have order four. The doubling map \(T\to2T\) has kernel \(\Gamma[2]\), giving exactly \(t^2(r-1)(r-2)\) valid pairs. Since \(2T\) is elementary abelian, \(r\ge4\) is equivalent to the existence of two independent order-four directions and thus a subgroup \((\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z)^2\). Moreover a generative strong pair makes \(\Gamma\) a quotient of \((\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z)^2\); the two independent doubles make that quotient an isomorphism. Conversely, over \((\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z)^2\), every such pair generates by reduction modulo two. There are \(4^2(4-1)(4-2)=96\) pairs.

**4. Full graph symmetries and Burnside's lemma.** Interleave the vertices \((t_0,b_0,t_1,b_1,\ldots,t_{n-1},b_{n-1})\). The graph is the square of this \(2n\)-cycle. For \(n\ge4\), distance-one cycle edges lie in exactly two graph triangles, whereas distance-two cycle edges lie in exactly one. Every graph automorphism must therefore preserve the original cycle, so the full automorphism group is the dihedral group of this cycle. The corresponding interleaved label word repeats \((a,c,b)\), and a cycle rotation and a reflection generate all six permutations of \((a,b,c)\). Thus graph-automorphism orbits correspond exactly to multisets of three nonzero group elements summing to zero. Under the resulting \(S_3\)-action, the identity fixes \((q-1)(q-2)\) triples. Any transposition fixes \(q-|\Gamma[2]|\) triples, since a fixed triple is \((a,a,-2a)\) with \(2a\ne0\). Either three-cycle fixes \(|\Gamma[3]|-1\) triples, since all three coordinates are equal and nonzero. Burnside's lemma yields
\[
\frac{(q-1)(q-2)+3(q-|\Gamma[2]|)+2(|\Gamma[3]|-1)}6
=\frac{q^2-3|\Gamma[2]|+2|\Gamma[3]|}6.
\]
A strong triple cannot have equal entries, since \(a=a\) would force \(c=-2a\) of order at most two. Thus each strong orbit contains six distinct triples, proving \(S_n(\Gamma)/6\) and the \(16\) orbits in the strong-generative case.

## Verification

The standalone standard-library Python 3 program `verify_antiprism.py` enumerates all solutions from two initial labels, checks every facial boundary and order condition, compares the counts with the closed formulas for twelve cyclic-product groups over \(3\le n\le13\), and independently exhausts all vertex assignments for ten small cases. It also computes each permutation orbit and verifies the exact orbit-count formula. All tests pass. Separately, exact integer Smith-form computations for \(4\le n\le12\) find only unit nonzero invariant factors with the predicted ranks; small graph automorphism enumerations for \(4\le n\le7\) return \(4n\) automorphisms. These finite checks corroborate the all-order proof above, not replace it. The script checks \(n=3\) only as an algebraic boundary sanity check; the graph-automorphism theorem explicitly starts at four.

## Relationship to prior work

Bowling introduced arbitrary-abelian-group, generative and strong zonal labelings, studied wheels, and exhibited a strong \((\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z)^2\)-labeling of the **octahedron** (the excluded \(3\)-antiprism). His general necessity theorem for certain 3-connected graphs already implies the subgroup requirement for a strong antiprism; this requirement alone is **not** asserted as new. The present result sharpens the fixed-family sufficiency criterion, proves a mod-three obstruction for every group, and provides complete enumerations and dihedral orbit classes at all \(n\ge4\). Bowling and Low later developed Smith-normal-form methods for identifying compatible groups of plane graphs; the general method does not itself provide these family's explicit invariant factors or counts. Directed-antiprism group-distance-magic labelings from separate literature use neighborhood/arc sums rather than the facial boundary sums here, so they do not imply this result.

## Limitations

The enumeration is for the stated planar embedding and \(n\ge4\); the exceptional octahedron is deliberately excluded from graph-automorphism orbit formulas. Counts for arbitrary infinite groups are not assigned finite numbers. Palette automorphisms are not identified in the orbit census. Independent mathematical validation and exhaustive historical-priority verification have not been performed, and unindexed equivalent formulations may exist. The all-order proof is elementary but has not been independently audited.
