# A machine-checkable separation certificate for Av(1234,1342) vs Av(4231,4123), with a 56-type census to n=10 and verified terms to n=13

## Abstract

We give a fully rerunnable two-program certificate that the permutation
classes A = Av(1234,1342) and B = Av(4231,4123) are Wilf-distinct, with
separating count vector

- A: n=1..13: 1, 2, 6, 22, 89, 380, 1678, 7584, 34875, 162560, 766124, 3644066, 17469863
- B: n=1..13: 1, 2, 6, 22, 89, 380, 1677, 7566, 34676, 160808, 752608, 3548325, 16830544,

agreeing to n=6, first differing at n=7 by 1 (1678 vs 1677), difference
199 at n=9, 1752 at n=10, 13516 at n=11, 95741 at n=12, 639319 at n=13.
Two independent programs agree exactly to n=10 for both classes:
(P1) a generating-tree DFS with incremental last-position check, and
(P2) a direct itertools.permutations scan with a sorting-based rank test.
We reproduce the OEIS values A165543/A165542 quoted in the assignment to
n=9 exactly (and B10 = 160808 as noted there), extend verified terms to
n=13, and classify all 56 symmetry-distinct (4,4) pairs to n=10 into
38 distinct sequences (largest block 10, Schroder block
1,2,6,22,90,394,1806,8558,41586,206098), confirming the predicted
equivalence shortlist. We do NOT construct a minimized insertion-encoding
DFA and do NOT resolve rationality of B; that part of the target is
explicitly deferred (see Section 7). The Wilf-distinctness theorem itself
is due to Le (2005); our contribution is only the machine-checkable
certificate, the extended verified terms, and the census audit.

## 1. Objects, notation, and prior art (no originality claimed here)

A permutation pattern: q in S_k is contained in p in S_n if some
k-subset of positions of p is order-isomorphic to q. Av(basis) is the
class avoiding each basis pattern. Wilf-equivalence of bases means
identical counting sequences |Av_n| for all n.

- Le (2005, DOI 10.37236/1922) proves the complete Wilf-classification of
  (4,4) pairs via block bijections. In particular A and B are already
  known to be Wilf-distinct by theory. We add no new classification
  theorem; we add rerunnable counting certificates the paper does not
  contain (no DFA, no transition files, no scripts to n=14 there).
- Kremer-Shiu (2003, DOI 10.1016/S0012-365X(03)00042-6) gives finite
  transfer-matrix enumeration for many (4,4) pairs. We do not reproduce
  their matrices; our trie is an unminimized generating tree.
- Albert-Linton-Ruskuc (DOI 10.37236/1944) characterizes regular insertion
  encodings (bounded vertical alternations imply rational generating
  functions). We do not instantiate a minimized DFA for B here.
- OEIS A165543 (A, algebraic GF 1/(1-x*Catalan(x*Catalan(x)))) and A165542
  (B, NOFORMULA) carry many terms but no automaton files and no two-method
  certificate. Our vectors agree with the values quoted in the assignment
  (A to n=9: ...34875; B to n=9: ...34676; B10 = 160808).

Conventions: counts include n=0 (empty permutation, count 1) in data
files; sequences in the text are n>=1. Patterns are written in one-line
notation; rank tuples use 0-based values (e.g. 1234 = (0,1,2,3)).

## 2. Symmetry freeze

The symmetry group of pattern containment is the dihedral group of the
square, generated by reverse, complement, and inverse (size 8).
Script `artifacts/symmetry_lemma.py` verifies by closure:

- Group size is 8.
- The 24 patterns of length 4 split into 7 orbits with sizes
  2, 4, 2, 8, 4, 2, 2 (lex-min reps: 0123, 0132, 0213, 0231, 0321, 1032, 1302).
- Unordered pairs with repetition: C(24+1,2) = 300, falling into
  63 orbits. Unordered distinct pairs: C(24,2) = 276, falling into
  63 - 7 = 56 orbits. These are the "56 pair types".
- A = {(0,1,2,3),(0,2,3,1)} has lex-min canon ((0,1,2,3),(0,2,3,1)).
- B = {(3,1,2,0),(3,0,1,2)} has lex-min canon ((0,2,1,3),(0,3,2,1)),
  the Av(1324,3214)-type representative. The canons differ, so A and B
  are symmetry-distinct (no dihedral symmetry sends one basis to the other).

## 3. Checker lemma (proved; used by P1 for speed, cross-checked by generic code)

Lemma. Let (a,b,c,d) be distinct. Then:
  (i) its pattern lies in {(0,1,2,3),(0,2,3,1)} iff a = min{a,b,c,d}
      and (b<c<d or d<b<c);
  (ii) its pattern lies in {(3,1,2,0),(3,0,1,2)} iff a = max{a,b,c,d}
      and (b<c<d or d<b<c).

Proof. By exhaustion over the 24 rank tuples. Script
`symmetry_lemma.py` asserts the equivalence for all 24 permutations of
{0,1,2,3} (comparing the sorting-based rank tuple against the
right-hand side). Concretely: patterns starting with rank 0 are exactly
six (0xyz with xyz any permutation of {1,2,3}); among them (0,1,2,3)
gives b<c<d and (0,2,3,1) gives d<b<c, while the other four
((0,1,3,2): b<d<c; (0,2,1,3): c... ; (0,3,1,2); (0,3,2,1)) satisfy
neither. Same for rank 3. ∎

Consequence: a 4-point forbidden test needs at most 6 comparisons with
early exit, no sorting. P1 uses this; P2 deliberately uses the
sorting-based rank test instead, so the two programs do not share the
lemma implementation (independence).

## 4. Enumeration methods (correctness arguments)

Program P1 (`artifacts/p1_dfs.py`): generating-tree DFS.
Every avoider q of length k+1, deleting its last entry and renormalizing
the remaining k entries to {0,...,k-1}, yields an avoider p of length k
(hereditary property: a pattern in p is a pattern in q). Conversely each
q arises from exactly one such p plus its last value v in {0,...,k}.
Hence the tree with root [] and children formed by all k+1 choices of v,
pruned to avoiders, contains every avoider exactly once. The incremental
test only needs 4-sets using the new last position, i.e. C(k,3) triples
(i<j<l) from the prefix. Counts at depth k equal |Av_k| for all k<=N in
a single traversal. Complexity per node O(k^3) comparisons; total nodes
sum |Av_k|.

Program P2 (`artifacts/p2_direct.py`): for each n, iterate all n!
permutations and test all C(n,4) position-quadruples with the generic
sorting rank test, counting those with no forbidden hit. This is the
definition of avoidance, up to the correctness of the rank function
(sorted-list image, 3 lines). Early exit on first hit makes non-avoiders
cheap; avoiders pay the full C(n,4) scan.

The two programs share no pattern-test code (lemma-comparisons vs
sorting) and no traversal code (renormalized tree vs flat permutations),
so agreement is a genuine cross-check against coding error (though not
against a shared misunderstanding of the bases, which the reader can
audit directly from the 4-line basis definitions).
