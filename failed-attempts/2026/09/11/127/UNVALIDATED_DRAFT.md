# Symmetry-breaking no-go lemma for cyclic 4-ary 3-edge terms

## 1. Statement

**Theorem.** Let `f : {0,1}^4 -> {0,1}` be invariant under cyclic rotation of
its arguments, i.e. `f(x1,x2,x3,x4) = f(x2,x3,x4,x1)` for all inputs.
Then `f` satisfies *none* of the following three 4-ary 3-edge identity
systems (universally quantified over `x,y in {0,1}`):

- **(B) Berman-style:** `e(y,y,x,x)=x; e(y,x,y,x)=x; e(x,x,y,x)=x; e(x,x,x,y)=y`;
- **(A) all-x:** `e(y,y,x,x)=x; e(y,x,y,x)=x; e(x,x,y,x)=x; e(x,x,x,y)=x`;
- **(C) alternate:** `e(y,y,x,x)=x; e(y,x,y,x)=x; e(y,x,x,y)=x; e(x,y,y,y)=y`.

**Corollary (transfer to A4).** Let `A` be any idempotent algebra on
`{0,1,2,3}` generated by an idempotent cyclic 4-ary operation for which every
2-element subset is a subuniverse (the standing hypothesis on A4). Then `Clo(A)`
contains **no rotation-invariant** 4-ary 3-edge term under any of the systems
(B), (A), (C). Hence any 3-edge witness for A4, if one exists, must break
cyclic symmetry; the symmetry-preserving ("symmetry-derived table") route is
impossible.

## 2. Proof (exhaustive census)

Encode a Boolean 4-ary operation as 16 bits: bit `i` is the value on the tuple
with index `i = x1 + 2*x2 + 4*x3 + 8*x4`. Cyclic rotation
`(x1,x2,x3,x4) -> (x2,x3,x4,x1)` permutes the 16 input tuples; invariance is
equality of bits on each rotation orbit. There are `2^16 = 65536` operations
to test — a finite, exact, stdlib-only check.

Result of the exhaustive run (`output/artifacts/verify.py` -> `VERIFY_OK`):

- cyclic-invariant operations: **64** (rotation orbits collapse 16 bits to 6
  free bits, `2^6 = 64`);
- unconstrained 3-edge operations per system: **64** each (B, A, C);
- cyclic-invariant 3-edge operations: **0** under (B), **0** under (A),
  **0** under (C).

Since the count of invariant edge operations is exactly zero in all three
systems, no invariant `f` satisfies any of them. The triple-system coverage
removes any risk that the conclusion is an artefact of one particular
formulation of the edge identities. ∎

*Corollary proof.* Suppose `e in Clo(A)` is rotation-invariant and satisfies,
say, (B) on `A`. Let `S subset A`, `|S| = 2`, be a subuniverse. The restriction
`e|_S`, identified with a Boolean operation via the unique order-isomorphism,
is still rotation-invariant (invariance is an identity in the arguments) and
still satisfies (B) (identities persist under restriction to a subuniverse).
This contradicts the Theorem. The same argument applies to (A) and (C). ∎

forces edge terms" is false at arity 4 in general. This is a structural
