# Taut and R-covered status of the spun depth-one foliation on (7,1) filling of the (−2,3,7)-pretzel complement

## 1. Fixed data and granted hypotheses

Let $K$ be the $(-2,3,7)$-pretzel knot, $N = S^3 \setminus \nu(K)$ its compact
complement, and $M_K$ the closed $3$-manifold obtained by $(7,1)$ Dehn filling.
We take as granted (per the admitted scope):

- (H1) $M_K$ is closed, oriented, hyperbolic (hence irreducible and atoroidal:
  it contains no embedded essential $2$-sphere and no embedded incompressible
  torus).
- (H2) $F_d$ is an explicit $C^2$, transversely oriented (cooriented),
  Reebless, depth-one foliation of $M_K$ obtained by spinning, with a unique
  compact leaf $S$ that is closed, connected, orientable, $2$-sided, of genus
  $g(S) \geq 2$ (quasi-Fuchsian). Every other leaf is noncompact and spirals
  onto $S$.
- (H3) The scope is this fixed pair $(M_K, F_d)$ up to isotopy of the spinning
  construction.

Standard background: a Reeb component is a solid torus with a torus boundary
leaf and interior plane leaves; every Reeb component therefore exhibits a
torus leaf. A vanishing cycle is a loop on a leaf that is essential on nearby
leaves but null-homotopic in its own leaf, detected by a transverse singular
disk. Taut (Sullivan) means there exists a closed transversal meeting every
leaf (we use the cooriented version).

## 2. Main result

**Theorem.** For the fixed pair $(M_K, F_d)$:

1. (Non-tautness.) $F_d$ is **not** taut. In particular alternative (A) of the
   target — "certified closed transversal meeting every leaf, hence taut" —
   is impossible.
2. (No Reeb/torus/vanishing-cycle obstruction.) $F_d$ has **no** Reeb
   component, **no** torus leaf at all, **no** sphere leaf, all leaves are
   $\pi_1$-injective, and there is **no** vanishing cycle and **no**
   null-homotopic transverse loop bounding a singular disk forcing a compact
   leaf of genus $\leq 1$. In particular alternative (B) of the target as
   specified — "explicit embedded Reeb torus/solid-torus component or a
   null-homotopic transverse vanishing-cycle loop forcing a compact
   genus-$\leq$-$1$ leaf" — cannot occur.
3. (Dichotomy refutation and true status.) The stated dichotomy is therefore
   **not exhaustive**: $F_d$ is a Reebless, depth-one, $C^2$ foliation that is
   $\pi_1$-injective and vanishing-cycle-free, yet non-taut. Non-taut
   Reebless foliations of this kind are the missing third case.

**Corollary (complete target resolution by refutation-classification).**
Neither horn of the "taut with full Novikov/$R$-covered package" versus
"Reeb/torus/vanishing-cycle obstruction" disjunction holds for $(M_K,F_d)$.
The correct classification is: Reebless, depth-one, $\pi_1$-injective, no
vanishing cycles, non-taut. The $R$-covered status of the leaf space is left
open (see §5); it is not needed since horn (A) already fails at tautness.

## 3. Proofs

**Lemma 1 (homology).** $H_1(M_K;\mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}/7$ and
$H_2(M_K;\mathbb{Z}) = 0$. In particular every closed orientable surface in
$M_K$, including $S$, is null-homologous.

*Proof.* $H_1(N) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ generated by the meridian $\mu$; the
longitude $\lambda$ is null-homologous in $N$. Filling along $7\mu+\lambda$
imposes the single relation $7\mu + \lambda = 0$, i.e.\ $7\mu = 0$. Since the
meridian coefficient $7 \neq 0$, $H_1(M_K) \cong \mathbb{Z}/7$, finite. For a
closed oriented $3$-manifold, Poincaré duality plus the universal coefficient
theorem give $H_2(M_K;\mathbb{Z}) \cong H^1(M_K;\mathbb{Z}) \cong
\mathrm{Hom}(H_1(M_K),\mathbb{Z}) = 0$. ∎

**Lemma 2 (no closed transversal meets $S$; $F_d$ not taut).**
No closed transversal of $F_d$ meets the compact leaf $S$. Hence $F_d$ is not
taut.

*Proof.* $F_d$ is transversely oriented, so every intersection of a closed
transversal $\gamma$ with a leaf has the same sign; each geometric
intersection contributes $+1$ to the algebraic intersection pairing
$\langle [\gamma],[S]\rangle$. By Lemma 1, $[S] = 0$ in $H_2(M_K)$, so the
algebraic pairing is $0$ for every $[\gamma]$. If $\gamma$ met $S$, the
pairing would be a positive integer — contradiction. Thus no closed
transversal meets $S$, while tautness requires a closed transversal through
*every* leaf. ∎

**Lemma 3 (Thurston-norm corroboration).** The same conclusion follows from
W. Thurston's theorem that a compact leaf of a taut foliation minimizes the
Thurston norm in its homology class.

*Proof.* Recall Thurston, *A norm for the homology of 3-manifolds*,
Mem. Amer. Math. Soc. 339 (1986), §3: if $\mathcal{F}$ is a transversely
oriented taut foliation and $S$ a compact connected leaf with $\chi(S)\le 0$,
then $x([S]) = \chi_-(S) = -\chi(S)$. Here $[S]=0$ so $x([S]) = x(0) = 0$,
whereas $\chi_-(S) = 2g(S)-2 \geq 2 > 0$ since $g(S)\geq 2$. So $S$ cannot be
a compact leaf of any taut foliation; $F_d$ is not taut. This is independent
of Lemma 2. ∎

**Lemma 4 (Novikov package; no Reeb, no vanishing cycle, $\pi_1$-injectivity).**
All leaves of $F_d$ are $\pi_1$-injective in $M_K$; there are no vanishing
cycles, no null-homotopic transverse loops bounding singular disks, and no
Reeb components.

*Proof.* This is S. P. Novikov, *Topology of foliations*, Trans. Moscow Math.
Soc. 14 (1965), applied to a $C^2$ foliation of a closed $3$-manifold:
Reebless implies every leaf includes $\pi_1$-injectively and there are no
vanishing cycles (see also Candel–Conlon, *Foliations I*, Ch. VIII–IX, or
Calegari, *Foliations and the Geometry of 3-Manifolds*). Reeblessness is
granted in (H2); the $C^2$ hypothesis is exactly what Novikov's theorem
needs. ∎

**Lemma 5 (no torus-leaf or sphere-leaf obstruction).** $F_d$ has no torus
leaf, no sphere leaf, and no compact leaf of genus $\leq 1$.

*Proof.* By (H2) the only compact leaf is $S$ with $g(S)\geq 2$. Every torus
or sphere is compact, so any torus or sphere leaf would be a compact leaf
distinct from $S$ — impossible. Hence no Reeb-boundary torus can exist
(every Reeb component has a torus boundary leaf), consistent with Lemma 4.
Belt and braces: hyperbolicity (H1) independently forbids any embedded
incompressible torus or essential sphere in $M_K$ (atoroidal + irreducible),
so no transverse essential torus exists either. ∎

*Proof of Theorem.* Claim 1 follows from Lemma 2 (primarily) and Lemma 3
(independently). Claim 2 follows from Lemmas 4–5: Reeblessness plus Novikov
gives $\pi_1$-injectivity and excludes vanishing cycles/singular disks, and
the unique-compact-leaf structure plus hyperbolicity excludes all torus-leaf
and genus-$\leq$-$1$ compact-leaf obstructions. Claim 3 is the conjunction:
horn (A) fails by Claim 1, horn (B) is impossible by Claim 2, while the
conjunction of the proven properties is exactly the stated third case. ∎

## 4. Remarks on originality and method

No new general theorem is claimed; the argument applies standard tools
(Dehn-filling homology, intersection pairing, Thurston's norm theorem,
Novikov's theorem, atoroidality of closed hyperbolic manifolds) to the fixed
admitted pair. The original increment is the refutation itself: identifying
that the target dichotomy is not exhaustive for this pair and establishing
the correct third-case classification with two independent proofs of
non-tautness. No computation (e.g.\ SnapPy) was required: hyperbolicity and
Reeblessness are granted inputs, and the deduction is purely homological plus
cited foliation theorems. A SnapPy install was attempted but the sandbox had
no package installer; it is unnecessary for the proof.

## 5. Limitations, conjectures, and uncertainty (explicitly separated)

- **Proved:** non-tautness (two routes); $\pi_1$-injectivity; no vanishing
  cycles; no Reeb components; no torus/sphere/genus-$\leq$-$1$ leaves.
- **Assumed (granted, not re-proved):** hyperbolicity of $M_K$; existence and
  regularity ($C^2$, Reebless, depth-one, unique compact quasi-Fuchsian leaf
  of genus $\geq 2$) of the spinning construction; transverse orientability
  of the explicit $F_d$ (standard for spun fibrations; if a non-coorientable
  variant were meant, it is non-taut a fortiori under Sullivan's definition).
- **Open (not claimed):** whether the leaf space of the lift of $F_d$ to the
  universal cover is $\mathbb{R}$ ($R$-covered status). This is irrelevant to
  the refutation because horn (A) already fails at tautness, but a full
  $R$-covered determination would need further analysis (e.g.\ branching of
  the lifted leaf space at lifts of $S$) and is left for future work.
- The argument uses only that the filling slope has nonzero meridian
  coefficient (here $7$); it applies verbatim to any such nonzero-meridian
  filling with the same foliation inputs.
