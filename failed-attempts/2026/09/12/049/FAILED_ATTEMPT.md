## Problem

Prove or disprove: let E1, E2 be bounded disjoint open subsets of R^2 with finite perimeter, areas |E1|=|E2|=1, and let P denote total cluster perimeter (H^1 measure of the union of boundaries, shared interface counted once). Assume the cluster E=(E1,E2) is regular stationary for perimeter under area-preserving variations: its boundary is a finite union of C^1 circular arcs meeting only at interior triple junctions with 120-degree angles, each arc has constant signed curvature satisfying outer-arc curvature k_i=p_i and interface curvature k_12=p_1-p_2 for Lagrange multipliers p_1,p_2. Assume further each Ei is connected (up to null sets) and the singular set consists of exactly two triple junctions. Then E is congruent (by a Euclidean isometry, up to null sets) to the standard equal-area planar double bubble: two congruent circular lobes of area 1 separated by a straight segment, with three arcs meeting at the two vertices at 120 degrees. Scope is fixed equal areas (1,1); by scale invariance this covers all equal areas A>0. A complete answer is either a rigorous proof of congruence from these hypotheses, or an explicit counterexample cluster with verification of areas, connectedness, two-vertex 120-degree stationarity with stated pressures, and proof it is not congruent to the standard configuration.
