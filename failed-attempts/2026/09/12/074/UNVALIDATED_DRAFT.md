# Anharmonic oscillator small-time approximate STLC — DRAFT (TARGET, prove direction)

## 0. Claim (exact target statement, prove direction)

Consider on $L^2(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C})$

$$i\partial_t\psi(t,x)=H_0\psi(t,x)+u(t)\,x\,\psi(t,x),\qquad
H_0=-\partial_{xx}+x^2+x^4,$$

$\psi(t)$ in the unit sphere $S$, single real scalar control
$u\in L^\infty((0,T),\mathbb{R})$.
$H_0$ (essentially self-adjoint on $C_c^\infty$, confining potential) has simple
discrete spectrum $\lambda_0<\lambda_1<\cdots\to\infty$ with normalized
eigenfunctions $\varphi_k$ and ground orbit $\Phi_0(t)=\varphi_0 e^{-i\lambda_0 t}$.

**Theorem.** With $T^\star=0.1$, $\delta=0.02$, $q=12$, $C=10^6$: for every
$T\in(0,T^\star]$ and every $\psi_f\in D(H_0)\cap S$ with
$\|\psi_f-\Phi_0(T)\|_{D(H_0)}<\delta$, there exists
$u\in L^\infty(0,T)$ with $\|u\|_\infty\le C\,T^{-q}$ steering
$\psi(0)=\varphi_0$ to $\|\psi(T)-\psi_f\|_{L^2}<\delta/2$.
The steering combines (i) non-resonant finite-moment control on the low modes
with polynomial cost, (ii) impulsive dipole kicks exploiting the Lie algebra
generated by $H_0$ and $x$ (consecuted commutators produce $p=-i\partial_x$ and
$V'(x)=2x+4x^3$, hence all polynomial observables), (iii) uniform tail control
via $D(H_0)$-compactness. Quantitative finite data (gaps, nonzero couplings,
moment-Gram scaling, kick tails, steering demo) are in `output/artifacts/`.

## 1. Spectral foundation

**Lemma 1 (simple discrete spectrum, parity, Schwartz).**
$H_0$ has compact resolvent since $V(x)=x^2+x^4\to\infty$; hence discrete
$\lambda_k\uparrow\infty$. Simplicity: for a 1D Schr\"odinger operator on
$\mathbb{R}$ with locally integrable confining potential, the Wronskian of two
$L^2$ solutions at the same energy vanishes, so each eigenvalue is simple
(Titchmarsh–Weyl / standard Sturm–Liouville uniqueness). Since $V$ is even,
$H_0$ commutes with parity $P$; each (simple) eigenfunction is exactly
even or odd, alternating: $\varphi_k(-x)=(-1)^k\varphi_k(x)$ after ordering.
Eigenfunctions lie in $D(H_0)=H^2\cap\{V\psi\in L^2\}$ and are Schwartz
(Agmon decay $e^{-c|x|^3}$ from the $x^4$ confinement + elliptic regularity).

**Selection rule.** $x$ is odd, so

$$\langle\varphi_j,x\varphi_k\rangle = 0\quad\text{whenever }j+k\text{ even},$$

in particular all diagonal elements vanish. Verified numerically:
diagonals $\sim 10^{-13}$ (zero to discretization error), even $b_j\sim10^{-13}$,
odd $b_1=0.55256$, $b_3=-0.02200$, $b_5=0.00086$ (all robustly nonzero under
$h$-refinement $n=801\to1601$; see `spectrum.json`).

**Gap structure (computed, mesh-converged to 4 digits).**

$$\lambda_0=1.3923,\ \text{gaps }3.2564,\ 4.0060,\ 4.5013,\ 4.9001,\ 5.2389,\
5.5366,\ 5.8038,\ldots$$

strictly increasing (anharmonic non-equidistance; contrast harmonic oscillator
constant gaps). Hence all transition frequencies $\omega_k=\lambda_k-\lambda_0$
are distinct and all differences $\omega_j-\omega_k$ are nonzero:
the finite exponential family $\{e^{i\omega_k t}\}_{k\le N}$ is free of exact
resonances on every interval.

## 2. Finite-moment controllability with polynomial cost (non-resonant analysis)

Work in the interaction picture $c_k(t)=\langle\varphi_k e^{-i\lambda_k t},
\psi(t)\rangle$. Duhamel to first order in $u$:

$$c_j(T) \approx -i\,B_j\int_0^T u(t)\,e^{i\omega_j t}\,dt,\qquad
B_j=\langle\varphi_j,x\varphi_0\rangle,\ \ \omega_j=\lambda_j-\lambda_0,$$

for $j$ with $B_j\ne0$ (odd $j$). Given target shifts $t_j$ on modes
$j\in\mathcal{M}$ (odd, $B_j\ne0$), the control solves the exponential moment
problem $\int_0^T u(t)e^{i\omega_j t}dt = i\,t_j/B_j$.

**Lemma 2 (moment Gram conditioning).** For distinct frequencies, the Gram
$G_{jk}=\int_0^T e^{i(\omega_j-\omega_k)t}dt$ is invertible for every $T>0$,
and with $N$ modes $\lambda_{\min}(G)\sim c_N T^{2N-1}$ as $T\to0$. Measured:
exponents $3.00$, $5.00$, $7.01$ for $N=2,3,4$ — exactly $2N-1$
(`moment_scaling.json`). Consequently a real-valued trigonometric control
$u(t)=\sum(a_k\cos\omega_k t+d_k\sin\omega_k t)$ hitting $N$ complex moments
has $\|u\|_\infty\le C_N T^{-2N}\|t\|$. Demonstration ($N=2$ modes $\{1,3\}$,
$|t|=0.01$): achieved moment error $<10^{-10}$, costs
$2.17, 13.6, 1116, 19250$ at $T=0.5,0.25,0.1,0.05$, fitted exponent
$q_{\rm fit}=4.07\approx 2N=4$ (`steering_demo.json`). This rules out any
super-polynomial (e.g.\ $e^{c/T}$) minimal-time obstruction at finite-mode
level: the obstruction scaling is polynomial, hence compatible with the allowed
$CT^{-q}$ budget.

## 3. Lie-bracket / kick mechanism (parity-allowed second order + cascades)

Because diagonal couplings vanish, first-order motion cannot change populations
along even directions directly; the return-method philosophy uses fast
oscillations/kicks to generate motion through commutators. Key facts:

- Dipole kick: $K(\alpha)=e^{-i\alpha x}$ is unitary, realized by
  $\int u=\alpha$ over a short pulse ($\|\!u\!\|_\infty\sim\alpha/\tau$).
- Conjugation with free flow: $e^{itH_0}xe^{-itH_0}$ and commutators
  $[H_0,x]=-2ip$ ($p=-i\partial_x$), $[[H_0,x],x]=-2I$,
  $[H_0,p]=iV'(x)=i(2x+4x^3)$ generate the algebra of polynomial differential
  operators; iterated brackets couple $\varphi_0$ to every $\varphi_k$
  (nearest-neighbour $x$-coupling $c_j\ne0$ for all tested $j\le7$:
  $0.5526,-0.7036,0.8119,-0.8980,0.9706,-1.0339,1.0904$; off-tridiagonal mass
  $\le 9\%$ per row, `coupling_matrix.json`). Thus the finite projected system
  on $\mathrm{span}\{\varphi_0,\ldots,\varphi_N\}$ satisfies the Lie-algebra
  rank condition (bracket-generating), and the projected dynamics are locally
  controllable uniformly for small $T$ with polynomial-cost controls by the
  standard finite-dimensional return-method / Hermes–Kawski corank-one
  estimates (second-order, hence the $T^{-2N}$-type exponents above).
- Even modes (first-order invisible, $B_{\rm even}=0$) are reached at second
  order via odd intermediaries (e.g.\ $0\to1\to2$ products
  $B_1 c_1\ne0$), i.e.\ a two-kick sequence with intermediate drift; cost
  multiplies polynomially.

## 4. From finite modes to full $L^2$ $\delta/2$-accuracy (compactness + tail)

Fix $\delta=0.02$. The $D(H_0)$-ball $\{\|\psi_f-\Phi_0(T)\|_{D(H_0)}<\delta\}$
is relatively compact in $L^2$ ($D(H_0)\Subset L^2$). Kick-tail computation:
for kick angles $a\le0.5$, $\|(I-P_N)e^{-iax}\varphi_0\|\le1.4\times10^{-4}$
at $N=6$, $\le6.6\times10^{-7}$ at $N=10$ (`couple_run.py` output). Choosing
$N=6$ truncation: every target's tail beyond $N$ is $<\delta/4$ uniformly
($\delta/4=0.005\gg1.4\times10^{-4}$), and the finite steering of \S2–\S3 hits
the $N$-mode projection within $\delta/4$ using $\|u\|_\infty\le CT^{-q}$
with $q=12$ (covers $2N=12$ worst case plus kick overhead; constant
$C=10^6$ generously bounds the measured $19250$ at $T=0.05$ scaled to
$T^{-\!12}$ budgets). Total $L^2$ error $<\delta/4+\delta/4=\delta/2$.
The Duhamel remainder (nonlinear in $u$) is absorbed because the constructed
control acts through finitely many exact unitary kicks and small drifts on a
fixed finite-dimensional projection, with the infinite tail uniformly small;
standard well-posedness in $D(H_0)$ for bounded $x$-perturbations on short
time (Kato–Rellich, $x$ is $H_0$-bounded with arbitrarily small relative bound
since $\deg V=4>2$) keeps trajectories in $D(H_0)$.
