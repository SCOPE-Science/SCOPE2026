# Hyperbolic fixed-window vertex fluctuation law and CLT

## Claim (TARGET)

Let $\mathbb H^2$ be the hyperbolic plane of curvature $-1$, $\eta_\lambda$ a
Poisson process of intensity $\lambda>0$ (w.r.t. hyperbolic area), and $B_1$ the
geodesic ball of radius $1$ about a fixed origin $o$. Let $N_\lambda$ be the
number of vertices of the Poisson–Voronoi tessellation generated by
$\eta_\lambda$ that fall in $B_1$.

Define
$$\mu^* = 4\pi(\cosh 1-1) = 2\,\mathrm{vol}(B_1) \approx 6.8246,\qquad
  v^* = 3.$$

Then:

1. **Mean.** $\displaystyle \frac{\mathbb E N_\lambda}{\lambda}\to\mu^*$ as
   $\lambda\to\infty$, with
   $\mathbb E N_\lambda = \mu^*\lambda + O(\lambda^{1/2}\log^4\lambda)$,
   the error coming from boundary and curvature corrections.
2. **Variance lower bound.**
   $\displaystyle \liminf_{\lambda\to\infty}\frac{\mathrm{Var}(N_\lambda)}{\lambda}
   \ge 8\pi(\cosh\tfrac12-1)\approx 3.2076 > v^*.$
3. **CLT.** With $\sigma_\lambda^2=\mathrm{Var}(N_\lambda)$,
   $$\frac{N_\lambda-\mathbb EN_\lambda}{\sigma_\lambda}\ \Longrightarrow\ N(0,1),
     \qquad \lambda\to\infty,$$
   with Wasserstein rate $O(\lambda^{-1/4}\log^K\lambda)$ for some absolute $K$.

The proof localizes hyperbolic geometry to Euclidean geometry at the
interaction scale $\lambda^{-1/2}$ (hyperbolic-to-Euclidean charts with
relative distortion $O(\lambda^{-1})$), then transfers the Euclidean
stabilization / Malliavin–Stein machinery.

## 1. Setup and hyperbolic-to-Euclidean localization

Work in geodesic normal coordinates at $o$: $ds^2 = dr^2+\sinh^2 r\,d\theta^2$,
area element $dA = \sinh r\,dr\,d\theta = J(r)\,r\,dr\,d\theta$ with
$J(r)=\sinh r/r = 1+r^2/6+O(r^4)$, verified numerically in
`artifacts/compute_constants.py` ($(J(s)-1)/s^2\to 1/6$).
For a disk of radius $R$, $A_{\mathbb H}(R)=2\pi(\cosh R-1)=\pi R^2(1+R^2/12+O(R^4))$;
in particular $A_{\mathbb H}(R)\ge \pi R^2$ for all $R\ge 0$ (since
$\cosh R-1\ge R^2/2$), and $A_{\mathbb H}(R)\le \pi R^2(1+CR^2)$ for $R\le 1$.
The fixed window has $\mathrm{vol}(B_1)=2\pi(\cosh 1-1)\approx 3.4123$.

Rescale: put $\varepsilon=\lambda^{-1/2}$. A ball of Euclidean-normal radius
$s$ has hyperbolic area $\lambda^{-1}\pi s^2(1+O(s^2\lambda^{-1}))$; i.e. at
interaction scale $s=O(1)$ (hyperbolic radius $O(\varepsilon)$) the metric is
Euclidean up to relative error $O(\lambda^{-1})$. This is the sole source of
curvature error, and it is one full power of $\lambda$ smaller than the main
terms — hence the fixed-curvature, high-intensity limit inherits Euclidean
first-order asymptotics with an explicit $\mathrm{vol}(B_1)$ volume factor.

The Voronoi vertex functional is *stabilizing* in the sense of Penrose–Yukich:
adding a point $x$ changes the tessellation only inside the union of Delaunay
disks incident to $x$, whose radii have exponential tails because a disk of
radius $R$ is empty with probability
$\exp(-\lambda A_{\mathbb H}(R))\le \exp(-\lambda\pi R^2)$.
Hence there is a stabilization radius $R_x$ with, for $t$ large,
$\mathbb P(R_x > t\varepsilon)\le C\exp(-c t^2)$ uniformly in $\lambda$ and in
$x\in B_{1+\delta}$ (Lemma 3.1 below). Moment bounds
$\sup_{\lambda\ge 1, x}\mathbb E|D_xN_\lambda|^p<\infty$ for all $p$ follow
from the same empty-disk estimate plus standard Poisson moment bounds
($D_x$ = add-one cost operator).

## 2. Mean asymptotics (Theorem A)

**Step 1 — Slivnyak–Mecke representation.** A vertex is determined by an
unordered triple $\{y_1,y_2,y_3\}\subset\eta_\lambda$ in general position whose
circumdisk $D(y_1,y_2,y_3)$ (hyperbolic circumdisk) is empty of other points,
with circumcenter in $B_1$. By the order-3 Slivnyak–Mecke formula,
$$\mathbb EN_\lambda = \frac{\lambda^3}{6}\int_{(\mathbb H^2)^3}
  \mathbf 1\{c(y)\in B_1\}\,e^{-\lambda A_{\mathbb H}(D(y))}\,dA^3(y),$$
$c(y)$ the circumcenter, $A_{\mathbb H}(D(y))$ the hyperbolic area of the
circumdisk. General-position failure (collinear/concircular degeneracies) is a
null set for the diffuse intensity measure.

**Step 2 — Centered coordinates and transfer.** Write each triple via its
circumcenter $c$ and circumradius $R$, plus angular variables. The Jacobian of
$(y_1,y_2,y_3)\mapsto(c,R,\theta_1,\theta_2,\theta_3)$ in constant curvature
$-1$ equals its Euclidean counterpart times $\prod_i J(d(c,y_i))/J$-factors
$\to 1$ as $\lambda\to\infty$ at the contributing scale $R=O(\varepsilon)$
(see e.g. the Blaschke–Petkantschin formula in $\mathbb H^2$; the curvature
correction to the Jacobian is $1+O(\lambda^{-1}(1+\rho^2))$ after the change of
variables $\rho=R/\varepsilon$). Substituting $u=\lambda A_{\mathbb H}(D)$,
$dA^3$-density $\asymp R^5\,dR\,dc\,d\theta^3$ at small $R$, the integral
factorizes:
$$\mathbb EN_\lambda = \lambda\int_{B_1}\!\!\Big[\int_0^\infty\!\!\int
  \Phi(\rho,\theta)\,e^{-u(\rho,\theta,\lambda)}\,d\theta\,d\rho\Big]dc
  + \mathrm{Err},$$
where $\Phi$ is the universal Euclidean kernel and
$u(\rho,\theta,\lambda)=\pi\rho^2+O(\lambda^{-1}\rho^4)$.
The bracket converges, uniformly for $c$ at distance $\ge M\varepsilon\log\lambda$
from $\partial B_1$, to the Euclidean vertex intensity $2$ (Miles' formula:
unit-intensity Poisson–Voronoi in $\mathbb R^2$ has vertex intensity $2$;
equivalently mean $2$ vertices per unit area). The Euclidean value $2$ is
corroborated computationally in `artifacts/check_vertex_density.py`
(estimated density$/\lambda = 1.93, 2.03, 2.03$ at $\lambda=15,30,60$).

**Step 3 — Errors.** (i) Curvature: relative $O(\lambda^{-1})$ on the kernel
$\times$ volume $O(\lambda)$ gives $O(1)$. (ii) Boundary strip of width
$M\varepsilon\log\lambda$ around $\partial B_1$: area $O(\varepsilon\log\lambda)$,
contribution $O(\lambda^{1/2}\log\lambda)$. (iii) Large-$R$ tails:
$\exp(-c\lambda R^2)$ makes $R\gg\varepsilon\log\lambda$ negligible
(superpolynomially small). Altogether
$$\mathbb EN_\lambda = 2\,\mathrm{vol}(B_1)\,\lambda
  + O(\lambda^{1/2}\log^4\lambda),$$
i.e. $\mathbb EN_\lambda/\lambda\to \mu^*=2\,\mathrm{vol}(B_1)=4\pi(\cosh1-1)
\approx 6.8246>0$.

## 3. Variance lower bound (Theorem B)

Use the Fock-space / chaos representation of Poisson functionals:
for $F=N_\lambda$ with $\mathbb EF^2<\infty$,
$$\mathrm{Var}(F)\ge \lambda\int_{\mathbb H^2} (\mathbb E D_xF)^2\,dA(x),$$
the first-chaos (Hoeffding) projection lower bound
($D_xF=F(\eta+\delta_x)-F(\eta)$ is the add-one cost).
This holds for any square-integrable Poisson functional; square-integrability
follows from $\mathbb EN_\lambda^2<\infty$ via stabilization moments.

**Key lemma.** For $x$ in the inner ball $B_{1/2}$,
$\mathbb E D_xN_\lambda \to 2$ as $\lambda\to\infty$, uniformly on $B_{1/2}$.

*Proof.* $D_xN_\lambda$ = (# vertices created) − (# destroyed) when inserting
$x$. Created vertices are the circumcenters of Delaunay triangles incident to
$x$ — all lie within $O(\varepsilon\log\lambda)$ of $x$, hence inside $B_1$
for $x\in B_{1/2}$ with overwhelming probability (stabilization tail).
The local picture at scale $\varepsilon$ converges to the Euclidean
Poisson–Delaunay picture (curvature relative error $O(\lambda^{-1})$), in which
the expected net add-one cost is identified exactly by the torus Euler
argument: on a flat torus every Voronoi vertex is trivalent, so with
$F=n$ cells, $3V=2E$ and $V-E+F=0$ give $V=2n$ exactly; inserting one point
increases the vertex count by exactly $2$. Localizing via stabilization
(insertion effects are confined to $O(\varepsilon\log\lambda)$ with
exponential tails, so torus-vs-plane boundary discrepancies are
superpolynomially small in expectation) yields $\mathbb ED_xN_\lambda\to 2$,
consistently with the Russo–Margulis identity
$\frac{d}{d\lambda}\mathbb EN_\lambda=\int\mathbb ED_xN_\lambda\,dA$ since
$\frac{d}{d\lambda}\mathbb EN_\lambda\to\mu^*=2\,\mathrm{vol}(B_1)$.
Uniformity on $B_{1/2}$ holds because the distance to $\partial B_1$ is
$\ge 1/2\gg\varepsilon\log\lambda$, so boundary effects are exponentially
small uniformly. ∎

Consequently, by Fatou's lemma on $B_{1/2}$,
$$\liminf_{\lambda\to\infty}\frac{\mathrm{Var}(N_\lambda)}{\lambda}
  \ge \int_{B_{1/2}} \lim_{\lambda\to\infty}(\mathbb ED_xN_\lambda)^2\,dA
  = 4\,\mathrm{vol}(B_{1/2}) = 8\pi(\cosh\tfrac12-1)\approx 3.2076.$$
Since $3.2076>3$, we take $v^*=3$: 
$\liminf \mathrm{Var}(N_\lambda)/\lambda\ge v^*>0$.

## 4. CLT (Theorem C)

Apply the second-order Poincaré inequality of Last–Peccati–Schulte for Poisson
functionals: for normalized $F=(N_\lambda-\mathbb EN_\lambda)/\sigma_\lambda$,
$$d_W(F,Z)\le C(\gamma_1+\gamma_2+\gamma_3),\qquad Z\sim N(0,1),$$
where $\gamma_i$ are integrals of $|D F|$, $|D^2F|$ moments. With exponential
stabilization, $|D_xN_\lambda|$ has uniformly bounded $p$-th moments and
$D^2_{x,y}N_\lambda=0$ unless $|x-y|\lesssim \varepsilon\log\lambda$; the
standard computation for stabilizing functionals (cf. Lachièze-Rey–Peccati,
Schulte–Yukich) gives $\gamma_i = O(\lambda^{-1/4}\log^K\lambda)$ provided
$\sigma_\lambda^2\ge c\lambda$ — which is Theorem B
($\sigma_\lambda^2\ge 3\lambda$ eventually). Curvature enters only through the
uniformity of stabilization tails (Section 1: $A_{\mathbb H}\ge A_{\mathrm{Eucl}}$
makes hyperbolic empty-disk tails *lighter* than Euclidean ones, so Euclidean
tail bounds transfer verbatim as upper bounds) and through the
$O(\lambda^{-1})$-relative Jacobian errors, which are absorbed. Hence
$d_W\to 0$: Gaussian convergence with an explicit polynomial rate.

The fixed window causes no Golomb-type obstruction: $B_1$ has fixed positive
volume, $\sigma_\lambda^2\asymp\lambda$ (upper bound $\mathrm{Var}\le C\lambda$
also follows from stabilization: $\mathrm{Var}(N_\lambda)\le
\lambda\int\mathbb E(D_xN)^2\le C\lambda$), so the normalization is
non-degenerate and the limit is standard normal.
