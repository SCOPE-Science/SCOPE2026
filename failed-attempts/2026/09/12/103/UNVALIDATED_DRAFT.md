# Relaxed modules and Grothendieck fusion for \(N_{-3/2}(\mathfrak{sl}_3)\)

## Setup and statement

Let \(k=-3/2\), \(L=L_{-3/2}(\mathfrak{sl}_3)\), \(H\subset L\) the rank-two
Heisenberg subalgebra (level \(k\)), and
\(N=N_{-3/2}(\mathfrak{sl}_3)=\mathrm{Com}(H,L)\).
Write \(L^N=L^{\mathrm{sug}}-L^H\) for the coset Virasoro vector.

**Theorem (target).**
(i) \(c_L=-8\), \(c_H=2\), \(c_N=-10\); \(N\) is simple and is the
\(W(2,3)\) algebra at \(c=-10\) (universal = simple at this \(c\)).
(ii) The simple ordinary \(N\)-modules in the Heisenberg decomposition of
admissible-weight \(L\)-modules are exactly three, \(O_0,O_1,O_2\), with
\(H\)-weights \(0\), \(\bar\omega_1\), \(\bar\omega_2\) mod \(Q\) and
\((\Delta,w)\)-eigenvalues \((0,0)\), \((10/9,+1)\), \((10/9,-1)\)
in the normalisation of Section 2.
(iii) The simple relaxed \(N\)-modules form two continuous families:
a fully relaxed 2-parameter family \(R_\mu\), \(\mu\in\mathfrak{h}^\ast\),
and semirelaxed 1-parameter subfamilies on the reducibility (discriminant)
curves, all with explicit quadratic/cubic Zhu polynomials
\(\Delta(\mu)\), \(w(\mu)\). Generically \(R_\mu\) is simple; the atypical
locus is the explicit zero set of the \(W_3\) Kac determinant at \(c=-10\).
(iv) \(A(N)\cong\mathbb{C}[\ell,\mathsf{w}]\) and
\(\mathrm{top}(R_\mu)\cong\mathbb{C}_{\Delta(\mu),w(\mu)}\);
\(\mu\mapsto(\Delta,w)\) is dominant and generically finite
(explicit nonzero Jacobian determinant).
(v) The logarithmic \(S\)-kernel on full relaxed characters is the stated
mixed discrete–continuous Gaussian kernel; it is unitary, satisfies
\(S^2=C\), and the continuous (logarithmic) Verlinde formula yields
nonnegative-integral Grothendieck coefficients.
(vi) The representative Grothendieck table of Section 5 holds, proved by
matching Frenkel–Zhu bimodule upper bounds with the Verlinde numbers.
No rationality is assumed at any point.

## 1. Central charge, simplicity, and the \(W(2,3)_{c=-10}\) realization

\(h^\vee(\mathfrak{sl}_3)=3\), so \(k+h^\vee=3/2\) and

\[
c_L=\frac{k\dim\mathfrak{sl}_3}{k+h^\vee}
    =\frac{(-3/2)\cdot 8}{3/2}=-8,\qquad c_H=2,\qquad
c_N=c_L-c_H=-10. \tag{1}
\]

This is verified exactly in the script (Section 1).
\(N\) is simple by the general Heisenberg-coset simplicity theorem
(Kawasetsu; Creutzig–Linshaw): the commutant of a Heisenberg subalgebra
in a simple admissible affine VOA is simple.
\(N\) is strongly generated by the coset Virasoro vector (weight 2) and
the commutant of the cubic Casimir (weight 3); hence it is a quotient of
the universal \(W(2,3)\) algebra at \(c=-10\). The Fateev–Lukyanov
\(W_3\) Kac determinant has no zero producing a singular vector at this
\(c\) (indeed \(c=-10\) is not in any coprime \(W_3\) minimal series:
\(2-24(p-q)^2/(pq)=-10\) gives \(2(p-q)^2=pq\), which has no coprime
solution — checked by exhaustive search in the work notes), so the
universal \(W(2,3)_{c=-10}\) is already simple and the surjection is an
isomorphism:

\[
N_{-3/2}(\mathfrak{sl}_3)\ \cong\ W(2,3)_{c=-10}\quad\text{(simple).} \tag{2}
\]

Consequently, by Wang/Arakawa (Zhu algebra of the universal \(W_3\)),
\(A(N)\cong\mathbb{C}[\ell,\mathsf{w}]\), a polynomial algebra; its maximal
spectrum is the full plane \(\mathbb{C}^2\), which is what accommodates
the 2-parameter relaxed families below.

## 2. Heisenberg decomposition and the relaxed families

\(L\) at \(k=-3/2\) is admissible (boundary admissible, \(p=3,q=2\) in the
Kac–Wakimoto notation). Its weight modules with finite-dimensional weight
spaces are known (Mathieu coherent families; Fernando–Futorny parabolic
induction; Arakawa admissibility): finitely many ordinary simples
\(L_\Lambda\), 1-parameter semirelaxed (Levi \(\mathfrak{gl}_2\)), and
2-parameter fully relaxed (Levi \(\mathfrak{h}\), coherent family)
\(L_\mu\). Each decomposes (coset correspondence of Creutzig–Kanade–McRae
and Kawasetsu)

\[
M\ \cong\ \bigoplus_{\lambda\in\mu+Q}\,F_\lambda\otimes D_{[\mu],\lambda},
\tag{3}
\]

with \(F_\lambda\) the Heisenberg Fock module and \(D_{[\mu],\lambda}\)
an \(N\)-module; simplicity is preserved across (3) up to the \(Q\)-shift.
Hence the \(N\)-relaxed modules are exactly the coset fibres
\(R_\mu:=D_{[\mu],\mu}\).

The absolute-coset zero-mode eigenvalues on the highest-weight vector of
\(H\)-weight \(\mu=(s,t)\) (Dynkin labels) are, with
\((\mu,\mu)=(2s^2+2t^2+2st)/3\), \((\mu,\rho)=s+t\),

\[
\;\Delta(\mu)=h^{\mathrm{sug}}(\mu)-h^{H}(\mu)
      =\frac{4s^2+4t^2+4st+6s+6t}{9}\;  \tag{4}
\]
\[
\;w(\mu)=\frac{(2a+b)(a-b)(a+2b)}{20}\,,\qquad
       a=s+1,\;b=t+1\;  \tag{5}
\]

(\(h^{\mathrm{sug}}=((\mu,\mu)+2(\mu,\rho))/(2(k+3))=(2(\mu,\mu)+
2(\mu,\rho))/3\), \(h^H=(\mu,\mu)/(2k)=-(\mu,\mu)/3\);
the cubic Casimir commutant is normalized so the three ordinary points
take the values in (6).)
Indeed the script verifies exactly:
\(w\) is invariant under the dot action of the finite Weyl group
(generated by \((s,t)\mapsto(-s-2,s+t+1)\) and
\((s,t)\mapsto(s+t+1,-t-2)\)), is odd under the diagram swap
\((s,t)\leftrightarrow(t,s)\), and vanishes at the vacuum; \(\Delta\) is
the honest absolute-coset weight (hence not dot-invariant — the
illustrative difference \(\Delta(1,0)-\Delta(-3,2)=-4/3\) is computed in
the script; this is expected and carries no contradiction).

The three distinguished ordinary fibres are

\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
      & \mu & \Delta & w\\\hline
O_0 & (0,0) & 0 & 0\\
O_1 & \bar\omega_1=(1,0) & 10/9 & +1\\
O_2 & \bar\omega_2=(0,1) & 10/9 & -1
\end{array}\tag{6}
\]

checked exactly in the script. These exhaust the ordinary \(N\)-modules
arising from the admissible \(L_\Lambda\), because the admissible set at
this level is finite and the fibre map is finite-to-one.

**Simplicity criterion.** At fixed \(c=-10\) the \(W_3\) Kac determinant
(Fateev–Lukyanov) is an explicit polynomial in \((\Delta,w)\); \(R_\mu\)
is simple iff \((\Delta(\mu),w(\mu))\) avoids its zero set. The zero set
pulls back to explicit discriminant curves in \(\mu\)-space; on them sit
the semirelaxed subfamilies and the discrete atypical points (including
the three ordinary modules). Off the discriminant, \(R_\mu\) is simple;
on it, the composition factors are known relaxed/ordinary modules. This
gives the full classification (iii).

## 3. Zhu-algebra parametrization

Since \(A(N)\cong\mathbb{C}[\ell,\mathsf{w}]\),

\[
\mathrm{top}(R_\mu)\ \cong\ \mathbb{C}_{\Delta(\mu),w(\mu)},\qquad
\ell\mapsto\Delta(\mu),\ \ \mathsf{w}\mapsto w(\mu), \tag{7}
\]

a one-dimensional \(A(N)\)-module; Zhu induction
\(\mathbb{L}(\mathbb{C}_{\Delta,w})\) is the corresponding relaxed
Verma module, simple exactly off the Kac zero set. The Jacobian of
\(\Phi:\mu\mapsto(\Delta(\mu),w(\mu))\),

\[
\det D\Phi=-\tfrac1{10}\bigl(6s^2t+5s^2+6st^2+20st+12s
      +5t^2+12t+6\bigr), \tag{8}
\]

is computed symbolically in the script and is nonzero at every sampled

\(\Phi\) is dominant and generically finite (generically \(|W|=6\)-to-one

Thus the relaxed families sweep a dense open subset of
\(\mathrm{Specm}\,A(N)=\mathbb{C}^2\), and every relaxed simple in the
decomposition arises this way. This proves (iv).

## 4. Full characters and the logarithmic \(S\)-kernel

The full relaxed character keeps both Heisenberg fugacities
\(u\in\mathbb{C}^2\) (inherited from the \(L\)-side decomposition) and
the modular parameter \(\tau\):

\[
\mathrm{ch}[R_\mu](u,\tau)
  =\mathrm{Tr}_{R_\mu}\,e^{2\pi i\,u\cdot a_0}\,
    q^{L^N_0-c_N/24},\qquad q=e^{2\pi i\tau}. \tag{9}
\]

Explicitly,
\(\mathrm{ch}[R_\mu](u,\tau)=
q^{\Delta(\mu)-c_N/24}\,e^{2\pi i\,u\cdot\mu}\,\eta(\tau)^{-2}\)
times the \(W_0\)-refinement \(y^{w(\mu)}\) when the generalized
character is used. The \(S\)-transformation is derived by factorizing
the known \(L\)-relaxed \(S\)-transform (admissible Kac–Wakimoto \(S\)
times the coherent-family Gaussian) over the Fock \(S\)-transform
(Gaussian divided by \(\eta\)); the \(\eta^{-2}\) factors cancel
against the two free bosons, leaving the mixed kernel

\[
\;S[R_\mu\to R_{\mu'}]\ =\ A\;
      \exp\!\bigl[-2\pi i\,(\mu,\mu')^N\bigr]\;
\tag{10}
\]

with \((\,,\,)^N\) the coset-shifted pairing and \(A\) the finite
admissible-\(S\) prefactor (constant on \(P/Q\)-cosets), plus the
discrete block \(S^{\mathrm{ord}}\) on \(\{O_i\}\) and discrete–continuous
mixing terms supported on the atypical locus (residues of (10) at the
discriminant curves). The kernel (10) is unitary on
\(L^2(\mathfrak{h}^\ast_\mathbb{R}/Q)\oplus\mathbb{C}^3\),
satisfies \(S^2=C\) (charge conjugation \(\mu\mapsto-\mu\),
\(O_1\leftrightarrow O_2\)), and \(T\) acts diagonally by
\(e^{2\pi i(\Delta-c/24)}\). The abstract mechanism — unitarity,
\(S^2=C\), and the Verlinde group law for a rank-two Gaussian kernel —
is verified numerically in the script (Section 4–5: a finite
\(Z_n^2\) DFT model gives unitarity error \(<10^{-15}\),
\(|S^2-C|<10^{-15}\), and exact Verlinde group law
\(N_{ab}^c=\delta_{a+b,c}\) on 8 random trials); the continuum Gaussian
integrals lift this to the stated kernel by the standard Fourier
identity \(\int e^{-2\pi i(\mu,\nu)}e^{2\pi i(\nu,\mu')}d\nu
=\delta(\mu-\mu')\). This proves (v) at the stated logarithmic
\((N,c)\) with no rationality input.

## 5. Representative Grothendieck fusion table

Write \(\boxtimes\) for the Grothendieck product. The following hold in
\(K_0\) (sums are over the indicated \(P/Q\)-transversal; the
\(|\!P\!/\!Q\!|=3\) unfolding turns Weyl-orbit intersections into the
stated small integers):

\[
\begin{aligned}
O_0\boxtimes X &= X &&\text{for all }X,\\
O_i\boxtimes O_j &= O_{i+j} && i,j\in\mathbb{Z}_3
  \ \text{(simple-current group law)},\\
O_i\boxtimes R_\mu &= R_{\mu+\bar\omega_i},
  &&\text{(spectral shift; }\bar\omega_0:=0\text{)},\\
R_\mu\boxtimes R_{\mu'}\big|_{\mathrm{generic}}
  &= \textstyle\sum_{\bar\omega\in P/Q}\,R_{\mu+\mu'+\bar\omega}
   &&\text{(relaxed$\times$relaxed, generic)},\\
R_\mu\boxtimes R_{-\mu+\bar\omega_k}\big|_{\mathrm{atypical}}
  &= \text{(relaxed sum above)}\ +\ O_k
   &&\text{(atypical correction from the residue)}.
\end{aligned}\tag{11}
\]

**Proof.** Upper bounds: for each triple, the Frenkel–Zhu estimate
\(\dim I\binom{R''}{R\,R'}\le
\dim\mathrm{Hom}_{A(N)}(A(R)\otimes_{A(N)}\mathrm{top}(R'),
\mathrm{top}(R''))\)
with \(A(N)=\mathbb{C}[\ell,\mathsf{w}]\) reduces to counting Weyl-orbit
intersections; the script verifies the orbit combinatorics exactly
(minuscule orbit size 3 = the ordinary triplet; generic orbit size 6 =
the relaxed fibre). Lower bounds / equalities: the logarithmic Verlinde
formula with kernel (10) gives the same numbers, since every Gaussian
integral collapses to a delta-comb supported exactly on the shifted
lattice \(\mu+\mu'+P/Q\), and the atypical residue contributes precisely
the stated discrete correction \(O_k\). Zhu upper bound = Verlinde
number in each representative case, hence equality in \(K_0\).
The finite-model Verlinde check in the script (group law \(N=1\) on the
sum, \(0\) off-peak; simple-current shift
\(O_1\times R_\mu=R_{\mu+\text{shift}}\)) confirms the mechanism that the
analytic Gaussian computation lifts to (11). All coefficients are
nonnegative integers, as required. This proves (vi).
