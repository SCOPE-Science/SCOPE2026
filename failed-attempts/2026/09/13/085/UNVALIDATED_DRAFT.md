## 3. Lemma 1 — Y3 contains a nonseparating essential sphere

**Lemma 1.** `Y3` contains an embedded 2-sphere meeting a closed loop
transversely once; in particular the sphere is nonseparating and essential, and
`Y3` is reducible.

*Proof.* View `W = S^3 ∖ ν(K_0 ∪ K_1)` with the standard Whitehead picture: each
`K_i` is an unknot and `lk(K_0,K_1) = 0`. Since `K_0` is unknotted it bounds a
disk `D_0 ⊂ S^3`; because the linking number is zero, after eliminating circle
intersections `D_0 ∩ K_1` is a finite set of transverse points whose signed
count is zero. The Whitehead link is a standard 2-component link whose one
component bounds a disk meeting the other component in exactly two points of
opposite sign after the usual innermost-circle cleanup (equivalently, the
Whitehead clasp is visibly a twice-punctured disk region; this is the classical
picture of the Whitehead link as the twisted Whitehead double pattern with
clasp number 2). Fix such a `D_0` meeting `K_1` in exactly 2 points of opposite
signs. Then `P = D_0 ∩ W` is a twice-punctured disk: `∂P = ℓ_0 ∪ {two small
meridian circles of K_1}`, where `ℓ_0` is the longitude of `K_0`.

Fill `V_0` along slope `(0,1) = ℓ_0`: cap `P` with the meridian disk of `V_0`
along `ℓ_0`. The result is an annulus `A ⊂ W ∪ V_0` whose two boundary
components are longitudes on `∂ν(K_1)` (up to the standard framing). Now fill
`V_1` along slope `(7,1)`, which has longitudinal coordinate `q = 1`: the
meridian disk of `V_1` meets each longitude once. Capping the two boundary
circles of `A` with two parallel copies of the `V_1` meridian disk yields a
2-sphere `S' ⊂ Y3`. The core arc of `V_0` together with a spanning arc of `D_0`
gives a closed loop `m_0 ⊂ Y3` (the meridian of `K_0`, i.e. the free `Z`
generator of `H_1(Y3)`) meeting `S'` transversely in exactly one point (the
capping disk of `V_0` is hit once by its own core). Hence `[S'] · [m_0] = ±1`:
`S'` represents the generator of `H_2(Y3;Z)/torsion ≅ Z` and is nonseparating,
therefore essential (in particular not null-homotopic and not bounding a
3-ball). So `Y3` is reducible. ∎

**Corollary (not hyperbolic).** A closed reducible 3-manifold is not hyperbolic

## 4. Lemma 2 — no taut foliations on Y3

**Lemma 2.** `Y3` admits no taut foliation (hence no fibration over `S^1` and no
taut depth-one foliation).

*Proof.* A taut foliation is Reebless: every compact leaf has nontrivial
holonomy on both sides, equivalently there is a closed transversal meeting
every leaf (Novikov–Sullivan; see e.g. Calegari, *Foliations and the geometry
of 3-manifolds*, Thm 4.2.1 / Cor. 4.2.6). If `Y3` contained a taut foliation,
put the essential sphere `S'` of Lemma 1 in general position: the induced
singular foliation on `S^2` has only saddle and center singularities; a center
forces a vanishing cycle, and Novikov's theorem (an embedded vanishing cycle
bounds a Reeb component) yields a Reeb component — contradiction. Equivalently,
taut implies irreducible (no essential spheres). Since `Y3` is reducible, no
taut foliation exists. A fibration over `S^1` and a taut depth-one foliation
are both taut, so neither exists on `Y3`. ∎

## 5. Lemma 3 — Thurston norm and foliation-cone decomposition

**Lemma 3.** The Thurston norm on `H_2(Y3;Z)` is identically `0`; the norm "ball"
is the whole line; there are no fibered faces and no depth-one foliation cones.
The Altman foliation-cone decomposition of `Y3` is empty.

*Proof.* The class `[S']` generating `H_2/torsion` is represented by a sphere
(`χ = 2`), so `x([S']) = max(0, −χ) = 0`; by homogeneity and the triangle
inequality `x ≡ 0`. Since every class has a sphere representative (multiples of
`[S']` connect-summed with torsion irrelevance), the unit "ball" is all of
`H_2(R) ≅ R` and there are no top-dimensional faces at all — fibered or
otherwise. By Lemma 2 there is no taut foliation, so no class lies in any
foliation cone (fibered cones require fibrations; Altman depth-one cones
require taut depth-one foliations). Hence the decomposition is: no cones. ∎
