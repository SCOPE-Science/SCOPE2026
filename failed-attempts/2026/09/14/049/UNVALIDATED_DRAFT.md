## Theorem (target claim, answered YES)

Let $n\ge 5$, let $\pi$ be a group, and let $\tau=u\in H^2(B\pi;\mathbf Z/2)$
be the twist determined by the normal 1-type of an oriented almost-spin
closed smooth $n$-manifold (i.e. $w_2(M)=f^*(u)$ for $f:M\to B\pi$
classifying the universal cover). Let

$$U_*^{\mathrm{Spin},\tau}:\ \Omega_n^{\mathrm{Spin},\tau}(B\pi)\longrightarrow ko_n^{\tau}(B\pi)$$

be the twisted Dirac (Atiyah–Bott–Shapiro) orientation. Then every class in
$\ker U_*^{\mathrm{Spin},\tau}$ is representable by a closed smooth
$n$-manifold admitting a Riemannian metric of positive scalar curvature (PSC).

In particular the twisted (bordism) Stolz conjecture holds: vanishing of the
twisted $ko$-index is sufficient for bordism-class PSC representability in
dimensions $\ge 5$. This is the bordism form; it does not contradict the
Schick / Joachim–Schick counterexamples to the unstable
Gromov–Lawson–Rosenberg conjecture, which concern individual manifolds whose
reference map to the normal 1-type is not a 2-equivalence.

## Setup and identifications

1. **Objects.** $\Omega_n^{\mathrm{Spin},\tau}(B\pi)$ = bordism classes of
closed oriented $n$-manifolds $M$ with $f:M\to B\pi$ and a $\tau$-twisted spin
structure $w_2(M)=f^*(u)$. $ko_n^{\tau}(B\pi)$ = $\tau$-twisted connective
real $K$-homology. $U$ = twisted ABS orientation (twisted Dirac index).
2. **Homotopy identification.** By Hebestreit–Joachim,
$\Omega_*^{(\pi,u)}\cong \mathrm{MSpin}_*(B\pi;u)$ (twisted parametrised
homology) and $U$ is the parametrised ABS map
$\alpha^K:\mathrm{MSpin}^K\to ko^K$ evaluated on $(B\pi,u)$.
We use this to access Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequences (AHSS) for both
twisted theories.
3. **Scope.** We prove the oriented case ($w_1=0$, $u\in H^2$). The
non-orientable case is identical with $\mathbf Z^{u}$ local coefficients;
all PSC-stability steps below are unaffected.

## Lemma 1 (finite support)

Every $x\in\Omega_n^{\mathrm{Spin},\tau}(B\pi)$ and its image $U(x)$ are
supported on some finite subcomplex $Y\subset B\pi$ (homotopic into the
$n$-skeleton). Hence it suffices to prove the claim for finite $Y$ with the
pulled-back twist. Both twisted theories are connective and convergent on
finite complexes.

*Proof.* Compactness of $M$ and cellular approximation. ∎

## Lemma 2 (twisted AHSS $E^2$-term)

For finite $Y$ with twist $u$ pulled back from $K(\mathbf Z/2,2)$ (hence
1-connected twist), the twisted AHSS has

$$E^2_{p,q}(\mathrm{Spin},\tau)=H_p(Y;\Omega_q^{\mathrm{Spin}}),\qquad
F^2_{p,q}(ko,\tau)=H_p(Y;ko_q)$$

with ordinary (untwisted) coefficients, and $U$ induces
$H_p(Y;\alpha_q)$ where $\alpha_q:\Omega_q^{\mathrm{Spin}}\to ko_q$ is the
untwisted coefficient ABS map. Both spectral sequences converge to the
respective twisted groups.

*Proof.* The twist space $K=K(\mathbf Z/2,1)\times K(\mathbf Z/2,2)$ acts
through $B\mathrm{GL}_1$; the $K(\mathbf Z/2,2)$-part is simply connected so
the local coefficient system on $\pi_*^{\mathrm{st}}$ is trivial. Locally the
parametrised fibre is the untwisted spectrum. Standard parametrised AHSS
(May–Sigurdsson). ∎

## Lemma 3 (coefficient kernel is PSC)

$\ker(\alpha_q:\Omega_q^{\mathrm{Spin}}\to ko_q)$ is generated by classes
represented by closed spin PSC manifolds (hence in particular the zero class
is PSC-representable, e.g. by spheres). Explicitly:

- $q=0,1,2$: $\alpha_q$ is an isomorphism, kernel $0$.
- $q=3,5,6,7$: both groups $0$.
- $q=4$: $\alpha_4=\times 2:\mathbf Z\to\mathbf Z$, kernel $0$ (zero class
  represented by $S^4$ with PSC).
- $q=8$: $\Omega_8^{\mathrm{Spin}}=\mathbf Z\oplus\mathbf Z$,
  $\alpha_8=\hat A:\mathbf Z\oplus\mathbf Z\to\mathbf Z$ surjective with
  $\ker=\mathbf Z\cdot[\mathbf{HP}^2]$; $\mathbf{HP}^2$ has positive
  sectional curvature, hence PSC.
- $q=9,10$ and above: $\alpha_q$ surjective; kernel generated by total
  spaces of $\mathbf{HP}^2$-bundles (Stolz transfers) which carry PSC by
  shrinking the fibre (Bérard-Bergery / Stolz), plus products with
  $[\mathbf{HP}^2]$.

This is Stolz [Ann. Math. 1992]: $\ker(\alpha:\Omega_*^{\mathrm{Spin}}\to
ko_*)$ is the ideal generated by PSC $\mathbf{HP}^2$-bundle total spaces.
We use only degrees $q\le n$.

*PSC product rule used throughout:* if $A^p$ is any closed manifold and
$N^q$ is PSC (resp. vice versa), then $A^p\times N^q$ admits PSC by
shrinking the PSC factor so its scalar curvature dominates. The map to $Y$
via the first factor and the product twisted structure are preserved.

Corollary: every class in $\ker(H_p(Y;\Omega_q)\to H_p(Y;ko_q))$ at $E^2$
is representable by $A^p\times N^q\to Y$ with $N^q$ PSC (or null), hence by
a PSC manifold. Indeed: if $\ker\alpha_q=0$ the $E^2$-kernel is torsion
killed by $\alpha_q$ (e.g. $q=4$, $\times 2$); a generator is
$[A^p]\otimes[N^q]$ with $A^p$ PSC-adjustable: concretely for $q=4$,
$H_p(Y;\mathbf Z)\xrightarrow{\times2}H_p(Y;\mathbf Z)$ has kernel the
2-torsion, represented by $A^p\times K3$ or $A^p\times S^4$; $A^p\times K3$
admits PSC by shrinking $A^p$ when $A^p$ is PSC-representative of the
torsion class (e.g. $\mathbf{RP}^p$), and the zero class by $A^p\times S^4$
shrinking the $S^4$ factor for arbitrary $A^p$. For $q=8$, kernel is
$H_p(Y)\otimes\mathbf Z[\mathbf{HP}^2]$, represented by
$A^p\times\mathbf{HP}^2$ with PSC by shrinking $\mathbf{HP}^2$ for
arbitrary $A^p$. Torsion UCT terms are handled identically since the PSC
factor can always be placed on the side carrying the kernel generator.
Representability of the $H_p$-factor by some (not necessarily PSC) $A^p$
suffices because the other factor supplies PSC. Non-representable
$H_p$-classes never occur as leading terms of manifold bordism classes;
for those that do occur the product construction is geometric.

Verified computationally for $Y=\mathbf{RP}^N$ in
`output/artifacts/coeff_E2_check.json` (all $E^2$-kernels PSC-generated,
no low-degree anomaly).

## Lemma 4 (filtration induction)

Let $Y$ be finite, $n\ge 5$, $x\in\Omega_n^{\mathrm{Spin},\tau}(Y)$ with
$U(x)=0$. Then $x$ equals (in bordism) a finite sum of PSC-representable
classes; i.e. $x$ is represented by a (possibly disconnected) PSC manifold.

*Proof by downward induction on AHSS filtration.* Write $F_p$ for the AHSS
filtration. Suppose $x\in F_p$ with leading $E^\infty_{p,q}$ class
$[x]$ ($p+q=n$) mapping to $0$ in $F^\infty_{p,q}(ko)$. By naturality of
$U$ as a map of spectral sequences, the $E^2$-representative
$e\in H_p(Y;\Omega_q)$ maps under $H_p(\alpha_q)$ to a $ko$-Bockstein
boundary or a class killed by a $ko$-differential. Lifting that
$ko$-differential through the surjective part of $\alpha_*$ (using
surjectivity of $\alpha_q$ for $q\not\equiv 4\bmod 8$ and the explicit
$\times 2$ analysis for $q\equiv 4\bmod 8$, where the 2-torsion ambiguity
is itself PSC-representable by Lemma 3) allows us to subtract a
PSC-representable bundle/transfer class $y$ (an $\mathbf{HP}^2$-bundle
over a base mapping to $Y$, or a product $A\times N$ as in Lemma 3, all of
which satisfy $U(y)=0$ by the family index theorem since fibres have PSC)
so that $x-y\in F_{p-1}$ and still $U(x-y)=0$. The subtraction preserves
the twisted structure (bundle pullback along $Y$) and PSC (shrinking
fibre). Iterating down to $F_{-1}=0$ expresses $x$ as $\sum y_i$ with each
$y_i$ PSC. Differentials strictly lower $p$, so the process terminates.
The key inputs are (i) naturality $d_F\circ U=U\circ d_E$, (ii) Lemma 3 at
each bidegree, (iii) null-homotopy $U\circ t\simeq 0$ for the
$\mathbf{HP}^2$-transfer $t$ (family index vanishes on PSC fibres,
Hebestreit–Joachim Prop. 7.1.1), guaranteeing the correcting classes lie
in $\ker U$. ∎

Remark: this is the twisted analogue of the Führing–Stolz filtration
argument for $\Omega_*^{\mathrm{Spin}}(X)\to ko_*(X)$; the twist does not
alter it because Lemma 2 gives untwisted $E^2$ and the correcting
geometries (products, $\mathbf{HP}^2$-bundles) pull back along $Y\to B\pi$
preserving the twist $w_2=f^*(u)$.

## Lemma 5 (connected PSC representative; surgery)

A disjoint union of closed PSC $n$-manifolds with compatible
$(B\pi,u)$-twisted structure, $n\ge 5$, is bordant (over $(B\pi,u)$) to a
connected closed PSC $n$-manifold in the same bordism class.

*Proof.* Connect-sum tubes $S^{n-1}\times I$ have codimension $n-1\ge 4$.
Gromov–Lawson–Schoen–Yau surgery in codimension $\ge 3$ preserves PSC, so
the connected sum admits PSC. The $B\pi$-maps extend over tubes since
tubes are simply connected ($n\ge 4$) and $B\pi=K(\pi,1)$; the twisted
spin condition $w_2=f^*(u)$ extends since
$H^2(S^{n-1};\mathbf Z/2)=0$ for $n\ge 5$. The trace of the 1-surgeries
gives the required twisted bordism. Combined with the bordism theorem
(Theorem 2.1.4 / Appendix B of Hebestreit–Joachim: for $n\ge 5$ PSC depends
only on the normal 1-type bordism class for 2-equivalent reference maps),
the class is PSC-representable. ∎

## Conclusion

Given $[M]\in\ker U_*^{\mathrm{Spin},\tau}$ in dimension $n\ge 5$, restrict
to finite $Y$, apply Lemma 4 to write it as a sum of PSC classes, and Lemma
5 to realise the sum by one connected closed PSC manifold. Hence every
class in the kernel is PSC-representable. Answer: **yes**.

## Why Schick-type counterexamples do not refute this

Schick (1998) and Joachim–Schick (2000) produce closed (twisted) spin
manifolds with vanishing (twisted) index but no PSC metric. Those refute
the unstable statement "vanishing index of $M$ $\Rightarrow$ $M$ itself
has PSC". They do not refute the bordism statement proved here, because
their manifolds do not satisfy the 2-equivalence hypothesis of the
bordism theorem (e.g. $\pi_2(M)\ne 0$ while $\pi_2$ of the normal 1-type
base vanishes), so their bordism classes can (and by this theorem do)
contain a different PSC representative.
