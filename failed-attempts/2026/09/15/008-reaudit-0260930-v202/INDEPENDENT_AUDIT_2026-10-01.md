# Independent mathematical audit — 2026-10-01

Record: `SCOPE-20260915-008`

## Correctness — PASS

The suspension provides a valid counterexample to the stated density dichotomy. A unipotent projective monodromy with image the integer translations gives a flat algebraic ruled surface over a curve of genus at least two and a regular Riccati foliation transverse to the fibers. Thus \(T_{\mathcal F}\simeq\pi^*T_B\) and \(K_{\mathcal F}\simeq\pi^*K_B\), which is nef with numerical dimension one. The infinity section is invariant; finite points have infinite translation orbits, so there is no additional compact invariant leaf and no rational first integral. A leaf through a finite fiber coordinate meets each lifted fiber in the integer translate orbit, whose closure in the projective line adds only infinity, so its global closure is the leaf together with the infinity section and is proper. The RESULT's phrase calling this closure a 'proper analytic subset' is incorrect: a proper analytic curve cannot have an infinite discrete generic-fiber intersection. The needed conclusion is only that it is a proper closed subset, so the theorem-level counterexample survives this proof defect.

## Originality — FAIL

Brunella's standard description of Riccati foliations already gives the transverse rational fibration, projective monodromy representation, and leaf-as-covering structure. Choosing the elementary parabolic subgroup of integer translations and taking its orbit closures mechanically produces the asserted non-dense leaves with the unique invariant infinity section. The bespoke target hypothesis package does not add a new mechanism. Hence the counterexample is a routine specialization of classical suspension theory rather than an original structural result.
