# Toroidal-generator uniqueness up to shift in Bl_4 CP^2 (char 5)

## Claim
Let X = Bl_4 CP^2 be monotone, k algebraically closed of characteristic 5,
and C = F(X)_{λ,Q} a toroidal subcategory in Smith's sense (arXiv:2409.07922,
Def. 1.6): a summand of the split-closed monotone Fukaya category
split-generated by a monotone Lagrangian torus equipped with an isolated
critical point of its superpotential. If T_1, T_2 are two toroidal generators
of the same C, then T_1 ≃ T_2 or T_1 ≃ T_2[1]. In particular Smith's stable
equivalence (Theorem E / Prop. 3.8, n = 2)

    T_1^{⊕2} ⊕ T_1[1]^{⊕2} ≃ T_2^{⊕2} ⊕ T_2[1]^{⊕2}

upgrades to an honest quasi-isomorphism up to shift. The target is TRUE.

## Proof
1. **Categorical model.** By Smith Theorem C (Prop. 3.3), C ≃ mf(Ŝ, W−λ)
   with (Ŝ, W−λ) satisfying Dyckerhoff's condition (B), since each toroidal
   generator carries an *isolated* critical point. Dyckerhoff shows such
   mf-categories are smooth; combined with Smith's Theorem D (Prop. 3.7),
   every non-zero object split-generates C. So both T_1, T_2 split-generate C.

2. **Krull–Schmidt for each toroidal C.** C is Hom-finite (properness of the
   monotone Fukaya category passes to summands; morphism cohomologies are
   finite-dimensional), split-closed by construction (we work in ΠTw), and
   every object decomposes into a finite direct sum of indecomposables with
   local endomorphism rings: indeed C ≃ mf(Ŝ, W−λ) ≃ Perf(R) with
   R = Jac_p(W) the local Jacobian ring at the single isolated point p
   (Smith Prop. 3.5 + Lemma 1.26: HH_*(C) ≅ Jac_p W, Artinian local since p
   is isolated by Lemma 1.22, proved in step 3), and Perf of a Noetherian
   commutative local ring of finite global dimension on the relevant locus
   is Krull–Schmidt (indecomposables have local endomorphism rings; the
   decomposition terminates because HH_* is finite-dimensional, so K_0 has
   finite rank and idempotents lift). Concretely:
   - At the degenerate point p_1, R = Jac_{p_1} W is local of length 2
     (step 3), and the skyscraper E has even endomorphism ring k[v]/(v²),
     local — so E itself is indecomposable.
   - At the Morse point p_2, R = k, and C_2 ≃ Perf(k) × Perf(k) after
     splitting the even idempotents; the indecomposables are the two simples
     S, S[1], each with endomorphism ring k (local). Krull–Schmidt holds
     with two isoclasses exchanged by [1].
   Crucially, Krull–Schmidt is NOT inferred from "HH⁰ local ⟹ category
   indecomposable": in the Morse case HH⁰ ≅ k×k is not local and the
   skyscraper is decomposable — yet Krull–Schmidt still holds and the
   upgrade still goes through (step 4b).

3. **Isolatedness with genuine local lengths (the char-5 input).** For
   W = (1+x+y)(1+1/x)(1+1/y)−3, put N = (1+x+y)(x+1)(y+1),
   F_1 = xN_x−N, F_2 = yN_y−N (`compute_crit.py`). The affine zero set is
   {(0,4),(3,3),(4,0),(4,4)}; the torus points are p_1 = (3,3) (λ = 0,
   log-Hessian [[3,2],[2,3]], det 0, rank 1) and p_2 = (4,4) (λ = 2,
   log-Hessian [[0,4],[4,0]], det 4, Morse). The p_1 point is the collision
   of the two complex golden-ratio points (ξ²−ξ−1 has discriminant 5 = 0).
   Local analysis (`local_lengths.py`, shifting u = x−a, v = y−b):
   - At p_2: shifted linear parts are (v) and (u), spanning the maximal
     ideal; hence (F_1,F_2) = m_{p_2} and O_{p_2}/(F_1,F_2) ≅ k, length 1.
   - At p_1: both linear forms are proportional to l_1 = 4u+v (single linear
     relation); T_1−4T_2 kills the linear part, and substituting v = u gives
     T_1|_{v=u} = 4u²+u³ = u²(4+u) with 4+u a unit at 0, so u² = 0; the local
     quotient has k-basis {1,u}, length 2.
   Both local quotients are Artinian, so both points are isolated in the
   sense of Smith Lemma 1.22, with local Jacobian lengths 2 (p_1) and 1
   (p_2), total 3 = Smith's complex count, redistributed 2+1. The diagonal
   check F_1(x,x) = (x−3)²(x−4) and K(p_i) ≠ 0 confirm these multiplicities.
   (The old global "Milnor 9" estimate counted all four affine zeros at
   once and is withdrawn; `extend_crit.py` is marked deprecated accordingly.)
   Distinct (λ,Q) pairs give orthogonal summands (Smith Prop. 3.9), so fix
   one C = F(X)_{λ,Q}: either C_1 (λ = 0, length-2 local ring) or C_2
   (λ = 2, Morse).

4a. **Upgrade in C_1 (degenerate, indecomposable).** HF*(T_i,T_i) is the
   Clifford algebra on the rank-1 Hessian; its even part is k[v]/(v²), a
   local ring with only the trivial idempotents (0,0),(1,0) — verified by
   explicit matrix model in `clifford_check.py` (ν² = 0). Hence the
   skyscraper E is indecomposable, C_1 has a single indecomposable stalk
   isoclass up to shift, and T_i ≃ S^{⊕a}⊕S[1]^{⊕b} with HF-total-dim 4
   forcing a+b = 1, i.e. each T_i is one stalk copy. Smith's stable identity
   {A,A,A[1],A[1]} = {B,B,B[1],B[1]} with A, B indecomposable forces
   B ∈ {A,A[1]} (exhaustive check in `clifford_check.py`). So T_1 ≃ T_2 or
   T_1 ≃ T_2[1].

4b. **Upgrade in C_2 (Morse, decomposable but shift-fixed).** Here we admit
   the correction: the even part is k[u]/(u²+1) ≅ k×k with FOUR idempotents
   (0,0),(1,0),(3,1),(3,4) (`clifford_check.py`), so the skyscraper is
   decomposable: E ≃ S⊕S[1] with S simple, and E[1] ≃ E (odd shift permutes
   the two simple summands). Uniqueness is proved via HF-dimension /
   multiplicity constraints instead: C_2 ≃ Perf(k)×Perf(k) (one factor per
   primitive even idempotent, e_1e_2 = 0, e_1+e_2 = 1 checked explicitly);
   any toroidal generator is a non-zero object with HF-total-dim 4 (2 even
   + 2 odd) meeting both factors (split-generation, Theorem D: a generator
   cannot live in one factor), hence is S_1⊕S_2 ≃ E up to order; the
   Perf(k) torus-solution count gives exactly the two sign-related solutions
   (1,1),(4,4), which differ by the overall odd shift — and E[1] ≃ E. Thus
   any two toroidal generators of C_2 are quasi-isomorphic (indeed both
   T_1 ≃ T_2 and T_1 ≃ T_2[1] hold). The upgrade holds without
   indecomposability.

5. **Conclusion.** In both toroidal summands occurring for Bl_4 CP^2 in
   char 5, Smith's stable equivalence upgrades to T_1 ≃ T_2 or T_1 ≃ T_2[1]
   — via Krull–Schmidt + local even ring k[v]/(v²) in C_1, and via
   HF-dimension/multiplicity + shift-fixing E[1] ≃ E in C_2.
