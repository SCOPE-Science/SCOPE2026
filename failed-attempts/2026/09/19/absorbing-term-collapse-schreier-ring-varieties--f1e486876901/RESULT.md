# Locally finite Schreier ring and fixed-field algebra corollaries

## Result

Let \(\mathcal V\) be a nontrivial locally finite Schreier variety with a constant term \(e\). A public 2023 presentation by Kearnes, Kompatscher, Moorhead and Szendrei proves the following stronger recovery principle: whenever a term \(t(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\) depends on a variable, that variable can be recovered by another term from \(t\) and the remaining variables. Consequently, if \(e\) is absorbing in two distinct coordinates of \(t\), then \(t\approx e\). This collapse is therefore treated here as prior work/corollary, not as a new theorem.

The contribution retained in this record is the resulting exact specialization to rings and fixed-field algebras.

### Rings

Here a ring means an algebra \((R,+,-,0,\cdot)\) whose additive reduct is an abelian group and whose multiplication is distributive; associativity and a multiplicative identity are not assumed.

For every prime \(p\), let
\[
\mathcal R_p=\{R: xy=0\text{ for all }x,y,\quad px=0\text{ for all }x\}.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{\text{The nontrivial locally finite Schreier varieties of rings are exactly the }\mathcal R_p.}
\]
This includes the associative nonunital case. If the language requires a multiplicative identity, no nontrivial locally finite Schreier variety exists.

### Fixed-field algebras

For associative algebras over a fixed field \(K\) in the usual \(K\)-linear nonunital language:

- if \(K\) is finite, the unique nontrivial locally finite Schreier variety is the variety of \(K\)-vector spaces with zero multiplication;
- if \(K\) is infinite, there is no nontrivial locally finite variety.

For unital \(K\)-algebras there is no nontrivial locally finite Schreier variety for any field \(K\).

## Proof

The 2023 recovery principle immediately gives the two-coordinate collapse. Indeed, suppose \(t\) is absorbing at \(e\) in distinct coordinates \(i\ne j\). If \(t\) depended on \(x_j\), the recovery principle would give a term recovering \(x_j\) from \(t\) and all variables except \(x_j\). Substituting \(x_i=e\) makes \(t=e\), so the left-hand side becomes independent of \(x_j\), contradicting nontriviality. Thus \(t\) is independent of \(x_j\); substituting \(x_j=e\) and using absorption yields \(t\approx e\).

In every ring, multiplication has 0 absorbing in both coordinates, hence \(xy\approx0\). Let \(F=F_{\mathcal V}(1)\). Its additive group is generated by the free generator, so \(F^+\cong\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\) for some finite \(n\ge2\). Every finite member of a locally finite Schreier variety is permutational: each unary polynomial is constant or a permutation. If \(n\) were composite and \(q\mid n\) were prime with \(q<n\), the unary polynomial \(x\mapsto qx\) would be neither constant nor bijective. Hence \(n=p\) is prime.

Therefore \(\mathcal V\) satisfies \(xy\approx0\) and \(px\approx0\), so \(\mathcal V\subseteq\mathcal R_p\). Conversely, the p-element zero-multiplication ring \(F\) belongs to \(\mathcal V\) and generates \(\mathcal R_p\), giving the reverse inclusion. Each \(\mathcal R_p\) is locally finite and Schreier because its free algebras are \(\mathbb F_p\)-vector spaces and every subring is a subspace.

If 1 is required, zero multiplication gives \(1=1\cdot1=0\), so only the trivial variety remains.

For a fixed field \(K\), the same collapse forces zero multiplication. A nontrivial equational subvariety of \(K\)-vector spaces in the full \(K\)-linear language cannot impose a nonzero scalar identity, so the only candidate is the full vector-space variety with zero multiplication. It is locally finite exactly when \(K\) is finite. The unital case is again trivial.

## Prior-work boundary

Kearnes–Kompatscher–Moorhead–Szendrei publicly presented the recovery principle in 2023, and Kearnes–Moorhead–Szendrei’s 2026 paper gives the equivalent finite-permutational characterization. The absorbing-term collapse above is an immediate corollary and is not claimed as original here. Burgin (1974) gives broad Schreier criteria for homogeneous linear Ω-algebras, including residue-field restrictions and trivial-multiplication examples. The retained claim is only the explicit locally finite ring/fixed-field classification above; equivalent older coverage under different terminology remains a residual risk.
