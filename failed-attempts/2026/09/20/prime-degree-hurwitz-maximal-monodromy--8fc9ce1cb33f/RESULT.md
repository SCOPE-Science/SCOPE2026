# A local branch-profile criterion forcing maximal monodromy in prime degree

## Statement

Let \(p\ge 5\) be prime, and let
\[
\mathcal D=(\Lambda_1,\ldots,\Lambda_k),\qquad \Lambda_i\vdash p,
\]
be branch data over \(S^2\) compatible with a connected orientable source, so
\[
\sum_{i=1}^k v(\Lambda_i)=2p-2+2g,
\qquad
v(\Lambda)=p-\ell(\Lambda).
\]

For a partition \(\Lambda\vdash p\), define its **Jordan-visible prime set**
\[
J(\Lambda)=\left\{q:\ q\text{ prime},\ q\le p-3,\ q\text{ occurs exactly once as a part of }\Lambda,\ q\nmid e\text{ for every other part }e\right\}.
\]

**Theorem.** Suppose \(J(\Lambda_i)\neq\varnothing\) for at least one branch profile. Then:

1. the datum \(\mathcal D\) is realized by a connected degree-\(p\) branched cover;
2. every connected realization has monodromy group
\[
\boxed{
G=\begin{cases}
A_p,& v(\Lambda_j)\equiv0\pmod 2\text{ for every }j,\\[2mm]
S_p,& v(\Lambda_j)\equiv1\pmod 2\text{ for at least one }j;
\end{cases}}
\]
3. every connected realization has trivial deck-transformation group;
4. if
\[
m_i=\operatorname{lcm}\{e:e\text{ is a part of }\Lambda_i\},
\]
then the Galois closure has degree \(|G|\) over \(\mathbf P^1\) and genus
\[
\boxed{
\widetilde g
=1+\frac{|G|}{2}\left(k-2-\sum_{i=1}^k\frac1{m_i}\right).
}
\]

Thus on this explicit passport locus the new prime-degree Hurwitz existence theorem upgrades to **passport-level monodromy rigidity**: every connected cover has the largest monodromy group allowed by the parity of its branch cycles.

## Exact local criterion

The definition of \(J(\Lambda)\) is not merely sufficient for the group-theoretic step; it exactly detects when the cyclic inertia generated by a permutation of type \(\Lambda\) contains a small prime cycle.

**Lemma.** Let \(\sigma\in S_p\) have cycle partition \(\Lambda\), and let \(q\le p-3\) be prime. Then some power of \(\sigma\) is a single \(q\)-cycle fixing all other letters if and only if \(q\in J(\Lambda)\).

### Proof

Assume first that \(q\in J(\Lambda)\). Let \(M\) be the least common multiple of all parts of \(\Lambda\) other than the unique part \(q\). By definition \(q\nmid M\). Hence \(\sigma^M\) kills every other disjoint cycle, while on the \(q\)-cycle it acts as a nontrivial power coprime to \(q\). Therefore \(\sigma^M\) is a single \(q\)-cycle.

Conversely, suppose \(\sigma^a\) is a single \(q\)-cycle. On an original cycle of length \(e\), the power \(\sigma^a\) decomposes it into \(\gcd(e,a)\) cycles of length \(e/\gcd(e,a)\). To obtain exactly one nontrivial cycle of length \(q\), its supporting original cycle must therefore have length exactly \(q\), and it occurs only once. Every other original cycle must be fixed by \(\sigma^a\), so its length divides \(a\). Since the distinguished \(q\)-cycle remains nontrivial, \(q\nmid a\); consequently no other part is divisible by \(q\). This is exactly \(q\in J(\Lambda)\). ∎

## Proof of the theorem

Song, Wen and Zhang prove that every compatible branch datum of prime degree is realized by a connected branched cover. This gives nonemptiness.

Now take any connected realization. Its branch cycles \(\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_k\in S_p\) have the prescribed cycle types, product one, and generate a transitive subgroup \(G\le S_p\). Since the degree \(p\) is prime, every transitive subgroup of \(S_p\) is primitive.

Choose \(i\) and \(q\in J(\Lambda_i)\). By the lemma, the cyclic subgroup generated by \(\sigma_i\) contains a \(q\)-cycle. Jordan's classical theorem says that a primitive subgroup of \(S_p\) containing a prime \(q\)-cycle with \(q\le p-3\) contains \(A_p\). Hence
\[
A_p\le G\le S_p.
\]

The sign of a permutation with cycle partition \(\Lambda\) is
\[
\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)
=(-1)^{\sum(e-1)}
=(-1)^{p-\ell(\Lambda)}
=(-1)^{v(\Lambda)}.
\]
If every defect is even, every branch cycle lies in \(A_p\), so \(G=A_p\). If some defect is odd, \(G\) contains an odd permutation, so \(G=S_p\). This proves the monodromy formula for every realization, not just for a particular construction.

For the deck group, view the original degree-\(p\) cover as the quotient of its Galois closure by a point stabilizer \(H\) in the natural action of \(G=A_p\) or \(S_p\). The deck group is \(N_G(H)/H\). The subgroup \(H\) fixes exactly one letter. Any element normalizing \(H\) must preserve that unique fixed letter, hence already lies in \(H\). Therefore \(N_G(H)=H\), and the deck group is trivial.

Finally, the inertia order in the Galois closure above the \(i\)-th branch value is
\[
m_i=\operatorname{ord}(\sigma_i)=\operatorname{lcm}(\Lambda_i).
\]
Riemann--Hurwitz for the \(G\)-Galois cover gives
\[
2\widetilde g-2
=|G|\left(-2+\sum_{i=1}^k\left(1-\frac1{m_i}\right)\right),
\]
which is the displayed genus formula. ∎

## Consequences for Hurwitz spaces

Song--Wen--Zhang show that every compatible prime-degree passport has nonempty connected Hurwitz locus with full support over the branch-value configuration space. On the Jordan-visible sublocus above, every point of that connected-cover locus has the same maximal parity-compatible geometric monodromy, \(A_p\) or \(S_p\), and every corresponding cover has trivial deck stabilizer.

Under the standard Hurwitz weighting \(\sum_f 1/|\operatorname{Aut}(f)|\) for fixed ordered branch values, the connected Hurwitz number is therefore a positive integer on this locus: every summand has weight one.

For three branch values, these give Belyi passports whose connected realizations all have the forced group \(A_p\) or \(S_p\).

## Two degree-seven examples

### An \(A_7\) passport

Take
\[
\Lambda_1=\Lambda_2=\Lambda_3=(3,2,2).
\]
Each defect is \(4\), so the total defect is \(12=2\cdot7-2\), and the source has genus zero. The prime \(q=3\) lies in \(J(3,2,2)\). Hence every connected realization has monodromy \(A_7\) and trivial deck group.

Here every local inertia order is \(6\), so \(|A_7|=2520\) and
\[
\widetilde g
=1+\frac{2520}{2}\left(1-\frac36\right)
=631.
\]
Thus the passport \((3,2,2)^3\) is realized by degree-seven sphere covers, and every connected realization has \(A_7\) normal closure of genus \(631\).

### An \(S_7\) passport

Take
\[
\Lambda_1=\Lambda_2=(5,2),
\qquad
\Lambda_3=(3,1,1,1,1).
\]
The defects are \(5,5,2\), again totaling \(12\), so the source has genus zero. In \((5,2)\), the unique part \(2\) is Jordan-visible. Because the first two local permutations are odd, every connected realization has monodromy \(S_7\) and trivial deck group.

The inertia orders are \(10,10,3\). Since \(|S_7|=5040\),
\[
\widetilde g
=1+\frac{5040}{2}\left(1-\frac1{10}-\frac1{10}-\frac13\right)
=1177.
\]

## Context and originality boundary

The existence input is entirely due to Song--Wen--Zhang (2026): every compatible prime-degree branch datum over the sphere is realizable by a connected cover. Their paper also states the Hurwitz monodromy criterion and derives full-support consequences for prime-degree Hurwitz cycles.

The group-theoretic implication from a small prime cycle in a primitive group to containment of \(A_p\) is Jordan's classical theorem; it is not new. Neumann's 1975 paper is a standard reference for the prime-power-cycle extension. The use of Jordan-type arguments inside Hurwitz-space theory is also established: for example, Cadoret (2005) explicitly uses prime-degree transitivity/primitivity together with a 3-cycle to obtain alternating monodromy in a Hurwitz-space construction.

The contribution here is the combination of the new universal prime-degree realizability theorem with an exact **partition-level** criterion for a local cyclic inertia group to contain a Jordan prime cycle. This gives a passport-intrinsic theorem that forces the monodromy of **every** connected realization, followed by the exact parity dichotomy, triviality of deck groups, and the normal-closure genus formula directly from the passport.

To the best of our knowledge, this passport-level forced-monodromy statement is not made in the current Song--Wen--Zhang preprint, whose accessible text contains no occurrence of “Jordan”, “alternating”, “symmetric”, “Galois closure”, or “automorphism”. Searches under prime-degree Hurwitz/passport, branch-cycle, Jordan-cycle, alternating/symmetric monodromy, and equivalent ramification-profile formulations located classical uses of Jordan's theorem but not this exact criterion combined with universal prime-degree realizability.

## Limitations

- Jordan visibility is sufficient, not necessary. Passports with \(J(\Lambda_i)=\varnothing\) for every \(i\) can still have monodromy \(A_p\) or \(S_p\).
- The result does not classify the smaller transitive prime-degree groups that may occur outside the Jordan-visible locus.
- The normal-closure genus formula is standard once the monodromy group is known; the new content is that the passport forces that group under the stated local criterion.
- Jordan's theorem and its use in Hurwitz theory are prior work and are not claimed as new.
- The main existence source is a very recent preprint. A later revision or unindexed parallel work could contain the same corollary, so originality is only to the best of our knowledge.
