# Exact strict s-numbers of pointwise multipliers on C0-spaces

## Statement

Let \(\Omega\) be a locally compact Hausdorff space, let \(\varphi\in C_b(\Omega)\), and let
\[
M_\varphi:C_0(\Omega)\to C_0(\Omega),\qquad (M_\varphi f)(x)=\varphi(x)f(x).
\]
For \(n\ge 1\), define the topological order statistic
\[
\lambda_n(\varphi)
:=\inf\{t\ge0:\#\{x\in\Omega:|\varphi(x)|>t\}<n\}.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\lambda_n(\varphi)
=\inf_{\substack{F\subset\Omega\\ |F|<n}}
\sup_{x\in\Omega\setminus F}|\varphi(x)|.
\]

**Theorem 1.** For every strict s-number sequence \((s_n)\) in the sense of Pietsch,
\[
\boxed{s_n(M_\varphi)=\lambda_n(\varphi)\qquad(n\ge1).}
\]
Consequently the approximation, Bernstein, Gelfand, Kolmogorov, isomorphism, and Mityagin numbers of \(M_\varphi\) all have the same value at every finite index.

The lower-bound witnesses can be chosen as 1-complemented isometric copies of \(\ell_\infty^n\) generated by pairwise disjoint compactly supported functions.

Set
\[
\rho(\varphi):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\lambda_n(\varphi)
=\inf_{F\subset\Omega\ \mathrm{finite}}
\sup_{x\in\Omega\setminus F}|\varphi(x)|.
\]

**Theorem 2.** The three operator-ideal distances coincide exactly:
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{dist}(M_\varphi,\mathcal K)
=\operatorname{dist}(M_\varphi,\mathcal{FSS})
=\operatorname{dist}(M_\varphi,\mathcal{SS})
=\rho(\varphi).
}
\]
Hence
\[
M_\varphi\text{ is compact}
\iff M_\varphi\text{ is finitely strictly singular}
\iff M_\varphi\text{ is strictly singular}
\iff \rho(\varphi)=0.
\]
For every \(0<t<\rho(\varphi)\), \(M_\varphi\) is bounded below by \(t\) on an isometric copy of \(c_0\). No complementability assertion is made for this infinite-dimensional witness.

If \(K\) is compact Hausdorff and \(K'\) is its derived set of non-isolated points, then
\[
\boxed{\rho(\varphi)=\sup_{x\in K'}|\varphi(x)|,}
\]
with the supremum of the empty set interpreted as zero. In particular, if \(K\) is perfect, then for every strict s-number sequence and every \(n\),
\[
\boxed{s_n(M_\varphi)=\|\varphi\|_\infty.}
\]
Thus on a perfect compact space the complete strict s-number profile of every nonzero pointwise multiplier is flat.

## Proof

### 1. Upper bound for every strict s-number

Fix \(n\ge1\) and \(t>\lambda_n(\varphi)\). Then
\[
U_t:=\{x\in\Omega:|\varphi(x)|>t\}
\]
is finite and has cardinality less than \(n\). Since \(U_t\) is both open and finite in a Hausdorff space, it is clopen. Therefore
\[
R_t:=M_{\varphi\mathbf 1_{U_t}}
\]
is a well-defined finite-rank operator on \(C_0(\Omega)\) with
\[
\operatorname{rank}R_t\le |U_t|<n,
\qquad
\|M_\varphi-R_t\|\le t.
\]
For a strict s-number, the rank axiom and perturbation axiom give
\[
s_n(M_\varphi)\le t.
\]
Letting \(t\downarrow\lambda_n(\varphi)\) yields
\[
s_n(M_\varphi)\le\lambda_n(\varphi).
\]

### 2. A 1-complemented finite-dimensional witness

Let \(0<t<\lambda_n(\varphi)\). Then \(U_t\) contains distinct points
\(x_1,\dots,x_n\). Choose pairwise disjoint open neighborhoods
\(V_i\subset U_t\) of the \(x_i\). Local compactness and the Hausdorff property give functions
\(u_i\in C_c(V_i)\) such that
\[
0\le u_i\le1,\qquad u_i(x_i)=1.
\]
Define
\[
J:\ell_\infty^n\to C_0(\Omega),
\qquad
J(a_1,\dots,a_n)=\sum_{i=1}^n a_i u_i,
\]
and
\[
Q:C_0(\Omega)\to\ell_\infty^n,
\qquad
Qf=(f(x_1),\dots,f(x_n)).
\]
Because the supports of the \(u_i\) are disjoint,
\[
\|J\|=1,\qquad \|Q\|=1,\qquad QJ=I_{\ell_\infty^n}.
\]
Thus \(P=JQ\) is a projection of norm one onto \(J(\ell_\infty^n)\): complementability is explicit rather than inferred from a basis property.

Let
\[
D=\operatorname{diag}(\varphi(x_1),\dots,\varphi(x_n)).
\]
Then
\[
QM_\varphi J=D.
\]
Since \(|\varphi(x_i)|>t\), \(D\) is invertible and \(\|D^{-1}\|<1/t\). Hence
\[
I_{\ell_\infty^n}=D^{-1}QM_\varphi J.
\]
The ideal axiom and strict normalization therefore imply
\[
1=s_n(I_{\ell_\infty^n})
\le \|D^{-1}Q\|\,s_n(M_\varphi)\,\|J\|
<\frac{s_n(M_\varphi)}{t}.
\]
Thus \(s_n(M_\varphi)>t\). Letting \(t\uparrow\lambda_n(\varphi)\) proves Theorem 1.

### 3. Exact distances to compact, FSS, and SS operators

The sequence \(\lambda_n(\varphi)\) decreases to
\[
\rho(\varphi)=\inf_{F\ \mathrm{finite}}
\sup_{\Omega\setminus F}|\varphi|.
\]
If \(t>\rho(\varphi)\), then \(U_t\) is finite. The same finite-rank truncation \(R_t\) gives
\[
\operatorname{dist}(M_\varphi,\mathcal K)\le t.
\]
Hence
\[
\operatorname{dist}(M_\varphi,\mathcal K)\le\rho(\varphi).
\]

Now let \(0<t<\rho(\varphi)\). Then \(U_t\) is infinite. An infinite locally compact Hausdorff space contains a countably infinite family of pairwise disjoint nonempty open sets; applying this inside \(U_t\), choose disjoint open sets \(V_j\subset U_t\), points \(x_j\in V_j\), and functions \(u_j\in C_c(V_j)\) with \(0\le u_j\le1\) and \(u_j(x_j)=1\).

The map
\[
J:c_0\to C_0(\Omega),
\qquad
J(a)=\sum_{j=1}^\infty a_j u_j,
\]
is an isometry. Indeed, disjoint supports give the norm identity, and \(a_j\to0\) makes the series a uniform limit of finite sums. Moreover
\[
\|M_\varphi Ja\|_\infty
\ge t\|a\|_\infty
\qquad(a\in c_0).
\]
Thus \(M_\varphi\) fixes an isometric copy of \(c_0\) with lower bound \(t\).

If \(S\) were strictly singular and \(\|M_\varphi-S\|<t\), then \(S\) would be bounded below on \(J(c_0)\), a contradiction. Therefore
\[
\operatorname{dist}(M_\varphi,\mathcal{SS})\ge t.
\]
Letting \(t\uparrow\rho(\varphi)\), and using
\[
\mathcal K\subset\mathcal{FSS}\subset\mathcal{SS},
\]
gives
\[
\rho(\varphi)
\le\operatorname{dist}(M_\varphi,\mathcal{SS})
\le\operatorname{dist}(M_\varphi,\mathcal{FSS})
\le\operatorname{dist}(M_\varphi,\mathcal K)
\le\rho(\varphi),
\]
which proves Theorem 2.

### 4. Compact spaces

Let \(K\) be compact Hausdorff. If \(x\in K'\) and \(|\varphi(x)|>t\), continuity gives a neighborhood of \(x\) contained in \(\{|\varphi|>t\}\); because \(x\) is non-isolated, every such neighborhood is infinite. Hence
\[
\rho(\varphi)\ge\sup_{K'}|\varphi|.
\]
Conversely, if \(t>\sup_{K'}|\varphi|\), then \(\{|\varphi|>t\}\) cannot be infinite: an infinite subset of a compact Hausdorff space has an accumulation point, which would lie in \(K'\) and, by continuity, satisfy \(|\varphi|\ge t\). Thus this superlevel set is finite and \(\rho(\varphi)\le t\). This proves the claimed formula. If \(K'=K\), then \(\rho(\varphi)=\|\varphi\|_\infty\); since \(\lambda_1(\varphi)=\|\varphi\|_\infty\) and \(\lambda_n\downarrow\rho\), every \(\lambda_n\) equals \(\|\varphi\|_\infty\).

## Prior literature and originality boundary

Pietsch introduced the Banach-space s-number framework in 1974. Hutton, Morrell, and Retherford studied approximation numbers and Kolmogorov diameters of diagonal operators in 1976. Faried and Abd El Ghaffar (2012) explicitly report the exact approximation-number formula for diagonal multiplication operators on \(\ell_p\), and report that the same estimate for \(\ell_\infty\) goes back to earlier work of Pietsch. Edmunds and Lang (2012) give non-Hilbert examples in which all strict s-numbers coincide, for integral operators and Sobolev embeddings. Schep (2023) proves general essential-norm and essential-spectral-radius results for abstract multiplication operators in Banach lattices, and Kiwerski and Tomaszewski (2026) compute essential and weak essential norms for pointwise multipliers between Köthe spaces.

Accordingly, no novelty is claimed here for the abstract notion of strict s-numbers, for the discrete diagonal approximation-number formula, for qualitative compact-multiplier phenomena, or for essential-norm formulas by themselves. The contribution claimed **to the best of our knowledge** is the simultaneous finite-level identity
\(s_n(M_\varphi)=\lambda_n(\varphi)\) for every strict s-number on arbitrary \(C_0(\Omega)\), together with the exact \(\mathcal K/\mathcal{FSS}/\mathcal{SS}\) distance collapse and the derived-set/perfect-space consequences above.

The closest incompletely inspected source is Aksoy and Lewicki, *Diagonal Operators, S-Numbers, and Bernstein Pairs* (1997): its accessible bibliographic page and abstract indicate substantial use of diagonal operators and general s-numbers, but the full theorem text was not inspected. Older monographs and the broader literature on diagonal, central, and disjointness-preserving operators remain a residual priority risk because the argument above is elementary and may admit an equivalent older formulation.

## Limitations

- The theorem concerns scalar pointwise multipliers on \(C_0(\Omega)\); it does not claim the same formula for weighted composition operators or arbitrary central operators on general Banach lattices.
- The infinite-dimensional \(c_0\) obstruction used for strict singularity is not asserted to be complemented. Only the finite-dimensional \(\ell_\infty^n\) witnesses are explicitly 1-complemented.
- Originality is asserted only to the best of our knowledge and is subject to the literature risk described above.
