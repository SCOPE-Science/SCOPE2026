# Single-automorphism compression of bounded-to-one commutative monoid orbits
## Finding
Let \(M\) be a countable commutative monoid acting Borelly on a standard Borel space \(X\). Assume that for every \(m\in M\), the map \(x\mapsto mx\) is bounded-to-one; the finite bound may depend on \(m\). Define
\[
x\,E_M^X\,y
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
ax=by\text{ for some }a,b\in M.
\]

Then there exists a Borel automorphism
\[
T:X\to X
\]
such that the orbit equivalence relation generated by \(T\) is exactly \(E_M^X\). Thus a countable commutative family of possibly noninvertible bounded-to-one maps can be compressed, at the level of orbit equivalence, to one invertible Borel transformation.

In addition, exactly one of the following alternatives holds:
\[
E_M^X\text{ is smooth},
\]
or
\[
E_M^X\equiv_B E_0,
\]
where \(E_0\) is eventual equality on \(2^{\mathbb N}\). Hence this action class contains no nonsmooth Borel-reducibility degree strictly below \(E_0\).

## Assumptions and scope
A Borel reduction from an equivalence relation \(E\) on \(X\) to an equivalence relation \(F\) on \(Y\) is a Borel map \(r:X\to Y\) such that
\[
x\,E\,x'
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
r(x)\,F\,r(x').
\]
Write \(E\leq_B F\) when such a map exists and \(E\equiv_B F\) when both reductions exist. A Borel equivalence relation is smooth when it is Borel reducible to equality on a standard Borel space.

The monoid is only assumed countable and commutative, not finitely generated or cancellative. The action need not be free. Bounded-to-one means that each acting map has finite fibers with some finite bound depending on the monoid element.

The automorphism \(T\) is asserted only to reproduce the same equivalence relation. No claim is made that \(T\) algebraically simulates the original monoid action or belongs to the monoid generated by its maps.

## Proof
First, \(E_M^X\) is a countable Borel equivalence relation. For Borelness,
\[
E_M^X
=
\bigcup_{(a,b)\in M^2}
\{(x,y)\in X^2:ax=by\}.
\]
Each set in the union is Borel because the maps \(x\mapsto ax\) and \(y\mapsto by\) are Borel and equality is Borel on a standard Borel space. Since \(M^2\) is countable, the union is Borel.

For countability, fix \(x\in X\). If \(y\,E_M^X\,x\), then \(by=ax\) for some \(a,b\in M\). For fixed \(a,b\), the possible \(y\) lie in the finite fiber \(b^{-1}(\{ax\})\). A countable union of these finite fibers is countable, so every \(E_M^X\)-class is countable.

Shinko, Weilacher, and Yu prove that every bounded-to-one Borel action of a countable commutative monoid has hyperfinite orbit equivalence relation. Therefore \(E_M^X\) is hyperfinite. An independent 2026 argument of Wang, Yu, and Zhu recovers the same conclusion from finite Borel asymptotic dimension of finitely generated subactions and an increasing-union theorem.

The Dougherty--Jackson--Kechris characterization of hyperfinite countable Borel equivalence relations now gives two consequences simultaneously:
\[
E_M^X\leq_B E_0,
\]
and there exists a Borel action of \(\mathbb Z\) on \(X\) whose orbit equivalence relation is \(E_M^X\). If \(T\) is the action of \(1\in\mathbb Z\), then \(T\) is a Borel automorphism and its orbit relation is exactly \(E_M^X\). This proves the compression statement.

If \(E_M^X\) is smooth, this is the first alternative. Suppose instead that \(E_M^X\) is nonsmooth. The Harrington--Kechris--Louveau Glimm--Effros dichotomy gives
\[
E_0\leq_B E_M^X.
\]
Together with \(E_M^X\leq_B E_0\), this yields
\[
E_M^X\equiv_B E_0.
\]
The two alternatives are exclusive because \(E_0\) is nonsmooth.

## Verification
The argument was checked at each logical interface. The recent monoid theorem applies to countable commutative monoids and allows the fiber bound to depend on the acting element. The orbit relation used here agrees with the source definition \(ax=by\) for some \(a,b\in M\).

Countability was verified directly rather than inferred from hyperfiniteness: each equivalence class is a countable union of finite fibers. This is the hypothesis needed for the Dougherty--Jackson--Kechris characterization.

The single-automorphism conclusion uses the same characterization, not an unproved conversion of the original monoid maps: a Borel \(\mathbb Z\)-action exists with exactly the same equivalence relation, and its generator is a Borel automorphism.

For the degree dichotomy, the two classical comparison theorems are used in opposite directions. Hyperfiniteness supplies \(E_M^X\leq_B E_0\), while nonsmoothness supplies \(E_0\leq_B E_M^X\). No stronger implication is assumed.

## Relationship to prior work
Shinko--Weilacher--Yu establish hyperfiniteness for bounded-to-one Borel actions of countable commutative monoids. Wang--Yu--Zhu independently recover that theorem from sharp asymptotic-dimension bounds for finitely generated commutative monoid actions. Neither source states the single-automorphism compression together with the exact smooth-versus-\(E_0\) Borel-degree classification above.

The new consequences come from combining the recent hyperfiniteness theorem with two classical descriptive-set-theoretic results. Dougherty--Jackson--Kechris identify hyperfinite countable Borel equivalence relations as precisely those reducible to \(E_0\), and equivalently as orbit relations of Borel \(\mathbb Z\)-actions. Harrington--Kechris--Louveau show that every nonsmooth Borel equivalence relation admits \(E_0\) below it.

The synthesis isolates two concrete consequences for the newly enlarged action class: countably many possibly noninvertible commuting maps become one invertible transformation at orbit level, and every nonsmooth instance has the same benchmark Borel degree.

## Limitations
The automorphism \(T\) reproduces only the orbit equivalence relation. It need not preserve the original semigroup dynamics, identify individual monoid elements, or lie in the transformation monoid generated by the action.

The result is an exact consequence of a recent hyperfiniteness theorem combined with classical general theorems; it does not provide a new proof of hyperfiniteness.

Smooth instances can still have many distinct dynamical forms. No claim is made for noncommutative monoids, for actions whose maps have infinite fibers, for partial actions, or for reducibility notions finer than ordinary Borel reducibility.
