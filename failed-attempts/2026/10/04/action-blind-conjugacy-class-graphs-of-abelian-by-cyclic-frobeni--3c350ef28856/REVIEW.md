# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

The Frobenius calculation is mathematically sound. Fixed-point-free action gives trivial center; nonzero kernel classes are complement orbits of size \(m\), while every nonkernel class meets the cyclic complement. Abelianity of the kernel makes the kernel-class component a clique and cyclicity of the complement makes the complement-class component a clique. A mixed nilpotent edge would put nontrivial coprime Hall parts in a nilpotent subgroup and force commuting representatives, contradicting fixed-point-freeness. The group is metabelian, so every pair of nontrivial conjugacy classes generates a solvable subgroup. The two \(\mathbf F_p^2\rtimes C_3\) actions are nonisomorphic because the scalar action fixes every projective line whereas the split action has only two invariant lines.

## originality

FAIL

The final graph decomposition is implication-covered by earlier primary theorems. Herzog–Longobardi–Maj Theorem 16 already gives the two commuting-conjugacy-class components for a finite solvable Frobenius group, and Mohammadian–Erfanian Theorem 3.4 gives the analogous two NCC components for a finite solvable Frobenius group. Specializing to an abelian kernel and cyclic complement makes both components complete, and standard Frobenius conjugacy-class counts give the displayed clique sizes. Completeness of the solvable-class graph is immediate from metabelianity. The advertised action-blind collision is then a routine application of those formulas to two elementary \(C_3\)-modules. Under an implication-based originality standard, the absence of the exact title or numerical collision in the old papers does not rescue the claim.

## value

PASS

The action-blindness observation is a mathematically natural graph-recognition warning: three conjugacy-class graphs can fail simultaneously to distinguish nonisomorphic Frobenius actions. Its structural interpretation is useful even though the underlying graph formulas are already implication-covered.
