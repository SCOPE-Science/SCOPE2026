# Canonical-dual scalability phase diagram for diagonal Hadamard pairs
## Finding
Let \(n\ge 2\) admit a real Hadamard matrix. Normalize it to an orthogonal matrix \(Q\) whose entries are \(\pm 1/\sqrt n\), and write its row vectors as \(x_1,\ldots,x_n\in\mathbb R^n\). Let
\[
D=\operatorname{diag}(d_1,\ldots,d_n),\qquad d_j>0,
\]
and form
\[
\Phi_D=\{x_i:1\le i\le n}\cup\{Dx_i:1\le i\le n}.
\]
Put \(T=\{d_j^2:1\le j\le n}\). Then the canonical dual of \(\Phi_D\) is scalable if and only if one of the following holds:

1. \(|T|=1\); or
2. \(T=\{u,v}\) with \(u\ne v\) and \(uv\le 1\).

In the two-level case, an explicit Parseval scaling of the canonical dual is obtained by multiplying every canonical-dual vector arising from \(x_i\) by
\[
\sqrt{1-uv}
\]
and every canonical-dual vector arising from \(Dx_i\) by
\[
\sqrt{2+u+v}.
\]
Thus the product surface \(uv=1\) is the exact boundary between scalable and non-scalable two-level canonical duals. Under the usual convention allowing zero scaling coefficients, the boundary is included. For strict scalability, the exact criterion is \(|T|=1\), or \(T=\{u,v}\) with \(uv<1\).

## Assumptions and scope
A frame is called scalable when its vectors can be multiplied by nonnegative real scalars to produce a Parseval frame; zero coefficients are allowed. Strict scalability requires all scaling coefficients to be positive. The result concerns the real finite-dimensional family generated by one normalized Hadamard basis and its image under a positive diagonal operator. No existence assertion for Hadamard matrices is made.

The frame operator of \(\Phi_D\) is
\[
S=I+D^2,
\]
because \(\{x_i}\) is an orthonormal basis and
\[
\sum_i (Dx_i)(Dx_i)^{\mathsf T}=D^2.
\]
The original frame \(\Phi_D\) is always scalable since its first block already forms an orthonormal basis.

## Proof
Casazza--De Carli--Tran prove that for a frame \(\{f_k}\) with frame operator \(S\), its canonical dual \(\{S^{-1}f_k}\) is scalable if and only if there are scaling coefficients on the original frame whose weighted frame operator is \(S^2\). Apply this criterion to the two blocks of \(\Phi_D\).

Suppose first that the canonical dual is scalable. Then there are real coefficients \(a_i,b_i\) such that
\[
\sum_i a_i^2 x_i x_i^{\mathsf T}+
\sum_i b_i^2 D x_i x_i^{\mathsf T}D
=(I+D^2)^2.
\]
Write
\[
A=\sum_i a_i^2\ge0,\qquad B=\sum_i b_i^2\ge0.
\]
Since every coordinate of every Hadamard row has squared modulus \(1/n\), the \(j\)-th diagonal entry of the preceding identity gives
\[
\frac{A+B t_j}n=(1+t_j)^2,
\qquad t_j=d_j^2.
\]
Hence the affine function \(A+Bt\) agrees, at every \(t\in T\), with the genuinely quadratic function \(n(1+t)^2\). Therefore \(|T|\le2\).

If \(T=\{u,v}\) with \(u\ne v\), subtracting the two equations yields
\[
\frac Bn=2+u+v,
\]
and then
\[
\frac An=1-uv.
\]
Because \(A\ge0\), necessarily \(uv\le1\).

Conversely, suppose \(T=\{u,v}\) and \(uv\le1\). Choose block-constant squared scaling coefficients
\[
a^2=1-uv,\qquad b^2=2+u+v.
\]
Then
\[
a^2I+b^2D^2=(I+D^2)^2,
\]
because for each eigenvalue \(t\in\{u,v}\),
\[
1-uv+(2+u+v)t=(1+t)^2.
\]
Therefore the scaled canonical dual has frame operator
\[
S^{-1}(a^2I+b^2D^2)S^{-1}=I,
\]
so it is Parseval.

If \(T=\{t}\), then \(D^2=tI\) and the conclusion is immediate; for example, scaling the first canonical-dual block by \(1+t\) and the second by zero gives a Parseval frame. A strict scaling is also available by splitting \((1+t)^2\) into two positive contributions. In the two-level case the diagonal equations force \(A/n=1-uv\), so strict scalability holds exactly when \(uv<1\).

## Verification
The proof reduces the question to an exact affine-versus-quadratic interpolation identity. The accompanying `verify.py` checks the symbolic two-level identity, the sharp boundary case, and the three-level obstruction used in the motivating Hadamard example. These checks are supplementary; the theorem itself follows from the displayed identities for arbitrary admissible \(n\) and \(D\).

For the source example with squared diagonal levels \(1,4,9\), the first two levels force an affine interpolant whose residual at \(9\) equals \(-40\), so the third level is incompatible. This recovers the published obstruction as one point in the general phase diagram.

## Relationship to prior work
Casazza, De Carli, and Tran prove the general canonical-dual scalability criterion used above and give a Hadamard construction with diagonal multipliers \(1,\ldots,1,2,3\) whose canonical dual is not scalable. Their argument establishes existence of an obstruction but does not state the full classification of the diagonal Hadamard family. The result here converts that construction into an exact all-parameter criterion: at most two squared diagonal levels are possible, and the two-level region is cut sharply by \(uv\le1\).

The later paper by Heydarpour, Arefijamaal, and Ghaani Farashahi characterizes scalable frames in \(\mathbb R^2\) and \(\mathbb R^3\) and studies scalable dual frames. Its accessible abstract and bibliographic material do not state this arbitrary-Hadamard-order canonical-dual phase diagram. The full theorem body of that later paper was not materially available in the present comparison, so it remains the principal literature risk.

## Limitations
The theorem uses flat coordinate magnitudes of a real Hadamard basis in an essential way. It does not classify canonical-dual scalability for arbitrary orthonormal bases, non-diagonal operators, complex Hadamard matrices, or general dynamical frames. It also does not resolve Hadamard existence. The originality assessment is limited by incomplete access to the full 2024 dual-scalability paper; no claim is made beyond the stated family.
