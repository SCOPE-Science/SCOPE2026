# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

For a product point, if the strict upper set has a minimum, increasing one coordinate forces the other coordinate of that minimum to stay fixed; both coordinates therefore cannot be nonmaximal. This gives the stated up-beat iff criterion, and the opposite order gives the down-beat criterion. Minimality equivalence follows by choosing a maximal or minimal point in the other nonempty finite factor. Products of the factor core deformation retractions produce a strong deformation retract onto the product core, which is minimal, so core cardinality multiplies.

## originality

FAIL

Barmak’s 2011 monograph already states Proposition 2.7.4: if \(X_c\) and \(Y_c\) are cores, then \(X_c\times Y_c\) is a core of \(X\times Y\). Its proof explicitly establishes that a product of minimal finite spaces is minimal by analyzing beat points. Thus the principal product-core theorem and cardinality multiplicativity are directly prior; the audited pointwise iff criterion is only a short local sharpening of the same proof.
