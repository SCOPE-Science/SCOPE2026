# Closure spectrum of the universal homogeneous Steiner quasigroup
## Finding
Let \(M_{\mathrm F}\) be the countable universal homogeneous locally finite Steiner quasigroup, with product \(\cdot\), and let
\[
G=\operatorname{Aut}(M_{\mathrm F})\leq \operatorname{Sym}(\Omega).
\]
For a positive integer \(k\), write \(G^{(k)}\) for the Wielandt \(k\)-closure: the permutations of \(\Omega\) that preserve every \(G\)-orbit on ordered \(k\)-tuples. Then
\[
G^{(1)}=G^{(2)}=\operatorname{Sym}(\Omega),\qquad
G^{(k)}=G\ 	ext{ for every }\ k\geq 3.
\]
Hence the exact closure threshold of this action is \(3\).

## Assumptions and scope
The argument uses the standard Fraïssé-limit homogeneity of \(M_{\mathrm F}\) in the Steiner-quasigroup language and the usual definition of \(k\)-closure. It does not assert that the relational complexity itself equals \(3\); it only determines the full \(k\)-closure spectrum and therefore gives the sharp lower bound \(3\) for relational complexity.

## Proof
A Steiner quasigroup satisfies \(x\cdot x=x\), commutativity, and \(x\cdot(x\cdot y)=y\). If \(a
eq b\), the subquasigroup generated by \(a,b\) is exactly
\[
\{a,b,a\cdot b\},
\]
the unique three-point Steiner quasigroup.

First, \(G\) is transitive and 2-transitive. Given ordered distinct pairs \((a,b)\) and \((c,d)\), the map
\[
a\mapsto c,\qquad b\mapsto d,\qquad a\cdot b\mapsto c\cdot d
\]
is an isomorphism between the corresponding three-point subquasigroups. Homogeneity extends it to an automorphism of \(M_{\mathrm F}\). Therefore the \(G\)-orbits on \(\Omega^2\) are exactly the diagonal and the off-diagonal. Every permutation of \(\Omega\) preserves those two sets, so
\[
G^{(2)}=\operatorname{Sym}(\Omega).
\]
Transitivity similarly gives \(G^{(1)}=\operatorname{Sym}(\Omega)\).

Now consider ordered triples \((a,b,c)\) of distinct elements satisfying \(a\cdot b=c\). Any two such triples enumerate isomorphic three-point subquasigroups, so homogeneity shows that all ordered distinct block triples form a single \(G\)-orbit \(\mathcal B\subseteq\Omega^3\).

Let \(\pi\in G^{(3)}\). By definition, \(\pi\) preserves each \(G\)-orbit on \(\Omega^3\), hence it preserves \(\mathcal B\). Thus for all distinct \(a,b\),
\[
\pi(a\cdot b)=\pi(a)\cdot\pi(b).
\]
The same identity for \(a=b\) follows from idempotence and injectivity of \(\pi\). Hence \(\pi\) preserves the quasigroup product and belongs to \(G\). Since always \(G\leq G^{(3)}\),
\[
G^{(3)}=G.
\]
Finally, the standard monotonicity of closures gives
\[
G^{(k)}\leq G^{(3)}=G\qquad(k\geq3),
\]
while \(G\leq G^{(k)}\), so \(G^{(k)}=G\) for every \(k\geq3\).

## Verification
The proof above is purely structural. A bundled finite sanity check independently computes the automorphism group and the first three closure sizes for the Fano Steiner triple system. It obtains
\[
|G^{(1)}|=|G^{(2)}|=5040,\qquad |G^{(3)}|=168=|G|,
\]
which matches the same mechanism in the smallest nontrivial finite example. This finite computation is not used as a proof for \(M_{\mathrm F}\).

## Relationship to prior work
Barbina and Casanovas establish the model-theoretic setting and identify \(M_{\mathrm F}\) as the countable universal homogeneous locally finite Steiner quasigroup/Fraïssé limit. The modern \(k\)-closure literature defines \(G^{(k)}\), records the descending closure chain, and explains its connection with relational complexity. Cameron's orbit theorem for Steiner triple systems concerns a block-orbit versus point-orbit inequality, not the closure spectrum determined here.

Targeted searches for “Steiner quasigroup 3-closed”, “Steiner triple system 2-closure”, “Fraïssé limit Steiner k-closure”, and semantic searches for the full closure-spectrum statement did not locate this exact result. This is evidence of noncoverage, not a proof of priority; an unindexed permutation-group observation remains a residual risk.

## Limitations
The result determines orbit-reconstruction by \(k\)-closures, not the full relational complexity parameter. The originality assessment is literature-based and cannot exclude unpublished or unindexed folklore. The finite Fano calculation is only a reproducibility check of the mechanism.
