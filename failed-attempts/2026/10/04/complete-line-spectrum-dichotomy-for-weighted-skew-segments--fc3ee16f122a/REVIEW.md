# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

For a spectrum contained in a line, all exponentials factor through the scalar projection. Completeness therefore forces that projection to be essentially one-to-one on the two segment components. The projected measure has constant density on its support; after scaling the line direction by that density one obtains Lebesgue measure on two disjoint intervals whose lengths are \(c_1T_1\) and \(c_2T_2\) and sum to one. Łaba's exhaustive two-interval theorem then gives exactly the integer-gap lattice case and, only for equal half-lengths, the two-coset case with \(2g+1\in\mathbb N\). The converse follows from the same projection equivalence. The concrete non-lattice example also checks directly.

## originality

FAIL

The final classification is already covered by direct implication of published results. Wu's primary paper states and proves the exact projection equivalence for line spectra under essential injectivity, and its density-uniformity argument supplies the constant-density reduction. Łaba's Theorem 1.1 exhaustively classifies every spectrum of a two-interval set of total length one as precisely the lattice case or the equal-half-length two-coset case. Substituting the projected lengths and gap into Łaba's notation yields every branch of the assigned theorem mechanically, including the equal-mass restriction. That the weighted skew geometry is not written in the identical notation does not avoid implication coverage.

## value

FAIL

The classification is a useful explanatory corollary, but under the audit value bar it does not fill an independent mathematical gap: once the published projection equivalence and Łaba's exhaustive theorem are placed side by side, the result is a direct change of variables. No new structural lemma, boundary construction, or unresolved invariant is required.
