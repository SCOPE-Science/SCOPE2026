# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

For a primitive degree-\(d\) core, its roots are \(\alpha,\alpha^Q,\ldots,\alpha^{Q^{d-1}}\), so the focal source's exterior-product formula gives modes \((-1)^k\alpha^{E_I}\) with \(E_I=\sum_{i\in I}Q^i\). For \(1\le k<d\), these subset exponents are distinct integers strictly between 0 and \(Q^d-1\), hence distinct modulo the order of primitive \(\alpha\). Frobenius multiplication of the exponent by Q rotates the binary support, so minimal-polynomial orbits are exactly cyclic-rotation orbits. A word with orbit size \(d/h\) is an h-fold repetition and forces \(h\mid k\); Möbius inversion gives the stated primitive-necklace count. The coprime case and the irreducibility boundary follow immediately. An independent enumeration reproduced the necklace degree formula for all \(2\le d\le10\), \(1\le k<d\).

## originality

FAIL

The factorization is a direct specialization of the focal paper's classical subset-product eigenvalue formula to a primitive finite-field companion matrix, followed by the standard facts that irreducible factors over \(\mathbb F_Q\) are Frobenius orbits and that cyclic orbits of fixed-weight binary words are counted by primitive-necklace Möbius inversion. The focal paper itself stresses that the root-product modes are classical. Under the required implication test, recombining those standard ingredients does not create an original theorem merely because the exact Toeplitz wording was not found.

## value

FAIL

The factor-degree distribution is tidy and may be convenient for users of the new Toeplitz profile, but mathematically it is a routine application of standard Frobenius-orbit and necklace machinery to a root-product formula already supplied by the focal source. Under the stated value standard, that packaging alone does not clear the bar for a standalone finding.
