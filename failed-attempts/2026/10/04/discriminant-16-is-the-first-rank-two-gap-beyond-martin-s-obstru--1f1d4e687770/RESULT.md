# Discriminant \(16\) is the first rank-two gap beyond Martin's obstruction
## Finding
Let \(A\) be a complex abelian surface with Picard number
\[
\rho(A)=2,
\]
and write
\[
\Delta(A)=\left|\det\operatorname{NS}(A)\right|.
\]
Then the following threshold is sharp.

If
\[
\Delta(A)<16,
\]
then
\[
\operatorname{irr}(A)=3.
\]
At
\[
\Delta(A)=16,
\]
there exist abelian surfaces with
\[
\operatorname{NS}(A)\cong U(4),
\qquad
[U(4)]=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&4\\
4&0
\end{pmatrix},
\]
and every such surface with this exact Néron--Severi lattice satisfies
\[
\operatorname{irr}(A)=4.
\]

This boundary also gives a smallest-discriminant example where Martin's earlier cohomological obstruction is silent: for \(U(4)\), the image of the intersection pairing is
\[
4\mathbf Z,
\]
so it contains \(12\), while the complete 2026 criterion still forces degree of irrationality \(4\).

## Assumptions and scope
The degree of irrationality is
\[
\operatorname{irr}(A)
=
\min\left\{
\deg(f):
f:A\dashrightarrow\mathbf P^2
\text{ is dominant}
\right\}.
\]

For a rank-two even hyperbolic lattice \(K\), its discriminant in this note is the positive number
\[
\Delta=-\det K.
\]
The half-intersection form
\[
f(v)=\frac{K(v,v)}2
\]
is an integral indefinite binary quadratic form.

Huang, Jiang, and Yang prove that a complex abelian surface has degree of irrationality \(3\) exactly when it has a polarization of type
\[
(1,1),\ (1,2),\ (1,3),\ \text{or }(1,6);
\]
otherwise its degree of irrationality is \(4\). Their positivity lemmas identify a primitive class of positive square \(2d\) with a polarization of type \((1,d)\), up to sign.

Martin's earlier obstruction says that if \(12\) is not in the image of
\[
\operatorname{Sym}^2\operatorname{NS}(A)\longrightarrow\mathbf Z,
\]
then \(\operatorname{irr}(A)=4\). The sharp example below is deliberately outside the range where that sufficient obstruction applies.

## Proof
Let \(K\) be any even integral lattice of signature \((1,1)\), and put
\[
\Delta=-\det K>0.
\]
Write
\[
f(X,Y)=\frac12 K((X,Y),(X,Y)).
\]
Let \(m\) be the least positive integer represented by \(f\). A vector representing \(m\) is primitive: otherwise division by its integral content would give a smaller positive represented integer.

Complete such a vector to an integral basis and reduce the second basis vector modulo the first. In the resulting coordinates,
\[
f(X,Y)=mX^2+eXY-\nu Y^2,
\qquad
-m<e\le m,
\qquad
\nu\ge0.
\]
The binary-form reduction in the proof of Lemma 9.1 of Huang--Jiang--Yang gives
\[
m^2\le\Delta.
\]
For completeness, the argument is short. If \(\nu=0\) and \(|e|<m\), one of \(f(1,1)\) and \(f(1,-1)\) is a positive integer smaller than \(m\), contradiction; hence \(|e|=m\). If \(\nu>0\) and \(\Delta=e^2+4m\nu<m^2\), then
\[
4m\nu<(m-|e|)(m+|e|)\le2m(m-|e|),
\]
so
\[
\nu<\frac{m-|e|}{2}.
\]
Choosing the sign that makes \(eY=-|e|\) gives
\[
0<f(1,Y)=m-|e|-\nu<m,
\]
again a contradiction.

Therefore, if
\[
\Delta<16,
\]
then
\[
m\le3.
\]
The lattice \(K\) consequently represents, on a primitive vector, one of
\[
2,\ 4,\ 6.
\]
If \(K=\operatorname{NS}(A)\), the corresponding positive class is, up to sign, ample and gives a polarization of type
\[
(1,1),\ (1,2),\ \text{or }(1,3).
\]
The classification theorem of Huang--Jiang--Yang yields
\[
\operatorname{irr}(A)=3.
\]

Now consider
\[
L=U(4),
\qquad
[L]=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&4\\
4&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its discriminant is
\[
\Delta(L)=16.
\]
For a vector \((x,y)\),
\[
(x,y)^2=8xy,
\]
so every self-intersection is divisible by \(8\). In particular, \(L\) represents none of
\[
2,\ 4,\ 6,\ 12.
\]
It does contain the primitive positive vector \((1,1)\) of square \(8\).

Such Néron--Severi lattices occur on complex abelian surfaces. Indeed,
\[
H^2(A,\mathbf Z)\cong U^3
\]
for a marked abelian surface. With hyperbolic-plane bases \(e_i,f_i\), the map
\[
a\longmapsto e_1,
\qquad
b\longmapsto 4f_1+e_2
\]
embeds \(U(4)\) primitively into \(U^3\): the two images are isotropic, have mutual pairing \(4\), and the \(2\times2\) minor on the \(e_1,e_2\) coordinates equals \(1\). The orthogonal complement has signature \((2,2)\), so its abelian-surface period domain is nonempty. Shioda's surjectivity of the period map realizes its points by abelian surfaces. Choosing a period outside the countable union of additional integral \((1,1)\)-hyperplanes gives
\[
\operatorname{NS}(A)=U(4)
\]
exactly. The positive square-\(8\) class makes such a surface algebraic.

For this lattice the intersection-pairing image is generated by its Gram entries:
\[
\operatorname{im}\!\left(
\operatorname{Sym}^2L\to\mathbf Z
\right)
=
4\mathbf Z.
\]
Hence
\[
12\in4\mathbf Z,
\]
so Martin's sufficient obstruction does not decide the degree of irrationality. But the absence of self-intersections \(2,4,6,12\) excludes all four polarization types in the complete 2026 classification. Therefore
\[
\operatorname{irr}(A)=4.
\]
Since no rank-two Néron--Severi lattice of discriminant below \(16\) can have this property, the threshold is sharp.

## Verification
The accompanying `verify.py` uses exact integer arithmetic. It checks
\[
\det U(4)=-16,
\]
that the pairing ideal is \(4\mathbf Z\), that \(12\) lies in that ideal, and that the target self-intersections \(2,4,6,12\) are absent because all norms have the form \(8xy\).

It also verifies the explicit primitive embedding
\[
U(4)\hookrightarrow U^3
\]
used in the existence argument: the Gram matrix is correct and a \(2\times2\) coordinate minor equals \(1\).

The general lower-discriminant statement is proved symbolically above from
\[
m^2\le\Delta.
\]
The script checks the resulting integer cutoff
\[
\Delta<16\Longrightarrow m\le3.
\]
The replay output ends in `VERIFY_OK`.

## Relationship to prior work
Martin proved the sufficient obstruction
\[
12\notin\operatorname{im}
\left(
\operatorname{Sym}^2\operatorname{NS}(A)\to\mathbf Z
\right)
\Longrightarrow
\operatorname{irr}(A)=4.
\]
For \(U(4)\), the image is \(4\mathbf Z\), so the hypothesis fails and Martin's theorem is silent.

Huang--Jiang--Yang give the complete 2026 classification by the four polarization types. Their Appendix proves a stronger lattice lemma needed for cubic maps and, inside its proof, establishes the reduction inequality
\[
m^2\le\Delta
\]
used here. The paper does not state the discriminant-\(16\) threshold, the \(U(4)\) sharpness example, or the minimal-gap comparison with Martin's obstruction.

Harder's exposition of Shioda's period theorem supplies the standard existence mechanism for abelian surfaces with a prescribed primitive rank-two lattice polarization; generic periods have no additional integral \((1,1)\)-classes.

Targeted searches for \(U(4)\), discriminant \(16\), Martin's \(12\)-obstruction, and equivalent smallest-discriminant formulations located no statement of this sharp threshold.

## Limitations
The finding is specific to Picard number \(2\). It does not claim that discriminant \(16\) is a universal threshold in higher Picard rank.

The existence argument produces a nonempty generic family with exact Néron--Severi lattice \(U(4)\); it does not give an explicit period matrix or projective equation for a member.

The result compares two criteria for degree of irrationality. It does not strengthen the global bound
\[
3\le\operatorname{irr}(A)\le4
\]
for abelian surfaces.
