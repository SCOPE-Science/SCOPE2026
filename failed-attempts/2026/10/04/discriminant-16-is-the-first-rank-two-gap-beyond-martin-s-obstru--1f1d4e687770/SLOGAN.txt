For Picard-rank-two complex abelian surfaces, discriminant \(16\) is the sharp first place where degree of irrationality \(4\) can occur: every smaller Néron--Severi discriminant forces a \((1,1)\), \((1,2)\), or \((1,3)\) polarization, while generic surfaces with \(\operatorname{NS}(A)\cong U(4)\) have \(\operatorname{irr}(A)=4\). The \(U(4)\) example is also the smallest-discriminant case where Martin's older \(12\)-intersection obstruction is silent.
