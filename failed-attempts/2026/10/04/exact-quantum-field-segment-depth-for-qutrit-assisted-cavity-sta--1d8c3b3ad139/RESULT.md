# Exact quantum-field segment depth for qutrit-assisted cavity-state synthesis
## Finding
Consider the alternating control architecture of Luo and Yu for one cavity mode coupled to a three-level ancilla with levels \(|g\rangle,|m\rangle,|e\rangle\). Let
\[
N_{\mathrm{cav}}=\sum_{n\ge 0} n|n\rangle\langle n|,
\qquad
A=|m\rangle\langle m|+2|e\rangle\langle e|,
\qquad
\mathcal N=N_{\mathrm{cav}}+A.
\]
A classical-field segment is any unitary generated by the source Hamiltonian \(H_C\), so it preserves cavity photon number. A quantum-field segment is any unitary generated by the source Hamiltonian \(H_Q\), so it commutes with \(\mathcal N\).

Starting from \(|0,g\rangle\), after any control sequence containing \(k\) quantum-field segments, with arbitrary classical-field segments between them, the cavity state has zero support on every Fock level \(|n\rangle\) with \(n>2k\). Consequently, exact universal synthesis of all source-admissible target states with maximum total excitation \(N\) requires at least
\[
k\ge \left\lceil\frac{N}{2}\right\rceil
\]
quantum-field segments. The Luo--Yu construction attains exactly \(\lceil N/2\rceil\) such segments by processing the excitation blocks \((N,N-1),(N-2,N-3),\ldots\), with the final singleton handled when \(N\) is odd. Therefore the universal quantum-field-segment depth of their alternating architecture is exactly \(\lceil N/2\rceil\).

## Assumptions and scope
The statement concerns the ideal Hamiltonian architecture in arXiv:2609.32845v1. During a classical-field segment, the cavity number is unchanged; during a quantum-field segment, the total excitation \(\mathcal N\) is conserved. The ancilla excitation operator \(A\) has spectrum \(\{0,1,2\}\). Controls may have arbitrary allowed amplitudes, phases, durations, and selective subspace choices; the lower bound does not assume the particular pulse parameters used in the constructive protocol.

The universal target class is the source's finite-excitation subspace: states with total excitation at most the integer \(N\ge0\), including the Fock target \(|N,g\rangle\). The result measures the number of quantum-field segments, equivalently the number of alternating cycles that contain such a segment. It does not claim optimality of physical runtime, integrated control strength, or total laboratory pulse count.

## Proof
For a state \(|\psi\rangle\), let \(M(\psi)\) denote the largest cavity photon number having nonzero amplitude. More generally, for a subspace of possible states, let \(M\) denote the largest photon number occurring in its support.

A classical-field segment leaves \(N_{\mathrm{cav}}\) unchanged. Hence if the cavity support before the segment is contained in \(0\le n\le M\), the same is true afterward. Because the ancilla has excitation number at most \(2\), after any number of classical-field segments the total-excitation support is contained in \(0\le \mathcal N\le M+2\).

Now apply one quantum-field segment. Since its generator commutes with \(\mathcal N\), the segment preserves each total-excitation eigenspace. Therefore it cannot create any component with total excitation larger than \(M+2\). Since cavity photon number is nonnegative and never exceeds total excitation, the cavity support after that quantum-field segment is contained in \(0\le n\le M+2\).

Starting from \(|0,g\rangle\), the initial cavity support has \(M=0\). Induction over the number of quantum-field segments gives
\[
M\le 2k
\]
after every sequence containing \(k\) quantum-field segments. In particular, generating the admissible target \(|N,g\rangle\) requires \(N\le2k\), hence \(k\ge\lceil N/2\rceil\).

For the converse, Luo and Yu construct an exact reversal protocol that removes two consecutive excitation blocks per cycle. Their quantum-field blocks are indexed as \(Q_m=Q_{N,N-1}\), \(Q_{m-1}=Q_{N-2,N-3}\), and so on, and the paper gives the separate final one-block operation needed at odd parity. Thus the construction uses \(m=\lceil N/2\rceil\) quantum-field segments and exactly synthesizes every state in the stated target class. The lower and upper bounds coincide.

## Verification
The support argument is an infinite-family induction and does not depend on finite enumeration. The accompanying `verify.py` is only a reproducibility check of the abstract support rules: a classical closure changes only the ancilla level, while a quantum closure preserves \(n+a\) with \(a\in\{0,1,2\}\). It verifies for \(0\le k\le20\) that the largest photon number permitted by the abstract closure model is \(2k\), and for \(0\le N\le40\) that \(\lceil N/2\rceil\) is the smallest integer \(k\) satisfying \(2k\ge N\). Running `python3 verify.py` prints `VERIFY_OK`.

The decisive proof step is not the finite check: it is the exact conservation law \([H_Q,\mathcal N]=0\), together with the exact cavity-number preservation of \(H_C\) and the ancilla excitation bound \(A\le2I\).

## Relationship to prior work
Law and Eberly introduced deterministic arbitrary cavity-state synthesis with a two-level ancilla. Luo and Yu replace that ancilla by a three-level system and explicitly show that two excitation blocks can be processed in parallel, reducing the number of evolution steps by a factor of two. Their paper gives the constructive halving but does not state or prove a matching lower bound on the number of quantum-field segments.

Santos developed universal harmonic-oscillator manipulation using selective interactions with an auxiliary three-level system. That framework is broader in a different direction: it composes chosen two-Fock-level rotations, and its trapped-ion discussion also allows multi-quantum couplings. Such interactions are outside the present Hamiltonian assumptions and therefore do not imply, or contradict, the segment lower bound proved here.

The contribution here is the exact architecture-level optimality statement: the factor-two reduction of the Luo--Yu construction saturates the excitation-capacity lower bound for its own alternating \(H_C/H_Q\) model.

## Limitations
The result is architecture-specific. It does not exclude fewer segments if classical and quantum channels are active simultaneously, if the ancilla has more than three excitation levels, if higher-order multiphoton couplings are added, or if one changes the source Hamiltonian class. It also does not compare continuous-time quantum speed limits or robustness under noise. A literature search found no prior statement of this exact segment-minimality theorem, but equivalent resource-counting language could exist under a different control-theory formulation.
