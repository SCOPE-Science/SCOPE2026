# Finite-type blindness to infinite hyperbolic embedding antichains
## Finding
Let \(M\) be a compact oriented \(3\)-manifold that embeds in a rational homology sphere. For every integer \(d\ge 0\) and every integer \(n\ge 2\), there is an infinite sequence
\[
(M_i)_{i\ge 1}
\]
of connected compact hyperbolic \(3\)-manifolds with all three properties below:

1. every \(M_i\) is \((Y_{d+1},T_n)\)-equivalent to \(M\);
2. for every \(i\ne j\), \(M_i\) does not embed in \(M_j\);
3. every finite-type invariant \(F\) of degree at most \(d\), in the Torelli-twist sense on the common \(Y_1\)-equivalence class, satisfies
\[
F(M_i)=F(M)
\qquad\text{for every }i.
\]

Thus, for every finite degree cutoff, the entire degree-bounded finite-type profile is constant on an infinite hyperbolic antichain under embeddability.

## Assumptions and scope
A \(Y_k\)-move is a Torelli twist whose gluing map lies in the \(k\)-th term of the lower central series of the relevant Torelli group. The relation \((Y_k,T_n)\) is the refinement in which the gluing maps also lie in the subgroup generated by \(n\)-th powers of Dehn twists.

The finite-type invariants used here are those defined by alternating sums over disjoint Torelli twists. They are considered on a fixed \(Y_1\)-equivalence class of compact oriented \(3\)-manifolds with the same boundary type. The conclusion is about all such invariants with values in an arbitrary abelian group.

The hypothesis that \(M\) embeds in a rational homology sphere is exactly the hypothesis used for the infinite antichain statement in Belletti--Detcherry.

## Proof
Fix \(d\ge0\) and \(n\ge2\). Apply Belletti--Detcherry, Corollary 6.2(2), with
\[
k=d+1.
\]
That corollary gives an infinite sequence of connected compact hyperbolic \(3\)-manifolds \((M_i)_{i\ge1}\), all \((Y_{d+1},T_n)\)-equivalent to \(M\), such that for every ordered pair \(i\ne j\), the manifold \(M_i\) does not embed in \(M_j\).

Because \((Y_{d+1},T_n)\)-equivalence implies \(Y_{d+1}\)-equivalence, every \(M_i\) is \(Y_{d+1}\)-equivalent to \(M\). In particular, all \(M_i\) and \(M\) lie in a common \(Y_1\)-equivalence class.

Massuyeau, Proposition 3.32, states that if two members of a fixed \(Y_1\)-equivalence class are \(Y_{d+1}\)-equivalent, then every finite-type invariant of degree at most \(d\) takes the same value on them. Therefore, for every such invariant \(F\),
\[
F(M_i)=F(M)
\]
for every \(i\). Hence also
\[
F(M_i)=F(M_j)
\]
for every pair \(i,j\).

Combining this equality with the pairwise non-embedding conclusion from Belletti--Detcherry proves the claim.

## Verification
The argument is a direct implication check between two published statements.

The parameter substitution is \(k=d+1\). Belletti--Detcherry provide infinitely many hyperbolic manifolds that are \((Y_k,T_n)\)-equivalent to \(M\) and pairwise non-embeddable. Their definition makes \((Y_k,T_n)\)-equivalence a strengthening of \(Y_k\)-equivalence.

Massuyeau's Proposition 3.32 applies for every \(d\ge0\) and every abelian target group for a finite-type invariant. It requires \(Y_{d+1}\)-equivalence inside a fixed \(Y_1\)-class, which is automatic here.

No computation, census, asymptotic estimate, or unproved converse is used.

## Relationship to prior work
Belletti--Detcherry prove the infinite pairwise non-embedding family in Corollary 6.2(2). Their article discusses finite-type invariants as one classical source of embedding obstructions, but it does not state that their antichain can be chosen to have an identical complete finite-degree finite-type profile.

Massuyeau proves the general one-way implication
\[
Y_{d+1}\text{-equivalence}
\Longrightarrow
\text{agreement of every finite-type invariant of degree at most }d.
\]
The converse is known in special settings and is not used here.

The finding is the synthesis of the new infinite embedding antichain with the established finite-type invariance theorem. It identifies a precise limitation of every fixed finite-degree layer: no such layer can distinguish any two members of the antichain, even though no distinct member embeds in another.

## Limitations
The statement does not say that all finite-type invariants of unbounded degree agree. It fixes \(d\) first and then constructs the infinite family.

The finite-type notion is the Torelli-twist theory used in Massuyeau's Proposition 3.32. Other inequivalent notions of finite type for arbitrary \(3\)-manifolds are not covered.

The conclusion is not an algorithmic impossibility theorem for deciding embeddability. It only proves blindness of every fixed degree-bounded family of these finite-type invariants.

The hypothesis that \(M\) embeds in a rational homology sphere is retained from the source antichain theorem.
