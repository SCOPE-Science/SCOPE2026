# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

The restricted non-arithmeticity claim is mathematically correct once the source theorem is restricted to arithmetic sentences. The primary paper defines \(N(x)=\Diamond F(x)\), \(x<y\), and \(x\equiv y\) using exactly the three displayed conditional forms, then defines \(Z,S\), Ax1--Ax8, and the arithmetic translation by ordinary first-order quantification around those abbreviations. Thus expanding every abbreviation leaves each conditional at depth one with quantifier-free, conditional-free operands. The paper proves that arithmetic truth reduces to weakly Stalnakerian validity and that weakly Stalnakerian and Stalnakerian frames have the same logic; its explicit countermodel exercise was also checked in the audited package. Therefore the computable reduction lands inside the stated flat fragment, proving non-arithmeticity there. No finite experiment is needed.

## originality

FAIL

The primary source itself contains every load-bearing ingredient of the restricted theorem. Pages 7--11 define \(N,<,\equiv\), Ax1--Ax8, and the arithmetic translation and prove the reduction to validity; page 11 further says that the argument already works with one unary and two ternary predicates and asks which natural fragments remain axiomatizable. Inspecting the literal definitions shows immediately that every conditional occurrence has one of the three audited quantifier-free templates. Hence the asserted flat-fragment lower bound is a direct syntactic corollary of the published reduction, not an independent strengthening under an implication-based originality standard. Naming the fragment and spelling out the expansion does not create new mathematical coverage.

FAIL

Flat quantified fragments are a natural boundary, but here the result requires only inspecting and expanding the formulas of an already-published arithmetic reduction. The source already records the three-predicate restriction and leaves fragment questions open; observing that its conditionals are syntactically shallow is useful exposition but not a sufficiently substantive new mathematical step under the common value bar.
