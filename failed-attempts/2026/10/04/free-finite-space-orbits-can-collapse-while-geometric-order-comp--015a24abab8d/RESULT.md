# Free finite-space orbits can collapse while geometric order-complex orbits retain circles
## Finding
For every finite nontrivial group \(G\) and every generating set \(H=\{h_1,\ldots,h_r\}\) of distinct nonidentity elements, with \(r\ge 1\), consider the finite \(T_0\)-space (equivalently, finite poset)
\[
X_H=G\times\{-1,0,\ldots,r\}
\]
with order generated by
\[
(g,i)\le (g,j)\quad(-1\le i\le j\le r)
\]
and
\[
(gh_i,-1)\le (g,j)\quad(1\le i\le j\le r).
\]
Let \(G\) act by left translation on the first coordinate. This action is free on points. Nevertheless, the finite orbit space \(X_H/G\) is the total chain on \(r+2\) points and therefore has contractible order complex, while the topological orbit of the geometric order complex satisfies
\[
|\mathcal K(X_H)|/G\simeq \bigvee^r S^1.
\]
Consequently, the natural comparison map
\[
|\mathcal K(X_H)|/G\longrightarrow |\mathcal K(X_H/G)|
\]
is not a weak homotopy equivalence for any \(r\ge1\). Moreover, the finite-space orbit map \(X_H\to X_H/G\) is not a covering map, despite the action being free on points.

## Assumptions and scope
The generating elements \(h_1,\ldots,h_r\) are required to be distinct and nonidentity, exactly as in the reduced form of the Barmak--Minian group-realization construction. The phrase “geometric order-complex orbit” means the ordinary topological quotient of the geometric realization \(|\mathcal K(X_H)|\); it is not the naive abstract simplicial quotient obtained by identifying edge orbits and then discarding multiple edges.

The conclusion concerns finite \(T_0\)-spaces and their associated order complexes. It does not assert that arbitrary free actions on finite spaces have the same behavior, nor that every quotient comparison fails.

## Proof
The left action is
\[
a\cdot(g,i)=(ag,i).
\]
If \(a\cdot(g,i)=(g,i)\), then \(ag=g\), hence \(a=e\). Thus the action on points is free.

There is one \(G\)-orbit for each level \(-1,0,\ldots,r\). The vertical relations \((g,i)\le(g,j)\) show that the quotient levels satisfy \(i\le j\) whenever \(i\le j\). No relation in \(X_H\) decreases the level, so no reverse inequality can be created in the quotient. Hence \(X_H/G\) is exactly the chain
\[
-1<0<\cdots<r.
\]
Its order complex is an \((r+1)\)-simplex, so it is contractible.

Now let
\[
Y=G\times\{-1,r\}\subseteq X_H.
\]
Define \(\rho:X_H\to Y\) by
\[
\rho(g,-1)=(g,-1),\qquad \rho(g,i)=(g,r)\quad(0\le i\le r).
\]
The defining relations of \(X_H\) imply that \(\rho\) is order-preserving. It fixes \(Y\), commutes with the left \(G\)-action, and satisfies \(x\le\rho(x)\) for every \(x\in X_H\). Thus the inclusion \(Y\hookrightarrow X_H\) and \(\rho\) give a \(G\)-equivariant finite-space homotopy equivalence. Passing to order complexes and then taking topological orbit spaces gives
\[
|\mathcal K(X_H)|/G\simeq |\mathcal K(Y)|/G.
\]

The order complex \(\mathcal K(Y)\) is a graph. Its vertices are the \(2|G|\) points of \(Y\). For each \(g\in G\), there is one vertical edge
\[
(g,-1)<(g,r)
\]
and, for every \(1\le i\le r\), one additional edge
\[
(gh_i,-1)<(g,r).
\]
Under left translation, the two levels form two vertex orbits. The vertical edges form one edge orbit, and the edges indexed by each fixed \(h_i\) form one further edge orbit. Distinct generators give distinct edge orbits. Therefore the geometric quotient \(|\mathcal K(Y)|/G\) is the graph with two vertices joined by \(r+1\) parallel edges. Its first Betti number is
\[
(r+1)-2+1=r,
\]
so it has homotopy type \(\bigvee^r S^1\). This proves the orbit-space comparison.

Finally, the orbit map is not a covering. Fix \(g\in G\). At level \(1\), the minimal open neighborhood of \((g,1)\) contains both \((g,-1)\) and \((gh_1,-1)\). These are distinct because \(h_1\ne e\), but the quotient map sends them to the same level \(-1\) orbit. Thus the restriction over that minimal neighborhood is not injective, contradicting the finite-poset covering criterion.

## Verification
The proof is symbolic and applies to every finite nontrivial \(G\) and every generating set satisfying the hypotheses. A standalone checker independently constructs the defining graphs for three test groups, verifies the orbit-level chain, verifies the edge-orbit count, checks the graph Betti ranks, and checks the explicit local obstruction to the quotient map being a covering. Running

`python3 artifacts/verify.py`

produces

`VERIFY_OK samples=C3,V4,S3 finite_orbit=chain geometric_orbit_betti=1,2,2 quotient_not_covering`

The finite checks are stress tests of the formulas; they are not used as a substitute for the quantified proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Barmak and Minian introduced the posets \(X_H\) in their realization of arbitrary finite groups as automorphism groups of finite posets. Their construction includes the two-level deformation retract \(Y\) and computes the weak homotopy type of \(X_H\), but it does not state the comparison between the finite orbit space and the topological orbit of the order complex.

Their later paper on coverings of posets proves that topological coverings of finite Alexandrov spaces agree with categorical poset coverings and that coverings correspond under the order-complex construction. The local noninjectivity above places the present example exactly outside that covering regime.

Cianci and Ottina give the homological order-complex framework for finite \(T_0\)-spaces used to interpret finite-space homology through simplicial chains. The checked literature did not locate the parameterized conclusion that this free point action has a contractible finite orbit space while its geometric order-complex orbit is \(\bigvee^r S^1\).

## Limitations
The statement is restricted to the displayed Barmak--Minian family and to generating sets of distinct nonidentity elements. It separates two quotient constructions; it does not classify when order-complex realization commutes with arbitrary group quotients. Broader equivariant-poset literature contains general quotient constructions, so an unlocated general theorem could subsume this family even though no such implication was found in the inspected sources.
