# Minimum-generator independence graphs of elementary-abelian generalized dihedral groups

## Finding

Let \(p\) be an odd prime, let \(t\ge2\), and let
\[
V=\mathbf F_p^t.
\]
Let
\[
G=\operatorname{Dih}(V)=V\rtimes\langle s\rangle,
\qquad
s^2=1,
\qquad
svs=-v
\]
for every \(v\in V\).

Then
\[
d(G)=t+1.
\]

For Lucchini's fixed-size independence graph
\[
\Gamma_{d(G)}(G)=\Gamma_{t+1}(G),
\]
the adjacency relation is completely explicit:

- the identity is isolated;
- two distinct translations \(u,v\in V\setminus\{0\}\) are adjacent exactly when \(u\) and \(v\) are linearly independent over \(\mathbf F_p\);
- every nonzero translation is adjacent to every reflection \(vs\);
- every two distinct reflections are adjacent.

Therefore, after deleting the isolated identity,
\[
\boxed{
\Delta_{t+1}(G)\cong
K_{p^t}\vee
K_{\underbrace{p-1,\ldots,p-1}_{(p^t-1)/(p-1)\text{ parts}}}.
}
\]

The clique \(K_{p^t}\) is the set of reflections. The complete multipartite factor is \(V\setminus\{0\}\), partitioned by its one-dimensional subspaces.

The degrees in \(\Gamma_{t+1}(G)\) are
\[
\boxed{
\deg(0)=0,\qquad
\deg(u)=2p^t-p\quad(0\ne u\in V),\qquad
\deg(vs)=2p^t-2.
}
\]

Since every nonzero translation has order \(p\) and every reflection has order \(2\),
\[
p\mid 2p^t-p
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
2\mid 2p^t-2.
\]
Thus
\[
\boxed{
|g|\mid\deg_{\Gamma_{d(G)}(G)}(g)
\quad\text{for every }g\in G.
}
\]

This gives an affirmative answer to Lucchini's Question 21 for an infinite nonnilpotent family with unbounded \(d(G)\).

## Assumptions and scope

The prime \(p\) is odd and \(t\ge2\). The group operation is written additively on \(V\), and a reflection is an element \(vs\) with \(v\in V\).

For a finite group \(X\), \(\Gamma_u(X)\) has vertex set \(X\), with two distinct elements adjacent when they occur together in a minimal generating set of cardinality \(u\). We use \(u=d(G)\), the minimum size of a generating set. Any generating set of size \(d(G)\) is automatically minimal.

The groups considered here are nonnilpotent: their Sylow \(2\)-subgroups are generated by reflections and are not normal when \(p\) is odd.

## Proof

Let \(S\subseteq G\). Split it into translations
\[
A=S\cap V
\]
and reflections
\[
\{bs:b\in B\}.
\]
If \(B=\varnothing\), then \(\langle S\rangle\le V\), so \(S\) does not generate \(G\).

Assume \(B\ne\varnothing\), and fix \(b_0\in B\). Since
\[
(bs)(b_0s)=b-b_0,
\]
the subgroup generated by \(S\) contains all differences
\[
b-b_0\qquad(b\in B).
\]
Conversely, every reflection \(bs\) equals
\[
(b-b_0)(b_0s),
\]
and conjugation by \(b_0s\) sends each translation to its negative. Hence
\[
\langle S\rangle
=
U\rtimes\langle b_0s\rangle,
\]
where
\[
U=
\operatorname{span}_{\mathbf F_p}
\bigl(
A\cup\{b-b_0:b\in B\}
\bigr).
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\langle S\rangle=G
\iff
B\ne\varnothing
\text{ and }
\operatorname{span}_{\mathbf F_p}
\bigl(
A\cup\{b-b_0:b\in B\}
\bigr)=V.
}
\]

A set of \(n\) elements containing a reflection contributes at most \(n-1\) vectors to the displayed spanning set. Thus a generating set has at least \(t+1\) elements. Equality is attained by one reflection together with a basis of \(V\), so
\[
d(G)=t+1.
\]

We now determine which pairs extend to a generating set of that minimum size.

First take two translations \(u,v\). Any completion to \(t+1\) elements must contain a reflection. After reserving one of the \(t-1\) new elements for a first reflection, at most \(t-2\) further spanning vectors can be contributed. Hence the pair can be completed exactly when
\[
\dim\langle u,v\rangle=2.
\]
If \(u,v\) are independent, extend them to a basis of \(V\), and add one reflection. If they are dependent, the maximum possible spanning rank is at most \(t-1\). Thus two translations are adjacent exactly when they are linearly independent.

Next take a translation \(u\) and a reflection \(bs\). The reflection is already present, so the remaining \(t-1\) positions can contribute \(t-1\) further vectors. The pair extends to a minimum generating set exactly when \(u\ne0\): for \(u\ne0\), extend \(u\) to a basis of \(V\) using \(t-1\) translations. If \(u=0\), the maximum possible spanning rank is only \(t-1\).

Finally take two distinct reflections \(bs\) and \(cs\). Their product gives the nonzero translation
\[
b-c.
\]
Extend \(b-c\) to a basis of \(V\) with \(t-1\) translations. This produces a generating set of size \(t+1\). Thus every two distinct reflections are adjacent.

The graph decomposition follows. There are \(p^t\) reflections, forming a clique. Among nonzero translations, nonadjacency is precisely membership in the same one-dimensional subspace, so the translation subgraph is complete multipartite with
\[
\frac{p^t-1}{p-1}
\]
parts, each of size \(p-1\). Every reflection is adjacent to every nonzero translation.

For \(0\ne u\in V\), the translations independent of \(u\) number
\[
p^t-p,
\]
and there are \(p^t\) reflections. Hence
\[
\deg(u)=p^t+(p^t-p)=2p^t-p.
\]
For a reflection there are \(p^t-1\) other reflections and \(p^t-1\) nonzero translations, giving
\[
\deg(vs)=2p^t-2.
\]
The identity is isolated by the pair-extension argument above.

The order-divisibility statement is now immediate.

## Verification

The included replay implements the semidirect-product law directly:
\[
(v,\varepsilon)(w,\delta)
=
\bigl(v+(-1)^\varepsilon w,\varepsilon+\delta\bigr).
\]

For
\[
(p,t)=(3,2)
\quad\text{and}\quad
(p,t)=(5,2),
\]
it enumerates every \((t+1)\)-subset of \(G\), computes the subgroup generated by that subset by closure, records every pair occurring in a minimum generating set, and compares the resulting graph with the theorem.

It obtains:

  \[

  \]

  \]

For

\]
the replay checks every predicted edge by constructing the basis-completion witness from the proof and verifies by direct subgroup closure that the resulting \(4\)-set generates the \(54\)-element group. It also checks the exact predicted degree distribution
\[

\]

The replay returns `VERIFY_OK`.

Finite enumeration is not used as a proof of the universal statement.

## Relationship to prior work

Lucchini introduced \(\Gamma_u(G)\) and asked in Question 21 whether, for every finite group \(G\) and every \(g\in G\),
\[
|g|\mid\deg_{\Gamma_{d(G)}(G)}(g).
\]
The same paper records that the divisibility is already known for \(2\)-generated groups, while a related divisibility statement fails for arbitrary fixed \(u\). Thus the open regime begins genuinely beyond minimum generator number \(2\).

The present family has
\[
d(G)=t+1\ge3
\]
and unbounded minimum generator number. It is also nonnilpotent, so it is not covered by nilpotent formulas for the same question.

García-Marco and Knauer later studied generalized dihedral groups through minimal Cayley graphs. Their Theorem 2.6 proves that every minimal Cayley graph of a generalized dihedral group is \(3\)-colorable. That graph has vertex set \(G\) and edges determined by one fixed minimal generating set used as a Cayley connection set. It is different from \(\Gamma_{d(G)}(G)\), where two group elements are adjacent when they co-occur in some minimum generating set. Their result therefore neither states nor implies the graph decomposition or degree formulas above.

Targeted searches using the independence-graph, minimum-generator, generalized-dihedral, elementary-abelian, and semidirect-product formulations did not locate the displayed decomposition or degree formulas.

## Limitations

The base group is elementary abelian of odd prime exponent. The argument uses vector-space rank and does not by itself classify \(\Gamma_{d(G)}(G)\) for \(\operatorname{Dih}(A)\) with a general finite abelian group \(A\).

The theorem settles Question 21 only for this family, not for all finite soluble groups or all generalized dihedral groups.

The exact graph statement concerns \(\Gamma_{d(G)}(G)\), not the full independence graph obtained by allowing minimal generating sets of arbitrary cardinality.

An equivalent result may exist under generating-hypergraph or irredundant-generation terminology not captured by the searches used here.
