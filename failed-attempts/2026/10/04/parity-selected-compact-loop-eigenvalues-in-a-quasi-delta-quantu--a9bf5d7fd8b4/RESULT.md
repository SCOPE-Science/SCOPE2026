# Parity-selected compact loop eigenvalues in a quasi-delta quantum circuit

## Finding
Consider the unit-interval loop-chain used in Eqs. (4.24)-(4.27) of Balmaseda, Di Cosmo and Pérez-Pardo. At each vertex \(i\), one chain edge enters, one chain edge leaves, and a loop \(I_i^v\) begins and ends at the same vertex. For any \(\mathbb{Z}\)-invariant quasi-\(\delta\) extension in the stated regime, define the vertex-independent loop relative phase by \(\alpha=\alpha_2^i-\alpha_1^i\).

A nonzero eigenfunction supported on a single loop \(I_j^v\), and identically zero on every chain edge and all other loops, exists at positive energy \(E=k^2\) if and only if
\[
k=n\pi,\qquad n\in\mathbb{N},\qquad e^{i\alpha}=(-1)^n.
\]
Thus \(\alpha\equiv0\pmod{2\pi}\) selects precisely the even values of \(n\), while \(\alpha\equiv\pi\pmod{2\pi}\) selects precisely the odd values. If \(\alpha\not\equiv0,\pi\pmod{2\pi}\), no eigenfunction with support on exactly one loop exists. For every allowed \(n\), placing the same mode on any one loop gives mutually orthogonal eigenfunctions at the same energy, so the eigenvalue has countably infinite multiplicity.

## Assumptions and scope
The graph and boundary conditions are those of the source's explicit unit-interval realization: \(I_i^u\) joins vertices \(i-1\) and \(i\), and \(I_i^v\) is a loop at \(i\). The quasi-\(\delta\) parameter is in the regime in which Eqs. (4.24)-(4.27) are written, equivalently \(e^{i\delta}\neq-1\). Translation symmetry requires \(\delta\) and \(\alpha_2^i-\alpha_1^i\) to be independent of \(i\), but the individual phases may vary with the vertex.

The claim classifies only eigenfunctions supported on exactly one loop and zero on the chain. It does not classify all compactly supported eigenfunctions or the entire point spectrum.

## Proof
Fix a vertex \(j\). Set all chain coefficients and all loop coefficients away from \(I_j^v\) equal to zero. On the remaining loop write
\[
\Phi_j^v(x)=A e^{ikx}+B e^{-ikx},\qquad 0\le x\le1.
\]
Because every chain value at vertex \(j\) is zero, Eq. (4.24) gives \(A+B=0\). Equation (4.25) then gives
\[
A e^{ik}+B e^{-ik}=0.
\]
With \(B=-A\) and \(A\neq0\), this is \(\sin k=0\). Since the zero-energy Dirichlet problem on an interval has only the zero solution, a positive-energy one-loop state therefore has \(k=n\pi\) with \(n\ge1\).

For such \(k\), Eq. (4.27) has zero left-hand side because the common vertex value is zero. All chain terms on its right-hand side also vanish. What remains is
\[
-e^{i\alpha_1^j}(A-B)+e^{i\alpha_2^j}\left(Ae^{ik}-Be^{-ik}\right)=0.
\]
Using \(B=-A\) and \(e^{ik}=e^{-ik}=(-1)^n\) reduces this exactly to
\[
e^{i(\alpha_2^j-\alpha_1^j)}=(-1)^n.
\]
This proves necessity. Conversely, if this phase equation holds, the function proportional to \(\sin(n\pi x)\) on \(I_j^v\) and zero elsewhere satisfies Eqs. (4.24)-(4.27), hence lies in the self-adjoint domain and is an eigenfunction with energy \(n^2\pi^2\). The argument does not use \(\delta\), because its term is multiplied by the zero common boundary value, and it does not use \(\alpha_3^j\), because the chain amplitudes vanish.

The compactly supported function lies in \(L^2\), so the source's additional finiteness condition for generalized eigenfunctions is automatic. The relative phase is the same at every vertex, so for each allowed \(n\) the construction works on every loop. Different one-loop states have disjoint supports and are therefore orthogonal, giving countably infinite multiplicity.

## Verification
The accompanying `verify.py` replays the boundary reduction using exact integer arithmetic after substituting \(e^{in\pi}=(-1)^n\). For \(1\le n\le20\), it verifies that the loop endpoint equations vanish and that the remaining derivative equation vanishes exactly for the phase root \(e^{i\alpha}=(-1)^n\), and fails for the opposite root. The all-\(n\) proof above is algebraic and does not depend on this finite replay.

## Relationship to prior work
Balmaseda, Di Cosmo and Pérez-Pardo characterize the translation-symmetric quasi-\(\delta\) family and write the exact boundary system (4.24)-(4.27). Their later elimination formulas (4.30)-(4.31) contain factors that vanish at Dirichlet momenta, while the text concentrates on generalized-eigenfunction candidates and does not state the one-loop parity classification above.

Compactly supported eigenfunctions at Dirichlet energies are a standard phenomenon on periodic quantum graphs. Kuchment proves that point spectrum of periodic quantum graphs is generated by compactly supported eigenfunctions, but that structural theorem does not determine which quasi-\(\delta\) phase admits a one-loop state. Likewise, the magnetic ring-chain analysis of Exner and collaborators has infinitely degenerate compact states at special momenta, but its cells are rings connecting adjacent vertices with standard \(\delta\) coupling and magnetic flux; it does not imply the pendant-loop quasi-\(\delta\) condition \(e^{i\alpha}=(-1)^n\).

## Limitations
The result is an exact classification of one-loop-supported states, not of all compactly supported states. Multi-loop compact eigenfunctions may exist for other phase choices. The statement is for the unit-interval spectral equations used explicitly in the source; rescaling to other loop lengths requires tracking the metric normalization of the Laplace-Beltrami coordinate. No claim is made about perturbative stability of these infinitely degenerate levels.
