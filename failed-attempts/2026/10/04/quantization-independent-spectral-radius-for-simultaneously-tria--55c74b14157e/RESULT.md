# Quantization-independent spectral radius for simultaneously triangularizable matrix tuples
## Finding
Let \(V=(\mathbb C^d,\|\cdot\|_V)\) be a finite-dimensional normed space, let \(\mathcal E\) be any operator-space quantization of \(V\), and let \(X=(X_1,\ldots,X_d)\in M_n(\mathbb C)^d\) be simultaneously triangularizable. In any common upper-triangular basis, write \(\lambda^{(k)}=((X_1)_{kk},\ldots,(X_d)_{kk})\in\mathbb C^d\) for \(1\le k\le n\). Then the Shalit--Shamovich spectral radius satisfies \[\rho_{\mathcal E}(X)=\max_{1\le k\le n}\|\lambda^{(k)}\|_V.\] Thus \(\rho_{\mathcal E}(X)\) is independent of the quantization on every simultaneously triangularizable matrix tuple, even when the matrices do not commute. In particular, the same formula holds whenever the complex Lie algebra generated by \(X_1,\ldots,X_d\) is solvable; consequently, if two quantizations of the same normed space give different spectral radii on a matrix tuple, the generated Lie algebra must be non-solvable.

The conclusion strictly extends the commuting matrix case: simultaneous triangularizability allows nonzero commutators. For example,
\[
X_1=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
X_2=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}
\]
satisfy \([X_1,X_2]=X_2\neq0\), while their generated Lie algebra is solvable and the formula gives \(\rho_{\mathcal E}(X)=\|(1,0)\|_V\) for every quantization of the same level-one norm.

## Assumptions and scope
The field is \(\mathbb C\), the matrices are finite-dimensional, and \(\mathcal E\) is an operator-space structure on \(\mathbb C^d\) whose level-one norm is \(\|\cdot\|_V\). Only the Shalit--Shamovich spectral radius \(\rho_{\mathcal E}\) is considered; no claim is made here for the minimal spectral radius \(\rho_{\mathcal E}^{\min}\). The diagonal tuples are taken from any one simultaneous triangularization.

## Proof
For a matrix tuple \(Y\in M_n(\mathcal E)\), the similarity characterization of the Shalit--Shamovich radius gives
\[
\rho_{\mathcal E}(Y)=\inf_{S\in GL_n(\mathbb C)}\|S^{-1}YS\|_{M_n(\mathcal E)}.
\]
Indeed, \(\rho_{\mathcal E}\) is homogeneous and similarity invariant, and the cited similarity theorem says that \(\rho_{\mathcal E}(Y)<1\) exactly when some scalar similarity sends \(Y\) into the open unit ball. The reverse inequality also follows from the ordinary spectral-radius bound inside the defining maximal C*-algebra.

After one fixed similarity, assume all \(X_j\) are upper triangular. For \(0<t<1\), set
\[
D_t=\operatorname{diag}(1,t,t^2,\ldots,t^{n-1}).
\]
For every strict upper-triangular entry, conjugation by \(D_t\) contributes the factor \(t^{j-i}\). Hence
\[
D_t^{-1}XD_t\longrightarrow
\operatorname{diag}(\lambda^{(1)},\ldots,\lambda^{(n)})
\]
in \(M_n(\mathcal E)\) as \(t\downarrow0\). Ruan's direct-sum axiom therefore yields
\[
\rho_{\mathcal E}(X)
\le \max_k\|\lambda^{(k)}\|_V.
\]

For the converse, fix \(k\) and choose a norming functional \(\varphi_k\in V^*\) with \(\|\varphi_k\|=1\) and
\[
|\varphi_k(\lambda^{(k)})|=\|\lambda^{(k)}\|_V.
\]
Every bounded scalar-valued functional on an operator space has completely bounded norm equal to its Banach norm. Thus, for every \(S\in GL_n(\mathbb C)\),
\[
\|S^{-1}XS\|_{M_n(\mathcal E)}
\ge
\|\varphi_k^{(n)}(S^{-1}XS)\|.
\]
But
\[
\varphi_k^{(n)}(S^{-1}XS)
=S^{-1}\varphi_k^{(n)}(X)S,
\]
and \(\varphi_k^{(n)}(X)\) is an upper-triangular scalar matrix having \(\varphi_k(\lambda^{(k)})\) on its diagonal. Therefore its spectral radius, and hence the norm of every similar copy, is at least \(\|\lambda^{(k)}\|_V\). Taking the maximum over \(k\) and then the infimum over \(S\) proves the reverse inequality.

Finally, Lie's theorem implies that every finite-dimensional complex representation of a solvable Lie algebra is simultaneously upper triangularizable. Conversely, a Lie algebra of upper-triangular matrices is solvable. This gives the stated solvable-Lie corollary and the necessary non-solvability condition for quantization dependence.

## Verification
The proof uses only four ingredients: the published similarity characterization of \(\rho_{\mathcal E}\); homogeneity; the direct-sum matrix-norm axiom; and the standard equality \(\|\varphi\|_{cb}=\|\varphi\|\) for scalar-valued operator-space functionals. The diagonal-scaling limit was checked entrywise: an entry in position \((i,j)\), \(i<j\), is multiplied by \(t^{j-i}\), so every strict upper-triangular entry vanishes and no diagonal entry changes.

The lower bound is not inferred from a finite experiment. It holds simultaneously for every scalar similarity because each norming functional produces a scalar matrix whose eigenvalue \(\varphi_k(\lambda^{(k)})\) is similarity invariant.

## Relationship to prior work
Scherer--Shalit--Shamovich prove quantization independence for commuting tuples and show that quantizations can differ on general matrix tuples. Their full text states the commuting theorem and the general separation phenomenon, but searches within the inspected text found no triangularizable, solvable-Lie, or commutator formulation. Shalit--Shamovich supply the similarity characterization used above.

Classical work of Beltiţă studies analytic, algebraic, and geometric joint spectral radii for families lying in solvable operator Lie algebras. Those radii are defined through different constructions and, in the finite-family case, use classical \(\ell_p\)-geometry; they do not identify the operator-space spectral radius \(\rho_{\mathcal E}\) for arbitrary quantizations of an arbitrary norm \(\|\cdot\|_V\). Classical triangular-matrix formulas for the Rota--Strang joint spectral radius are likewise a special spectral notion, not a statement about all operator-space quantizations.

## Limitations
The result is finite-dimensional and complex. It gives a necessary condition for quantization dependence, not a converse: a non-solvable generated Lie algebra need not distinguish two specified quantizations. It does not address infinite-dimensional triangularizable families or the minimal spectral radius. Because the proof is short and algebraically natural, an equivalent older corollary under different terminology remains the principal originality risk.
