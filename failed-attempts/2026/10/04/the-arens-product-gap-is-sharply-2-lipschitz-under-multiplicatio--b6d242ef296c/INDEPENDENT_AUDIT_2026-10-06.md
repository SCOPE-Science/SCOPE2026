# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

Each Arens extension depends linearly and isometrically on the underlying bounded bilinear map, so the discrepancy operator \(D(h)=h^{\square}-h^{\diamond}\) is linear with norm at most \(2\). The reverse triangle inequality gives the Lipschitz estimate, and comparison with any regular product gives the distance bound. In the explicit \(\ell_1\oplus_1\ell_1\oplus_1\mathbb C\) algebra, every triple product vanishes and the triangular sign matrix has opposite iterated ultrafilter limits \(1\) and \(-1\), so \(\Delta_A=2\) at multiplication norm one. The zero multiplication is regular at distance one, proving both sharpness statements.

Best-of-knowledge searches found no source stating the intrinsic discrepancy norm with the sharp global modulus and cube-zero equality example. ## value

FAIL

The proposed defect is natural, but the substantive theorem beyond the explicit example is a direct norm-triangle calculation: the \(2\)-Lipschitz law is immediate from two norm-one extensions, and the distance-to-zero-set inequality is the standard consequence for any Lipschitz defect. The triangular sign matrix is a classical double-limit mechanism for Arens irregularity. Under the common value bar, packaging these routine facts into a named gap with a sharpness witness does not add enough mathematical structure to qualify as a worthwhile new result.
