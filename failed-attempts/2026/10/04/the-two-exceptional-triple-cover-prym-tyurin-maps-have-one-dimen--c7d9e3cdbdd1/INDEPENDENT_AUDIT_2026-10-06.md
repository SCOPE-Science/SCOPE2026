# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

The primary source gives the codifferential as the sum of complementary-character multiplication maps and, in Lemma 5.1(c), gives \((\deg M_1,\deg N_1)=(1,1)\) for \(m=2\) and \(\{1,2\}\) for \(m=3\). For \(p=3\) there is only one complementary pair. On an elliptic curve a positive-degree line bundle has \(h^0=\deg\), and multiplication by a nonzero section of a degree-one line bundle is injective, so the multiplication-map ranks are exactly \(1\) and \(2\). The source tangent space is dual to \(H^0(E,\mathcal O_E(D))\) and has dimension \(m\), hence the generic fiber dimension is one in both cases. For Hodge multiplicities \((a,b)=(1,1)\) or \((1,2)\), the invariant part of the symmetric-square tangent space is the cross term of dimension \(ab\), again \(1\) or \(2\).

## originality

FAIL

The numerical ranks and one-dimensional fibers are mechanically implied by the initiating paper's own stronger structural data. It already gives the exact codifferential multiplication map for the same family and the exact degree patterns in the two omitted \(p=3\) cases. With only the textbook facts \(h^0(L)=\deg L\) on an elliptic curve and injectivity of multiplication by a nonzero section, the ranks follow immediately. The invariant-tangent interpretation is likewise the standard fixed-part calculation for an order-three Hodge decomposition. Under the required implication standard, the fact that the paper does not print these two ranks as a theorem does not make the claim original.

## value

PASS

Resolving the two parameter values deliberately excluded from the source's generic-finiteness theorem is mathematically natural, and the one-dimensional fiber description clarifies the boundary behavior. The record is rejected only because the result is too direct a corollary of the source's own structural formulas.
