# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

Burnside's basis theorem gives \(G/\Phi(G)\cong\mathbb F_p^2\); nonidentity Frattini elements are isolated and the remaining elements split into \(p+1\) projective-line classes of size \(s=(p-1)|\Phi(G)|\), with edges exactly across classes. Isolates must be in every forcing set. For \(s\ge2\), an equal-part complete multipartite graph has minimum zero forcing sets exactly the complements of cross-part pairs, yielding the three polynomial coefficients; these pairs are exactly unordered generating pairs. The degree and coefficient of \(x^{N-1}\) recover \(|G|\) and the nonisolated count, hence \(|\Phi(G)|\) and \(p\). The rank values agree with the classical complete-multipartite minimum-rank formula: one for \(K_3\), two when the third-largest part has size below three, and three once at least three parts have size at least three. Fresh exhaustive graph reconstructions reproduced the minimum-set counts \(3,12,24\) for \((p,f)=(2,1),(2,2),(3,1)\).

## originality

FAIL

The final claim is assembled from stronger prior ingredients. Burnside's basis theorem gives the projective-line complete-multipartite decomposition. Boyer et al.'s general extremal-coefficient theorem already determines the \(n,n-1,n-2\) coefficients once the graph is known, and the published all-\(k\) complete-multipartite result gives the minimum zero forcing sets directly. Most decisively, Fallat--Hogben's 2007 survey gives an explicit exact real minimum-rank formula for every complete multipartite graph: \(1\) for the complete rank-one case, \(2\) when the third-largest part is below three, and \(3\) when the third-largest part is at least three. Specializing to \(p+1\ge3\) equal parts of size \(s\) yields exactly the submitted \(1/2/3\) phase transition. The remaining recovery of \(p\) and \(|\Phi(G)|\) from graph order and the \(x^{N-1}\) coefficient is elementary algebra.

## value

FAIL

The group-theoretic interpretation is neat, but after the classical Frattini quotient identifies an equal-part complete multipartite graph, the zero-forcing polynomial, minimum sets, and real minimum rank follow from already-published general graph results. Recovering \(p\) and \(|\Phi(G)|\) from degree and one coefficient is straightforward algebra. Under the common value bar, this is a useful synthesis rather than an independently substantive new theorem.
