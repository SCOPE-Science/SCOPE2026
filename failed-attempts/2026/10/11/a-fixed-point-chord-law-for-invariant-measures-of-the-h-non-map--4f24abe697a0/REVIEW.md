# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

Let a>0, b∈R and μ be compact invariant. By the y-coordinate map and invariance, y#μ=(bx)#μ, so E[y]=bm and Var(y)=b²Var(x). By x-coordinate invariance, aE[x²]=1-(1-b)m. The roots x± of ar²+(1-b)r-1 have Δ=(1-b)²+4a>0; Vieta gives Var(x)=(x_+-m)(m-x_-). Nonnegativity forces m between roots. Thus the barycenter lies on y=bx between the fixed points, and the total variance (1+b²)Var(x) equals the Euclidean product of its distances to p±. Endpoint zero variance forces both x,y constant and μ a corresponding fixed-point atom; equal fixed-atom mixture attains the sharp L²/4 bound. Works also at b=0, b<0, |b|≥1 without needing invertibility. Independent exact fractions at a=7/5,b=3/10 gave Δ=609/100, max Var(x)=609/784 and max total variance=66381/78400. No attractor existence, covariance off-diagonal, or dynamical chaos is established; checker verifies only identities, not the invariant-measure premise.

## originality

FAIL

A complete earlier public mathematical record (2026-10-04, Resultary SCOPE-ace815aa3687) already proves precisely the invariant first-coordinate variance parabola, exact scaled y-marginal/variance, barycenter line and endpoint rigidity for the canonical dissipative Hénon regime 0<b<1. These are exactly the only inputs required for the submitted fixed-point Euclidean chord law, its sharp variance bound and fixed-point-mixture equality. The extension to arbitrary real b is obtained by applying the same two coordinate-invariance equations without the previous paper’s strip-crossing-specific positivity assumption. This is direct mathematical prior implication, not merely a title or parameter match. The published earlier work additionally proves a nontrivial strip-crossing consequence absent from the submitted claim.

## value

FAIL

The object (stationary Hénon invariant measures) and their second moments are genuinely natural, and the chord visualization can be a useful pedagogical or numerical diagnostic. However the entire claimed new exact geometric invariant is a routine Euclidean rewriting of the prior Hénon variance parabola and prior y-marginal scaling: total variance is merely (1+b²) times the already known scalar formula. The sharp length bound and equal-mixture extremizer are elementary one-variable quadratic maximization, the all-real-b extension uses the identical two stationary coordinate equations, and the classical rational constants are straightforward substitution. No additional nonstandard structure, motivated unknown invariant or valuable separate gap is supplied beyond that published result. Its clarity as exposition does not suffice as a new mathematical-value claim under the shared standard.
