# Mathematical assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The claimed minimum enclosing balls and contact formulas for the standard Euclidean fractional stable-set polytopes of cycles are correct. For even cycles the fractional polytope is the integral stable-set polytope. Complementary alternating maximum stable sets force the center at \(\mathbf1/2\) with squared radius \(n/4\), and every 0–1 vertex lies on that sphere. For odd cycles, the maximum stable sets average to \(\alpha\mathbf1/n\) with \(\alpha=(n-1)/2\), give squared radius \(\alpha-\alpha^2/n\), and every smaller stable-set incidence vector is strictly closer. The additional half-integral vertex \(h=\mathbf1/2\) lies at squared distance \(1/(4n)\), also strictly inside.

The analytic contact-barycenter identity proves uniqueness for every \(n\ge3\). Direct finite calculations corroborated vertex lists and exact distances, but are not relied on for the general proof.

## Originality — FAIL
A separate published result, *A sharp apex-screening transition for the circumradius of graph-cone stable-set polytopes* (record SCOPE-20261011-a4388ee8bd83), proves within its general vertex-transitive-graph argument that the stable-set polytope of an \(N\)-vertex transitive graph with independence number \(a\le N/2\) has center \((a/N)\mathbf1\) and squared radius \(a-a^2/N\). Its proof gives the squared-distance formula \(a^2/N+k(1-2a/N)\) for any stable set of size \(k\).

Specializing to the cycle \(C_n\), with \(a=\lfloor n/2\rfloor\), already yields all integral-vertex sphere and radial-shell claims. Hamano, Hibi and Ohsugi, *Ehrhart Series of Fractional Stable Set Polytopes of Finite Graphs*, Lemma 2.2, gives exactly one additional fractional vertex \(h=\mathbf1/2\) for odd cycles and none for even cycles. At odd order \(h\) is strictly inside the previously established sphere, so adding it changes nothing. Hence the complete submitted theorem is an immediate mathematical corollary of distinct published work. Different titles or same-day publication dates do not erase the implication; no historical discovery-order conclusion is claimed.

## Mathematical value — FAIL
The object and invariant are natural and the numerical statements are correct. However, after recognizing the general published sphere lemma and earlier cycle-vertex theorem, the remaining calculation is the single interior-point inequality; no independently unknown exact invariant or substantive additional geometry remains. The submitted package is best treated as a covered special case, with possible expository utility but not a separate new mathematical contribution.
