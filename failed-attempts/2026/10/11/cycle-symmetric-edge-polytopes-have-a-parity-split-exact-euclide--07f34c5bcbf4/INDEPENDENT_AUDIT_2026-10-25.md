## correctness

PASS

For n>=3, the standard symmetric edge polytope P_n in H0 has polar the mean-zero graph Lipschitz polytope, and central symmetry guarantees the origin attains the global inradius 1/M_n where M_n=max||g||. The facet-label theorem gives n-balanced +/-1 increments when n is even and one zero step with balanced +/-1 increments when n is odd. The submitted proof’s range-conditioned variance bounds are valid by mandatory two visits to each intermediate level, a convex transfer of surplus visits to endpoints, and elementary balancing of the endpoint counts. Exact symbolic differences V_even(h+1)-V_even(h)=(2h(k-h-1)+k)/2 and V_odd(h+1)-V_odd(h)=(k-h)(2hk+h+k+1)/(2k+1) are positive for h<k, forcing the sharp sawtooth profiles. Their centered squared norms are n(n^2+8)/48 for n even and (n^2-1)(n^2+3)/(48n) for n odd. Opposite contact normals have zero pairing with every maximum-ball center; in even n=2k the cyclic contact span is exactly the anti-periodic k-dimensional subspace and the center affine hull is the k-periodic zero-sum space (dimension k-1), while odd contact rotations span all H0 by the nonvanishing Fourier symbol and the center is unique. Noncontact facets have strictly positive clearance at the origin, establishing a relative neighborhood of admissible even centers. All cases include n=3 and n=4 boundaries. This is a correct proof, even though the conclusion is prior-covered.

## originality

FAIL

Decisive prior mathematical implication: the polar of the standard cycle symmetric-edge polytope is exactly the zero-mean space of 1-Lipschitz functions on C_n. Yancey (arXiv:1705.09725, pp. 20–22, Theorem 2.9) identifies odd-cycle optimal Lipschitz profiles, and on p.24 Remark 2.12 explicitly extends the strict-convexity/permutation argument to VARIANCE maximizers; p.20 recounts the established unique even-cycle extremal mountain shape from Bobkov–Houdre–Tetali. Hence the complete centered Euclidean-norm maximizing polar facets are already determined for both parities. Their norms, orbit sizes, contact facets, and the exact radius formulas are immediate finite moments of these prior profiles. The remaining center-locus parity is a short standard cyclic Fourier rank calculation on that predetermined contact orbit (full mean-zero span when n odd; anti-periodic span when n even), combined with the usual facet-clearance equalities and strict slack. The submitted theorem is therefore covered by a direct and explicitly documented prior-theorem-plus-elementary-duality/linear-algebra implication, despite not sharing the older papers’ vocabulary. This is not an appeal to failed searches or unread sources.

## value

FAIL

The cycle symmetric-edge polytope and its Euclidean incenter are natural objects, and the submitted classification is accurate and expository. However, its purported new optimization is exactly the previously solved graph-spread maximization for C_n in the canonical metric, as established by Yancey’s 2017 primary preprint and the earlier even-cycle subgaussian work it cites. The complete maximizing polar normals already follow from those sources. The parity incenter dimension is then standard Fourier rank on the already identified orbit plus the elementary support-clearance rule for convex balls. That short deterministic corollary does not introduce an independently motivated unknown parameter, new structural obstruction, nonroutine new lemma, or previously undetermined family of extremizers beyond the prior classification. The proof is a valid alternative presentation but not a distinct valuable new research claim under the required substantive-value bar.
