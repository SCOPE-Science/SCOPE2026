# Cyclic tournaments retain one integral first path-homology class
## Finding
For every integer \(m\ge 1\), let \(R_{2m+1}\) be the cyclic tournament on \(\mathbb Z/(2m+1)\mathbb Z\) with an arc \(i\to i+a\) exactly when \(1\le a\le m\), where residues are read modulo \(2m+1\). In standard regular path homology with integral coefficients,
\[
H_1(R_{2m+1};\mathbb Z)\cong \mathbb Z.
\]
A generator is the directed Hamiltonian cycle
\[
h=\sum_{i\in\mathbb Z/(2m+1)\mathbb Z}(i,i+1).
\]
More precisely, every arc \((i,i+a)\), with \(1\le a\le m\), represents the same first-homology class as the consecutive unit-arc chain
\[
(i,i+1)+(i+1,i+2)+\cdots +(i+a-1,i+a).
\]

## Assumptions and scope
Regular path homology is used in the standard sense: \(A_p\) is generated by allowed directed \(p\)-paths, \(\Omega_p\subseteq A_p\) consists of chains whose regular boundary is again allowed, and the homology is that of \((\Omega_*,\partial)\). The result concerns only \(H_1\) over \(\mathbb Z\). It makes no assertion about higher path-homology groups, path cohomology, directed Hom complexes, or neighborhood complexes.

## Proof
Write \(e_{i,a}=(i,i+a)\) for \(1\le a\le m\). If \(2\le a\le m\), then
\[
q_{i,a}=(i,i+1,i+a)
\]
is an allowed \(2\)-path and all three terms of its boundary are allowed because the increments are \(1\), \(a-1\), and \(a\). Hence \(q_{i,a}\in\Omega_2\), and
\[
\partial q_{i,a}=e_{i+1,a-1}-e_{i,a}+e_{i,1}.
\]
Therefore
\[
[e_{i,a}]=[e_{i,1}]+[e_{i+1,a-1}],
\]
and induction on \(a\) gives
\[
[e_{i,a}]=\sum_{t=0}^{a-1}[e_{i+t,1}].
\]
Thus every integral \(1\)-cycle is homologous to a chain supported on the unit arcs. If \(c=\sum_i x_i e_{i,1}\), then
\[
\partial c=\sum_i(x_{i-1}-x_i)i.
\]
Consequently \(c\) is a cycle exactly when all \(x_i\) are equal. It follows that \(H_1\) is generated by \([h]\).

It remains to show that \([h]\) has infinite order. For distinct vertices \(u,v\), let \(\delta(u,v)\in\{1,\ldots,2m\}\) be the positive residue of \(v-u\) modulo \(2m+1\), and extend the integer weight by
\[
\widetilde W(u,v)=\delta(u,v)
\]
on every regular ordered pair. Every allowed elementary \(2\)-path has the form \((i,i+a,i+a+b)\) with \(1\le a,b\le m\). Since \(a+b\le 2m<2m+1\),
\[
\widetilde W\bigl(\partial(i,i+a,i+a+b)\bigr)=b-(a+b)+a=0.
\]
By linearity, \(\widetilde W\) annihilates the full regular boundary of every allowed \(2\)-chain. If that chain lies in \(\Omega_2\), its boundary is an allowed \(1\)-chain, so the restriction \(W(e_{i,a})=a\) annihilates every path-homology \(2\)-boundary. On the Hamiltonian cycle,
\[
W(h)=2m+1\ne0.
\]
Hence no nonzero multiple of \(h\) is a boundary. Since \([h]\) already generates \(H_1\), the group is infinite cyclic.

## Verification
The accompanying verifier reconstructs the tournament definition, checks every local \(2\)-path relation used in the reduction, checks the winding functional on every allowed elementary \(2\)-path, and confirms the Hamiltonian cycle condition for \(1\le m\le12\). As a separate finite cross-check, it constructs \(\Omega_2\) over \(\mathbb F_2\), \(\mathbb F_3\), and \(\mathbb F_5\) for \(1\le m\le5\) and obtains first Betti number \(1\) in every case. These finite computations are checks of the implementation, not substitutes for the all-parameter integral proof above.

## Relationship to prior work
Lin, Wang, and Yau develop discrete Morse theory for digraph path homology and list primary classification 55U15 in the first public version of their paper. Their treatment supplies the path-homology framework but does not state a computation for cyclic, carousel, or circulant tournaments. A separate literature comparison with work on first path-homology Betti numbers of random directed graphs concerns probabilistic regimes rather than this deterministic tournament family. Database searches also found a result about the directed Hom complex of \(R_9\) and a first path-homology computation for an alternating-pole digraph family; neither invariant/family combination implies the statement here.

## Limitations
Only first integral path homology is determined. The proof does not identify higher path homology, a chain homotopy model, or persistence under edge modifications. The literature comparison found no covering statement under the searched cyclic, carousel, circulant, and regular-tournament aliases, but an unindexed equivalent computation could still exist.
