# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

For any connected smooth face arc with unit tangent T, free-boundary contact means the surface conormal is normal to the face. The surface unit normal N is consequently tangent to the face and perpendicular to T, hence N=±n_pl with constant sign on the arc. Thus |dN/ds|=|kappa_pl|. Finch (arXiv:2610.00434v1, §2.2-2.4, §3 and Appendix A.1-A.5) identifies one physical face arc with one spherical great-circle side of a degree-one, conformally uniformized cuboctahedral Gauss square. Adjacent vertices p=(1,0,-1)/sqrt(2) and q=(0,1,-1)/sqrt(2) satisfy p·q=1/2, so one side length is arccos(1/2)=pi/3. Four congruent sides sum to 4pi/3. No multiplicity arises because the Schwarz triangle map is injective on its fundamental domain and reflections recover the square. Nonvanishing derivative on the open boundary side (Finch §2.6, Smyth Lemma 3) gives nonzero signed planar curvature throughout; its sign cannot switch along a connected smooth arc, so each arc is strictly convex. Vertices/corner turning excluded explicitly. Direct exact symbolic verification of both coordinate and stereographic vertices yields dot=1/2; this is just arithmetic corroboration, not an independent proof of Gauss boundary mapping.

## originality

FAIL

The exact numerical turn is mechanically implied by already published stronger geometric information. Finch (2026), Section 3 and Appendix A.2-A.4, identifies the Gauss map of this very surface as a one-to-one Schwarz image onto one spherical cuboctahedral square and gives all four boundary-side vertices explicitly. Section 7 states that along a face arc the surface normal lies in that face. Its adjacent sphere vertices p=(1,0,-1)/sqrt(2) and q=(0,1,-1)/sqrt(2) have p dot q=1/2, so their great-circle side has length acos(1/2)=pi/3. Because on the face arc N is (up to sign) the planar normal, planar absolute curvature integrates exactly to this side length; the Schwarz boundary correspondence already gives one traversal. Smyth (1984), Lemmas 3-4, independently establishes the boundary Gauss geodesic quadrilateral, excludes boundary-edge umbilics and proves convexity of the trace pieces. This is a direct corollary/reexpression of established exact data, not a separately unknown theorem. No word-for-word pi/3 quote is needed for prior implication.

## value

FAIL

Boundary total turning is a geometrically natural scale-free invariant, and an exact numerical datum can in principle be worthwhile. Here, however, the value is a single routine spherical inner-product calculation from Finch’s already complete one-to-one Gauss-square description plus the standard planar-normal identity. Smyth already establishes convexity of the trace pieces and excludes boundary umbilics. No independent structural lemma, genuine previously undetermined invariant or motivated deeper classification survives the prior implication. Correctness, independent symbolic arithmetic and a different expository interpretation do not by themselves establish research value.
