# Exact Euclidean radii and tangent facets of vertex-transitive zonotopes
## Finding
Let \(Z\subset\mathbb R^d\) be a full-dimensional vertex-transitive zonotope and let \(R=\operatorname{Gen}Z\) be its canonical centrally symmetric generator set. Winter's classification implies that \(R\) is a finite root system and that \(Z\) is a permutahedron for the finite reflection group generated by the reflections in \(R\).

Fix a Weyl chamber \(C\). Let
\[
R_+=\{\alpha\in R:\langle \alpha,c\rangle>0\}
\]
for any \(c\) in the interior of \(C\), and put
\[
s=\sum_{\alpha\in R_+}\alpha.
\]
Let \(\omega_1,\ldots,\omega_d\) be arbitrary nonzero vectors on the \(d\) extreme rays of \(C\). Then
\[
R_{\mathrm{out}}(Z)=\|s\|
\]
and
\[
r_{\mathrm{in}}(Z)=
\min_{1\le i\le d}
\frac{\langle s,\omega_i\rangle}{\|\omega_i\|}.
\]
These formulas are independent of the chosen nonzero scaling of each \(\omega_i\).

The origin is a circumcenter and an incenter. The facets tangent to the centered maximal inscribed ball are exactly the facets whose outward normal rays belong to Weyl-group orbits of the chamber rays \(\mathbb R_{\ge0}\omega_i\) attaining the displayed minimum.

Equivalently, if one writes the generalized Weyl half-sum as
\[
\rho=\frac12s,
\]
then
\[
R_{\mathrm{out}}(Z)=2\|\rho\|,
\qquad
r_{\mathrm{in}}(Z)=
2\min_i\frac{\langle\rho,\omega_i\rangle}{\|\omega_i\|}.
\]

## Assumptions and scope
The metric is the ambient Euclidean metric on \(\mathbb R^d\). The zonotope is assumed full-dimensional and vertex-transitive. No crystallographic hypothesis is required: Winter's root-system convention only requires invariance under the reflections determined by the generators.

For reducible reflection groups, \(C\) is still a simplicial fundamental chamber and the formula applies without modification. Root lengths need not all agree. The theorem therefore includes products and the two-root-length families permitted by the vertex-transitive classification.

No uniqueness assertion is made for the incenter. Reducible examples can have more than one center of a largest inscribed ball. The statement only asserts that the origin is always an incenter and characterizes which facets are tangent to the centered maximal ball.

## Proof
Winter uses the convention
\[
Z=\operatorname{Zon}R
=\sum_{r\in R}\operatorname{conv}\{0,r\},
\]
where \(R\) is centrally symmetric. Grouping the two generators in each pair \(\{\alpha,-\alpha\}\) gives
\[
Z=\sum_{\alpha\in R_+}[-\alpha,\alpha].
\]
Hence the support function is
\[
h_Z(u)=
\sum_{\alpha\in R_+}|\langle u,\alpha\rangle|.
\tag{1}
\]

For any centrally symmetric full-dimensional convex body \(K=-K\), its Euclidean inradius satisfies
\[
r_{\mathrm{in}}(K)=\min_{\|u\|=1}h_K(u),
\tag{2}
\]
and the origin is an incenter. Indeed, if \(c+rB_2^d\subset K\), then for every unit \(u\),
\[
r+\langle c,u\rangle\le h_K(u)
\]
and
\[
r-\langle c,u\rangle\le h_K(-u)=h_K(u).
\]
Thus \(r\le h_K(u)\) for every unit \(u\). Conversely, the centered ball of radius \(\min_{\|u\|=1}h_K(u)\) satisfies every support inequality of \(K\), proving (2).

The Weyl group preserves \(R\), so (1) is Weyl-invariant. It therefore suffices to minimize over unit vectors in the closed chamber \(C\). For \(u\in C\), the sign of \(\langle u,\alpha\rangle\) is nonnegative for every \(\alpha\in R_+\), and consequently
\[
h_Z(u)=\langle s,u\rangle.
\tag{3}
\]

Because a finite reflection chamber is simplicial, every \(u\in C\) has a representation
\[
u=\sum_{i=1}^d c_i\omega_i,
\qquad c_i\ge0.
\]
Set
\[
m=
\min_i\frac{\langle s,\omega_i\rangle}{\|\omega_i\|}.
\]
Then
\[
\langle s,u\rangle
=
\sum_i c_i\langle s,\omega_i\rangle
\ge
m\sum_i c_i\|\omega_i\|
\ge
m\left\|\sum_i c_i\omega_i\right\|
=
m\|u\|.
\tag{4}
\]
Equality is attained by a unit vector on any chamber ray realizing the minimum. Combining (2)--(4) proves
\[
r_{\mathrm{in}}(Z)=m.
\]

The equality case in (4) also identifies the tangent directions. Distinct extreme rays of a pointed simplicial chamber are not positively collinear, so the triangle inequality is strict whenever at least two coefficients \(c_i\) are positive. Thus the unit minimizers in \(C\) are exactly the minimizing extreme rays. Weyl invariance gives all minimizing directions. Since the normal fan of the root zonotope is the reflection fan, its one-dimensional cones are exactly the facet-normal rays, proving the tangent-facet statement.

For the circumradius, \(R_+\) is a semistar of \(R\). Winter's semistar-to-vertex correspondence therefore makes
\[
s=\sum_{\alpha\in R_+}\alpha
\]
a vertex of \(Z\). Vertex-transitivity implies that every vertex has norm \(\|s\|\), hence
\[
Z\subseteq \|s\|B_2^d.
\]
Central symmetry makes \(-s\) a vertex as well. Every Euclidean ball containing \(Z\) must contain both \(s\) and \(-s\), so its radius is at least half their distance, namely \(\|s\|\). Therefore
\[
R_{\mathrm{out}}(Z)=\|s\|,
\]
and the centered ball realizes the minimum enclosing radius.

## Verification
The proof is symbolic and does not rely on finite enumeration. Its nonstandard ingredients were checked directly against the full text of Winter's classification: the canonical generator convention, the semistar-to-vertex bijection, and the equivalence between vertex-transitivity, root-system generators, and finite-reflection-group permutahedra.

The metric steps were independently reconstructed from support functions. Equation (2) follows directly from the two opposite support inequalities of a centrally symmetric body. Equation (3) uses the constant root-sign pattern on a closed Weyl chamber. Equation (4) reduces the continuous spherical minimization to finitely many chamber rays.

As sanity checks, the formulas specialize correctly to a rectangular box, where the chamber rays give the shortest half-edge as the inradius, and to the standard type-\(A\) permutahedron, where all vertices are in one reflection-group orbit and the centered circumradius is their common norm.

## Relationship to prior work
Winter classifies every vertex-transitive zonotope as a finite-reflection-group permutahedron and proves that its canonical generators form a root system. He also gives the precise correspondence between semistars and vertices. Those results provide the structural input used here.

The inspected full text does not state an inradius formula, a Weyl-half-sum radius formula, or a support-function minimization theorem for the class. Its occurrence of a circumradius is confined to an elementary two-dimensional normalization argument, not a general metric classification.

Targeted searches for vertex-transitive zonotope inradii, Coxeter-permutahedron radii, root-zonotope inradii, Weyl-vector radius formulas, and chamber-ray formulations did not locate a result implying the theorem above. Closely related literature on Coxeter permutahedra focuses on their combinatorial and deformation theory rather than these Euclidean radii. The residual possibility of an equivalent formula under a different normalization or terminology is recorded in the review.

## Limitations
The theorem uses Winter's vertex-transitive classification and therefore does not apply to arbitrary zonotopes. It does not classify all possible incenters when they are nonunique. It characterizes the facets tangent to the centered maximal ball, not the complete contact pattern of every possible maximal inscribed ball.

The originality assessment is based on the inspected source and targeted database and literature searches; it is not a formal proof that no equivalent statement exists in unindexed sources or older Coxeter-polytope literature under different normalization.
