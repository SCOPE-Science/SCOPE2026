# Exact first-two inverse logarithmic coefficient bodies for Sakaguchi symmetric classes

## Finding

Let
\[
\log\frac{f^{-1}(w)}{w}
=
2\sum_{n\ge1}\Gamma_n w^n
\]
define the inverse logarithmic coefficients of a normalized univalent function.

For the Sakaguchi class
\[
\mathcal S_S^*,
\]
the exact attainable set of the first two inverse logarithmic coefficients is
\[
|\Gamma_1|
\le
\frac12,
\]
together with the disk-fiber condition
\[
\left|
\Gamma_2-\Gamma_1^2
\right|
\le
\frac12
\left(
1-4|\Gamma_1|^2
\right).
\]
Conversely, every pair satisfying these two inequalities occurs for a function in
\[
\mathcal S_S^*.
\]

For the corresponding convex class
\[
\mathcal K_S,
\]
the exact attainable set is
\[
|\Gamma_1|
\le
\frac14,
\]
and
\[
\left|
\Gamma_2-\frac13\Gamma_1^2
\right|
\le
\frac16
\left(
1-16|\Gamma_1|^2
\right).
\]
Again every admissible pair occurs.

These two bodies immediately give complete sharp ranges for the moduli difference:
\[
-\frac1{\sqrt6}
\le
|\Gamma_2|-|\Gamma_1|
\le
\frac12
\qquad
\text{for }
f\in\mathcal S_S^*,
\]
and
\[
-\frac1{3\sqrt2}
\le
|\Gamma_2|-|\Gamma_1|
\le
\frac16
\qquad
\text{for }
f\in\mathcal K_S.
\]

The upper endpoints agree with the upper inequalities displayed in the recent source. The lower endpoints arise at
\[
\Gamma_2=0
\]
with
\[
|\Gamma_1|=\frac1{\sqrt6}
\]
for
\[
\mathcal S_S^*
\]
and
\[
|\Gamma_1|=\frac1{3\sqrt2}
\]
for
\[
\mathcal K_S.
\]

## Assumptions and scope

The class
\[
\mathcal S_S^*
\]
is the classical Sakaguchi class of normalized functions starlike with respect to symmetric points. The defining positive-real-part condition can be written
\[
\frac{2zf'(z)}
{f(z)-f(-z)}
=
p(z),
\qquad
\operatorname{Re}p(z)>0,
\qquad
p(0)=1.
\]

For the convex symmetric-point class
\[
\mathcal K_S,
\]
the coefficient relation used in the recent source is generated by
\[
\frac{
2(zf'(z))'
}{
(f(z)-f(-z))'
}
=
p(z),
\qquad
\operatorname{Re}p(z)>0,
\qquad
p(0)=1.
\]

Write
\[
p(z)
=
1+c_1z+c_2z^2+\cdots.
\]
The source computes
\[
a_2=\frac{c_1}{2},
\qquad
a_3=\frac{c_2}{2}
\]
for
\[
\mathcal S_S^*,
\]
and
\[
a_2=\frac{c_1}{4},
\qquad
a_3=\frac{c_2}{6}
\]
for
\[
\mathcal K_S.
\]

The inverse logarithmic coefficients satisfy
\[
\Gamma_1=-\frac{a_2}{2},
\qquad
\Gamma_2
=
-\frac{a_3}{2}
+
\frac34a_2^2.
\]

The result classifies only the joint range of
\[
(\Gamma_1,\Gamma_2).
\]
It makes no assertion about higher inverse logarithmic coefficients.

## Proof

Let
\[
p(z)
=
\frac{1+\omega(z)}
{1-\omega(z)}
\]
with
\[
\omega(0)=0,
\qquad
|\omega(z)|<1.
\]
Write
\[
\omega(z)
=
uz+
(1-|u|^2)vz^2+\cdots,
\qquad
|u|\le1,
\qquad
|v|\le1.
\]
This is the exact first-two Schur parameterization. Conversely, if
\[
|u|<1
\]
and
\[
|v|\le1,
\]
one may take
\[
\omega(z)
=
z\,
\frac{u+vz}
{1+\overline u\,vz}.
\]
When
\[
|u|=1,
\]
the limiting choice
\[
\omega(z)=uz
\]
applies.

Expanding the Cayley transform gives
\[
c_1=2u
\]
and
\[
c_2
=
2
\left(
u^2+(1-|u|^2)v
\right).
\]

For
\[
f\in\mathcal S_S^*,
\]
the source coefficient relations yield
\[
a_2=u,
\qquad
a_3
=
u^2+(1-|u|^2)v.
\]
Hence
\[
\Gamma_1
=
-\frac u2
\]
and
\[
\Gamma_2
=
\frac14u^2
-
\frac12(1-|u|^2)v.
\]
Since
\[
\Gamma_1^2=\frac14u^2,
\]
we obtain
\[
\Gamma_2-\Gamma_1^2
=
-\frac12
(1-|u|^2)v.
\]
Also
\[
|u|=2|\Gamma_1|.
\]
Therefore
\[
|\Gamma_1|
\le
\frac12
\]
and
\[
\left|
\Gamma_2-\Gamma_1^2
\right|
\le
\frac12
\left(
1-4|\Gamma_1|^2
\right).
\]

For
\[
f\in\mathcal K_S,
\]
the source relations instead give
\[
a_2=\frac u2,
\qquad
a_3
=
\frac13
\left(
u^2+(1-|u|^2)v
\right).
\]
Consequently
\[
\Gamma_1=-\frac u4
\]
and
\[
\Gamma_2
=
\frac1{48}u^2
-
\frac16
(1-|u|^2)v.
\]
Since
\[
\frac13\Gamma_1^2
=
\frac1{48}u^2,
\]
one gets
\[
\left|
\Gamma_2-\frac13\Gamma_1^2
\right|
\le
\frac16
\left(
1-16|\Gamma_1|^2
\right),
\]
with
\[
|\Gamma_1|\le\frac14.
\]

It remains to verify that these are exact attainable bodies, not merely necessary conditions.

Given any admissible pair in the first body, put
\[
u=-2\Gamma_1.
\]
If
\[
|u|<1,
\]
define
\[
v
=
\frac{
2(\Gamma_1^2-\Gamma_2)
}{
1-|u|^2
}.
\]
The body inequality is exactly
\[
|v|\le1.
\]
The Schur function above produces a Carathéodory function
\[
p.
\]
Write
\[
p=p_{\mathrm e}+p_{\mathrm o}
\]
for its even and odd parts. Define
\[
o(z)
=
z
\exp
\left(
\int_0^z
\frac{
p_{\mathrm e}(\zeta)-1
}{\zeta}
\,d\zeta
\right)
\]
and
\[
e(z)
=
\int_0^z
\frac{
p_{\mathrm o}(\zeta)o(\zeta)
}{\zeta}
\,d\zeta.
\]
Then
\[
o
\]
is odd,
\[
e
\]
is even, and
\[
f=o+e
\]
is normalized and satisfies
\[
\frac{
2zf'(z)
}{
f(z)-f(-z)
}
=
p(z).
\]
Thus
\[
f\in\mathcal S_S^*
\]
and has the prescribed pair. The boundary case
\[
|u|=1
\]
is obtained by the limiting linear Schur function.

For the convex body, given an admissible pair put
\[
u=-4\Gamma_1
\]
and
\[
v
=
\frac{
6\left(\frac13\Gamma_1^2-\Gamma_2\right)
}{
1-|u|^2
}.
\]
Again
\[
|v|\le1.
\]
With the same Carathéodory function
\[
p=p_{\mathrm e}+p_{\mathrm o},
\]
set
\[
y(z)
=
\exp
\left(
\int_0^z
\frac{
p_{\mathrm e}(\zeta)-1
}{\zeta}
\,d\zeta
\right),
\]
\[
o(z)
=
\int_0^z y(\zeta)\,d\zeta,
\]
and determine the even function
\[
e
\]
by
\[
(ze'(z))'
=
p_{\mathrm o}(z)y(z),
\qquad
e(0)=e'(0)=0.
\]
Then
\[
f=o+e
\]
is normalized and satisfies
\[
\frac{
2(zf'(z))'
}{
(f(z)-f(-z))'
}
=
p(z).
\]
Hence every point of the second body is also attained.

Now optimize the moduli difference over the fibers.

For
\[
\mathcal S_S^*,
\]
put
\[
r=|\Gamma_1|.
\]
The fiber for
\[
\Gamma_2
\]
is a disk with center modulus
\[
r^2
\]
and radius
\[
R(r)
=
\frac12-2r^2.
\]
Therefore
\[
\max |\Gamma_2|
=
r^2+R(r)
=
\frac12-r^2.
\]
Thus
\[
\max
\left(
|\Gamma_2|-|\Gamma_1|
\right)
=
\max_{0\le r\le1/2}
\left(
\frac12-r-r^2
\right)
=
\frac12.
\]

The minimum modulus in the same disk is
\[
\min |\Gamma_2|
=
\max
\left(
0,
3r^2-\frac12
\right).
\]
Hence
\[
\min
\left(
|\Gamma_2|-|\Gamma_1|
\right)
=
\min_{0\le r\le1/2}
\left[
\max
\left(
0,
3r^2-\frac12
\right)
-r
\right]
=
-\frac1{\sqrt6}.
\]
The minimum occurs at
\[
r=\frac1{\sqrt6},
\qquad
\Gamma_2=0.
\]

For
\[
\mathcal K_S,
\]
the fiber center has modulus
\[
\frac13r^2
\]
and radius
\[
\frac16-\frac83r^2.
\]
Thus
\[
\max |\Gamma_2|
=
\frac16-\frac73r^2,
\]
and
\[
\max
\left(
|\Gamma_2|-|\Gamma_1|
\right)
=
\frac16.
\]
Similarly,
\[
\min |\Gamma_2|
=
\max
\left(
0,
3r^2-\frac16
\right),
\]
so
\[
\min
\left(
|\Gamma_2|-|\Gamma_1|
\right)
=
-\frac1{3\sqrt2},
\]
attained at
\[
r=\frac1{3\sqrt2},
\qquad
\Gamma_2=0.
\]

The lower extremizers correspond to
\[
|v|=1.
\]
For example, if
\[
\Gamma_1
=
r e^{i\theta},
\]
one may take
\[
v=e^{2i\theta}
\]
with
\[
|u|^2=\frac23
\]
in
\[
\mathcal S_S^*
\]
and
\[
|u|^2=\frac89
\]
in
\[
\mathcal K_S.
\]
Thus the lower endpoints are attained, not merely approached.

## Verification

The two body formulas were replayed from the exact Schur parameterization
\[
c_1=2u,
\qquad
c_2
=
2
\left(
u^2+(1-|u|^2)v
\right).
\]
Substitution into the source formulas for
\[
a_2,
\qquad
a_3,
\qquad
\Gamma_1,
\qquad
\Gamma_2
\]
gives the displayed disk fibers identically.

The converse constructions were checked by even--odd decomposition. In the starlike case they satisfy
\[
zo'=p_{\mathrm e}o,
\qquad
ze'=p_{\mathrm o}o,
\]
and hence
\[
zf'=po.
\]
Since
\[
f(z)-f(-z)=2o(z),
\]
the defining Carathéodory relation follows.

In the convex case,
\[
o'=y
\]
satisfies
\[
(zo')'=p_{\mathrm e}o',
\]
while
\[
(ze')'=p_{\mathrm o}o'.
\]
Adding the two relations gives the required defining identity.

The scalar extrema were checked both symbolically and by direct one-variable minimization of the exact disk fibers. No finite sampling is used as proof.

## Relationship to prior work

Ahamed and Roy's 2026 preprint studies exactly the moduli difference
\[
|\Gamma_2|-|\Gamma_1|
\]
for the same symmetric-point classes. In the displayed full text, Theorems 2.1 and 2.2 state the sharp upper inequalities
\[
|\Gamma_2|-|\Gamma_1|
\le
\frac12
\]
and
\[
|\Gamma_2|-|\Gamma_1|
\le
\frac16.
\]
The paper then gives two-sided inequalities for its lune subclasses. Its abstract uses broader upper-and-lower language, but the two symmetric-point theorem statements and their proofs display only the upper side.

Mandal, Ahamed, and Zaprawa determine sharp individual bounds for
\[
|\Gamma_1|,
\qquad
|\Gamma_2|,
\qquad
|\Gamma_3|
\]
for
\[
\mathcal S_S^*
\]
and
\[
\mathcal K_S.
\]
Those separate extrema do not determine the joint attainable set of
\[
(\Gamma_1,\Gamma_2).
\]

Ahamed and Mandal's 2026 article on logarithmic coefficients of the same two symmetric classes has an abstract announcing sharp moduli-difference results for logarithmic coefficients and results for inverse logarithmic coefficients, but its publicly accessible record does not state an exact joint first-two inverse-logarithmic coefficient body.

Targeted searches for coefficient bodies, variability regions, the lower constants
\[
1/\sqrt6
\]
and
\[
1/(3\sqrt2),
\]
and equivalent Schur-fiber formulations did not locate a published statement of the two exact bodies above.

## Limitations

The result is confined to the first two inverse logarithmic coefficients.

The argument uses the exact two-coefficient Carathéodory parameterization and therefore does not by itself determine the three-dimensional body involving
\[
\Gamma_3.
\]

The publicly accessible record of one September 2026 article on the same symmetric classes does not expose its full subscription text. Its abstract does not state the joint body, but this remains a residual literature-access risk.

The result does not alter the previously known sharp individual coefficient bounds.
