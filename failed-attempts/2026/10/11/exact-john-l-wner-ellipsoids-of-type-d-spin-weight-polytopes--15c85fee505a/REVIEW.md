## correctness

PASS

For every integer n>=4 the fixed-sign-parity orbit is the half-scaled even parity polytope; the other spin polytope is an orthogonal single-coordinate-sign reflection. The established irredundant H-description comprises coordinate facets x_i=±1/2 and 2^(n−1) opposite-parity facets s·x<=(n−2)/2, ||s||₂=√n. The respective distances are 1/2 and (n−2)/(2√n), equal exactly at n=4 and strictly separated n>=5. Opposing coordinate slabs upper-bound any contained ball radius by 1/2 and fix any optimal center at zero. All 2^(n−1) orbit vertices have norm √n/2; irreducible signed-permutation symmetry forces the circumcenter zero. Uniqueness of both John and Löwner ellipsoids plus even-sign permutation invariance forces their centers zero and quadratic forms scalar, so they are exactly centered in- and circumballs. Even n gives central symmetry and standard sharp √n containment. All domain endpoints n=4 and every n>=5 correctly handled. Independently verified the facet-offset inequalities, contact counts and vertex radii exhaustively from parity vectors for n=4..10; those finite cases corroborate but do not replace the all-dimensional proof.

## originality

FAIL

The asserted type-D_n body is exactly one half-scaled parity polytope. Saunderson–Parrilo–Willsky (2014), Section 3.2 equations (15), (18), already specifies its full irredundant facet system for every n>=4 and Section 5.2 identifies its evenly signed permutation symmetry. Güler–Gürtuna (2011/2012), Theorem 3.5 (itself attributed there to Danzer et al.), proves both extremal ellipsoids inherit all automorphisms. Even sign flips and coordinate permutations force invariant centers zero and positive definite shape matrices scalar. Reading the known facet inequalities then gives exactly min(1/2,(n-2)/(2 sqrt(n)))=1/2 for the John radius, and the already specified parity vertices give sqrt(n)/2 for the Löwner radius. All 16-versus-2n tangencies follow by the single inequality (n-2)^2>n at n>=5; all vertices share the same norm. Thus the entire claimed classification, not just overlapping terminology, is a direct routine corollary of the pre-existing all-dimensional exact object/facet/symmetry descriptions together with the published general extremal-ellipsoid symmetry theorem. No independently undetermined mathematical statement remains; failure does not concern correctness. Historical priority between any 2026 finding and other work is not assumed.

## value

FAIL

Type-D spin orbit polytopes are genuinely natural Coxeter/demicube objects and John/Löwner ellipsoids are natural invariants, so narrow exact classifications can in principle be worthwhile. In this specific case however the claimed entire metric result is obtained by one scaling of a known irredundant facet system, one normal-length comparison, a uniform vertex-norm computation and a standard symmetry inheritance theorem. The n=4 contact transition is the elementary equality (n−2)/(2√n)=1/2, and the n>=5 orbit count is already given in the facet theorem. It neither determines an unknown structural invariant requiring new mathematics nor resolves an independently motivating gap once the published exact parity object and symmetry framework are taken into account. Reproducibility, infinite-dimensional phrasing n>=4 and canonical terminology alone cannot upgrade this textbook-level deduction to independent research value.
