# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

For every k≥1 and all integer d_j≥1, affine normalization to standard simplices is legitimate. At vertex 0 the strict inward simplex tangent cone has all coordinate components >0; at vertex e_i it has u_j>0 for all j≠i and Σ_j u_j<0. Any two such cones are disjoint (for d=1 the two open rays are disjoint). Product vertex tangent cones are Cartesian products, so two different product vertices have disjoint illuminating-direction sets. Thus any illuminating direction can serve at most one product vertex; summing unit requirements across these pairwise disjoint direction sets proves i*(P)≥f0(P) as well as i(P)≥f0(P). One strictly inward vector at each vertex illuminates all boundary points because a face point has a subset of the active facets at a vertex of the same face; hence i(P)≤f0(P) and also i*(P)≤f0(P). The inequalities d+1≤2^d (equality only d=1) and d+1≤3·2^{d−2} for d≥2 (equality only d=2), with other factors at most 2^{d_j}, give the sharp n-dimensional noncube bound and exact equality family. Independent checker reconstructed facet sign tests for integer directions d=1..6 and enumerated integer partitions n≤12, with no contradictions; those finite checks are corroborative, not infinite proof. Definition of finite-support fractional measures, nonzero directions and boundary points, n≥1 edge case all checked. Correctness does not rescue covered originality.

Relevant exact, alias, antipodality, table and semantic searches and three primary full-text/section readings support this statement.
