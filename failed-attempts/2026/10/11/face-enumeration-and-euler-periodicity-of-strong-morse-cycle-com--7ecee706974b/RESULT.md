# Face enumeration and Euler periodicity of strong Morse cycle complexes

## Finding

For every integer \(n\ge3\), let \(C_n\) denote the one-dimensional simplicial complex with vertices \(v_0,\ldots,v_{n-1}\) and edges \(\{v_i,v_{i+1}\}\), with subscripts modulo \(n\). For \(n=3\), this is the three-edge circle, *not* a filled triangle. Let \(\mathcal{SM}(C_n)\) be the strong discrete Morse matching complex, defined as the simplicial complex generated by the matchings produced by injective vertex filtrations. Define the face polynomial, including the empty face, by

\[
F_n(t)=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{SM}(C_n)\cup\{\varnothing\}}t^{|\sigma|}.
\]

Then, for **every** \(n\ge3\),

\[
F_n(t)=\operatorname{tr}\!\left(\begin{array}{ccc}1&t&t\\1&t&0\\1&0&t\end{array}\right)^{n}
-2\operatorname{tr}\!\left(\begin{array}{cc}0&t\\1&t\end{array}\right)^{n}.
\]

Consequently,

\[
\widetilde\chi(\mathcal{SM}(C_n))=-(-1)^n-2\cos\!\left(\frac{\pi n}{2}\right)+4\cos\!\left(\frac{2\pi n}{3}\right).
\]

The reduced Euler characteristic has least positive period \(12\). For example, the values in the consecutive residue classes starting at \(n=3\) are
\[
5,-5,-1,5,-1,-5,5,-1,-1,1,-1,-1.
\]
The polynomial gives the entire face vector at any cycle length, not only the top-dimensional simplices. Specifically, \(F_{22}(1)=263{,}593{,}441\), so there are \(263{,}593{,}440\) nonempty faces at \(n=22\), with \(\widetilde\chi=-1\).

## Assumptions and scope

The strong matching complex uses the downward closure of all strong discrete Morse matchings arising from injective filtrations, as in Knudson and Owens–White [1, Definitions 3.2 and 4.2]. Each matching vertex is a vertex–edge incidence of the original cycle. We count a subset of matching vertices as a face if it is contained in at least one filtration-induced strong matching. The empty face contributes the constant coefficient \(1\) by the usual face-polynomial convention, although it does not represent a vertex of the geometric realization. No assertion is made about cycles with fewer than three edges.

## Proof

**1. Encode filtrations by oriented cycle edges.** Fix an injective filtration \(g\) on the vertices. Put \(s_i=+\) when \(g(v_i)<g(v_{i+1})\), and \(s_i=-\) otherwise. Not all signs can agree, since a cyclic chain of strict inequalities is impossible. Conversely, every nonconstant assignment of signs to the edges gives an acyclic orientation of the cycle: its only possible directed cycle would use all edges consistently. A topological ordering realizes the assignment by an injective filtration.

**2. Encode possible matched incidences by marked vertex colors.** The descending link of \(v_i\) has exactly one vertex precisely when \(s_{i-1}=s_i\). If both signs are \(+\), then \(v_i\) is paired with its left edge \(\{v_{i-1},v_i\}\); encode this as a mark \(+\) at \(i\). If both signs are \(-\), it is paired with its right edge \(\{v_i,v_{i+1}\}\); encode this by a mark \(-\). Otherwise it is unpaired. A face is therefore represented uniquely by a cyclic word in the alphabet \(\{0,+,-\}\), where \(0\) means that the vertex contributes no chosen incidence. This representation is unique because a matching cannot choose both incident edges at the same vertex.

The word is realizable by a filtration if and only if two conditions hold: **(i)** no two cyclically neighboring nonzero symbols have opposite signs; **(ii)** if all nonzero symbols carry just one sign, then the marked positions do not cover every edge of the underlying cycle. To prove necessity, adjacent opposite marks force contradictory signs on their common edge; a single-color vertex cover forces every edge sign to equal that color, contradicting the strict cycle ordering. Conversely, condition (i) makes all forced edge signs consistent. If both colors occur, the resulting orientation is nonconstant. If only one color occurs, condition (ii) leaves an edge with both endpoints unmarked, whose unforced sign can be chosen opposite to the marked color. If no marks occur, choose any nonconstant sign assignment. In each case the sign assignment extends to an acyclic orientation and hence to a filtration, so the given marks form a face. This proves the criterion without assuming that an arbitrary subset is itself a complete strong matching.

**3. Count the cyclic words.** For the adjacent-compatibility condition alone, assign weight \(1\) to an unmarked state and weight \(t\) to either marked state. In state order \((0,+,-)\), the transfer matrix is
\[
T(t)=\begin{pmatrix}1&t&t\\1&t&0\\1&0&t\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its trace \(\operatorname{tr}(T(t)^n)\) counts all cyclic words obeying condition (i), each with weight \(t^{\text{number of marks}}\), exactly once. An inadmissible word among these is precisely a single-color word whose marked vertices form a vertex cover. For one fixed color the forbidden transition is \(0\) followed by \(0\), and the corresponding two-state transfer matrix is
\[
U(t)=\begin{pmatrix}0&t\\1&t\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Thus \(\operatorname{tr}(U(t)^n)\) counts the vertex covers with that color, including the fully marked word. The two colors give disjoint forbidden sets for \(n\ge3\), so the exclusion subtracts exactly \(2\operatorname{tr}(U(t)^n)\). This proves the face polynomial formula.

**4. Evaluate the alternating face sum.** By definition of reduced Euler characteristic for a nonempty finite complex,
\[
\widetilde\chi=-\sum_{\sigma}(-1)^{|\sigma|}=-F_n(-1).
\]
The eigenvalues of \(T(-1)\) are \(-1,i,-i\), while the eigenvalues of \(U(-1)\) are the two primitive cube roots of unity. Taking traces of their powers gives the claimed trigonometric formula. Periodicity divides \(12\), and the listed values do not repeat with a smaller positive period; hence the least period is exactly \(12\). Evaluating the integer matrix powers at \(t=1\) yields the stated \(n=22\) count. These evaluations concern all faces, including nonpure maximal faces.

## Verification

The accompanying standalone Python program `verify_face_enumerator.py` requires only Python 3's standard library. Run `python3 verify_face_enumerator.py` next to this file. It independently generates all filtration-induced matching faces from **every permutation** of the underlying vertices for \(3\le n\le8\), generates faces from the proved cyclic local conditions, and compares both enumerations against the polynomial transfer matrices. It also checks the top-face count \(n(n-1)\), reduced Euler formula through \(n=100\), and the complete \(n=22\) face count. All checks completed successfully. Finite checks corroborate but do not replace the all-\(n\) argument above.

## Relationship to prior work

Knudson and Owens–White [1] introduce these strong matching complexes and calculate their maximal dimension, the count \(n(n-1)\) of top-dimensional faces, the homotopy types of the pure cycle subcomplexes, and the homology of the complete cycle complexes through \(n=21\). Their Section 8 and Table 1 do not give the complete face polynomial for general \(n\). The present result explicitly counts **all** faces of the complete nonpure complex, including those not contained in a top-dimensional facet; it is not a consequence of the count of top-dimensional faces alone.

Chari and Joswig [2, Sections 3–4] give enumerative formulas for the **ordinary** discrete Morse complex of the cycle, where more matchings are allowed. The strong matching complex studied here is a generally proper subcomplex, so their formula does not determine its face numbers. Fernández [3] establishes the filtration and descending-domination machinery but does not enumerate this family of strong matching complexes.

## Limitations

The formula calculates face counts and an Euler characteristic. It does **not** determine individual homology groups, torsion, connectivity, homotopy type, or shellability. Euler characteristics of two complexes can coincide even when their homology groups differ. No claim of exhaustive precedence screening is made; a differently indexed equivalent enumeration may exist. Numerical checks stop at the explicitly listed bounds, while the general proof is symbolic.
