For every finite connected simple graph \(G\) with \(n\) vertices, \(m\) edges, minimum degree at least \(2\), and girth at least \(5\), the Lovász neighborhood complex collapses to a graph: it is \(\bigvee^{2m-2n+1}S^1\) when \(G\) is non-bipartite, while in the bipartite case it has exactly two components, each homotopy equivalent to \(\bigvee^{m-n+1}S^1\). The proof is an explicit facetwise collapse because distinct neighborhood simplices meet only in vertices.
