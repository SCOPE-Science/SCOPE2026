# Scale-two Čech homotopy and persistence kernel of circular ladder graphs

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge5\), the closed-ball graph Čech complex at scale two for the circular ladder \(G_n=C_n\square K_2\) has the homotopy type
\[
\mathcal C_2(G_n)\simeq S^1\vee\bigvee^{n}S^2.
\]
There is an explicit sequence of exactly \(4n\) elementary simplicial collapses onto a union of \(n\) tetrahedral boundaries. The scale-one inclusion induces a surjection on integral first homology, with kernel freely generated by the \(n\) square face cycles:
\[
\ker(H_1(G_n;\mathbb Z)\to H_1(\mathcal C_2(G_n);\mathbb Z))\cong\mathbb Z^n.
\]
In particular \(\widetilde H_1(\mathcal C_2(G_n);\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z\), \(\widetilde H_2(\mathcal C_2(G_n);\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^n\), and the other reduced integral homology groups vanish. These are integral results, not statements only over a field.

## Assumptions and scope
Let \(n\) be any integer at least \(5\). The vertex set consists of \(a_i,b_i\) for \(i\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\). Its edges are \(a_i a_{i+1}\), \(b_i b_{i+1}\), and \(a_i b_i\). Each edge has length one; distances are intrinsic shortest-path distances in the resulting metric graph, not distances in a Euclidean embedding. Define \(\mathcal C_r(G_n)\) as the nerve of closed balls of radius \(r/2\) with centers at these graph vertices. In particular, at \(r=1\) its nerve is exactly \(G_n\): edges are included because adjacent half-radius balls meet, whereas no three centers have a common half-radius point. At \(r=2\), a set of centers spans a face if and only if it is contained in the closed vertex neighborhood \(N[v]\) of some graph vertex \(v\). This even-scale graph Čech description is recorded in Adams–Shukla–Singh, Lemma 2.2.

## Proof
Write \(K=\mathcal C_2(G_n)\). The graph is cubic, triangle-free, and has no common three-neighbor set among distinct vertices for \(n\ge5\). The maximal simplices of \(K\) are precisely the \(2n\) tetrahedra \(N[a_i]\) and \(N[b_i]\). Their face vector is
\[
(f_0,f_1,f_2,f_3)=(2n,7n,8n,2n).
\]
The counts follow directly by examining the closed neighborhoods: the \(2n\) opposite open-neighborhood triples, the \(2n\) ring triples, and the \(4n\) square-boundary triangles are all distinct when \(n\ge5\). The seven-edge-per-column count includes all horizontal, rung, diagonal, and distance-two ring edges.

**First \(2n\) collapses.** For each \(v\) in the vertex set, delete the pair \((N(v),N[v])\), where \(N(v)\) is the face consisting of the three neighbors of \(v\). This triangle has exactly one maximal coface, namely the tetrahedron \(N[v]\): any other possible common center would force a triangle or an additional short common-neighbor configuration, excluded by the explicit adjacency relations for \(n\ge5\). Therefore each pair is a valid elementary simplicial collapse. These pairs are pairwise disjoint, and deleting any earlier pair does not invalidate later freeness.

**Next \(2n\) collapses.** For each \(i\) and each of the two rings \(x=a,b\), collapse the ring triangle \(\{x_{i-1},x_i,x_{i+1}\}\) along its unique skipping edge \(\{x_{i-1},x_{i+1}\}\). Following the first stage, that skipping edge is contained in exactly this surviving triangle. Indeed, any different center for both endpoints would give a four-cycle within one ring; these are distinct for \(n\ge5\). Each such deletion is again an elementary simplicial collapse.

The remaining complex \(K'\) has exactly the proper faces of the \(n\) four-vertex simplices
\[
Q_i=\{a_i,a_{i+1},b_i,b_{i+1}\},\qquad i\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z.
\]
In other words, \(K'=\bigcup_i\partial\Delta(Q_i)\). This can be checked face-by-face by classifying every face of a closed neighborhood: after the two stages, all and only the square-boundary triangles, their edges and vertices survive. The residual face vector is \((2n,5n,4n)\), with no tetrahedra.

Each \(\partial\Delta(Q_i)\) is a 2-sphere. Consecutive sphere subcomplexes meet exactly in the rung edge \(\{a_i,b_i\}\), nonconsecutive sphere subcomplexes are disjoint, and there are no triple intersections (all require \(n\ge5\)). Collapsing each contractible common rung edge to a point is a homotopy equivalence of CW complexes. The resulting graph of spaces is the cyclic arrangement of \(n\) simply connected 2-spheres, with a circle of attachment vertices and edges. A spanning-tree contraction of the attaching graph gives
\[
K'\simeq S^1\vee\bigvee^{n}S^2.
\]
This completes the homotopy proof, using only valid elementary simplicial collapses and homotopy equivalences of CW complexes.

For the persistence assertion, \(H_1(G_n;\mathbb Z)\) has rank \((3n)-(2n)+1=n+1\). Its integral cycle lattice has the \(n\) square-boundary cycles together with the positively oriented upper-ring cycle as a \(\mathbb Z\)-basis: a spanning tree comprising all rungs and all but one upper-ring edge shows that the missing upper-ring edge and the \(n\) bottom-ring edges give \(n+1\) independent fundamental cycles, to which this set is related by a unimodular triangular change of basis. Each quadrilateral cycle bounds two triangles in \(K'\), hence maps to zero in \(H_1(K;\mathbb Z)\). Conversely the upper-ring cycle traverses the cyclic incidence graph of sphere attachments once, so maps to a generator of \(H_1(K;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z\). Therefore the map is surjective and its kernel is precisely the claimed rank-\(n\) direct summand.

## Verification
The self-contained Python 3 script `verify_prism.py` reconstructs the graph, all closed-neighborhood faces, all \(4n\) elementary collapses (checking the unique maximal coface for each free face), the exact residual square-boundary union, its pairwise and triple intersections, face vectors, and independent mod-two simplicial boundary ranks. It was executed for the ten orders \(n=5,6,7,8,9,10,12,16,20,30\); every case satisfied the predicted mod-two Betti numbers \((1,1,n,0)\) for dimensions zero through three and finished with `VERIFY_OK`. The script uses no external packages. These finite checks corroborate, but do not replace, the all-order combinatorial proof above.

## Relationship to prior work
Adams, Shukla, and Singh (2022) define intrinsic graph Čech complexes and identify the even-scale neighborhood facets. Their homotopy classifications concern hypercube graphs, not the cyclic Cartesian-product family treated here. Matsushita (2025) studies closed-neighborhood complexes and obtains general homotopy and fundamental-group information; the inspected theorems do not calculate this family's sphere-wedge type or the exact inclusion-induced homology kernel. Shen and collaborators (2025) study Lovász **open**-neighborhood complexes of circular ladders (as well as related constructions), which differ from the closed-ball nerve here. A separately published graph-metric Vietoris–Rips calculation for circular ladders also concerns a different simplicial filtration; even when its vertices and numerical scale agree, a Vietoris–Rips complex need not have the homotopy type of this Čech complex. These are comparisons between mathematical statements, not mere differences of titles.

## Limitations
The result is restricted to finite integers \(n\ge5\); no assertion is made for the four-rung cube or the triangular prism. It describes the Čech nerve of balls centered only at graph vertices, not a nerve indexed by all metric-graph points or Euclidean balls. Although a homotopy classification and one persistence inclusion map are proved, no full-scale persistence barcode is claimed. Unindexed or inaccessible equivalent literature could still affect originality; independent external auditing has not been performed.
