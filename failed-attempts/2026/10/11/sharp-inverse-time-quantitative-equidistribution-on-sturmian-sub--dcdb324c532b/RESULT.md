# Sharp inverse-time quantitative equidistribution on Sturmian subshifts
## Finding
Let \(\alpha\in(0,1)\) be irrational and let \(0<\theta<1\). Let \(X_\alpha\) be the one-sided Sturmian subshift of slope \(\alpha\), equipped with the standard symbolic metric \(d_\theta\), and let \(\mu_\alpha\) be its unique invariant probability. For \(x\in X_\alpha\) and an integer \(n\ge1\), define the empirical orbit measure
\[
\eta_{x,n}=\frac1n\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\delta_{\sigma^k x}.
\]
Then
\[
W_\theta(\eta_{x,n},\mu_\alpha)\le \frac{1}{n(1-\theta)^3}
\]
for every \(x\in X_\alpha\) and every \(n\ge1\).

For \(x,y\in X_\alpha\), let \(F_n(x,y)\) be the minimum, over all permutations \(\pi\) of \(\{0,\ldots,n-1\}\), of
\[
\frac1n\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}d_\theta(\sigma^k x,\sigma^{\pi(k)}y).
\]
Equivalently, \(F_n(x,y)=W_\theta(\eta_{x,n},\eta_{y,n})\). For every \(n\ge1\),
\[
\frac1n\le \sup_{x,y\in X_\alpha}F_n(x,y)\le \frac{2}{n(1-\theta)^3}.
\]
Hence the uniform matching discrepancy has the exact order \(n^{-1}\). In the terminology of quantitative unique ergodicity based on \(F_n\), every exponent strictly below one has vanishing rescaled discrepancy, while the exponent-one rescaling cannot converge to zero.

## Assumptions and scope
The metric is the one-sided symbolic metric used in the source: if two points first differ at coordinate \(\ell\), then their distance is \(\theta^\ell\); in particular the diameter is at most one. The Wasserstein distance is the dual distance obtained by testing against real functions with Lipschitz seminorm at most one.

The claim is intrinsic to \(X_\alpha\). It does not assert an inverse-time equidistribution rate for the ambient beta-shift, for periodic-orbit approximation inside a larger subshift of finite type, or for observables with weaker regularity. The constant \(2(1-\theta)^{-3}\) in the matching bound is not claimed to be optimal; the sharp assertion is the exponent \(n^{-1}\).

## Proof
Write \(\Phi(x)=x_0\). Theorem D of Huang--Jenkinson--Xu--Zhang gives, for every Lipschitz function \(f:X_\alpha\to\mathbb R\), a scalar \(c_f\) and a Lipschitz transfer function \(\widetilde f\) such that
\[
f-\int f\,d\mu_\alpha
=c_f(\Phi-\alpha)+\widetilde f\circ\sigma-\widetilde f,
\]
with
\[
|c_f|\le \frac{|f|_{\mathrm{Lip}}}{(1-\theta)^2},
\qquad
|\widetilde f|_{\mathrm{Lip}}\le \frac{\theta|f|_{\mathrm{Lip}}}{(1-\theta)^3}.
\]
The Sturmian balance property gives
\[
|S_n(\Phi-\alpha)(x)|<1
\]
for every \(x\in X_\alpha\) and every \(n\ge1\). Telescoping the cohomological identity therefore yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
\left|S_nf(x)-n\int f\,d\mu_\alpha\right|
&\le |c_f|+|\widetilde f(\sigma^n x)-\widetilde f(x)|\\
&\le \frac{|f|_{\mathrm{Lip}}}{(1-\theta)^2}
 +\frac{\theta|f|_{\mathrm{Lip}}}{(1-\theta)^3}\\
&=\frac{|f|_{\mathrm{Lip}}}{(1-\theta)^3}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here the second term uses \(\operatorname{diam}(X_\alpha,d_\theta)\le1\). Dividing by \(n\) and taking the supremum over observables with Lipschitz seminorm at most one proves
\[
W_\theta(\eta_{x,n},\mu_\alpha)\le \frac{1}{n(1-\theta)^3}.
\]
The triangle inequality gives the upper matching estimate because equal-weight atomic optimal transport is realized by a permutation:
\[
F_n(x,y)=W_\theta(\eta_{x,n},\eta_{y,n})
\le \frac{2}{n(1-\theta)^3}.
\]

For the lower estimate, \(\Phi\) has Lipschitz seminorm one. By balance, the number of ones in any length-\(n\) Sturmian factor takes only two adjacent integer values. Their \(\mu_\alpha\)-average is \(n\alpha\), which is nonintegral because \(\alpha\) is irrational. Therefore both values occur, and they are \(\lfloor n\alpha\rfloor\) and \(\lceil n\alpha\rceil\). Choose \(x,y\in X_\alpha\) whose initial length-\(n\) factors realize the two values. Then
\[
\left|\int\Phi\,d\eta_{x,n}-\int\Phi\,d\eta_{y,n}\right|=\frac1n.
\]
The Wasserstein dual inequality gives \(F_n(x,y)\ge1/n\), proving the lower bound.

## Verification
Every quantifier in the claim is covered by the proof: \(\alpha\) is any irrational slope, \(\theta\) is any symbolic-metric parameter in \((0,1)\), and the estimates hold for every positive integer \(n\), not only along continued-fraction denominators. The upper bound uses the source's explicit norm estimates from its Lipschitz cohomological rigidity theorem and the exact balance bound; no finite experiment is used.

The lower bound is not an asymptotic sampling argument. For each fixed \(n\), the two length-\(n\) digit counts must both occur because balance restricts them to adjacent integers while their invariant average is the noninteger \(n\alpha\). The digit observable itself then witnesses Wasserstein distance at least \(1/n\).

## Relationship to prior work
Huang--Jenkinson--Xu--Zhang prove that, modulo constants and Lipschitz coboundaries, the Lipschitz functions on \(X_\alpha\) form a one-dimensional space generated by the digit observable, with explicit slope-independent norm bounds. Their paper also records that the digit discrepancy has uniformly bounded Birkhoff sums by Sturmian balance. The inverse-time empirical Wasserstein estimate and the two-sided matching-rate estimate above are consequences of combining those ingredients, but are not stated as quantitative equidistribution results in the inspected source.

Wang--Zheng prove that Sturmian measures are quantitatively uniquely ergodic for every exponent strictly below one and derive sublinear periodic-measure approximation rates. Their object of periodic approximation is different from the intrinsic empirical orbit measure here. The two-sided estimate above identifies the missing endpoint scale for the intrinsic matching pseudometric: it is bounded above and below by fixed multiples of \(n^{-1}\), so the exponent-one rescaling fails for a concrete order-one reason rather than merely by an abstract non-singleton obstruction.

Classical Hecke--Ostrowski bounded-remainder theory gives bounded discrepancy for clopen sets in Sturmian systems, with constants depending on the clopen set. The present bound is uniform over the entire Lipschitz unit ball for the specified symbolic metric and therefore yields a Wasserstein estimate.

## Limitations
The result does not identify the sharp constant in
\[
n\sup_{x,y}F_n(x,y).
\]
It does not assert convergence of that rescaled quantity, only uniform positive lower and finite upper bounds. The proof depends on the quantitative Lipschitz cohomology estimate from arXiv:2610.03619v1 and therefore does not automatically extend to arbitrary balanced subshifts, two-sided metrics with a different normalization, or non-Lipschitz observables.
