For disjoint bipartite edges, conflict can come only from the two crossed edges; both are absent exactly when the pair transposes two deleted matching indices. Each edge has at most one such disjoint compatible partner. A third edge in a packing containing this pair would therefore have to meet both, but the only bipartite candidates are precisely the two deleted edges. In the remaining case the edges meet pairwise and have one common center: the alternative with three edges is a triangle. Each star subset of size ≥2 has a unique center; the four degree groups give the four binomial terms. The crossed pairs add exactly binom(t,2) at size two. The constant and linear terms are 1 and mn−t. At t=0 the exceptional term vanishes; H_{2,2,2} has only the compatible disjoint pair and enumerator 1+2x+x². The stated max(m,n) is a valid summation cutoff, not necessarily a nonzero top coefficient.
