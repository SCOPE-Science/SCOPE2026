# Exact degree of the exceptional-point divisor in the Wang--Wen vanishing theorem
## Finding
Let \(C\) be a smooth connected complex projective curve of genus \(g\ge2\). Let \(G\) be a connected reductive complex algebraic group and let
\[
\rho:G\longrightarrow \operatorname{GL}(V)
\]
be a nonzero finite-dimensional representation satisfying \(V^G=0\) and \(\rho|_{Z(G)}=1\). Put \(r=\dim V\). Fix a topological type of principal \(G\)-bundle, and, in the fixed-abelianization version, also fix the induced \(G^{\mathrm{ab}}\)-bundle.

For a general principal bundle \(P\) in either Wang--Wen moduli problem, set \(E=E_\rho(P)\) and define
\[
\alpha:C\longrightarrow \operatorname{Pic}^{g-1}(C),
\qquad
x\longmapsto \mathcal O_C((g-1)x).
\]
Let
\[
\Theta_E
=
\left\{
L\in\operatorname{Pic}^{g-1}(C):
H^0(C,E\otimes L)\ne0
\right\}.
\]
Then \(\Theta_E\) is a proper generalized theta divisor, and the scheme-theoretic pullback
\[
B_P:=\alpha^*\Theta_E
\]
is an effective divisor of exact degree
\[
\boxed{\deg B_P=r\,g\,(g-1)^2}.
\]
Thus the exceptional points at which
\[
H^0\!\left(C,E((g-1)x)\right)\ne0
\]
have total multiplicity exactly \(r\,g\,(g-1)^2\). In particular the exceptional set is finite, as in Wang--Wen, but it is never empty.

Because \(\deg E=0\),
\[
\chi\!\left(E((g-1)x)\right)
=
r(g-1)+r(1-g)
=
0.
\]
Hence \(H^0\) is nonzero exactly when \(H^1\) is nonzero. Therefore \(B_P\) is also the exact failure divisor for the simultaneous vanishing
\[
H^0\!\left(C,E((g-1)x)\right)
=
H^1\!\left(C,E((g-1)x)\right)
=
0.
\]

## Assumptions and scope
The ground field is \(\mathbf C\). The principal bundle \(P\) is general in one of the two moduli problems used by Wang--Wen: either the component with prescribed topological type, or the fiber with prescribed induced \(G^{\mathrm{ab}}\)-bundle.

The hypotheses on \(\rho\) are exactly those in Wang--Wen's vanishing theorem. Since the determinant of a representation is a character of \(G\), it is trivial on \(G_{\mathrm{der}}\). It is also trivial on \(Z(G)\) because \(\rho|_{Z(G)}=1\). A connected reductive group is generated by its connected center and derived group, so \(\det\rho=1\). Consequently
\[
\det E\simeq\mathcal O_C
\quad\text{and}\quad
\deg E=0.
\]

The divisor \(B_P\) is counted scheme-theoretically. No claim is made that it is reduced, so the theorem gives an exact total multiplicity rather than an exact number of distinct points.

## Proof
Wang--Wen prove that a general pair \((P,x)\) satisfies
\[
H^0\!\left(C,E_\rho(P)((g-1)x)\right)
=
H^1\!\left(C,E_\rho(P)((g-1)x)\right)
=
0.
\]
They explicitly interpret this as saying that, for general \(P\), the curve
\[
\alpha(C)\subset\operatorname{Pic}^{g-1}(C)
\]
meets the generalized theta locus \(\Theta_E\) in only finitely many points. Thus \(\alpha(C)\) is not contained in \(\Theta_E\), so \(B_P=\alpha^*\Theta_E\) is an effective Cartier divisor on \(C\).

It remains to compute its degree.

For a rank-\(r\), degree-zero vector bundle with proper generalized theta divisor, determinant-of-cohomology gives
\[
[\Theta_E]=r[\Theta]
\]
in the Néron--Severi group of \(\operatorname{Pic}^{g-1}(C)\), where \(\Theta\) is the principal theta class. In the trivial-determinant semistable case this is the classical statement that \(\Theta_E\in|r\Theta|\).

Choose \(p_0\in C\) and identify \(\operatorname{Pic}^{g-1}(C)\) with the Jacobian \(J(C)\) by tensoring with \(\mathcal O_C(-(g-1)p_0)\). If
\[
j:C\longrightarrow J(C),
\qquad
x\longmapsto\mathcal O_C(x-p_0),
\]
is the Abel--Jacobi embedding, then under this identification
\[
\alpha=[g-1]\circ j.
\]
For a principal polarization,
\[
[g-1]^*[\Theta]=(g-1)^2[\Theta].
\]
The Poincaré formula gives
\[
[j(C)]=\frac{[\Theta]^{g-1}}{(g-1)!},
\]
and the normalization of the principal polarization gives
\[
\int_{J(C)}\frac{[\Theta]^g}{g!}=1.
\]
Therefore
\[
\deg j^*\Theta
=
\int_{J(C)}[\Theta]\cdot[j(C)]
=
g.
\]
Hence
\[
\deg\alpha^*\Theta
=
(g-1)^2g.
\]
Multiplying by the rank-\(r\) theta class yields
\[
\deg B_P
=
\deg\alpha^*\Theta_E
=
r\,g\,(g-1)^2.
\]

Finally, Riemann--Roch gives \(\chi(E((g-1)x))=0\), so \(H^0\) and \(H^1\) have equal dimension. The support of \(B_P\) is therefore exactly the failure locus of the simultaneous vanishing.

## Verification
The critical inputs were checked directly against the initiating full text:

1. Wang--Wen define
   \[
   \Theta_E=\{L\in\operatorname{Pic}^{g-1}(C):H^0(C,E\otimes L)\ne0\}
   \]
   and state that their vanishing theorem makes the curve \(x\mapsto\mathcal O_C((g-1)x)\) meet \(\Theta_E\) only finitely for general \(P\).
2. Their Theorem 1.3 gives simultaneous \(H^0\)- and \(H^1\)-vanishing for a general pair \((P,x)\) under the representation hypotheses used here.
3. Beauville's classical generalized-theta construction records that a rank-\(r\) bundle with proper theta divisor has theta class \(r\Theta\) in the trivial-determinant setting.
4. The remaining intersection calculation is the standard Poincaré formula for the Abel--Jacobi curve together with \([m]^*\Theta\equiv m^2\Theta\).

No finite experiment is used as a substitute for the universal proof. The exact count is scheme-theoretic and allows nonreduced exceptional points.

## Relationship to prior work
Wang--Wen prove the qualitative statement needed in their Hecke-curve argument: for general \(P\), only finitely many \(x\in C\) fail the simultaneous cohomological vanishing. They do not state the total multiplicity of that finite failure locus.

Classical generalized-theta theory determines the divisor class \(r\Theta\), but by itself does not show that the specific curve \(x\mapsto\mathcal O_C((g-1)x)\) meets \(\Theta_E\) properly for the associated bundles occurring in the Wang--Wen moduli problem. Combining their new proper-intersection theorem with the classical theta class and the Abel--Jacobi intersection number produces the exact degree \(r\,g\,(g-1)^2\).

Targeted searches for the initiating source together with the formulas \(r\,g\,(g-1)^2\), \(g(g-1)^2\), “exact exceptional-point divisor,” “generalized theta divisor,” and the map \(x\mapsto\mathcal O_C((g-1)x)\) did not locate an implication-equivalent statement.

## Limitations
The formula is an exact scheme-theoretic length. It does not prove that the bad-point divisor is reduced or that the exceptional points are distinct.

The claim concerns a general principal bundle in the Wang--Wen setup. It does not assert the same proper-intersection statement for every semistable principal bundle.

The argument is over \(\mathbf C\) and uses the classical Jacobian theta polarization. No positive-characteristic analogue is claimed.

Because the initiating preprint is very recent, an equivalent short calculation could exist in material not indexed by the searches used here.
