# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

Two distinct edges cannot share both endpoints. A violating triple therefore has a left-sharing pair and a different right-sharing pair, so it is precisely a three-edge path. The resulting star forest is saturated if it spans, since any missing edge between nontrivial components makes a path. If it has a left isolate, a right isolate would permit an added edge, and every right vertex must be a leaf of a left-centered star of degree at least two; these conditions also suffice. The other orientation is symmetric. Spanning components contribute H(x,y); k spanning components give exactly 2q-k edges. The q!/(q-k)! factor assigns unlabeled leaf blocks to distinct labeled centers, and all nonspanning families have q edges. Two opposite stars and k-2 separate edges realize every size, with both stars regarded as separate edges in the matching case. At the maximum there are two opposite stars of degree q-1 and their centers determine the forest, giving q². This checks q=3 and the full range q>=3, with no quotient by label permutations.
