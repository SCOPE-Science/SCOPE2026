On common-Hessian quadratics, exact FedDyn splits into complementary mean-residual and client-disagreement modes with factors \(\alpha/(\alpha+\mu)\) and \(L/(L+\alpha)\), so their sharp minimax balance is \(\alpha=\sqrt{\mu L}\) with factor \(\sqrt{\kappa}/(1+\sqrt{\kappa})\). For isotropic curvature \(H=aI\), the balanced choice \(\alpha=a\) makes the server reach the exact global minimizer after one round while client disagreement halves each round.
