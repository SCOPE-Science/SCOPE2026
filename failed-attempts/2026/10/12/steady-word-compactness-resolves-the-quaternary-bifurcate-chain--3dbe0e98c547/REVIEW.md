# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

Every factor of a steady word remains steady: deleting a letter from the factor yields a factor of the full word with the corresponding letter deleted, hence remains square-free. Thus steady words over one four-letter alphabet form a prefix-closed finitely branching tree with arbitrarily deep vertices by the source Theorem 1. König’s infinity lemma produces an infinite ray, whose every prefix and factor is steady; Theorem 3 of the source then makes each prefix and factor bifurcate. This establishes the exact chain demanded by Conjecture 2, with no finite-experiment fallacy. Stronger still, the source Theorem 1 proof invokes Dejean’s existing infinite quaternary 7/5-power-avoidance construction and establishes that its finite words are steady; taking the prefixes of that previously existing infinite word directly gives the same chain. Scope is existence, not explicit algorithm or the source’s complete five-letter tree conjecture.

## originality

FAIL

FAIL: The mathematical conclusion is a direct pre-existing implication. Dębski–Grytczuk–Pawlik (2022/2024) already prove in Theorem 1 that the Dejean-avoiding quaternary words are steady, explicitly deriving arbitrary lengths from a classical INFINITE Dejean word. Their Theorem 3 proves each steady word over a fixed >=3-letter alphabet is bifurcate. Prefixes of the classical infinite word are therefore the required infinite bifurcate extension chain. Alternatively their Theorem 1 plus factor heredity and elementary König compactness gives the same conclusion, with no new substantive lemma. Although their paper paradoxically prints the implication as Conjecture 2, the stronger previous construction already mathematically covers it. The text of a conjecture is not proof of a novel consequence, and exact wording need not match.
