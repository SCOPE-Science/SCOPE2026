# The published MCMC LINEX formula has the exponent sign reversed
## Finding
The source gives the theoretical Bayes estimator under LINEX loss as
\[
\widehat g_{BL}
=
-\frac{1}{h}
\log E\!\left[
 e^{-h g(\alpha,\lambda)}\mid x
\right].
\]
This is Eq. (4.5) of the article.

If posterior simulation produces retained draws
\[
(\alpha^{(i)},\lambda^{(i)}),
\qquad i=B+1,\ldots,N,
\]
and
\[
g_i=g(\alpha^{(i)},\lambda^{(i)}),
\]
then the direct Monte Carlo approximation of Eq. (4.5) is
\[
\boxed{
\widehat g_{BL}^{\mathrm{MC}}
=
-\frac{1}{h}
\log\!\left[
\frac{1}{N-B}
\sum_{i=B+1}^{N}e^{-h g_i}
\right].
}
\]

The source's later MCMC Eq. (4.11) instead prints
\[
\widetilde g_{BL}^{\mathrm{print}}
=
-\frac{1}{h}
\log\!\left[
\frac{1}{N-B}
\sum_{i=B+1}^{N}e^{+h g_i}
\right].
\]
The exponent sign is reversed.

This is not an innocuous alternative convention because the prefactor remains
\[
-\frac1h.
\]
For the paper's main simulation choice
\[
h=0.5>0
\]
and any positive target quantity
\[
g_i>0,
\]
one has
\[
e^{h g_i}>1.
\]
Therefore
\[
\frac1{N-B}\sum_{i=B+1}^{N}e^{h g_i}>1,
\]
so
\[
\boxed{
\widetilde g_{BL}^{\mathrm{print}}<0.
}
\]

The article applies LINEX estimation to positive model quantities: the parameters
\[
\alpha>0,
\qquad
\lambda>0,
\]
as well as survival and hazard quantities. Hence the literal Eq. (4.11) cannot produce a valid positive estimate of any of these quantities when
\[
h>0.
\]

The source's own simulation table gives a decisive internal check. It simulates with
\[
\alpha=1.3
\]
and uses
\[
h=0.5.
\]
If Eq. (4.11) were used literally for estimating \(\alpha\), every replicate would satisfy
\[
\widetilde\alpha_{BL}^{\mathrm{print}}<0,
\]
and therefore
\[
(\widetilde\alpha_{BL}^{\mathrm{print}}-1.3)^2>1.3^2=1.69.
\]
Thus the Monte Carlo mean squared error would necessarily exceed
\[
1.69.
\]
For \((n,m,k)=(20,15,1)\), Scheme I, Table 1 instead reports the informative-prior LINEX MSE
\[
0.0032.
\]
That table cannot have been generated by the literal Eq. (4.11).

The correction is therefore
\[
\boxed{
e^{+h g_i}
\longrightarrow
e^{-h g_i}
}
\]
inside Eq. (4.11). With this change, the MCMC estimator is the direct sample analogue of Eq. (4.5).

## Assumptions and scope
The sign conclusion assumes
\[
h>0
\]
and
\[
g_i>0.
\]
These assumptions include the article's main simulation setting
\[
h=0.5
\]
and its positive parameter, survival, and hazard targets.

The result concerns the printed MCMC approximation in Eq. (4.11). It does not infer which expression was used in unavailable software.

The reported small MSEs are evidence against literal implementation of the printed positive exponent; they are not independently recomputed here from the authors' code.

## Proof
Start from the theoretical LINEX Bayes estimator printed earlier in the same article:
\[
\widehat g_{BL}
=
-\frac1h\log E[e^{-h g}\mid x].
\]
Replacing the posterior expectation by the empirical mean over retained posterior draws gives
\[
-\frac1h
\log\!\left[
\frac1{N-B}\sum_{i=B+1}^{N}e^{-h g_i}
\right].
\]
Thus the exponent in a Monte Carlo approximation must retain the minus sign.

Now suppose
\[
h>0
\]
and every retained target value satisfies
\[
g_i>0.
\]
Then every exponential term in the printed Eq. (4.11) satisfies
\[
e^{h g_i}>1.
\]
Its average is therefore greater than one, its logarithm is positive, and multiplication by
\[
-\frac1h
\]
makes the printed estimate negative.

For the article's simulated parameter
\[
\alpha=1.3,
\]
any negative estimate obeys
\[
(\widehat\alpha-1.3)^2>1.69.
\]
A reported Monte Carlo MSE of
\[
0.0032
\]
is therefore incompatible with literal use of Eq. (4.11).

## Verification
The bundled checker evaluates the correct and printed formulas on several positive synthetic posterior draw sequences.

For every tested sequence and every positive LINEX asymmetry parameter, the printed formula is negative while the corrected formula is positive.

The checker also verifies the deterministic MSE lower bound for the article's simulation truth
\[
\alpha=1.3.
\]
Any negative estimate has squared error strictly greater than
\[
1.69,
\]
which is incompatible with the source's reported informative-prior LINEX MSE
\[
0.0032.
\]

The numerical checks supplement the exact sign argument.

## Relationship to prior work
The target article itself states the standard theoretical LINEX estimator correctly in Eq. (4.5). The inconsistency appears only when that posterior expectation is converted to the sample average in Eq. (4.11).

Independent Bayesian-estimation literature likewise writes the LINEX Bayes action in terms of the posterior expectation of a negative exponential. This agrees with Eq. (4.5) and with the corrected Monte Carlo formula.

Focused searches using the target DOI, Gompertz–Lindley and progressive first-failure terms, LINEX terminology, the Eq. (4.11) sign pattern, Monte Carlo approximation language, and correction terminology did not locate a published correction of this source-specific formula.

## Limitations
The finding does not establish which expression was used in the authors' code.

It does not recompute the complete simulation study or real-data Bayesian tables.

For a negative LINEX asymmetry parameter, the simple negativity argument changes sign; the accepted correction is based on exact consistency with Eq. (4.5) and on the article's main positive choice
\[
h=0.5.
\]

A recent or unindexed correction may exist outside the material examined.
