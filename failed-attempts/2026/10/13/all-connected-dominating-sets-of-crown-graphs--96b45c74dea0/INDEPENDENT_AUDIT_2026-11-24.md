## correctness

If exactly one a_i is selected on one shore, domination forces b_i into the selected set, where it is isolated; an empty shore also cannot work. Two selections per shore suffice for domination. If either selected shore has at least three vertices, any two on the opposite shore share a selected neighbor, and all vertices of the larger shore have positive degree, giving connectivity. On two vertices per shore, the induced K_{2,2} loses at most one edge unless the two index pairs are equal, in which case it loses two and is disconnected. The counts follow by subtracting the excluded four-sets from the product. Every larger admissible set contains distinct index pairs, one per shore, so the inclusion-minima are exactly the admissible four-sets. This proof covers n≥3, including n=3.
