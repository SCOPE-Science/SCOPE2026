## correctness

The alternating classification is not well-defined as submitted: P is defined only on composites, but pt−1 and qt−1 can be prime. For k=7 the genuine exact cycle (3,10,5,12) has p=3,q=5,t=1 and P(2) is required. In addition the claimed exhaustive regression misses k=2: (3,5,7,9) is an exact four-cycle, disjoint from the orbit 2→4→2, and 2 is the only starting prime tested there. Das's arXiv Remark 2.8 likewise does not supply the claimed coverage at k=2 as printed. In my direct replay, if x is the minimum state in a cycle of length at most four, x is prime and the first composite is at most x+3k, so its successor is at least x and at most (x+3k)/2. Thus x<=3k and all states are <=6k. Exhaustion over this interval agrees with the classification after extending P to all n>=2, giving three period-three cycles and forty period-four cycles for k=1 through 180, not thirty-nine. The repaired interpretation has a sound symbolic argument: no all-prime or adjacent-composite pattern; for a one-composite cycle the opening prime divides 2k or 3k but not k, forcing 2 or 3; for the alternating pattern equate q(A+1)=p(B+1) for the composite cofactors, then apply the largest-prime-factor conditions to the products. Bertrand gives the stated infinite family. The theorem with the corrected domain is not disproved; both the literal statement and the stated finite coverage need correction.
