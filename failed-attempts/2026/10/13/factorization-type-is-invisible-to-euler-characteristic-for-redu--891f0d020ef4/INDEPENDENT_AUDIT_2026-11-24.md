# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

Multiplication has no base points and takes the product of projective factor spaces to a closed, diagonally invariant image. For B>d the monomial weights have distinct base-B digits, giving only monomial fixed points. Literally divide the d variable occurrences of each monomial into blocks of sizes d_i to see that every one of the binomial(d+m-1,m-1) fixed points is in the image. Localization does not require smoothness. Concretely, stratify by the support of the monomial coordinates. In a support of size at least two, divide by one nonzero coordinate to identify the ambient stratum with a torus. The nonzero vector of weight differences defines a one-dimensional subtorus; an integral torus coordinate change sends its primitive direction to the first coordinate. The corresponding invariant stratum of the image is the product of C* with its slice at first coordinate 1, so it has compactly supported Euler characteristic zero. Singleton supports contribute one each. Additivity and compactness give the ordinary Euler characteristic asserted. Over C the binary case is the entire space. For m>=3 the image is proper: x_0^(d-1)x_1+x_2^d is irreducible by primitivity and degree one in x_1. The conclusion does not concern the exact irreducible-factor stratum.

## originality

FAIL

Exact coverage exists on the all-linear subfamily. For arbitrary partitions, the established projective torus Euler localization theorem already determines the answer once one notes that every monomial admits every degree partition. This is a direct corollary, not evidence that an identical arbitrary-partition statement was printed or that a catalogue date establishes priority.

## value

FAIL

The family and invariant are natural, and the equality for proper loci is a useful observation. But it follows mechanically from torus Euler localization plus the fact that monomials can be split into blocks of any sizes. The calculation detects no new structure of the partition-dependent strata, which it expressly does not address.
