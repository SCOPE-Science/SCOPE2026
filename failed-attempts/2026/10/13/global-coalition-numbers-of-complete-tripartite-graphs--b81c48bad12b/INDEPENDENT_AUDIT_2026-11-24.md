The complement is the union of three cliques, so domination there is exactly meeting every part, which suffices to dominate the original graph as well. For c=1 the unique C-block misses A or B, and every other block both needs it and meets that missing part; hence at most a+1 blocks. For c=2 defect at most one allows at most one nonsingleton, whose support could complement singleton blocks from only one part. The other two parts cannot fit in a single block of size at most two. For c>=3, defect at most two would force a singleton in each part: if three or more vertices of a part P lie entirely in nonsingletons, they either occupy a block of at least three vertices (which consumes the defect and, if pure, still needs a mixed partner), or must lie in two pairs one of which is a pure P-pair with no eligible partner.
