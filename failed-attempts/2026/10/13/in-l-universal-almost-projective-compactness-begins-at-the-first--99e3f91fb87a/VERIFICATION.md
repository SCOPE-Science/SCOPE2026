# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

Calderoni–Yanowitz Theorem 1.1 gives the upper endpoint over arbitrary rings with identity, hence also under the stated ring-size restriction. For each regular uncountable λ below the first weakly compact cardinal, Shelah–Strüngmann Main Theorem 4.1 supplies under V=L an almost-free indecomposable group G of size λ. There is no need to extrapolate continuity from a subcase of their technical construction: enumerate G, and at α<λ take the subgroup generated by the elements indexed below α. Its cardinality is at most max(|α|,ℵ₀)<λ, so almost-freeness makes it free, and this increasing chain is continuous at limits. Calderoni–Yanowitz Proposition 2.7 then makes G a ≤λ-generated λ-projective Z-module. If it were projective it would be free over the PID Z and, since uncountable, would have rank greater than one and decompose, a contradiction. Z has size below λ. These two directions give exactly the stated minimum. No numerical test or formal verification is involved.

## originality

FAIL

The exact conclusion is a direct corollary of prior theorems, not a new compactness or noncompactness result. The prior positive theorem gives the same endpoint with strictly weaker ring hypotheses; the prior almost-free nonfree examples are already at every smaller cardinal in precisely the required range and over precisely Z. The translation consists of the cited filtration equivalence and the definition of almost-freeness. No explicit prior printing of the minimum notation is needed for this coverage finding.

## value

FAIL

It is a useful explanatory observation for a discussion of sharpness, but as a distinct mathematical finding it simply restates the juxtaposition of known sufficiency and the known obstruction family via a standard equivalence. Neither a new boundary case, a new counterexample nor an additional structural implication survives separation from that deduction.
