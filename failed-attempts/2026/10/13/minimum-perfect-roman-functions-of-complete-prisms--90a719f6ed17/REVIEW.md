A matched pair labeled 2 gives every zero exactly one 2-neighbor and has weight 4. At weight at most 4 there is a zero and hence at least one 2. If there is only one 2, its n−1 unmatched vertices in the opposite layer must all be positive, giving weight at least n+1>4. Thus a feasible labeling of weight at most 4 has exactly two 2s and no 1s. In one layer they would share a zero neighbor; in different layers but different columns the vertex matched to the opposite-layer defender would see both. The n matching pairs are therefore the entire optimum. For n=3 the one-defender lower bound is 4, and labeling the two nonneighbors 1 realizes that bound, confirming the stated boundary. The arbitrary-order proof is independent of the recorded finite run.
