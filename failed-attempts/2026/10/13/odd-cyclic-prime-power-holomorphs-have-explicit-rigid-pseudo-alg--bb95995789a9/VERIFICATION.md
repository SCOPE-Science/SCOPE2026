# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

The unit action on characters of the additive cyclic group has one orbit of each order p^k; the stabilizer in the cyclic complement is generated by b^φ(p^k). The explicit exhaustive character parametrization agrees with this little-groups construction. In a nonlinear representation of degree d=φ(p^k) the shift part first forces d|y for a^x b^y to be in the kernel. Comparing adjacent diagonal entries then forces p^k|x because the primitive multiplier r is not 1 mod p. The residual condition is ω^(y/d)=1. If ω has order p^s, the two independent divisibilities give kernel order p^(2(n−k)−s) and codegree p^(k+s). For fixed t=k+s, the nonlinear count telescopes to p^(t−1); linear characters contribute φ(p^t) below the top and nothing at p^n. For e>1 dividing p−1 the ep^s entries are entirely linear. The maximal codegree p^n strictly exceeds the largest possible linear codegree, and its multiplicity p^(n−1) yields p via their ratio and then n. The proof only claims rigidity within this family.

## originality

FAIL

The complete codegree distribution is a direct extraction from the older exhaustive monomial representation table, not just a consequence of its degree multiset. Its entries are read off by testing when a cyclic shift is diagonal, comparing two diagonal entries, and reading the order of the scalar in the wrap-around slot. Grouping by k+s is the elementary totient sum. The rigidity assertion is immediate from the resulting maximal entry and adds no distinct group-theoretic input. This is mechanical prior implication, not evidence of a literally printed earlier codegree table.

## value

FAIL

The family and invariant are natural, and the aggregation is useful reference arithmetic, but there is no independently unknown kernel or multiplicity left to determine once the full earlier monomial table is used. Recovery of p,n merely decodes its top order and multiplicity and does not distinguish the family from other groups.
