# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

In the finite poset representation H = Up(P), the pseudocomplement of U is P minus the down-closure of U. Thus excluded middle holds exactly when U is both an upset and a downset, i.e. a union of comparability components. With r components and n_i upsets on component i, there are 2^r excluded-middle elements out of product n_i. The nontriviality assumption ensures r ≥ 1 and n_i ≥ 2; disjoint unions of chains on n_i−1 points realize all lists of n_i. If product n_i = 2^s q and the reduced numerator is 2^a > 1, then a=r−s and Ω(q)+s≥r, since each factor contributes at least one prime. Conversely partition the odd denominator's prime factors into a nonempty groups to construct the requisite chain sizes. One chain of size 2q realizes every 1/q, including 1. This proves the claimed spectrum and verifies the positive and negative examples without a finite extrapolation.

## originality

FAIL

The spectrum follows directly from previously published trivial-center factorization, the product rule and the Boolean-center description, with chains supplying every factor size. The reduced fraction condition is equivalent arithmetic on that same spectrum, not a different structural result. A later-dated Resultary joint-spectrum result also covers the product spectrum by projection, but is not evidence of earlier priority. The rejection rests on the implication from the published Bumpus–Kocsis results, not on a keyword miss or the same-day partial result.

The set of attainable probabilities is a natural question, and the fraction test is convenient. In this record, however, the complete product description is a routine specialization of the known decomposition and degree product rule; the alternative test is prime counting and regrouping of those very factors. The record does not supply a distinct unresolved classification or nonroutine constraint beyond that existing structure.
