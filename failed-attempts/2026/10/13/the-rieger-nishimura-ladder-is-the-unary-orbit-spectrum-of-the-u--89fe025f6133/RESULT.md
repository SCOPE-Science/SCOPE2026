# The Rieger–Nishimura ladder is the unary orbit spectrum of the universal finite-Heyting Fraïssé limit

## Finding

Let \(L\) be the Fraïssé limit of the class of finite nontrivial Heyting algebras in the language \(\{0,1,\wedge,\vee,\to}\). For \(a\in L\), write \(\langle a\rangle\) for the Heyting subalgebra generated by \(a\), with the named constants included.

The point-orbits of \(\operatorname{Aut}(L)\), graded by generated-subalgebra size, satisfy
\[
c_2=2,\qquad c_n=1\quad(n\ge 3).
\]
Hence
\[
\sum_{n\ge2}c_n z^n=2z^2+\frac{z^3}{1-z},
\]
and \(\operatorname{Aut}(L)\) has exactly countably infinitely many unary orbits.

## Assumptions and scope

The result concerns the countable Fraïssé limit \(L\) of finite nontrivial Heyting algebras under embeddings. Heyting algebras are taken in the standard language with named \(0\) and \(1\). The grading parameter is the cardinality of the finitely generated subalgebra \(\langle a\rangle\), not the size of an arbitrary finite ambient subalgebra.

The classification input is the classical Rieger–Nishimura theory of one-generated Heyting algebras, in the finite form recorded by Citkin: for every integer \(n\ge2\) there is, up to isomorphism, a unique one-generated Heyting algebra \(Z_n\) of cardinality \(n\). The generator structure is also explicit: \(Z_2\), \(Z_4\), and \(Z_5\) each have two generators; the two generators of \(Z_4\) and \(Z_5\) are exchanged by an automorphism, whereas the two generators of \(Z_2\) are not; every other finite one-generated Heyting algebra has a unique generator.

## Proof

Yamamoto records that \(L\) is locally finite and ultrahomogeneous. Therefore, for each \(a\in L\), the generated subalgebra \(\langle a\rangle\) is a finite nontrivial one-generated Heyting algebra. Conversely, every finite one-generated Heyting algebra belongs to the age of \(L\), so it embeds into \(L\).

Two elements \(a,b\in L\) lie in the same \(\operatorname{Aut}(L)\)-orbit exactly when the pointed algebras \((\langle a\rangle,a)\) and \((\langle b\rangle,b)\) are isomorphic. The forward implication is immediate. For the reverse implication, a pointed isomorphism between the finite generated subalgebras extends to an automorphism of \(L\) by ultrahomogeneity.

Thus unary orbits are exactly pointed isomorphism classes of finite one-generated Heyting algebras. The Rieger–Nishimura classification gives one underlying algebra \(Z_n\) for every \(n\ge2\). For \(n=2\), the two generators are the named constants \(0\) and \(1\); no automorphism can interchange them, so there are exactly two pointed isomorphism classes. For \(n=4\) and \(n=5\), the two generators are interchanged by an automorphism, so each cardinality contributes one pointed class. For every other \(n\ge3\), the generator is unique, so again there is one pointed class.

This proves \(c_2=2\) and \(c_n=1\) for all \(n\ge3\). Summing the grading gives
\[
2z^2+\sum_{n\ge3}z^n=2z^2+\frac{z^3}{1-z}.
\]
Since the grading has one nonempty orbit class in every cardinality \(n\ge3\), while \(L\) itself is countable, the total number of point-orbits is exactly \(\aleph_0\).

## Verification

The accompanying script `verify_unary_orbits.py` constructs the standard finite Rieger–Nishimura algebras \(Z_n\) recursively by the two-point expansion used in the one-generated classification. It derives the lattice and Heyting implication tables from the finite order, closes each element under \(0,1,\wedge,\vee,\to\), and checks the generator counts through \(n=20\). It also brute-force enumerates automorphisms through \(n=8\), confirming two distinct generator orbits for \(Z_2\), one generator orbit for \(Z_4\) and \(Z_5\), and one generator orbit for every checked \(Z_n\) with \(n\ge3\).

The computation is a finite consistency check, not the proof of the all-\(n\) classification. The infinite statement relies on the cited one-generated Heyting-algebra classification and ultrahomogeneity of \(L\).

## Relationship to prior work

Yamamoto studies the automorphism group of \(L\), proves in particular that it is not Roelcke precompact, and notes that \(L\) is not \(\omega\)-categorical. His proof of non-Roelcke-precompactness deliberately constructs infinitely many two-generated configurations while keeping the relevant one-generated subalgebras fixed. The inspected paper does not state the exact unary orbit spectrum above.

The Rieger–Nishimura classification and Citkin's generator analysis supply the finite pointed one-generated algebras, but do not identify them with the point-orbits of the Fraïssé limit. The present observation is the exact bridge between those two pieces: the classical one-variable ladder is precisely the unary orbit grading of the universal homogeneous finite-Heyting limit.

A semantic-search comparison also found a result giving exact tuple-orbit counts for the random distributive lattice. That result concerns a different Fraïssé limit and a locally finite free distributive algebra; it does not imply the Heyting-algebra profile here, where the free one-generated Heyting algebra is infinite and the finite quotients form the Rieger–Nishimura ladder.

## Limitations

This finding classifies unary automorphism orbits only. It does not give the number of orbits on pairs or higher tuples, nor the complete spaces of possibly non-realized first-order types. The grading is by the size of the generated finite Heyting subalgebra. The originality assessment is limited by the possibility that the orbit interpretation appears in uncatalogued lecture notes or folklore; targeted searches located the two ingredients separately but not this exact statement.
