# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

Under finite duality a Heyting algebra is an upset lattice. The complement of an upset is an upset exactly when no comparability crosses its boundary, i.e. when it is a union of comparability components. Hence the degree is the product of 2/u(Q) over comparability components Q, where u(Q) counts upsets. Singleton components contribute 1. A component with m points has at least m+1 upsets by suffixes of a linear extension; a nonchain admits different linear extensions and has an extra upset. Thus any two nonsingleton components give at most 4/9. If there is one, it gives 2/u(Q), where u(Q)=3 occurs exactly for a two-chain and u(Q)=4 exactly for a three-chain. This proves the excluded cases, sharp second gap, both directions of equality and, by size 4·2^k, uniqueness of k. An independent forward-relation enumeration with relabelling and exact ideal-complement tests reproduced all 4473 labelled posets through five points and 0,0,6,24,60 second-rung cases. The symbolic argument is not bounded by five points.

## originality

FAIL

The second bound is not stated verbatim in the published paper, but it is mechanically implied by its explicit center-trivial product decomposition and factor-size proof, already expanded with the same 4/9 estimate in the distinct indexed two-thirds equality record. In Bumpus–Kocsis, Theorem 6.1's proof explicitly uses the factorization obtained by iterating Theorem 2.2, and the exact factor degree 2/|H_i|. Applying their published product rule, every algebra of degree at least 1/2 has at most one non-Boolean factor, since two give at most (2/3)^2. The sole non-Boolean factor then has at most four elements. Its only center-trivial possibilities at sizes three and four are C3 and C4, since the only other bounded lattice of size four is the Boolean square. This gives both the claimed ceiling and rigidity by inspection of the already published factor-size criterion. The indexed October 13 result also expressly gives 4/9 and 2/|H_i|; its shared date does not decide priority and is not needed for the implication from the earlier journal proof.

## value

FAIL

The next extremum is a natural question, but the assigned theorem takes only the next factor size in an already explicit product decomposition and inspects the two bounded lattices of size four. The finite verification is sound but does not supply a distinct structural advance beyond that deduction, and no harder remaining spectrum claim is made.
