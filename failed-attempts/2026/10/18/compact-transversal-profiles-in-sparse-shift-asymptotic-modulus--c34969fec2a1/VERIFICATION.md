# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

The n-th modulus root acts diagonally by exponentiating minus the spike mass in [k,k+n−1] divided by n. Relative to k=1, only a fixed initial contribution is removed and a window of k−1 sites after n is added. For n between p_j and p_(j+1), the first-coordinate exponent is at most b+o(1) by lacunarity. Along any convergent subsequence, extract one with the next-spike distance either tending to infinity (all fixed coordinates agree in the limit) or constant r (earlier mass is o(n), and only k≥r+1 sees the next spike). Compactness of K gives the parameter of the latter limit. Conversely n=p_j−r produces each step; along α_j→b, n=floor(α_j(p_j−1)/t) for 0<t<b gives e^(−t)I, n=p_j gives e^(−b)I, and the geometric midpoint before the next spike gives I. Bounded coordinate convergence is strong and the diagonal sequence is sequentially precompact in coordinates, giving the scalar cluster interval for every unit vector. A threshold strictly between e^(−min K) and 1 separates each fiber by the jump at r, and the tail projection bound gives uniform-in-α strong accumulation at I only. The example with singleton K has isolated step points; the interval example has no isolated points. Positivity, vanishing spike weights, and arbitrarily long all-one blocks give injectivity, failure to be bounded below, and all power norms equal to 1. No finite test is needed for the subsequence classification.

## originality

FAIL

A distinct indexed result, Compact-set universality for cluster limits of sparse weighted-shift modulus roots, gives the same arbitrary-compact-K operator and scalar cluster sets and the same clopen copies. Its spike sites are shifted by one, with identical masses. This is demonstrated coverage of the mathematical contribution, not a finding about who obtained it first: the two paths bear different dates, which alone do not establish priority. Ji–Yang is an earlier precursor but is not the reason for the full-coverage decision.

## value

FAIL

The underlying universality theorem is mathematically meaningful: it separates scalar asymptotic data from operator-limit topology. But the submitted finding gives no distinct unknown realization or invariant beyond the exact equivalent universality statement, and the one-site translation supplies no new regime or improvement.
