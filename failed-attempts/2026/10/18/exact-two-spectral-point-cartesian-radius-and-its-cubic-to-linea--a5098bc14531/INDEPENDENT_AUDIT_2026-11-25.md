# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

Normal functional calculus makes the integrand the largest squared projection of a spectral point. For two points v,w in the plane, assume a≥b and diagonalize vvᵀ−wwᵀ; its eigenvalues are h+s and h−s, with s=√(h²+c²). Relative to the larger-point mean a/2, the increment is the mean negative part of this quadratic form on the circle. In each of the two negative sectors the half-width α satisfies 2α=arctan(c/h) (π/2 if h=0<c). Integrating on the four half-sectors gives 2/π times −hα+(s/2)sin(2α), hence [c−h arctan(c/h)]/π. The c=0 correction vanishes, including the all-zero corner. Using only two spectral points gives a lower bound, exact for a spectrum contained in those points. Differentiating the correction gives c²/(h²+c²) for unequal radii; at h=0 it is linear. Its expansion c³/(3h²)+O_h(c⁵), with c=Rr|sin φ|, gives the stated fixed-modulus coefficient and |φ|⁵ remainder.

## originality

FAIL

The full two-parameter correction is mechanically implied by a published absolute moment for products of centered correlated Gaussians, after changing from angular to Gaussian averaging. The exact angular extrema, equal-modulus law and fixed-modulus cubic coefficient then require only differentiation or Taylor expansion. This is affirmative coverage, not an inference from missing literature or catalogue dates.

## value

FAIL

The two-spectral-point problem is natural and the formula useful, but the frozen contribution is an exact invariant already obtainable by direct substitution into the published product moment. The phase limits are routine consequences of that same formula. It does not establish a distinct non-mechanical exact fact or structural extension beyond that evaluation.

The dated certificate retains the supplied scientific assessment, sources and limitations.
