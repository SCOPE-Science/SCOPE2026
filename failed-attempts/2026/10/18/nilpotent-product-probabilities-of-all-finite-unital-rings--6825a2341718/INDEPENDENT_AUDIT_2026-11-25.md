## correctness

PASS

The unique Fitting decomposition counts all linear maps on a k-space by dimension j of their nilpotent summand, giving q^(k²)/|GL_k|=sum_{j=0}^k N_j/|GL_j|. Consecutive subtraction gives N_k=q^(k²-k), including N_0=1. Write fixed rank-r A=P diag(I_r,0)Q and conjugate AB by P. Its upper r-square block is uniform as B varies, and the product is nilpotent exactly when that block is. Thus each fixed A has q^(n²-r) successful B. Averaging q^(-rank A)=q^(-n)|ker A| by summing the individual zero-vector probabilities gives q^(-n)(2-q^(-n)). The kernel of the surjection M_m(R)→M_m(R/J) is nilpotent: a power of a matrix in M_m(J) has entries in the corresponding power of J. An element whose image is nilpotent therefore has a power in a nilpotent ideal, and is itself nilpotent. Equal-sized quotient fibers push the sampling forward without bias, and the independent Wedderburn factors give the product. For m=1 each factor f(Q)=(2Q-1)/Q² is decreasing for Q≥2 and strictly below one, so 3/4 is attained only for a single F2 factor. The example has basis 1,x,y,xy, radical spanned by x,y,xy with cube zero, and xy≠yx.
