## correctness

PASS

The associative ring identity T_{-1}(f)T_2(g) gives precisely the quandle product. Augmentation of an idempotent is idempotent; over any commutative base the corresponding idempotent splits the two branches, so it need not be globally 0 or 1 in a disconnected base. On the zero branch the equation doubles the augmentation-ideal order until the element vanishes. On the one branch J^p=0 makes the truncated exponential and logarithm inverse group maps: all surviving terms have total degree less than p and denominators invertible in the base. Thus the equations on the coefficients are diagonal with either a unit or zero multiplier. The free exponents are precisely the odd j with 2^{j-1}=1, up to p-1, giving the stated dimension and reduced affine component even on testing nonreduced bases. On this kernel the operators are -I and 2I, which gives the claimed law.
