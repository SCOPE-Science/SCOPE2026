# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

A normal in v_i^⊥ avoiding the other generator orthogonality hyperplanes exposes exactly a translate of [0,v_i]; every such edge is obtained from its relative-interior normal cone. Distinct sign chambers cannot expose the same edge: using the opposite endpoint of even one nonorthogonal generator strictly loses its support contribution. For fixed n, outside finitely many vertex offsets the vertices of the section are exactly transverse intersections with edges (a point in the relative interior of a higher-dimensional face cannot be an isolated extreme point of the section). Each edge contributes its projected interval length; edges perpendicular to n contribute zero, so no genericity in n is needed. Dividing the finite sum by the Minkowski-sum support width proves the formula. If the ratio is constant c, crossing only the i-th generator orthogonality hyperplane forces a slope jump of 2(r_i−c)||v_i||² to vanish. Independent generator d-subsets make the restrictions to v_i^⊥ a general-position central arrangement, with m−1 hyperplanes in dimension d−1 and therefore the stated region count. In d=3 the number of distinct restricted lines equals the number of distinct generator pair planes and each line contributes two sectors. Independently normalized the pairwise cross products in the three examples, obtaining zone counts (6,6,6,6), (8,8,8,8,8,8), and (4,4,4,6). In the balanced example the two other generators in the same plane together contribute one common span; three more generators give three separate mixed spans. This clarifies the wording in the example without changing any result.

Their Example 6.2 already includes the balanced six-generator arrangement presented here. The generic arrangement count is a classical direct corollary and does not leave a distinct result of the frozen scope. This is demonstrated prior coverage, not a failed search or uncertain attribution.
