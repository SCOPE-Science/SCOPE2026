# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

At fixed mass, summing over past periods gives the periodic susceptible trajectory with denominator 1−exp(−B−δT). Its integrated exponential of arc masses is strictly convex: if two controls agree on almost all arcs then the right-continuous cumulative function of their zero-total difference is almost everywhere constant, hence the controls agree. This covers singular controls as well as pulses. The integrated response H to an added atom agrees from both sides at the candidate pulse; redistributing mass has derivative equal to the integral of H(0)−H(s). Therefore the candidate is optimal precisely when H is maximized there. On each constant-transmission piece, H' has the form δC exp(δs)+βA exp(−δs) with A>0. A nonpositive left derivative forces strict subsequent decrease in the high season, and a nonnegative right derivative forces strict preceding increase in the low season. Reversing either derivative sign creates an improving two-atom transfer. Substituting the endpoint values yields exp(−B)≥max(α/β_H,β_L/α), with equality included. Integrating the single-pulse trajectory gives the stated J, and dividing by T(γ+μ) gives the scalar invasion normalization. An independent affine period-map check gave the cutoff 0.390979957597113 and, at B=0.5, a strict decrease of about 9.86×10^−9 on transferring 10^−4 to time 0.001.

The central finding has verified equivalent existing coverage in a distinct indexed publication: the same all-measure model, the same exact threshold including equality, the same uniqueness, and the same improving mass-transfer mechanism. The comparison is a statement-level coverage finding, not a priority judgment: the matching record is dated later in the index, and this review does not infer who first derived the result or that it was known at the frozen record's earlier date. The older papers supply the model, general calculus and numerical transition, but not the exact cutoff in the passages inspected.
