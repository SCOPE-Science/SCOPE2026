# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

The diagonal unit group acts on 1+J, the additive arrow space, by endpoint ratios. For a diagonal of order dividing d, powering an element over it by d projects its radical coordinate onto the equal-label subspace, up to multiplication by d, which is invertible. Unequal-label coordinates are freely translated by radical conjugation; hence the prime-to-p roots for each such diagonal form one class of the claimed size, with no mixing between diagonals. Powering by pd kills the whole radical contribution. Expanding the product of edge factors gives P_H and divisor inversion gives both exact-order counts. For each d infinitely many field sizes sample P_H(d,z), and varying d determines P_H(t,z). The top z degree is m by existence of a proper coloring with sufficiently many labels; the p-root count then recovers h. Under the displayed substitution the resulting component polynomial gives the standard Tutte rank-nullity sum. P_4 and K_{1,3} with no loops give nonisomorphic examples both with P_H(t,z)=t(1+(t−1)z)^3 and identical order profiles. Independent arithmetic checks reproduce both frozen order-count examples. The conjugacy assertion is only about u^d=1, not u^(pd)=1.

## originality

FAIL

The distinct indexed complete-power-map result covers every target and positive exponent for precisely this quiver family over every finite field. Its identity specialization includes both submitted torsion counts, the exact-order inversion and the same Fortuin–Kasteleyn expansion. Its all-field polynomial data give the proposed Tutte recovery by the direct interpolation and classical substitution spelled out in the review. The separate same-date full conjugacy spectrum gives the canonical radical orbits whose zero equal-label representative specializes to the submitted d-torsion class refinement. These are mathematical coverage findings, not claims of chronological priority or dependence. The inspected papers do not by themselves establish earlier priority for the order-profile theorem.

## value

PASS

An order-distribution law for a whole family of finite groups and its inverse graph invariant are natural structural results. Their usefulness lies in specifying what the coarse element-order statistic forgets and what it retains, not in a new expression for a single finite test case. The class refinement explains why the field-size weights occur. This assessment does not credit it as distinct from the broader indexed results.
