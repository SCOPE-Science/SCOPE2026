## correctness

PASS

Words of degree at least four vanish, so scalars and cubics are central. Write the remaining parts as u in degree one and Q in degree two. If u is nonzero, a linear change of generators makes it x. On the quadratic words the kernel of [x,-] is k x^2: the three images of xy, yx and y^2 are independent by their x^2y, yx^2 and xy^2 coefficients, including in characteristic two. On linear words the degree-two component has rank one, with kernel kx, and the degree-three image of that kernel is [Q,x], already in the quadratic-input image. This gives rank four. If u=0, only the map on the two linear inputs survives. For Q nonzero it cannot vanish on both, since the quadratic centralizers of x and y intersect trivially. It has rank one precisely when it has a nonzero kernel vector l, in which case the same word calculation in a basis starting at l forces Q to be a nonzero scalar multiple of l^2. For Q with coordinates (alpha,beta,gamma,delta), this locus with its vertex included is beta=gamma and alpha delta=beta^2; on alpha nonzero take l=x+(beta/alpha)y, and when alpha=0 the equation forces beta=0 and the nonzero case is a multiple of y^2. Over the algebraic closure the resulting hypersurface is the irreducible Veronese cone; its gradient vanishes on the hypersurface only at the vertex even in characteristic two (alpha=delta=0 then forces beta=0). The tangent dimension at the vertex is three, greater than the cone dimension two. Product with the nine free central coordinates gives the stated singular locus; the rank-at-most-two locus is the linear subspace u=0 of dimension thirteen. Over F_q the punctured cone has (q+1)(q-1) points, and the remaining quadratic coefficients and free central coordinates give the four indicated multiplicities. Weighting those multiplicities by q^(-r) and dividing by q^15 gives 2q^(-4)+q^(-5)-q^(-6)-q^(-7), the stated probability.
