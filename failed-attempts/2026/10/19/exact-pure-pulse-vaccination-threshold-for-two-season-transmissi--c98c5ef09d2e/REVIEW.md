# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

The explicit inclusive theorem in the Finding is correct for fixed log-dose mass q and jump multiplier exp(-q). The opening prose 'below' and 'At that threshold there is a sharp loss' is misleading: if read strictly it is incorrect, since equality remains uniquely optimal. For fixed q the periodic susceptible integral has a constant denominator and a strictly convex exponential in the measure of each arc. If two equal-mass measures differ, their periodic signed cumulative difference is nonconstant almost everywhere and their arc masses differ on a positive-measure set of phase pairs; hence strict convexity holds even for singular measures. Differentiating ordered past-future kernels yields the marginal G=S v; at a dose atom the forward and backward jump factors give the same product on both sides. The directional derivative for a same-mass competitor nu is q G(0)-integral G dnu. At the pure pulse, z=exp(-q), and the endpoint inequalities are z>=B/[H(1-r)] and z>=L(1-r)/B. Since L<B/(1-r)<H, both budget cutoffs are positive. The high-season adjoint is strictly below H/delta, giving G''<0 there. On the low season G' is a sum of a nonnegative decaying exponential and a signed growing one; when the growing coefficient is negative G' decreases, so its nonnegative terminal value suffices. Thus both weak inequalities give the global maximum at zero including equality. Violating either inequality gives a beneficial same-mass transfer just to the corresponding side. The zero-budget case has only one feasible measure. Independent evaluation of the formulas gives 0.390979957597113 and 1.554930191458201 for the motivating example; these are numerical checks only.
