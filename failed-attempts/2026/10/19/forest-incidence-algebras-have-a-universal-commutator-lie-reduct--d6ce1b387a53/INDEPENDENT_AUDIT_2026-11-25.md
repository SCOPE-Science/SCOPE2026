# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

The absence of three-element chains makes the arrow radical square zero. With one oriented difference d_u−d_v per edge, the diagonal-to-edge map is surjective by solving along the unique tree paths, and its kernel is exactly the component constants. In these coordinates the bracket on each edge is the alternating determinant of its two coordinates. Hence the direct sum, including characteristic two, and the additivity of common-centralizer codimension follow. For a single edge the possible ranks of an s×2 matrix have counts 1, (q^s−1)(q+1), (q^s−1)(q^s−q), from the zero matrix, the choice of a column line and nonzero pair on it, and the choice of two independent columns. Tuple pairwise commutativity means that every edge block has rank at most one. The star and alternating path have respectively left/right radical-annihilator dimensions (2m,m+1) and two dimensions at most 2m−1 for m≥3, excluding both isomorphism and anti-isomorphism. The proof’s annihilator formula stated for any connected height-one incidence algebra needs a nontrivial connected component (the singleton has radical zero), but its only application is to the stated m≥3 trees. A separate calculation in the original incidence coordinates for both seven-dimensional F₂ examples returned multiplicities (4,108,1044,4212,6264,3888,864) and 4000 commuting pairs out of 16384.

## originality

FAIL

The principal Lie classification is covered in the complex type-A formulation by the combination of the prior tree/Frobenius criterion and the direct-sum theorem for two-step solvable Frobenius Lie poset algebras. More decisively for the full characteristic scope, the earlier radical-square-zero adjoint-spectrum record already gives the very same coordinate bracket and the independence of oriented incidence rows of a forest over every field. The frozen Lie form is obtained directly by taking those independent rows as coordinate functionals; the all-arity polynomial is then the standard rank distribution of an arbitrary s×2 matrix on each known factor. A broader exact quiver theorem also gives the pairwise-commuting count directly. The frozen package makes these conclusions explicit but does not identify a non-mechanical new part of the forest classification.

## value

FAIL

Joint-centralizer distributions are natural, but for this forest slice the frozen spectrum and nonuniqueness claim are immediate consequences of the available direct-sum structure, elementary rank counts, and standard radical data. The frozen result adds no independent structural distinction or non-mechanical invariant beyond the established edge model. It would be useful as an exposition or as a baseline for a genuinely new result on dependent edges.
