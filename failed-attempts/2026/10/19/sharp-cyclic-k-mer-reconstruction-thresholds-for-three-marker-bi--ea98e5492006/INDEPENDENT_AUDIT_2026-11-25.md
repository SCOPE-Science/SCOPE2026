A length-k deck determines all shorter decks by prefix projection and counts global weight because every one contributes to exactly k reads. At n-c a read covers all three ones, leaving no other placement. With distinct gaps the reverse is not a rotation; below n-c there are only zero-, one-, and two-marker reads. For one-marker reads, either the smaller tail fits every gap or both tails can use exactly the two larger gaps, giving one eligible ordered adjacency in the latter case. For two-marker reads the total exterior tail fits even the smaller adjacent gap, so reversal preserves their counts. For repeated gaps, short decks with minimum gap at least k-1 factor into three occurrences of each one-marker pattern plus all-zero reads. This proves the equal-gap and two-small-gap collisions, with the fixed gap sum or projected short-gap counts giving uniqueness at the next length. For a<b=c, projection at b+1 forces the only short gap and hence both remaining gaps. At b the alternative (a,b-1,b+1) admits the same two-marker patterns (only across a), and one-marker counts are 1 plus the two indicators that the two respective tails fit a, even when a=b-1. The all-zero count follows by subtraction. For k=1 there is no shorter admissible length, including the all-one cycle.
