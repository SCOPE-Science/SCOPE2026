## correctness

PASS

Exactly triangular projected silhouettes satisfy sum_i|u_i|>2max_i|u_i|. The spherical normal locus consists of eight symmetry copies of a triangle; integrating the cap centered at (1,1,1)/sqrt3 and subtracting three nonoverlapping spherical side segments gives the proposed marked area tail on (2sqrt2,2sqrt3) and cap transition s*=4sqrt(2/3). Tangency yields a (s*-s)^(3/2) defect. Independently differentiated the source elementary marked tail at six s values, reproducing the density 6/(pi sqrt3)Theta_Sigma(s/8) already printed by Abas (2026); geometry and normalization agree. This finite derivative check supports, not alone proves, the continuous expression.

## originality

FAIL

FAIL due decisive full-primary-text coverage, not inaccessible literature. Omri Abas, Random Width and Brightness (arXiv:2609.26202, Aug 2026), §5.2 Fig.2 identifies TWO tetrahedral brightness chamber families F_max and F_Sigma split by x=y+z, with F_Sigma exactly the triangular-silhouette normal cone. Lemma 5.3, Eq.(16) gives the COMPLETE angular-aperture Theta_Sigma(t) piecewise on [tau0,tau2], and §5.4 Eq.(17) explicitly supplies its separate probability-density contribution f_{B_T,F_Sigma}(t)=48/(pi sqrt3)Theta_Sigma(t) for unit-edge tetrahedra, not merely the unmarked total density. The submitted tetrahedron edge=2sqrt2 scales area by 8; therefore the complete submitted marked law is precisely the upper-tail integral of (1/8)f_{B_T,F_Sigma}(s/8)=6/(pi sqrt3)Theta_Sigma(s/8). The support breakpoints scale to 2sqrt2, 4sqrt(2/3), 2sqrt3, exactly those submitted; the cusp follows from the explicit existing aperture. Thus the claimed conditional distribution, even if not printed as integrated elementary CDF, is a direct deterministic integration of an already exact separated marked density. This prior covering implication decisively fails originality; an earlier source abstract had been misread as giving unmarked only.

## value

FAIL

FAIL under the independent-contribution standard. Conditional tetrahedral brightness is naturally interesting, and the formula is correct, but its complete marked density and branch change already appear in prior §5 and Eq.(16)–(17); the new statement consists of an elementary one-variable antiderivative with the same support and cusp, with no new independent structural classification or unknown invariant. The prior paper even provides density antiderivatives in Appendix B. Reexpressing the same law as a CDF and adding numerical checks is useful exposition, not a new mathematical fact.
