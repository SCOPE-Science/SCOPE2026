# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

Polar integration gives the coefficient 2/[m(m+1)], so the energy is 2N plus the potential summed over ordered distinct pairs. In the Poisson integral the denominator is maximized at the antipode, giving the exact floor c*=2 log 2−1, strict away from that angle. Each family of cyclic arcs of step r has total 2πr, so the linear Taylor terms cancel. The ordered-pair sum of squared displacement differences is twice N times the centered sum of squares. These checks give the full nonnegative remainder and the stated normalization. Near a collision the curvature is logarithmically bounded, by writing the denominator as (1−s)^2+2s(1−cos θ), so the endpoint remainder is a convergent improper integral. For any displaced arc the remainder is positive; equality therefore requires all consecutive gaps equal. The N=2 antipodal perturbation has normalized deficit tending to c*, proving uniform sharpness.

The global law is correct and not expressly stated in the closest source, but is mechanically implied by its explicit global pair potential and Poisson curvature, not merely by its local theorem. Minimizing the integrand in angle gives the sharp scalar modulus immediately; standard strong Jensen or Taylor applied to each balanced arc family gives the bound and the ordinary integral remainder. The prior N=2 Hessian formula also gives the matching limiting coefficient. The collision extension is continuity with the integrable endpoint singularity. No independent quantitative remainder survives the prior implication check.
