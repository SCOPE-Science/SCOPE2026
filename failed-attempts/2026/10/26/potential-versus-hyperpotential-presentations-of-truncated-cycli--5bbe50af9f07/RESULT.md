# Potential versus hyperpotential presentations of truncated cyclic Nakayama algebras

## Finding

Let \(K\) be a field, \(n\ge 3\), and \(L\ge 2\). Let \(Q_n\) be the oriented cycle with vertices \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\) and arrows \(a_i:i\to i+1\). Write \(\widehat{KQ_n}=K\langle\!\langle Q_n\rangle\!\rangle\) for the completed path algebra, \(\mathfrak m\) for its arrow ideal, and
\[
A_{n,L}=\widehat{KQ_n}/\mathfrak m^L.
\]
The defining ideal \(\mathfrak m^L\) is generated by cyclic derivatives of a potential on this fixed quiver if and only if
\[
n\mid (L+1)\quad\text{and}\quad \bigl((L+1)/n\bigr)1_K\ne 0.
\]
It is generated by a reduced hyperpotential if and only if \(n\mid (L+1)\). Consequently, in characteristic \(p>0\), the canonical truncated-cycle presentation is hyperpotential but not potential exactly when \(L+1=nr\) for an integer \(r\) divisible by \(p\).

## Assumptions and scope

The statement concerns equality of defining ideals in the completed path algebra of the fixed oriented cycle, not arbitrary algebra isomorphisms or presentations on other quivers. Paths are multiplied in traversal order, from left to right. The arrow ideal power \(\mathfrak m^L\) is the closed ideal generated by all paths of length \(L\); hence \(A_{n,L}\) is finite-dimensional, with paths of length less than \(L\) as a basis. A potential is taken up to cyclic equivalence, and a hyperpotential is a family \((\rho_i)\) of endpoint-compatible relations satisfying the usual identity \(\sum_i[a_i,\rho_i]=0\). Since \(n\ge3\) and \(L\ge2\), the constructed potential and hyperpotential are reduced.

## Proof

Every oriented cycle in \(Q_n\) has length divisible by \(n\). There is exactly one cyclic-equivalence class of cycles of any fixed length \(nr\), represented by \(c^r\), where \(c=a_0a_1\cdots a_{n-1}\) is one traversal of the cycle. Let \(p_i\) denote the unique path of length \(L\) beginning at \(i+1\) and ending at \(i\), when such a path has the required endpoints.

**Potential direction.** If the Jacobian ideal equals \(\mathfrak m^L\), every cyclic derivative must lie in \(\mathfrak m^L\), and their degree-\(L\) terms must span \(\mathfrak m^L/\mathfrak m^{L+1}\). A degree-\(L\) term in a cyclic derivative can only come from the degree-\((L+1)\) component of the potential. Such a component exists only if \(n\mid L+1\). Write \(L+1=nr\). Up to cyclic equivalence, that component is a scalar multiple of \(c^r\), and
\[
\partial_{a_i}(c^r)=r p_i
\]
for every arrow \(a_i\). Thus its degree-\(L\) derivative is zero if \(r1_K=0\), and in that case no higher-degree term can generate the nonzero quotient \(\mathfrak m^L/\mathfrak m^{L+1}\). This proves necessity.

Conversely, if \(L+1=nr\) and \(r1_K\ne0\), take \(W=c^r\). Its cyclic derivatives are the nonzero scalar multiples \(r p_i\). The paths \(p_i\), one for each starting vertex, are exactly all paths of length \(L\), so the closed ideal they generate is \(\mathfrak m^L\). This proves the potential criterion.

**Hyperpotential direction.** A hyperpotential relation attached to \(a_i\) is a formal sum of paths from \(i+1\) back to \(i\). Every such path length is congruent to \(n-1\) modulo \(n\). If its generated ideal is \(\mathfrak m^L\), its relations must have degree-\(L\) components spanning \(\mathfrak m^L/\mathfrak m^{L+1}\); therefore \(L\equiv n-1\pmod n\), or \(n\mid L+1\).

For sufficiency, suppose \(L+1=nr\), and set \(\rho_i=p_i\). Then \(\rho_i\) has the required endpoints and the family generates \(\mathfrak m^L\). Moreover, \(a_i p_i\) and \(p_i a_i\) are the length-\((L+1)\) cycles based at adjacent vertices. As \(i\) ranges over all vertices, both sums contain each vertex-based cycle once, so \(\sum_i[a_i,p_i]=0\). Thus \((p_i)_i\) is a reduced hyperpotential. The hyperpotential criterion follows.

## Verification

The proof uses the path-length grading and the explicit cyclic-derivative and commutator identities above; it does not depend on finite enumeration or numerical experiments. The boundary example \(n=3\), \(L=5\), \(\operatorname{char}K=2\) is recovered: \(L+1=6=3\cdot2\), so the length-five relations form a hyperpotential but cannot be the cyclic derivatives of a potential because the coefficient \(2\) vanishes. No claim is made about presentations on different quivers or about right-equivalence beyond the fixed canonical presentation.

## Relationship to prior work

Geiß, Hartung, Labardini-Fragoso, and Schröer give the completed-path-algebra framework and an explicit characteristic-two example on a three-cycle with all length-five paths zero; they state that its relation ideal is generated by a hyperpotential but not by the displayed ordinary potential. The present result classifies the full oriented-cycle family by the two independent conditions: endpoint/length compatibility for hyperpotentials and the nonvanishing of the cyclic multiplicity for ordinary potentials. Earlier work on Jacobian algebras of cyclically oriented quivers studies finite-dimensionality for primitive potentials in a different setting; it does not give this characteristic-sensitive Loewy-length criterion. A recent matrix Golod–Shafarevich study addresses existence obstructions for Jacobi-finite potentials on other quiver families, not this exact presentation classification.

## Limitations

The theorem classifies the canonical ideal \(\mathfrak m^L\) on a fixed oriented cycle. It does not rule out that an abstractly isomorphic algebra admits a different presentation on another quiver. Novelty is assessed against the sources and result index inspected here; unindexed or differently phrased prior work remains a residual risk.
