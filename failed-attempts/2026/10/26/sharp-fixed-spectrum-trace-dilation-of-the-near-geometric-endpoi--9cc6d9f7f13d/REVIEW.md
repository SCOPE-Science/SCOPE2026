# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

Independently reconstructed (1)–(4). For X=A^(1/2) S A^(-1/2), XX* is the endpoint and X² is similar to B. The squared Frobenius norm of X−X* is twice the trace defect; entrywise evaluation gives the claimed positive weights and exact commuting equality. For two levels this is (a−b)²/(ab) times ||PS(I−P)||_F². The top partial sums of S−(2P−I)S(2P−I) are bounded by the sums of the paired spectral spreads by the variational principle; these are the partial sums of twice the off-block singular values. Squaring under weak majorization gives the upper bound. Rotating all disjoint extreme pairs through angle θ gives the maximum times sin²(2θ), so the whole interval is attained, including when spread terms vanish. In dimension two the projection onto the larger B eigenvector has off-diagonal square modulus q(1−q); the corresponding entries of B and S differ by the factor sqrt(c)+sqrt(d). The determinant and quadratic-formula consequences follow. The bundled rational tests corroborate examples, not the universal proof, and were inspected, not executed.

## originality

FAIL

The sharp orbit conclusion, not merely its upper bound, is covered as a direct consequence of known results. The endpoint's XX* form and the standard Frobenius expansion expose the defect in one step. With two levels the objective then becomes exactly the sharp off-diagonal energy in Li–Mathias (already the variance objective of their Theorem 1), multiplied by a scalar. Their Theorem 2 includes its bound and the concluding remark includes the same extremal two-by-two blocks. Interpolation of the block rotation or connectedness gives the interval without another optimization. The dimension-two formulas are the same reduction applied to a rank-one spectral projector and the determinant. This is coverage by implication, not a claim that prior authors printed equations (1)–(4) in this notation.

## value

FAIL

The endpoint and trace are natural, and the commutator formula is a useful presentation. But the proposed quantitative advance is exhausted by a standard norm expansion, the known sharp block invariant with the same optimizer, and elementary interpolation and two-dimensional specialization. No independent sharp phenomenon remains after the exact reduction. This is not a rejection for small scope or unavailable literature.
