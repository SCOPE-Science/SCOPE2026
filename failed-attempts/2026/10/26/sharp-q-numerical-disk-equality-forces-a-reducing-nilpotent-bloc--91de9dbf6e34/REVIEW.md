# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

The claim uses real 4/5<q<1, exact overlap q, norm attainment, and the closed scaled unit disk. Kaunisto–O'Loughlin–Virtanen's overlap bound makes a norming input x and its normalized output y orthogonal: the first branch is strictly less than rho_q for positive overlap, and the second is at most 1<rho_q. Under the first-variable-linear convention the test overlap numerator is 2z and its coefficient F is holomorphic, with constant term rho_q/2. The boundary bound at |z|=h and equality at zero imply a constant, yielding the zero second column of the compression. In the cross-block test the first-order coefficient in either perturbation is t times the appropriate mixed matrix element times epsilon or its conjugate; arbitrary complex phase would increase the modulus of the nonzero saturated value if that element were nonzero. The two input phases and the two output phases span the compression plane because c,s>0. This proves both invariances, not merely a compressed square-zero block. The general norm bound gives the converse. The unitary-dilation argument in the originality assessment independently verifies the same result. The excluded endpoints are not needed.

## originality

FAIL

The full reducing-block iff is a mechanical consequence of the stronger prior dilation theorem, not just related to a known constant or example. In arXiv:2610.00443v1, Theorem 2.2 and (3) give A^m=rho P_H U^m|_H for m>=1, with U unitary and rho=rho_q>1. Let Ax=rho y for unit x,y. Then P_H Ux=y has norm one, hence Ux=y. Now rho Ay=A^2x=rho P_H U^2x=rho P_H Uy=Ay, so Ay=0. Also A*y=rho x by U*y=x. Applying the m=1,2 identities for the adjoint dilation U* to that pair gives A*x=0. Thus x and y are orthogonal (Ax is orthogonal to x), and both A and A* preserve their span. Conversely the summand yields norm at least rho while the m=1 dilation bounds the norm by rho. The earlier identities cover the same quantifiers, including arbitrary dimension and arbitrary rank. No new conclusion survives by restricting the stated theorem.
