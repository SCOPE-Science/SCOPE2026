The bipartition is pure pairs versus mixed pairs. A selected pure set dominates all mixed pairs iff one host part is fully covered by selected pure edges; a selected mixed set dominates all pure pairs iff at most one row and at most one column is empty. These tests are independent, including for selected vertices because neighborhoods are open. Inclusion–exclusion gives U and V separately. A smallest pure choice uses a minimum edge cover on one qualifying side and no other pure edges: a matching for even size and one length-two path plus a matching for odd size. A smallest mixed choice occupies exactly s-1 long positions, once each, and uses either t-1 or t distinct short positions, giving the stated surjection count. The resulting total minimum minus Theorem 1.1 of Chinman et al. has precisely the two asserted exceptional cases.
