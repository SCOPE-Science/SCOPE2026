# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

The finite-rank cutoffs converge in operator norm with error 1/(N+1). The norm of K^m is the supremum of its products of m consecutive weights, attained at n=0 and equal to 1/m!, so the spectral radius is zero and K is nonzero compact quasinilpotent. For a general bounded forward shift with nonzero weights, put beta_n equal to the product of the initial n weights. It has cyclic e_0, and commutation gives A e_n=beta_n^{-1}T^n A e_0. If A is nonzero and A e_0 starts in index m, column n starts in index n+m, with nonzero entry there. For any nonzero x with first nonzero entry n, its contribution to coordinate n+m cannot cancel against later columns; finite basis truncations converge and A is bounded. Hence A is injective. Apply this to A=S-lambda I to exclude eigenvalues for non-scalar S. Finally, SF=FS would make the one-dimensional nonzero range of F invariant under S, forcing an eigenvalue. The Banach-space question includes this Hilbert-space example and requires a non-scalar S, so the negative answer is correct.

## originality

FAIL

The classical weighted-shift commutant description already implies the obstruction mechanically for the whole stated class. Turner (1973), pp. 379–380, records the Shields–Wallen theorem: the weakly closed unital algebra generated by a one-sided shift with nonzero weights is maximal abelian, hence equals the commutant. For every polynomial p(T), entry (j+k,j) is its kth coefficient times the product of the k weights beginning at j, and entries above the diagonal vanish. These entry relations persist in the weak operator closure; equivalently all commutant operators are formal series in T. For a nonzero series the first nonzero diagonal has nonvanishing entries all the way down, which immediately gives injectivity by the first nonzero coordinate of the operand. Equivalently, conjugate by the coefficient normalization to formal multiplication in the integral domain C[[z]]. Subtracting scalars gives empty point spectrum for each non-scalar member, and a commuting rank-one range would give an eigenvalue. The specific reciprocally weighted compact shift is an immediate instance. The application is not shown to have been previously printed as an answer to Problem 4.5; the failure is prior implication, not identical wording or an inaccessible-source inference.

## value

FAIL

The question is well motivated, and the example is a useful explanatory observation. As a standalone new finding, however, the response is a direct application of the known full commutant formula, the invariant range of a commuting rank-one map, and standard compact-shift norm formulas. It establishes no new obstruction beyond these immediate properties of the classical class.
