# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

Write λ=μ_c and m=λ(K). The atomic component is the norm limit of the finite-rank maps keeping only the first N point masses, with error bounded by the p-th root of the tail mass. In the direct sum, composing with the contractive diffuse projection gives one essential-norm inequality, and adjoining the compact atomic map to a diffuse approximant gives the other; thus the essential norm equals that of the diffuse component. Its ordinary norm is m^(1/p), giving the upper bound and resolving m=0. For a check of the lower bound that does not require generating Rademachers, cover the image of the continuous unit ball under any compact S:C(K)→L^p(λ) by finitely many ε-balls. Approximate the centers by simple functions within ε and refine their partitions to a common partition. Bisect each positive-mass cell using nonatomicity and let r be +1 on one half and −1 on the other. For any simple center g on the common partition, convexity gives (|1−g|^p+|−1−g|^p)/2≥1, even for complex g, hence distance at least m^(1/p). Radon density and clipping give f in the continuous unit ball with ||f−r||_p<ε. Use the center for Sf to obtain ||J_c−S||≥m^(1/p)−3ε. This proves the lower bound, including p=1. The frozen sequential proof achieves the same separation by independence against finite-coordinate approximants followed by a convergent subsequence of Sf_n; its standard nonatomic space and density hypotheses hold. Essential norm zero is equivalent to compactness by norm closure of compact operators.

## originality

FAIL

Kiwerski–Tomaszewski Theorem 4.1 gives an exact nonatomic multiplier norm from L∞ to L^p for every 1<p<∞. The same value for the continuous domain follows by a contractive bidual lift on the absolutely continuous band of measures; the atomic part is compact. The endpoint p=1, outside that theorem's hypotheses, follows from the p>1 values by approximating a compact map into L¹ by bounded simple-function maps and sending p down to 1. These implications recover the full claimed range and mixed-measure value from the stronger prior theorem and standard operator facts. The original argument is correct, but a direct proof is not evidence that the formula is a new exact invariant.

## value

FAIL

The essential norm is a meaningful natural invariant, and the Rademacher argument is a clean self-contained calculation. But the exact constant, atomic decomposition and p=1 endpoint follow from the prior general multiplier theorem by the bidual comparison and standard finite-rank approximation. The frozen result does not establish a distinct exact invariant or resolve a gap after this coverage is accounted for. Narrowing to the endpoint would leave the same direct consequence.
