# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

Separate palindromes by their number of ones. The one-one family has a common shape and the largest of three center radii; the two-one families overlap exactly for equal inner gaps (then the smaller family is contained in the larger); the three-one family occurs only for equal inner gaps. At integer gap level t the deficit in the resulting count is one exactly when both outer gaps, but neither inner gap, reach t. In the equal-inner-gap case the overlap correction and three-one family cancel that deficit. The argument includes empty gaps; at weight two the corresponding three-gap level deficit is identically zero, and at weight one the largest and smallest of the two gaps sum to their total. For a positive defect r, n is at least 2r+6: both outer gaps are at least r+h, where h is the larger inner gap, and the unequal inner gaps have sum at least one and h at least one. At n >= 8 and maximal r, h >= 2 would exceed the total zero budget even allowing its one unit of parity slack. Thus the inner gaps are 0 and 1 and the outer gaps differ by at most one. This proves the all-parameter law, all maximizers, and the two length-eight witnesses.
