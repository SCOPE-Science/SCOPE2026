# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

Orthant simplex moments give d!(2j)! binom(j+d−1,d−1)/(d+2j)! and zero odd moments. In the reverse Legendre recurrence, the ratios of the proposed I_(n−1)(j) and I_(n−2)(j+1) to I_n(j) are (2j+n+1)/(2(j+n)) and (2j+1)/(2(j+n)), respectively, verifying the displayed integral identity with the two flat base cases. Matching all moments on compact support identifies the law. The inverse-root factorization gives the shape and endpoint assertions and the moment series is the stated hypergeometric series. Integration by parts against sin(ts) is positive for 0<t≤π when p' is strictly negative on (0,1). In the complex endpoint expansion the leading oscillation is sin z and the correction is p'(1) cos z/(zp(1)); the ratio is −(d−1)(d−2)/2. The expansion is uniform relative to the sine off fixed disks about nπ at sufficiently large |z|. Rouché gives one simple zero in each disk and conjugation makes it real. The next sine term is of order sin z/z² and the next cosine term is of order cos z/z³, giving precisely the stated O_d(n^−3) displacement after expansion near nπ. The result is diagonal and fixed-dimensional, not a global Fourier zero-free ball or a growing-dimension assertion.

## originality

FAIL

The decisive overlap is not just the earlier catalogue's exact hypergeometric specialization. König's Proposition 2.1, crediting Meyer–Pajor and Brzezinski, already gives the exact parallel-section density by divided differences, and equation (2.3) gives the exact Fourier transform in a broader form. Taking coincident nodes by the standard derivative rule gives precisely the displayed reverse Legendre coefficients and the exact sine–cosine expansion containing the stated zero phase. Richards/Kalmykov–Karp additionally cover the real-zero assertion in stronger form. The record is correct but does not supply a distinct substantive theorem after removing these consequences.

## value

FAIL

The geometric/Fourier direction is natural, and the Legendre presentation is convenient. But after crediting prior exact coverage it is a standard confluent substitution and coefficient rewrite, followed by ordinary shape and endpoint estimates; even the phase constant comes from the first two terms of the older exact transform. No surviving independently valuable exact invariant or new phenomenon is established within the frozen claim. This is not a rejection of the correctness or expository usefulness of the identification.
