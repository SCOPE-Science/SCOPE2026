# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

The nilpotent conic C in P(L) has equation a^2+bc=0 in traceless coordinates and parametrizes kernel lines of rank-one nilpotents. At E its tangent c=0 consists of traceless upper-triangular matrices, and conjugation transports this description to all tangents. Conversely a nontangent meets C in two distinct points over the algebraic closure, with distinct kernels. Conjugating those kernel lines to the coordinate axes takes the nilpotents to nonzero multiples of E and F, which generate. Scalar extension commutes with the generated algebra, so this suffices over k. This proves the generation criterion in both characteristics. Rational secant lines are the unordered pairs of distinct rational conic points; external lines are Frobenius pairs of nonrational conic points over F(q^2). The conjugation action on the conic is the natural PGL2 action, transitive on both classes, with q(q+1)/2 and q(q-1)/2 lines respectively. In odd characteristic intersecting the plane with the conic gives binary discriminant 4Q(N) for the trace-normal N. In characteristic two the ordinary polarity degenerates with nucleus [I]; substitute a=alpha*b+beta*c. If alpha is nonzero the root criterion reduces to u^2+u+(alpha*beta)^2=0, exactly trace zero, while alpha=0 gives two distinct rational points directly. This recovers all stated invariants including at q=2.

## originality

FAIL

The two orbits are exactly the classical secant and external line orbits for a nonsingular conic, under an equivariant identification. The polynomial invariants are the standard splitting tests for the conic intersection, and the sizes are the counts of the two types of unordered pairs on the conic. The generating-plane phrasing does not add a third stratum or a new orbit refinement: the only excluded orbit is the tangent lines, equivalently the planes triangularizable over the algebraic closure. This is established broader/equivalent coverage, not a claim that the cited conic paper uses the language of generating matrix spaces.

## value

FAIL

The nilpotent-conic interpretation is a useful way to teach the matrix example, but the exact count and orbit invariant are routine transports of the known line types. The frozen finding supplies no additional classification within those orbits and no mathematically distinct consequence that survives the equivalence.
