# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

Deleting the middle hub leaves a join of two higher independence complexes of stars. Each star is a leaf simplex plus a cone on its (r-2)-skeleton, giving the stated star sphere count and hence the product multiplicity in degree 2r-1. For r>=2 the link requires that the components meeting the end hubs, merged through the selected middle hub, have total size at most r-1 before adding that hub. This is exactly Ind_(r-1) of the double star obtained by adding the end-to-end edge. Deshpande–Singh applies to that double star, not to the whole tree, and gives the claimed link wedge. Independently, at threshold s=r-1 the no-both-hubs subcomplex of the double star is contractible: its two pieces are cones from leaves at the opposite ends and intersect in the full leaf simplex. For s>=2 the both-hub piece is the hub edge joined with the skeleton of leaf sets of size <=s-2, attached along the boundary edge joined with that skeleton. This gives its double suspension and the same link count (s=2 gives one circle, s=1 gives a contractible link). The middle-link inclusion factors through the join of the two smaller-threshold star complexes; the maps to the larger-threshold stars are null by connectivity. For r=1 the middle link is a simplex, and the deletion is S^1. Thus the cone attachment splits as deletion wedge suspended link, establishing integral homotopy rather than only mod-two ranks. For r at least the order of the tree the complex is a simplex and both counts vanish.

It adds no independent scientific contribution as a separate finding.
