## correctness

PASS

On the torus, differentiating (∏X_i)ΣX_i^{-1} on its zero locus gives partials proportional to X_i^{-2}; the hyperplane Jacobian condition makes them equal, so injectivity of squaring forces the all-ones point. Exactly one zero coordinate is impossible; with exactly two zero coordinates the gradient is nonzero only in the two corresponding entries, with equal nonzero value there; with at least three it vanishes. This gives the 20 distinct lines and the isolated point. Reducibility (intersection of distinct positive-degree factors) or a repeated factor would give a singular subvariety of dimension at least two in the projective hyperplane; neither occurs. The quintic hypersurface is S₂, regular in codimension one, and adjunction gives trivial dualizing sheaf. In X₅=1, the coefficient of σ_r(v) in the translation expansion of σ₅ is 6−r modulo 2; with Σv_i=0 this leaves σ₃(v)+σ₅(v). Putting v=(u₀,u₁,u₂,u₃,Σu,0) gives the two displayed homogeneous forms. The four cubic derivatives are (Σ_{j≠i}u_j)²; their common vanishing forces each u_i=Σu and hence u=0. The blowup charts have strict-transform equation Q₃(1,w)+t²Q₅(1,w)=0, smooth along the cubic; adjunction subtracts multiplicity 3 from ambient discrepancy 3. The intrinsic tangent space is Hom(ke,V/ke); e is permutation-fixed and ΣX_i³ restricts to V invariant under translation by e, so both the exceptional surface and the Segre cone base are the zero scheme of this same cubic on P(V/ke), with the same permutation action. The actual Hessian vanishes by characteristic two. The binary projective points on the quintic have weights 2, 4 and 6 (15, 15, 1), of which weights 2 and 6 are singular.
