# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

The token graph is bipartite between mixed and pure pairs. A pure pair is missed exactly if both its labels are absent from the chosen mixed pairs, so at most one row and at most one column may be empty. A mixed pair is missed exactly if both its endpoints are uncovered by the chosen pure pairs; because all mixed pairs exist, one whole shore must be edge-covered. These independent constraints give R and E by inclusion–exclusion. Each optimum mixed choice uses one entry in each of n−1 columns and has an image of size m−1 or m; the Stirling count follows. The optimum pure choice lies entirely in one shore, and the larger shore is an option exactly when the two ceiling bounds agree. An even minimum edge cover is a matching; an odd one has exactly one vertex of degree two and all others of degree one, yielding one 3-vertex path and a matching. This works at m=n=2 and in the tied odd/even case. At (3,4) the two minimum counts are 96 and 6, giving 576 sets of size 5.
