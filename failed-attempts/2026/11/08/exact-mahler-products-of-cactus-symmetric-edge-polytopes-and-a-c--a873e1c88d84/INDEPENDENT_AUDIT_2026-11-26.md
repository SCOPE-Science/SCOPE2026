The edge-root spans at an articulation form a direct sum; affine invariance permits product coordinates. With block dimensions a,b the free-sum primal volume gains a!b!/(a+b)!, canceled by normalization; the polar is a Cartesian product. A bridge contributes one. On a cycle the signed edge differences identify the polar with the central section of the cube [-1,1]^n, with absolute determinant n on the zero-sum hyperplane. Coarea gives polar volume 2^(n−1)f_n(n/2)/√n for the [0,1]-uniform sum density, and inclusion–exclusion gives the stated finite density sum. For odd n the polar vertices have one zero coordinate and balanced remaining signs, giving simplicial primal facets. For even n they are balanced sign vectors; the resulting facet's unique affine dependence equates the positive- and negative-side barycenters. Subtracting the smallest positive-side convex coefficient from all that side's coefficients and adding it on the other side gives the stated triangulation, with disjoint interiors. That comparison is equivalent coverage, not evidence of ordering; the originality rejection rests on the direct implications of the earlier primary sources.
