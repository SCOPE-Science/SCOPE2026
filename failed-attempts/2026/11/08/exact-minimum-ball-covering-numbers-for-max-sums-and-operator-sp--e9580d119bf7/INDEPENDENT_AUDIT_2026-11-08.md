For any origin-avoiding max-sum ball B∞(c,r), if it meets the embedded j-th coordinate sphere then all other ||c_i||≤r and ||c_j||>r. Thus it cannot meet two distinct axis spheres, and restrictions to each axis give the exact lower bound ∑κ(E_j). Inflating any finite component cover while keeping radii below center norms and scaling centers gives radii >1; embedding these balls into each coordinate axis covers the full max-sum sphere, proving equality. Equality in the integer count yields localization and the unique dominant coordinate. The operator isometry T↦(T e_j) gives ||T||=max_j||Te_j|| from ℓ1 domain. For Euclidean E=ℓ2^n, n+1 follows from antipodal separation and a centered simplex construction, giving m(n+1). This is mathematically correct for finite-dimensional real normed spaces and m finite.

FAIL

Cheng, Cheng and Shi (Studia Mathematica 192, 2009), Lemma 3.1(iii) proves exactly minimal ball-covering number additivity for max direct sums under dual exposed-point localization, automatically true for arbitrary finite-dimensional max sums because the dual ball is an ℓ1 sum. Their Theorem 3.3 specializes to m(n+1) for m Euclidean n-spaces. The operator-space case follows routinely from the standard L(ℓ1^m,Y) ≅ ℓ∞^m(Y) isometry. 2017 Luo–Liu–Wang independently exhibit the same exclusive-axis lower-bound argument in max sums. The submitted rigidity condition is an immediate equality case of the already established mechanism.
