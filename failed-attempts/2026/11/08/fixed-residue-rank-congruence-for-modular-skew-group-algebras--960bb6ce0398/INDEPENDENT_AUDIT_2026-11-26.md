# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

With the right-multiplication convention for idempotents on row vectors, the image is a summand of a finite free left A-module and has constant rank by connectedness. The stable ideal yields a two-sided quotient and the trivial residue action makes the fiber ring K[G]. Reduction preserves the split image. Induction on a central subgroup of order p proves locality by a nilpotent central kernel; finite projectives over that local ring are free. Taking the K-dimension gives j|G| with 0≤j≤n, and the diagonal witnesses give all ranks. The K₀ range follows from the rank of R; in size 1 the rank-zero module or complement vanishes, making the idempotent 0 or 1. The involution t↦t⁻¹ fixes the residue at t=1 in the dihedral example, and the supplied coefficient argument also independently works in characteristic two. For translation, summing (x+i)^(p−1) over F_p gives −1, so a=−x^(p−1) has trace 1. With N the group sum, NaN=N, whence aN is an idempotent and its regular-left-module image is AN, a free rank-one A-module. This tests precisely the trivial-residue hypothesis: a trivial translation action on a residue field would imply 1=0. The frozen finite checker was inspected, not executed; none of the universal claims depend on it.

## originality

PASS

Only the formulation of the fixed-residue rank obstruction for arbitrary connected commutative A and its paired explicit boundary are distinct in the inspected comparisons. The dihedral result is an immediate corollary of a stronger known projective-module theorem, and the fixed-residue proof uses standard fiber and local-ring facts. No inspected source was shown to state a general skew-group rank result encompassing the arbitrary-base formulation; this scoped originality pass is not an endorsement of its scientific value.

## value

FAIL

The obstruction for general A is the immediate base-change rank calculation at one fiber where the group algebra is local. Attainment and the K₀ image then just count copies of the free module. The proposed distinctive group-algebra application is already implied by a stronger classification of projectives, and the boundary is the elementary trace-one projection for the standard translation action. Together these are a useful exposition of familiar tools, but leave no nonroutine rank calculation or independently valuable new example. The failure does not depend on inability to read Lorenz.
