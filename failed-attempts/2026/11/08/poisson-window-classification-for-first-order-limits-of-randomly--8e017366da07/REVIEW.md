## correctness

The convergence theorem and zero-one boundary check out. An existence sentence for an exactly-k red class has no-occurrence probability (1-p)^m; since p tends to zero, taking extended negative logs detects the limit of mp. For rank q the two-level clipping at q suffices by matching fresh classes of the same type and fresh vertices of the same color. The ratio of consecutive means tends to infinity, so at most one index is critical; its two disjoint color categories have a bivariate generating function converging to that of independent Poisson variables. The displayed exact spectrum nevertheless invokes a 'finite Boolean algebra' generated by all integer thresholds. There is no such finite algebra: for c>0 the events X>=j are all distinct, even modulo Poisson null sets. The proof establishes the intended algebra of finite Boolean combinations, an infinite union of the finite algebras from clipping at each q. This is a wording defect in the exact claim, not a counterexample to the probabilistic or logical argument. The supporting checker was inspected, not executed, and is not needed for the proof.
