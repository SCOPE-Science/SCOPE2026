For three or more omissions, a witness for one omission forces all other omissions into its own part; any witness for a member of that part would see at least two omissions. For two omissions, witnesses exist exactly across distinct nonsingleton parts. Thus admissible complements are empty, singletons or Q-edges. This gives the N+e forbidden bipartitions. With three blocks there must be at least two special blocks (singleton or Q-edge), and N≥7 makes them unique. The contributions are binom(N,2)−e, (N−2)e and m2, respectively. For four blocks an admissible pair leaves two singleton blocks; a partition with only two singleton blocks leaves those singleton blocks without partners, and N≥7 rules out four singleton blocks. Three singletons therefore all need the residual block as partner, which is equivalent to forming a triangle. Five blocks leave at least three omissions. The coefficient evaluation at K(2,2,2,2) is 95, 304, 32. The N≥7 restriction avoids e.g. three disjoint Q-edges partitioning N=6, where treating any two special blocks as unique would overcount.
