# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

The exception set is over all positive integers; zero relative density among the evens also follows since their count up to X is asymptotic to X/2. The cited prime-block lemma applies to the minimal consecutive-prime product for every sufficiently late starting index. Its deduction from the divisor-sum interval lemma is valid: sigma(A_k)/2 is integral and eventually exceeds cp_k (there are at least two prime factors, with the second tending to infinity), and by symmetry is below sigma(A_k)-cp_k. Oddness and squarefreeness are automatic for late starts, and disjoint blocks give pairwise coprimality. Minimality bounds the product of (1+1/p) by 8/3, and the corresponding finite product of (1-1/p^2) exceeds the full odd-prime Euler product 8/pi^2, giving the strict 3/pi^2 bound. For fixed Z, any N coprime to Z gives a reduced residue N-Z modulo Z^2. Choosing a prime in each of the finitely many reduced classes works uniformly once N exceeds twice their maximum; Dirichlet alone suffices. Then N-p is positive in the Z residue modulo Z^2, so it is Z times a positive coprime factor and is Zumkeller by lifting the divisor partition. CRT gives the exact product density and taking K arbitrarily large after the limsup in X gives zero. Conjecture 4.21 has two parts; this argument proves no square-summand result. The frozen verification files were inspected without running the uploaded program; finite checks on eleven blocks and on 945 and its progression multiples are not proofs of the infinite inputs.

## originality

FAIL

Li's Theorem 1.2 already proves the same all-integer density limit with a quantitative gain. The distinct earlier exponent-one catalogue result uses the very same minimal blocks, with substantially sharper coprimality factors and the same CRT argument. The fixed-class envelope for all integers follows by a direct fixed-modulus specialization of their representation mechanism, not an independent arithmetic conclusion.
