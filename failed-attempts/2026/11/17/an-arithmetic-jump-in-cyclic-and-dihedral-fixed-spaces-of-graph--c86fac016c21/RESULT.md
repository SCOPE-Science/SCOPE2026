# An arithmetic jump in cyclic and dihedral fixed spaces of graph-connectivity complexes

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge4\), let \(\Delta_n^j\) be the simplicial complex on the edges of the complete labeled graph with vertex set \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\); a face is an edge set whose spanning graph fails to be \(j\)-vertex-connected. The complexes carry the natural dihedral action on labels. If \(C_n\le D_n\) is the subgroup of all rotations, then for each \(j\in\{1,2,3\}\), there is an **equality of geometric fixed-point subspaces**
\[
|\Delta_n^j|^{C_n}=|\Delta_n^j|^{D_n}.
\]
Define \(e(n)=\varphi(n)/2\) and \(r(n)=\omega(n)\), where \(\varphi\) is Euler's totient and \(\omega\) counts distinct prime factors. Let \(Y_n\) be the simplicial complex on the noncoprime distances \(d\in\{1,\ldots,\lfloor n/2\rfloor\}\), a nonempty set of distances being a face when the distances have a common prime divisor with \(n\).

The **sharp fixed-space connectivity transition** is
\[
|\Delta_n^1|^{D_n}\cong_{\rm PL}|\Delta_n^2|^{D_n}\cong_{\rm PL}|Y_n|,
\qquad
|\Delta_n^3|^{D_n}\cong_{\rm PL}|Y_n|\sqcup\bigsqcup^{e(n)}\{\ast\}.
\]
All three displayed PL identifications are canonical through edge-orbit barycenters. The homotopy type of the core is
\[
|Y_n|\simeq
\begin{cases}
\varnothing,& n\text{ prime},\\
S^{r(n)-2},&n\text{ squarefree composite},\\
\ast,&n\text{ non-squarefree}.
\end{cases}
\]
Thus \(n=30\) gives a circle and four isolated points; \(n=210\) gives a two-sphere and twenty-four isolated points. The additional isolated points are absent for the first two connectivity thresholds, and present for all \(n\ge4\) at the third threshold.

## Assumptions and scope
Graphs are finite, undirected, simple, on all \(n\) specified labeled vertices, even when isolated vertices occur. A graph is \(j\)-vertex-connected if it has more than \(j\) vertices and removing fewer than \(j\) vertices leaves it connected; \(n\ge4\) throughout. Geometric fixed points mean fixed points in the usual linear realization of a finite simplicial complex, not invariant simplices alone and not an orbit-space quotient. The equality between rotational and dihedral fixed spaces is an equality **inside** the original geometric realization, not just a homotopy equivalence.

## Proof
**Orbit-simplex model.** The \(C_n\)-orbits of potential edges are indexed by the chord lengths \(d\in\{1,\ldots,\lfloor n/2\rfloor\}\). The orbit of \(d\) is the set of undirected edges \(\{i,i+d\}\) for all \(i\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\), with duplicates ignored. A geometric simplex point is fixed by rotations exactly when its barycentric coefficients are constant within every edge orbit. Its positive-support orbits must jointly form a face. Hence the fixed-point space is PL-homeomorphic, via the orbit-barycenter map, to the abstract simplicial complex of sets \(S\) of chord lengths for which the associated **circulant spanning graph** \(G(n,S)\) fails the prescribed connectivity threshold. Every reflection preserves each undirected chord-length orbit setwise; it therefore fixes the orbit barycenter individually, proving the claimed equality of rotational and full-dihedral geometric fixed spaces for each of the three complexes.

**Connectivity of the circulants.** The components of \(G(n,S)\) are the cosets of the subgroup of \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\) generated by \(S\); thus \(G(n,S)\) is connected exactly when \(\gcd(n,S)=1\). For a connected such graph, the degree is \(2|S|\), minus one if \(n\) is even and \(n/2\in S\). A connected graph of degree at least three is 3-vertex-connected: it is finite, connected and vertex-transitive, and Watkins's bound \(\kappa(G)\ge 2(\deg(G)+1)/3\) gives \(\kappa(G)\ge3\) for every integral degree \(\deg(G)\ge3\). A connected graph of degree two is necessarily the single-distance Hamiltonian cycle \(S=\{d\}\) with \(\gcd(n,d)=1\), since no connected degree-one circulant occurs for \(n\ge4\). Such a cycle is 2-connected but not 3-connected. It follows that for each \(j\in\{1,2\}\), the allowable sets \(S\) are exactly those with \(\gcd(n,S)>1\), while for \(j=3\) these sets are augmented by the singleton coprime distances. No coprime singleton can be joined to another permitted orbit, because the resulting connected graph has degree at least three. The \(e(n)=\varphi(n)/2\) coprime distances therefore give precisely the claimed additional isolated vertices.

**Prime-cover nerve.** For each distinct prime \(p\mid n\), let \(E_p\) denote the simplex consisting of all distances \(d\le\lfloor n/2\rfloor\) divisible by \(p\). The complex \(Y_n=\bigcup_{p\mid n}E_p\). All nonempty intersections of these simplices are either simplices or empty, so the nerve lemma applies. For a nonempty set \(J\) of primes dividing \(n\), the intersection \(\bigcap_{p\in J}E_p\) is nonempty exactly when \(\prod_{p\in J}p\le\lfloor n/2\rfloor\). If \(n\) is squarefree composite, every proper subset of its \(r(n)\ge2\) prime factors satisfies this inequality, since the missing prime is at least two, while the full product does not. Thus the nerve is the boundary of the \((r(n)-1)\)-simplex, yielding \(S^{r(n)-2}\). If \(n\) is non-squarefree, the radical \(\operatorname{rad}(n)\le n/2\) is a vertex of **every** \(E_p\), so \(Y_n\) itself is a cone and is contractible. If \(n\) is prime there is no noncoprime chord distance, hence \(Y_n\) is empty. This exhausts all \(n\ge4\).

## Verification
The standalone standard-library program `verify.py` enumerates the actual cyclic edge-orbits and, without using the theorem's circulant connectivity inequality, decides connectivity after every possible deletion of zero, one or two vertices. It exhaustively compares predicted and actual faces for every \(4\le n\le22\). For \(4\le n\le20\), it independently constructs full simplicial boundary matrices over \(\mathbb F_2\), checks their ranks and compares all ordinary Betti numbers with the theorem. It checks reflection preservation of every edge-orbit through \(n=30\), and directly enumerates intersections of the prime-cover simplices at \(n=6,10,12,15,30,42,60,105,210\), covering both a one-sphere and a two-sphere nerve. Run `python3 verify.py` from the directory containing the program; the accompanying `verification_output.txt` gives the expected output. This finite verification is corroboration, not an inference of the unbounded theorem.

## Relationship to prior work
Babson–Björner–Linusson–Shareshian–Welker introduced and analyzed the non-connectedness and higher nonconnectivity graph complexes, and Jonsson established the nonequivariant wedge-of-spheres theorem for \(\Delta_n^3\). The disconnected-graph complex \(\Delta_n^1\) is also a standard realization of the partition complex; its arithmetic fixed-point structure is closely related to published partition-lattice fixed-point theory. These established results account for the prime-cover **core** \(Y_n\), rather than being claimed new. The comparison proved here is the precise transition at the third connectivity threshold: every cyclic Hamiltonian edge-orbit creates a new isolated geometric fixed point, while the dihedral group adds no additional constraint. The proof combines that observation with the vertex-transitive connectivity bound of Watkins. The complete simultaneous \(\Delta_n^1,\Delta_n^2,\Delta_n^3\) fixed-space formula does not follow from the previously published nonequivariant wedge counts or homology representations alone.

## Limitations
This result addresses geometric fixed spaces under the full regular rotational and dihedral groups, not all subgroups, their orbit-space quotients, or the equivariant homotopy type of the entire ambient complexes. The prime-cover core is not claimed to be independently novel, nor is the classical vertex-transitive connectivity estimate. Similar formulas may exist under alternative names for invariant circulant subgraph complexes; a literature comparison cannot establish publication priority exhaustively. Independent mathematical review has not been performed.
