# An order-27 integral obstruction to quadrangle-cycle generation in the ternary projective plane

## Finding

Let \(M\) be the simple rank-three vector matroid of the 13 projective points of \(\mathrm{PG}(2,3)\), and let \(K=\operatorname{Ind}(M)\) be its simplicial independence complex. For each four-point projective quadrangle \(Q=\{p_0,p_1,p_2,p_3\}\), where no three points are collinear, set \(z_Q=\partial[p_0,p_1,p_2,p_3]\in C_2(K;\mathbb Z)\); the orientation of a quadrangle may be chosen arbitrarily.

There are exactly 234 quadrangles, forming a single orbit under \(\mathrm{PGL}_3(\mathbb F_3)\). Their 234 embedded tetrahedral-boundary spheres are exactly the support-minimal nonzero integral 2-cycles up to integral multiples, and their primitive orientations are the shortest primitive cycles. The underlying integral homology is free, with

\[\widetilde H_2(K;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{168},\qquad\widetilde H_j(K;\mathbb Z)=0\ \ (j\ne2).\]

However, if \(L=\sum_Q\mathbb Z[z_Q]\subseteq\widetilde H_2(K;\mathbb Z)\) is the sublattice generated by these *all* shortest sphere cycles, then

\[\boxed{\widetilde H_2(K;\mathbb Z)/L\cong(\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z)^3.}\]

In particular, 234 minimal sphere cycles span rational top homology but form a proper sublattice of exact index 27, despite every individual cycle being primitive in the full integral homology. The 3-torsion belongs to this **lattice quotient**, not to the homology group itself.

## Assumptions and scope

The 13 points are the one-dimensional linear subspaces of \(\mathbb F_3^3\); each is represented by its lexicographically sorted nonzero vector normalized so its first nonzero coordinate equals 1. A 3-element subset is a face precisely when its representing columns have nonzero determinant modulo 3. Every point pair is a face; no 4-element subset is a face. Chain groups and boundaries use the ordinary increasing-vertex orientations and integral coefficients, with \(\partial[v_0,\ldots,v_d]=\sum_{i=0}^d(-1)^i[v_0,\ldots,\widehat{v_i},\ldots,v_d]\). The theorem concerns these 13 projective points and the rank-three independence complex, not the clique, collinearity, line-avoidance or neighborhood complex of a projective-plane graph.

## Proof

**Faces and rank.** There are 13 lines, each containing four points. Every collinear triple lies on a unique line. Thus the 2-faces number \(\binom{13}{3}-13\binom43=234\). The graph of edges is the complete graph on 13 vertices, whose integral cycle lattice has rank \(\binom{12}{2}=66\). Fix vertex 0, and use the 66 edges \((i,j)\) with \(1\le i<j\le12\) as coordinates for this cycle lattice.

**An integral section of the boundary.** For each of those 66 edges choose a 2-face \(T_{ij}\) as follows. If \(\{0,i,j\}\) is noncollinear, choose that triangle. Otherwise \(0,i,j\) lie on one projective line: choose the smallest permissible vertex \(k\) off the line and take \(\{i,j,k\}\). All 66 choices are distinct. Of the 66 root-free edges, 54 are not collinear with 0 and 12 are; the boundary-coordinate matrix of these 66 triangles is block triangular when the 54 noncollinear root-free edges are ordered first, with diagonal entries \(\pm1\) throughout. Hence its determinant is \(\pm1\) (the prescribed lexicographic implementation obtains \(+1\)), and \(\partial_2\) surjects the *integral* 1-cycle lattice. Consequently \(\widetilde H_1(K;\mathbb Z)=0\) and \(\widetilde H_2(K;\mathbb Z)\) is free of rank \(234-66=168\). Connectivity and the dimension-two condition give all remaining homology statements.

**Quadrangles and minimality.** Every three-point independent triangle admits exactly four further points outside the union of its three pair lines. Each quadrangle contains four independent triangles; double counting yields \(234\cdot4/4=234\) quadrangles. A projective ordered frame (four points no three collinear) determines a unique projectivity to any other ordered frame, so the 234 unordered quadrangles form one \(\mathrm{PGL}_3(\mathbb F_3)\)-orbit. On at most three vertices there is no nonzero 2-cycle. On exactly four vertices a nonzero 2-cycle can exist only when all four triangles occur, and the kernel of their simplicial boundary is then precisely \(\mathbb Z\partial[p_0,p_1,p_2,p_3]\). Thus these are exactly the four-vertex supports of nonzero 2-cycles. The displayed boundary has coefficients \(\pm1\) and there are no 3-chains in \(K\), so each generator is primitive in the full homology lattice.

**Exact index and invariant factors.** The 66 selected triangles provide an integer direct complement to \(\ker\partial_2\). Forgetting their coordinates identifies \(\ker\partial_2\) with \(\mathbb Z^{168}\), indexed by the 168 *unselected* triangles. Let \(R\) be the \(168\times234\) integer matrix whose columns are the 234 quadrangle boundaries in those coordinates. The completely specified standalone verifier constructs \(R\) directly from the normalized vectors, and checks

\[\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_2}(R)=168,\qquad\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_3}(R)=165.\]

The 168 column indices selected by lexicographic Gaussian pivoting modulo 2 are recorded in the accompanying verification output. The corresponding *exact integer* maximal minor is \(-27\). Since \(R\) loses three ranks modulo 3, the Smith form has at least three invariant factors divisible by 3, so every full-size minor is divisible by \(3^3=27\). A minor of determinant \(-27\) proves that the gcd of maximal minors is exactly 27, hence that \(\mathbb Z^{168}/\operatorname{im}R\) has order 27. Exactly three of its Smith factors are divisible by 3; they must therefore all equal 3. An independent direct Smith-form computation agrees, giving \(165\) diagonal entries 1 and three entries 3. Thus the asserted quotient is \((\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z)^3\). This calculation concerns the finite, completely enumerated matrix \(R\), not a sampling of cycles or a floating-point rank estimate.

## Relationship to prior work

The rank-three projective matroid and the general sphere-wedge topology of independence complexes belong to classical matroid topology; neither the existence of top homology nor the count of projective frames is claimed as new. Havlicek's study of \(\mathrm{PG}(2,3)\) quadrangles and diagonal triangles supplies projective-geometric context, but the inspected introduction and geometric construction do not calculate the integral 2-homology lattice generated by tetrahedral boundaries. Falk's Orlik–Solomon construction uses **circuit boundaries as relations in a quotient of the full exterior algebra**; this is not a theorem asserting that four-circuit boundary cycles span the integral top homology of the *independence subcomplex*. In particular the matroid has also 3-point circuits, treated differently there. Moci and Pezzoli describe rational/complex group representations for matroid homology and its dual, not saturation of this explicit integral cycle lattice. No exact determination of the quotient of these 234 geometric sphere cycles was located in the checked sources and semantic mathematical-results searches. This is an evidence-relative originality assessment, not a uniqueness certification.

## Limitations

Only the rank-three ternary projective-plane matroid is treated. The result does not classify analogous indices in other finite projective planes or full integral equivariant homology modules. It does not claim intrinsic 3-torsion in \(H_2\), nor that shortest cycles fail to span over \(\mathbb Q\). Previously unindexed or inaccessible primary works could contain an equivalent integral calculation. Independent mathematical audit and publication-priority certification have not been performed.
