# Diagonal cyclic fixed spaces of complete-graph Hom complexes are joins of discrete sets

## Finding

Let \(m,n\) be integers with \(2\le m\le n\). On the source complete graph \(K_m\), fix the cyclic permutation \(\sigma=(0\ 1\ \cdots\ m-1)\); on the target \(K_n\), choose an arbitrary permutation \(\pi\in S_n\). The cyclic group generated by \((\sigma,\pi)\) acts on the polyhedral graph-homomorphism complex \(X=\operatorname{Hom}(K_m,K_n)\) by simultaneous precomposition and color relabeling.

Let \(q=q_m(\pi)\) be the number of cycles of \(\pi\) whose lengths are divisible by \(m\), and let \(D_m\) denote a discrete space with \(m\) points. The **actual geometric fixed-point space**, not merely its Euler characteristic, has a natural PL model as the join of the sets of \(\pi^m\)-orbits on eligible target-color cycles. After identifying each of these \(m\)-point orbit sets with \(D_m\), it satisfies a PL homeomorphism

\[
X^{\langle(\sigma,\pi)\rangle}\ \cong_{\mathrm{PL}}\ (D_m)^{*q},
\]

where the empty join for \(q=0\) denotes the empty space. Consequently, when \(q\ge1\),

\[
X^{\langle(\sigma,\pi)\rangle}\simeq\bigvee^{(m-1)^q} S^{q-1},
\]

and its only nonzero reduced integral homology is \(\widetilde H_{q-1}\cong\mathbb Z^{(m-1)^q}\). When \(q=0\), there are no geometric fixed points. Here \(\bigvee^{m-1}S^0\) is understood as \(m\) isolated points.

For \(m=3\), a single six-cycle on target colors produces exactly three geometric fixed points **even though it fixes no vertex of the Hom complex**. Two disjoint three-cycles yield \(D_3*D_3\cong K_{3,3}\simeq\bigvee^4S^1\). The distinction between geometric fixed points and fixed colorings is essential.

## Assumptions and scope

The graphs are finite, loopless, undirected complete graphs. The Hom complex has cells indexed by ordered \(m\)-tuples \((A_0,\ldots,A_{m-1})\) of pairwise disjoint nonempty subsets of \([n]\), with cell \(\prod_{i=0}^{m-1}\Delta(A_i)\). In its standard geometric realization, a point is a tuple \((p_0,\ldots,p_{m-1})\) of probability vectors on \([n]\) with pairwise disjoint nonempty supports. The generator acts by

\[
(p_0,\ldots,p_{m-1})\longmapsto
(\pi p_{m-1},\pi p_0,\ldots,\pi p_{m-2}).
\]

The claim is about its ordinary topological geometric fixed space under this simultaneous finite cyclic action; it is not a statement about homotopy fixed points or quotients. The source permutation must be a full \(m\)-cycle. The target permutation is unrestricted, including fixed colors and cycles of lengths not divisible by \(m\). The edge case \(m=n\) is included.

## Proof

**1. Fixed-point equations.** A tuple \(p\) is fixed by the generator exactly when \(p_i=\pi^i p_0\) for \(0\le i<m\) and \(p_0=\pi^m p_0\). Conversely any probability vector \(p_0\) satisfying these equations and having pairwise disjoint supports \(\operatorname{supp}(\pi^i p_0)\) gives a fixed point. Fixed points of the generated cyclic group are precisely those of the generator.

**2. One target cycle.** Let \(C\) be a cycle of \(\pi\) of length \(\ell\). The permutation \(\pi^m\) partitions \(C\) into \(d=\gcd(m,\ell)\) orbits, with \(\pi\) permuting these \(d\) orbits cyclically. Since \(p_0=\pi^m p_0\), every positive coordinate on such an orbit has equal mass, and the support of \(p_0\) is a union of these orbits. If it contains one orbit and \(d<m\), then two of the \(m\) supports \(\pi^i\operatorname{supp}(p_0)\), for \(0\le i<m\), overlap, contradicting the Hom condition. Therefore \(C\) can contribute positive mass only when \(d=m\), equivalently \(m\mid\ell\). In this case \(C\) consists of exactly \(m\) orbits under \(\pi^m\), one for each residue class modulo \(m\) along \(C\). The support of \(p_0\) can contain **at most one** of them; choosing any one gives \(m\) pairwise disjoint translated supports on \(C\).

**3. Gluing fixed cells.** For each of the \(q\) eligible target cycles \(C_j\), choose either no residue class or one of its \(m\) residue classes. If one is chosen, its probability weights in \(p_0\) are uniform on the \(\ell_j/m\) elements in that class; let \(t_j\) be its total mass. The \(t_j\) are nonnegative and sum to one. Thus fixing the choices on a nonempty subset \(J\) of eligible cycles gives a simplex of dimension \(|J|-1\), parameterized by \((t_j)_{j\in J}\). Its faces arise exactly when a coefficient \(t_j\) becomes zero. Residue classes on the same cycle are incompatible, while choices on distinct cycles are independent. These are exactly the simplices and face identifications of the join of \(q\) discrete \(m\)-point sets. Mapping their barycentric weights to the corresponding uniform probability vectors is a compatible affine bijection on each simplex, with an affine inverse. It yields the claimed PL homeomorphism. If \(q=0\), no probability vector of total mass one satisfies the support condition, so the fixed space is empty.

**4. Integral homotopy type.** Each discrete \(m\)-point complex has reduced integral homology \(\mathbb Z^{m-1}\) in degree zero and vanishing reduced homology otherwise. A join of \(q\) such complexes is pure shellable (a join of shellable zero-dimensional complexes); equivalently, the standard join splitting gives its homotopy type as a wedge of \((m-1)^q\) spheres of dimension \(q-1\). The join Künneth formula confirms this number and excludes torsion. For \(q=1\) the space is exactly \(m\) discrete points, consistent with the convention for wedges of zero-spheres. This completes the proof for every \(m,n,\pi\).

## Verification

The standalone Python file `verify.py` independently enumerates every candidate first-coordinate support that defines a setwise invariant Hom cell, requiring invariance under \(\pi^m\) and pairwise disjointness of its \(m\) target translates. It compares the resulting inclusion-poset supports with the residue-class partial-choice complex in every target cycle type through \(n=10\) and source cycle length \(2\le m\le\min(5,n)\). This covers 531 parameter combinations and 1,987 invariant nonempty cell supports. It also computes exact reduced mod-two homology of joins of \(q\in\{1,2,3\}\) copies of \(m\)-point sets for \(m\in\{2,3,4\}\), and checks additional mixed-length examples. The saved `verification.txt` records the complete reproducible output. Run `python3 verify.py` using Python's standard library. These bounded calculations corroborate, but do not replace, the proof above.

## Relationship to prior work

Babson and Kozlov's 1997 work on \(k\)-fold Boolean algebras treats an equivalent ordered-disjoint-subset poset and its symmetric quotients. Their 2003 paper, *Complexes of graph homomorphisms*, Definition 1.2, Proposition 4.5, and Proposition 4.6, establishes the general Hom-complex framework and ordinary sphere-wedge homotopy \(\operatorname{Hom}(K_m,K_n)\simeq\bigvee S^{n-m}\). Those results are prior work, not claimed as new here. The present claim classifies **geometric fixed spaces under a combined source-cycle and target-color permutation**, depending on divisibility of target cycle lengths; it is not a consequence of the ordinary homotopy type alone.

A published result titled *Full two-sided symmetry character of complete-graph Hom complexes* computes the rational \(S_m\times S_n\) top-homology character and certain color-quotient homologies. Its explicit cellular Lefschetz trace gives the Euler characteristic of each fixed locus, but not its homotopy type, PL model, component structure, or integral homology in other degrees. Direct comparison with its full result shows that this proposed fixed-space classification is a different, stronger topological type of statement; the character formula is acknowledged as prior coverage of numerical equivariant information.

Matsushita's paper on Hom-complex homotopy types treats equivariant aspects of graph coloring but does not formulate the simultaneous-cycle fixed-space decomposition. General deleted-product and diagonal-action terminology was included in searches. A 1997 full text could not be retrieved; an equivalent older theorem stated differently remains a publication-priority risk.

## Limitations

The theorem is specific to a full cycle on the source complete graph and does not classify fixed points for arbitrary source permutations or arbitrary subgroups of \(S_m\times S_n\). It does not determine the residual normalizer action, Borel cohomology, homotopy orbit spaces, or integral homology of orbit quotients. The result's geometric fixed spaces can have no fixed Hom vertices, so enumerating only equivariant colorings would be insufficient. Publication priority is not certified: relevant unindexed or inaccessible older work may contain an equivalent statement. Independent mathematical validation has not been performed.
