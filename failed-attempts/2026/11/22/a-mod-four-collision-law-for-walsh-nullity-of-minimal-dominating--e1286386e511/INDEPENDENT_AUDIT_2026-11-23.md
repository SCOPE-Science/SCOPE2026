# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

For shore sizes at least two, minimality leaves exactly the two entire shores and one vertex from each shore in a mixed set. The direct character sum is the product of the two shore sign sums plus the signs of the entire shores. Write x=a-2i, y=b-2j, e=(-1)^i, h=(-1)^j. If both sizes are odd, xy is odd; if both are even, the zero equation requires xy=0 and e=-h, giving exactly the stated parity-dependent overlap of the two central layers. For a=2r, b=2s+1, the opposite-sign class again gives x=0. In the equal-sign class x=±2, y=±1, e=-(-1)^r, h=(-1)^s sign(y), so y=-(-1)^(r+s) and x=-2(-1)^s. These weights exist even at the minimum admissible sizes and have exactly the stated binomial multiplicity. The triangle inequality is sharp at the trivial character (ab+2). Odd-odd admits |xy|=1 with opposite signs; an even shore admits its central weight and the other shore an equal sign, realizing absolute multiplier 2, the smallest possible positive even value. Stirling's estimate on central and adjacent binomials gives the stated fraction expansion, with the overlap or mixed correction O((ab)^(-1/2)).

As a new finding, this frozen record adds no worthwhile mathematical result: its main theorem is already published on the full domain and its additional comparable-size expansion is a routine estimate of already explicit central binomial terms. The finite replay and explicit parity signs are corroboration, not a surviving new contribution.
