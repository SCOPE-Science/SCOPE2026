# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

For s≥2 all displayed inequalities are genuine facets: a coordinate can be set to zero with the others small and positive, and on an edge its capacity can be split equally with all other coordinates sufficiently small since (s+1)/2<s. A tight-edge path component has one free alternating parameter. Anchoring one zero fixes it, while without an anchor both small signed perturbations are feasible; thus vertices have an anchor in each component. Their facet excess over d is the number of anchors in excess of one per component. Starting at a zero followed by a high edge cannot yield a second zero: the first coordinate after it is s+1 and a second tight step would give −1. Starting with a low edge gives the staircase (k,s−k) and first reaches a second zero after exactly 2s+1 edges. Below the respective first failures this is impossible; at the failures, the low-first staircase has no extra coordinate and the high-first one has exactly one extra endpoint that must either be zero or joined by the first high edge. Both are feasible, and each exceptional vertex has exactly one extra anchor. Appending zeros leaves the witnessing components and their facet excess unchanged. Changing signs of alternate columns exhibits the normals as the transpose of a reduced graph incidence matrix up to signs; total unimodularity gives integrality and smoothness at every simple vertex. A separate exact recomputation solved d-constraint bases by rational arithmetic, filtered feasible vertices and verified full active ranks over all 26 small cases. At the four walls for s=2,3 it found respectively 44,88,187,374 vertices, with 1,2,1,2 exceptional vertices and full active ranks; an independent count gave 68,589 feasible lattice points. The finite comparison corroborates rather than proves the infinite theorem.

## originality

FAIL

The exact formulas are not quoted in the inspected prior texts. The decisive evidence is instead a concrete specialization of Pegel's earlier marked-order face theorem, not an unsuccessful keyword search. For capacities bᵢ, let a₁=0 and aᵢ₊₁=aᵢ+(−1)ⁱ⁻¹bᵢ. Under yᵢ=aᵢ+(−1)ⁱ⁻¹xᵢ, the constraints become the order inequalities of a fence with a marked leaf of value aᵢ at each vertex: y_odd≤y_even on edges, and yᵢ≥aᵢ for odd i and yᵢ≤aᵢ for even i. This is a unimodular affine change for the integer capacities. By Proposition 3.2 and Theorem 3.14, vertex blocks are connected, each has a mark, and multiple marks in a block must agree. The Hasse graph is a tree: with b blocks there are 2d−b internal covers, hence that many incident facets since the elementary strict-inequality witnesses verify every cover is a genuine facet. Thus distinct marks force d blocks at vertices and simplicity. The low-first marks are (−k,s−k) on the successive odd-even pairs for k≥0; the high-first marks are (k,s+1+k). The first repetitions are respectively a₁=a₂ₛ₊₂=0 and a₂=a₂ₛ₊₃=s+1. At either first repetition a block creating excess must contain the whole path between the tied leaves, and its constant value meets all intervening one-sided leaf bounds. The path takes up all coordinates in the low-first case; in the high-first case the remaining vertex has only its own leaf value 0 or the joined block value s+1. This gives exactly d−1 blocks and d+1 facets at the one or two exceptions. The witnesses extend by appending zeros, and standard incidence unimodularity then turns the simple cutoffs into Delzant cutoffs. This requires neither a new face criterion nor a difficult partition census: the full result is recovered by elementary affine encoding and first-repeat and endpoint arithmetic in a stronger prior framework. The Ehrhart, Gorenstein and Euclidean-contact papers are not the basis for the negative originality decision.

## value

FAIL

Delzantness is a meaningful invariant and the two orientations are natural, but this particular exact cutoff does not clear the value bar for a mechanically implied specialization. After the standard signed marked-order reformulation, the dimension cutoff is a first repeated entry in two arithmetic sequences; the stated first-wall vertices and extra facet are forced by the unique tree path and at most one remaining endpoint. The finite examples are corroboration only and cannot turn this into a distinct scientific result. No separately useful new structural lemma or nonroutine surviving subclaim is shown in the frozen record.

The dated certificate retains the supplied scientific assessment, sources and limitations.
