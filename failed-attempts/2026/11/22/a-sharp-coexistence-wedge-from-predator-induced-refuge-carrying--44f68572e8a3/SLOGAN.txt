In a predator–prey model whose carrying capacity emerges from rapid refuge switching, two stable ecological outcomes coexist below the predator's linear invasion threshold exactly when \(2(\sqrt2-1)<\lambda=b/\mu-1<1\) and \(0<a<(1+\lambda)(\lambda-2\sqrt{1-\lambda})/\lambda^2\). A sharp saddle-node curve separates this coexistence wedge from predator extinction, and an exact rational example exhibits two sinks divided by a saddle.
