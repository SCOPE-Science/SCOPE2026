The stationary backward Poisson reset intervals give the compound-Poisson perpetuity; finite third moment suffices by summing its L3 norms. Its cumulants through degree three are those of one interval divided by 1-c^k at order k. Centering the generator gives L y=-q(1-c)y and L(y^2-V)=-q(1-c^2)(y^2-V)+2λm1(1-c)y. The first polynomial mode then gives identical covariance at every lag. The frozen checker was read rather than run; the proof is not a finite test. Zero contrast at the root is not a reversibility claim.

## originality

FAIL

The exact contrast and its qualitative refinement are direct corollaries of the prior stationary moment and Poisson affine-reset cross-moment formulas. Applying the latter in polynomial degrees one and two and integrating the initial state against the established invariant law gives the displayed two-rate contrast. The sign classification then requires only evaluating one increasing quadratic at its endpoints. The explicit laws are valid, independently useful illustrations, but their existence and matching conditions are direct two-point moment interpolation once the prior formulas are specialized. No assertion is made that the older papers print this particular threshold or these numerical examples. The reason for failure is demonstrated mathematical coverage, not lack of an exact-title hit.

## value

PASS

The statistic is a motivated diagnostic of temporal asymmetry that cannot be inferred from the complete covariance function. The positive jump-law witnesses give a transparent illustration, and the sharp all-lag sign criterion identifies which moment makes a reversal possible. That explanatory usefulness does not overcome the prior-coverage failure or imply an entropy-production interpretation.
