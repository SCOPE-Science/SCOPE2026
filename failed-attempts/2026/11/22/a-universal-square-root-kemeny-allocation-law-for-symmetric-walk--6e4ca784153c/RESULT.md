# A universal square-root Kemeny allocation law for symmetric walks on leaf coronas

## Finding
Let \(H\) be any finite connected simple graph on \(n\ge2\) vertices that is vertex-transitive and edge-transitive, and let its degree be \(d\ge1\). Fix an integer \(r\ge1\). Form \(G\) by attaching \(r\) private degree-one vertices to every vertex of \(H\). Among **all** irreducible symmetric entrywise-nonnegative stochastic transition matrices supported on edges of \(G\), with self-loops allowed and all individual transition probabilities freely variable, the minimum *zero-target* Kemeny constant is attained at exactly one matrix.

Write \(\lambda_2,\ldots,\lambda_n>0\) for the nonzero combinatorial-Laplacian eigenvalues of the unweighted core \(H\), and define
\[
 C_H=\sum_{j=2}^{n}\lambda_j^{-1}=\operatorname{tr}(L_H^+),\quad
 A=r\left(n-\frac1{r+1}\right),\quad R=rA,\quad S=(r+1)dC_H.
\]
For the unique optimal transition matrix, every core edge has probability \(a_*\) in each direction and every pendant edge has probability \(b_*\) in each direction, where
\[
 u_* =\frac{\sqrt R}{\sqrt R+\sqrt S},\qquad
 b_*=\frac{u_*}{r},\qquad
 a_*=\frac{1-u_*}{d}.
\]
Every core self-loop is zero; each pendant self-loop has probability \(1-b_*>0\). The exact minimum is
\[
 \boxed{\mathcal K_*(G)=(\sqrt R+\sqrt S)^2.}
\]
This is an unrestricted optimum: no asymmetric choice of probabilities can tie it.

Two consequences show sharply different behaviors as the core grows, with fixed \(r\):

- For \(H=C_n\), \(n\ge3\), \(C_H=(n^2-1)/12\), \(b_*\sim\sqrt{6/((r+1)n)}\), and \(\mathcal K_*(G)\sim (r+1)n^2/6\). The conventional maximum-degree symmetric walk, assigning each edge probability \(1/(r+2)\), has Kemeny cost divided by the optimum tending to \((r+2)/2\).
- For \(H=K_n\), \(C_H=(n-1)/n\), \(b_*\to1/(r+\sqrt{r+1})\), and \(\mathcal K_*(G)\sim(r+\sqrt{r+1})^2n\). For the conventional maximum-degree walk the cost/optimum ratio grows linearly with \(n\), with its ratio divided by \(n\) tending to \(r/(r+\sqrt{r+1})^2\).

In particular, for one private leaf per core vertex, the optimized pendant probability on long cycles decays as \(\sqrt{3/n}\), whereas on large complete cores it approaches \(\sqrt2-1\). The corresponding maximum-degree penalties tend to \(3/2\) and grow as \(n/(3+2\sqrt2)\), respectively.

## Assumptions and scope
The core must be connected, simple, vertex-transitive, and edge-transitive; no bipartiteness, nonbipartiteness, or aperiodicity is required. The private leaves are distinct and have no neighbors except their own core vertex. All transition matrices are symmetric, nonnegative, row-stochastic, irreducible, and have zero off-diagonal entries outside graph edges. Self-loops at any vertex are allowed. Thus the stationary law is uniform on the \(N=n(r+1)\) vertices. Define the spectral zero-target convention by
\[
 \mathcal K(P)=\sum_{j=2}^{N}\frac1{1-\lambda_j(P)}
 =\operatorname{tr}\left[(I-P+J_N/N)^{-1}\right]-1,
\]
where eigenvalues are counted with multiplicity and \(J_N\) is the all-ones matrix. Definitions of Kemeny's constant that count an additional stationary mean-return term differ by \(+1\); no result here uses that alternative normalization.

The exact quantities depend on the core only through \((n,d,C_H)\). The claimed uniqueness is among **all** feasible matrices, not only automorphism-invariant matrices. All integer parameters are in the indicated ranges; limits hold with \(r\) fixed as \(n\to\infty\) along the specified core families.

## Proof
**1. Strict convexity and forced symmetry.** Put \(Q=I-P+J_N/N\). On every connected feasible support, \(Q\) is positive definite: the graph Laplacian \(I-P\) has one-dimensional kernel generated by the all-ones vector and is positive definite on its orthogonal complement. For a nonzero symmetric direction \(D\),
\[
 \frac{d^2}{dt^2}\operatorname{tr}[(Q+tD)^{-1}]
 =2\operatorname{tr}\big[(Q+tD)^{-1}D(Q+tD)^{-1}D(Q+tD)^{-1}\big]>0
\]
whenever \(Q+tD\) is positive definite. Positivity follows by writing the trace as \(2\operatorname{tr}(B^2(Q+tD)^{-1})\) with symmetric \(B=(Q+tD)^{-1/2}D(Q+tD)^{-1/2}\ne0\). Hence \(\mathcal K\) is strictly convex in \(P\) over connected feasible matrices.

Every automorphism of \(H\), together with independent permutations of private leaves attached to each core vertex, induces an automorphism of \(G\). Averaging any feasible \(P\) over this finite group preserves feasibility and irreducibility and cannot increase \(\mathcal K\). By strict convexity, a minimizer must already be invariant under this group. Vertex and edge transitivity give exactly two off-diagonal edge orbits: core edges and private-leaf edges. Invariant probabilities are therefore \(a\) and \(b\) respectively, with \(a>0\), \(b>0\), \(da+rb\le1\), and \(b\le1\). The remaining diagonal probabilities are \(1-da-rb\) at core vertices and \(1-b\) at private leaves.

**2. Exact spectral transfer.** Let \(\mu\) be an eigenvalue of \(L_H\). On the subspace in which the \(r\) leaves at every core vertex move together, the corona Laplacian \(I-P\) acts on the corresponding core eigenspace through
\[
 M_\mu=\begin{pmatrix}a\mu+rb&-\sqrt r\,b\\-\sqrt r\,b&b\end{pmatrix}.
\]
There are also \(n(r-1)\) independent leaf-difference eigenvectors with eigenvalue \(b\). For \(\mu>0\), \(\det M_\mu=a\mu b\) and \(\operatorname{tr}M_\mu=a\mu+(r+1)b\), so the sum of reciprocals of its two eigenvalues is
\[
 \frac{\operatorname{tr}M_\mu}{\det M_\mu}
 =\frac1b+\frac{r+1}{a\mu}.
\]
For \(\mu=0\), the two eigenvalues are \(0\) and \((r+1)b\). Consequently, combining all strictly positive eigenvalues gives exactly
\[
 \mathcal K(a,b)
 =\frac{n(r-1)+(n-1)+1/(r+1)}{b}+\frac{r+1}{a}C_H
 =\frac{A}{b}+\frac{(r+1)C_H}{a}.
\]

**3. Solve the entire feasible region.** Because \(C_H>0\), this expression is strictly decreasing in \(a\) at each fixed \(b\). Therefore every minimizer saturates the core row constraint \(da+rb=1\). Set \(u=rb\). Connectedness forces \(0<u<1\), and substitution gives
\[
 \mathcal K=\frac{R}{u}+\frac{S}{1-u}.
\]
This strictly convex function diverges at both endpoints. Its unique critical point solves \(R/u^2=S/(1-u)^2\), which gives the stated \(u_*\), \(a_*\), \(b_*\), and \(\mathcal K_*=(\sqrt R+\sqrt S)^2\). Both optimal edge probabilities are positive, so the proposed walk is irreducible. Strict convexity from Step 1 rules out every other, including asymmetric, minimizer.

**4. Two families and the conventional allocation.** On \(C_n\), the nonzero eigenvalues are \(2-2\cos(2\pi j/n)\); the classical reciprocal trigonometric sum gives \(C_H=(n^2-1)/12\). On \(K_n\), all \(n-1\) positive eigenvalues equal \(n\), hence \(C_H=(n-1)/n\). Substituting into \(R,S\) and taking leading terms yields all claimed limits.

The maximum-degree symmetric walk assigns probability \(1/(d+r)\) to every permissible edge and absorbs the remaining probability into self-loops. It therefore has
\[
 \mathcal K_{\mathrm{md}}=(d+r)\big[A+(r+1)C_H\big].
\]
On cycles, its leading cost is \((r+2)(r+1)n^2/12\) versus the optimum \((r+1)n^2/6\). On cliques, it is \(rn^2+o(n^2)\) versus the optimum \((r+\sqrt{r+1})^2n+o(n)\). The claimed ratios follow.

## Verification
The standalone program `check_corona.py` builds unweighted cycle, clique and hypercube cores, attaches one to three leaves per vertex, constructs the full rational corona Laplacian independently of the block calculation, and compares its inverse trace with the exact transfer identity. It additionally checks the radical minimizer algebra and large-size asymptotics for multiple leaf multiplicities. Running `python check_corona.py` with Python 3 and SymPy 1.14 produced `PASS: 775 independent algebraic/matrix/asymptotic assertions`. The matrix tests are finite consistency checks; the preceding strict-convexity and spectral-block argument proves the full infinite family.

## Relationship to prior work
Hunter's analysis of Kemeny's constant (arXiv:1208.4716, first public on 2012-08-23) provides the general stationary-target hitting-time setting. Agharkar, Patel and Bullo (2014), *Robotic surveillance and Markov chains with minimal first passage time*, establish a general convex and SDP formulation for reversible Kemeny optimization. Those general frameworks are not asserted as new here. The present claim is the closed-form, unique, unrestricted optimizer for all transitive multi-leaf coronas, including the sharp contrasting cycle/clique asymptotics.

For related but **different** performance criteria, Boyd, Diaconis and Xiao (2004) optimize second-largest eigenvalue modulus, and the published record titled *Universal fastest mixing and pendant-edge rigidity on vertex- and edge-transitive leaf coronas* studies fastest mixing for the same graph class. Neither spectral mixing criterion is the spectral **inverse-sum** minimized here. A 2026 study of reversible Kemeny minimization via structure-preserving perturbations supplies general algorithms rather than this explicit corona family law. These distinctions are statements about the inspected material, not a proof that no equivalent formulation exists anywhere.

## Limitations
The theorem assumes symmetric (hence uniform-stationary) stochastic transitions, a connected vertex- and edge-transitive unweighted core, and exactly \(r\) private leaves per core vertex. It does not establish optimality for nonsymmetric chains or for a prescribed nonuniform stationary distribution. Numerical verification does not replace the proof. It remains possible that an equivalent form is present in unindexed specialized literature; independent external review has not been performed.
