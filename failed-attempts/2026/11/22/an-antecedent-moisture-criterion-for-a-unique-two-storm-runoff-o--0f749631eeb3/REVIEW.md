# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

The absolutely continuous solution of the loss equation multiplies post-first storage by q=exp(-K). Summing the overflows gives the displayed positive-part identity. If neither first pulse overflows, the small-first sequence has the larger second overflow argument by (1-q)(A-B), so the difference is nonpositive. If both first pulses saturate, they have the same post-first storage and their first-runoff difference A-B cannot be exceeded by the opposite second-runoff difference, by the 1-Lipschitz positive part. These cases include s+A=C and s+B=C respectively. In the strict straddling case put y=s+B and q0=s/y. The larger-first second overflow becomes active only above (C-B)/C, strictly above q0 because y<C. Until that activation the sign is the sign of s-qy: the larger-first first overflow is positive, and the small-first second positive part exceeds, equals, or falls short of that positive value precisely as qy exceeds, equals, or falls short of s. Above activation the difference simplifies to (1-q)(y-C)<0. This gives one zero in q and the stated strict signs in K, with arbitrary nonnegative integrable proportional-loss profiles.
