# Complete Frobenius descent of the thirty non-pairing genus-three Richelot kernels

## Finding

Let \(C/\mathbb F_q\) be any smooth genus-three hyperelliptic curve in odd characteristic, with eight geometric branch points \(B\) of its hyperelliptic double cover. Of the \(135\) geometric maximal Weil-isotropic subgroups of \(J(C)[2]\), exactly \(105\) are generated by differences of paired branch points (the *pairing* or *tractable* kernels), and exactly \(30\) have no nonzero difference of two branch points. The latter are canonically the \(30\) affine \(\mathbb F_2^3\)-structures on the eight-point set \(B\) modulo affine changes of coordinates. Their Frobenius cycle type is determined by, and explicitly tabulated below for, all \(22\) possible branch splitting partitions of eight.

In particular, if the branch divisor has one rational point and one orbit of length seven, then **two non-pairing maximal isotropic subgroup schemes descend over \(\mathbb F_q\), no pairing subgroup scheme descends, and the Jacobian has no nonzero rational two-torsion**. The other \(28\) non-pairing kernels form four Frobenius orbits of length seven, so the reduced non-pairing kernel-scheme zeta function is
\[
 Z_{\mathrm{np}}(T)=\frac{1}{(1-T)^2(1-T^7)^4}.
\]
This occurs for the explicit smooth genus-three curve \(y^2=x(x^7+x+1)\) over \(\mathbb F_5\).

## Assumptions and scope

The ground field is a finite field \(k=\mathbb F_q\) of odd characteristic; no restriction on \(q\bmod n\), automorphism group, or geometric simplicity is made. A kernel *descends* exactly when its geometric subgroup is Frobenius stable, equivalently when it defines a finite étale \(k\)-subgroup scheme of \(J(C)[2]\). This does **not** assert that its nonidentity points are rational, that an explicit Jacobian isogeny is available, or that the principally polarized quotient is the Jacobian of a curve over \(k\) itself.

The object counted is the reduced finite étale scheme of maximal Weil-isotropic order-eight subgroup schemes with no geometric difference of two branch points. For a given branch factorization partition the table applies if that partition is realized over the specified field; not all partitions are realizable over every small field.

## Proof

**Even-subset symplectic model.** Let \(B\) be the eight geometric branch points. The two-torsion is canonically, as a Frobenius module,
\[
V=J(C)[2](\overline{k})\simeq
\{S\subseteq B:|S|\equiv0\pmod2\}/(S\sim B\setminus S),
\]
with addition by symmetric difference and alternating Weil pairing \(\langle[S],[T]\rangle=|S\cap T|\bmod2\). The space has dimension six over \(\mathbb F_2\), and the number of Lagrangian three-spaces is
\[
(2+1)(2^2+1)(2^3+1)=135.
\]

**The \(105+30\) dichotomy.** Every perfect matching of \(B\) into four pairs supplies a Lagrangian generated by its four pair classes; its unique pair classes recover the matching, giving \(8!/(2^4 4!)=105\) distinct subgroups. Fixing a pair class \(v\), the symplectic quotient \(v^\perp/\langle v\rangle\) has dimension four and exactly \((2+1)(2^2+1)=15\) Lagrangian planes. Precisely \(15\) distinct perfect matchings contain the fixed pair, so **every** Lagrangian containing any pair class is a matching kernel. Thus the other \(30\) Lagrangians have seven nonzero classes all represented by complementary pairs of four-subsets. The seven affine hyperplane partitions of an affine \(\mathbb F_2^3\)-structure form just such an isotropic subgroup: two distinct hyperplanes intersect in two points, and symmetric difference modulo complementation closes the seven functional classes. Different affine structures have different hyperplane families. There are \(8!/|\operatorname{AGL}_3(\mathbb F_2)|=30\) structures; hence they exhaust the remaining thirty Lagrangians. A second independent enumeration of all \(8!\) affine labellings verifies this bijection. Its stabilizer is \(\operatorname{AGL}_3(\mathbb F_2)\) of order \(8\cdot168=1344\), with index \(8!/1344=30\) in \(S_8\).

**Frobenius and exhaustive table.** The action of arithmetic Frobenius on \(V\) comes directly from its permutation of \(B\). Therefore its action on the thirty-point scheme is the action on \(S_8/\operatorname{AGL}_3(\mathbb F_2)\). Every permutation of eight labels is conjugate to exactly one of the \(22\) partition representatives shown. For each representative, exact orbit traversal of the affine structures yields the table. The independent Lagrangian enumeration and affine-hyperplane enumeration agree on each of the \(22\) cycle types. Exponents in a displayed cycle expression indicate the *number of cycles*, not the number of elements.

**Why the seven-cycle exception is exact.** A Frobenius permutation with one fixed branch point and one seven-cycle has no invariant perfect matching, so it fixes no tractable kernel. On the thirty affine structures, the number of fixed points of such a seven-cycle is \(2\): there are \(48\) elements of order seven in \(\mathrm{GL}_3(\mathbb F_2)\), each of its eight affine translates has exactly one fixed point and one seven-cycle, hence \(384\) elements of cycle type \((1,7)\) in the affine stabilizer. There are \(8!/7=5760\) seven-cycles of that type in \(S_8\), so the permutation-coset fixed-point formula gives
\[
30\cdot\frac{384}{5760}=2.
\]
All remaining orbits have length seven, giving four seven-cycles. There are no fixed nonzero elements of the even-subset quotient \(V\), because a seven-cycle acting on \(B\) has no nontrivial stable even subset modulo complements (an odd-order permutation cannot exchange a subset with its complement). Consequently \(J(C)(\mathbb F_q)[2]=\{0\}\).

**Concrete realization.** Over \(\mathbb F_5\), the polynomial \(h(x)=x^7+x+1\) has degree seven, \(x^{5^7}\equiv x\pmod h\), and \(\gcd(h,x^5-x)=1\). Rabin's prime-degree irreducibility criterion therefore proves \(h\) irreducible. Since \(h(0)=1\), the degree-eight polynomial \(xh\) is squarefree. Its smooth hyperelliptic normalization has genus three and branch partition \((1,7)\), so the displayed arithmetic phenomena occur without further genericity assumptions.

## Verification

Run `python3 verify_exotic_richelot.py` with Python three and no third-party packages. The checker constructs all \(135\) symplectic Lagrangian subspaces, all \(105\) matching subspaces, and all \(30\) affine-hyperplane structures independently from \(8!\) label bijections. It proves their coincidence, traverses the \(22\) possible branch permutation types independently in both thirty-point models, verifies orbit lengths sum to thirty, enumerates \(168\) invertible linear maps with exactly \(48\) order-seven elements, and checks irreducibility of the \(\mathbb F_5\) example using polynomial arithmetic. The machine-readable `orbit_profiles.json` records the same complete twenty-two-row arithmetic classification.

## Relationship to prior work

B. Smith's 2008/2009 work, *Isogenies and the Discrete Logarithm Problem in Jacobians of Genus 3 Hyperelliptic Curves*, §3 Proposition 1, Remark 3, Lemma 1, and §9, develops the pairing (*tractable*) kernels, proves their rationality criterion and tabulates the number of rational tractable groups by branch factorization. Smith explicitly notes that non-tractable maximal isotropic groups exist. Consequently the existence of other groups or the fact that pairing groups can be absent is **not** presented as new. Moriya–Kudo (first posted 2022-09-07) develops genus-three Richelot arithmetic and decomposed-isogeny constructions, while Ohashi–Onuki–Kudo–Yoshizumi–Nuida (2024) treats all \(135\) isogenies in a theta-based superspecial graph. The additional result here is the full thirty-point **non-pairing** Galois cycle table and its concrete two-rational-kernels/no-rational-two-torsion consequence for a seven-cycle branch pattern.

A published classification titled *Rational (2,2,2)-isogeny kernels in a genus-three Klein-four pencil* concerns rational kernel counts on a restricted highly symmetric pencil, not the full twenty-two branch partitions or the thirty non-pairing orbit spectra. Its indexed summary, not the entire article, was accessible; no claim of whole-document exclusion is inferred from that limited inspection.

## Limitations

This is a classification of **kernel subgroup schemes**, not a formula for an explicit isogeny or its target curve. It does not classify the isomorphism type of the quotient abelian variety, or assert that a principally polarized quotient is a Jacobian over the original ground field. It does not assert that every abstract branch partition occurs for every field size. The affine-structure interpretation is standard finite geometry; the novelty assessment concerns the complete arithmetic orbit table and the precise non-pairing descent phenomenon, not the ambient group theory or the known \(135\)-kernel count. Published computations not indexed under the searched terminology could contain an equivalent orbit table.
