# Complete prescribed-commutator fibers in finite abelian regular wreath products

## Finding

Let \(A\) and \(B\) be finite abelian groups, written additively, with \(|A|=m\ge 2\), \(|B|=N\ge 1\), and let \(G=A^B\rtimes B\) be the regular wreath product. For \(z\in A^B\) and \(u\in B\), put
\[
F_G(z,u)=\#\{(g,h)\in G^2:ghg^{-1}h^{-1}=(z,u)\}.
\]
For \(s,t\in B\), write \(H_{s,t}=\langle s,t\rangle\), \(c_{s,t}=[B:H_{s,t}]\), and set \(\delta_H(z)=1\) precisely when the sum of the entries of \(z\) on every coset of \(H\) vanishes in \(A\); otherwise set \(\delta_H(z)=0\). Then the entire commutator word distribution is
\[
\boxed{F_G(z,u)=\begin{cases}
0,&u\ne0,\\
\displaystyle\sum_{s,t\in B}m^{N+c_{s,t}}\delta_{H_{s,t}}(z),&u=0.
\end{cases}}
\]
No field structure, coprimality assumption, or restriction on the exponents of \(A\) and \(B\) is needed.

For \(B=C_n\), \(n\ge2\), denote by \(S_d(z)\) the condition
\[
\sum_{j\equiv i\pmod d}z_j=0\text{ in }A\qquad(0\le i<d),
\]
for each divisor \(d\mid n\). The formula becomes the explicit divisor sum
\[
\boxed{F_G(z,0)=\sum_{d\mid n}J_2(n/d)m^{n+d}\mathbf1_{S_d(z)},}
\]
where \(J_2(k)=k^2\prod_{p\mid k}(1-p^{-2})\) and \(J_2(1)=1\). The commutator image is exactly the augmentation-zero subgroup \(\{(z,0):\sum_j z_j=0\}\). Moreover, **all nonidentity elements of this commutator subgroup have equal commutator-fiber size if and only if \(n\) is prime**. For prime \(n\), every nonidentity derived target has \((n^2-1)m^{n+1}\) preimages.

For composite \(n\), fiber multiplicities need not be totally ordered by the divisors: when \(n=10\) and \(A=C_2\), targets supported at positions \(0,2\) and at \(0,5\), each with nonzero lamp value, both have exactly \(245760\) preimages, whereas a target supported at \(0,1\) has \(147456\).

## Assumptions and scope

An element of \(A^B\) is a function \(x:B\to A\). For \(s\in B\), let \((\sigma^s x)(v)=x(v-s)\). The multiplication in \(G\) is
\[
(x,s)(y,t)=(x+\sigma^s y,s+t).
\]
The group commutator is \([g,h]=ghg^{-1}h^{-1}\). Targets are ordered pairs \((z,u)\), and fibers count ordered pairs \((g,h)\), including equal pairs and the identity. For \(B=C_n\) the positions are \(0,\ldots,n-1\) modulo \(n\). The indicator \(\mathbf1_{S_d(z)}\) is a statement about sums in the additive group \(A\), not about integer sums unless \(A\) is cyclic.

## Proof

**1. Exact commutator coordinates.** Since \(B\) is abelian, \((x,s)(y,t)\) and \((y,t)(x,s)\) have the same second component. Thus
\[
[(x,s),(y,t)]=((I-\sigma^t)x+(\sigma^s-I)y,0).
\]
In particular the map has no value with a nonzero second component. For fixed \(s,t\), it is a homomorphism \(\Phi_{s,t}:A^B\oplus A^B\to A^B\).

**2. Image is the zero-sum space on subgroup cosets.** A coordinate vector \(a e_v\) means \(a\in A\) in coordinate \(v\) and zero elsewhere. The images of \(I-\sigma^t\) and \(\sigma^s-I\) are generated by vectors of the form \(a(e_v-e_{v+t})\) and \(a(e_{v+s}-e_v)\). Construct the undirected Cayley graph on vertex set \(B\) with edges given by steps \(s\) and \(t\). Its connected components are precisely the cosets of \(H_{s,t}=\langle s,t\rangle\), of which there are \(c_{s,t}\).

Every edge difference has zero coordinate sum on its component, so \(\operatorname{im}\Phi_{s,t}\) lies in the group of vectors with zero sum on each coset. Conversely, on a spanning tree of any component, repeatedly subtract edge differences to move the value at each nonroot vertex to the root. This realizes every configuration with zero total sum on that component; the argument works over **any** abelian group \(A\) and requires no division. Therefore
\[
\operatorname{im}\Phi_{s,t}=\{z:\delta_{H_{s,t}}(z)=1\}.
\]
The coset-sum map \(A^B\to A^{B/H_{s,t}}\) is surjective, by putting an arbitrary element on one coordinate of each coset. Its kernel has size \(m^{N-c_{s,t}}\). Since the domain of \(\Phi_{s,t}\) has cardinality \(m^{2N}\), every attained value has exactly \(m^{N+c_{s,t}}\) preimages. Summing over the \(|B|^2\) choices of \((s,t)\) proves the first boxed formula.

**3. Cyclic divisor form.** For \(B=C_n\), the subgroup generated by \(s,t\) is the subgroup of multiples of \(d=\gcd(n,s,t)\), and its index is \(d\). The cosets are the residue classes modulo \(d\), giving exactly \(S_d(z)\). There are \(J_2(n/d)\) ordered pairs \((s,t)\) for which this gcd equals \(d\): divide \(s,t\) by \(d\) and count pairs modulo \(n/d\) which generate the whole cyclic group, by inclusion–exclusion over its prime divisors. Consequently the second boxed formula holds, including \(n\) divisible by the exponent of \(A\).

**4. Exact image and prime criterion.** Every commutator has total lamp sum zero by Step 2. Conversely, choose \(s=1,t=0\) in \(C_n\): its generated subgroup is all of \(C_n\), and Step 2 shows that every augmentation-zero target is attained. If \(n\) is prime, its only divisors are \(1,n\). A nonzero augmentation-zero vector satisfies \(S_1\) but not \(S_n\), because \(S_n(z)\) says every coordinate vanishes. Its fiber therefore has size \(J_2(n)m^{n+1}=(n^2-1)m^{n+1}\).

If \(n\) is composite, choose a proper divisor \(d\) with \(1<d<n\) and a nonzero \(a\in A\). Let \(z_1=a e_0-a e_1\), and \(z_d=a e_0-a e_d\). Both are nonzero augmentation-zero vectors. The first satisfies \(S_e\) exactly for \(e=1\), whereas the second satisfies \(S_e\) for every divisor \(e\) of \(d\), including \(e=d\). As all summands in the formula are positive, \(F_G(z_d,0)>F_G(z_1,0)\). This establishes the equivalence.

**5. Illustration of different divisors with equal multiplicities.** For \(n=10\), \(m=2\), the contribution from \(d=1\) is \(J_2(10)2^{11}=72\cdot2^{11}=147456\). The extra contribution for a vector supported at positions \(0,2\) is \(J_2(5)2^{12}=24\cdot2^{12}=98304\); that for support \(0,5\) is \(J_2(2)2^{15}=3\cdot2^{15}=98304\). These yield the stated equality \(245760\). Neither support satisfies \(S_{10}\), and each satisfies no additional divisor condition.

## Verification

The exact finite-group checker `checks/verify.py` enumerates ordered commutator pairs and compares every target fiber with the subgroup-coset formula for eleven configurations, including cyclic complements of orders two through five, the noncyclic complement \(C_2\times C_2\), and lamps \(C_2,C_3,C_4,C_2\times C_2\). Additional direct comparisons check the cyclic Jordan-totient specialization for four configurations. All tests passed; the complete recorded output is `checks/output.txt`. The proof above, not finite enumeration, establishes the general theorem.

## Relationship to prior work

Erovenko and Sury, *Commutativity Degrees of Wreath Products of Finite Abelian Groups* (2008), Theorem 1.1, prove the **identity fiber** \(\sum_{s,t\in B}m^{N+[B:\langle s,t\rangle]}\), including the cyclic gcd specialization. Their Section 3 solves commuting equations by coset systems. The present statement **credits** that formula and extends the calculation to **every fixed nonidentity commutator target**, recording the exact coset-sum conditions, zero fibers and the primality criterion for uniform nonidentity fibers. It is not a new identity-fiber or commuting-probability formula.

Riedl, *Commutator Calculus for Wreath Product Groups* (2015), studies commutator operators and central series of finite regular wreath-product groups; its abstract does not state a prescribed-output distribution. The complete paper was not available for comparison. Cox, *The Degree of Commutativity and Lamplighter Groups* (2016), treats infinite cyclic top groups and a growth-based degree, not the finite pointwise word distribution here. Searches of related finite-group word-map results located no explicit covering theorem. This is evidence for a provisional originality assessment, **not** proof of historical uniqueness.

## Limitations

The formula is a complete computable expression rather than a simple closed form for the histogram of fiber sizes for all arbitrary \(A,B\). Distinct coset-vanishing profiles can have equal numerical fiber sizes (as at \(n=10\)). The general finite abelian complement need not have every augmentation-zero target attained by a **single** commutator; only the cyclic specialization has that surjectivity result here. Historical equivalence in older commutator-calculus work cannot be excluded without further full-text comparison. There is no independent audit or machine-checked formal proof.
