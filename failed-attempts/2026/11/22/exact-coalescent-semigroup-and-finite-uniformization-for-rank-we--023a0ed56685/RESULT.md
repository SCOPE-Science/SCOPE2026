# Exact coalescent semigroup and finite uniformization for rank-weighted KL proximal maps

## Finding

A convex rank penalty on a probability simplex produces an unusual **exact composition law** for the Kullback–Leibler proximal operator, despite the absence of a general semigroup property for KL proximal mappings. For any integer \(n\ge2\), descending nonconstant weights \(a_1\ge\cdots\ge a_n\), and strictly positive probability input \(y\), define

\[
J_a(p)=\sum_{i=1}^n a_i p_{(i)},\qquad
T_t(y)=\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{p\in\Delta_n}
\left\{\sum_{i=1}^n p_i\log\frac{p_i}{y_i}+tJ_a(p)\right\},\quad t\ge0,
\]

where \(p_{(1)}\ge\cdots\ge p_{(n)}\), and \(0\log 0=0\). The minimizer is unique and strictly positive. The first claim is

\[
\boxed{T_s(T_t(y))=T_{s+t}(y)\quad\text{for all }s,t\ge0.}
\]

Write the input in descending order \(y_{\sigma(1)}\ge\cdots\ge y_{\sigma(n)}\), let \(z_i=\log y_{\sigma(i)}\), and \(\bar z=n^{-1}\sum_i z_i\), \(\bar a=n^{-1}\sum_i a_i\). The **exact first time of permanent uniformity** is

\[
\boxed{\tau_*(y,a)=\max_{1\le k<n}
\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{k}(z_i-\bar z)}{\sum_{i=1}^{k}(a_i-\bar a)}.}
\]

Every denominator is strictly positive. Exactly when \(t\ge\tau_*(y,a)\), the output is \((1/n,\ldots,1/n)\); for smaller \(t\) the output is nonuniform. This includes \(\tau_*=0\) for uniform input. The full trajectory is computed by ordinary Euclidean isotonic regression of shifted log-probabilities:

\[
 v(t)=P_C(z-ta),\quad C=\{v_1\ge\cdots\ge v_n\},\qquad
 T_t(y)_{\sigma(i)}=\frac{\exp v_i(t)}{\sum_j\exp v_j(t)}.
\]

## Assumptions and scope

All spaces in this statement are finite dimensional and real. The simplex is \(\Delta_n=\{p_i\ge0,\ \sum_i p_i=1\}\). For \(t=0\), \(T_0(y)=y\). The weights may have either sign, but they must be descending and not all equal. Adding a common constant to all weights changes the objective by a constant and not the minimizer. Ties among input coordinates, and ties arising during the evolution, are allowed. The ordering permutation within a tied group has no effect on the output. The claim applies to strictly positive initial inputs; no boundary-input extension is asserted.

## Proof

**1. Ordering and positive minimizer.** The ordered-weighted functional \(J_a\) is the support function of the permutahedron generated by \(a\), so it is convex and finite. The KL divergence is strictly convex on the simplex. At any candidate boundary minimizer, moving an arbitrarily small mass from a positive coordinate to a zero coordinate decreases the KL term with derivative tending to negative infinity, while the rank penalty changes by at most a bounded multiple of the movement. The minimizer is therefore interior and unique. Suppose \(y_i>y_j\) but \(p_i<p_j\); interchanging these two \(p\)-coordinates leaves the entropy term and \(J_a\) unchanged, but strictly decreases \(-\sum p_i\log y_i\). Hence the solution has the same descending order as \(y\), and equal input entries lead to equal output entries by uniqueness and permutation symmetry.

**2. Isotonic logarithmic formula.** Work in that fixed descending order and put \(u_i=z_i-ta_i\). On the ordered chamber the objective, up to an additive constant, is \(\sum_i p_i\log p_i-\sum_i u_ip_i\) with constraints \(p_1\ge\cdots\ge p_n>0\) and \(\sum_i p_i=1\). For any contiguous constant block of the KKT solution, summing the first-order equations over that block gives

\[
\log p_i=\frac{1}{|B|}\sum_{j\in B}u_j-1-\mu\quad(i\in B).
\]

The monotonicity multipliers are nonnegative precisely when the blockwise means are formed by pooling adjacent violations of the descending order. Thus these means are exactly the fitted values of the Euclidean isotonic projection \(v=P_C(u)\). One global multiplier \(\mu\) normalizes the exponentials. Equivalently, the KKT system has the same active-set conditions as \(P_C(u)\), since \(\log p_i-u_i\) is constant on each block up to its shared multiplier. This proves the displayed formula for \(T_t\).

**3. Coalescence and projection semigroup.** The following elementary lemma supplies the dynamical step. For arbitrary \(u\in\mathbb R^n\), descending \(a\), and \(s\ge0\),

\[
\boxed{P_C(P_C(u)-sa)=P_C(u-sa).}\tag{1}
\]

To prove (1), set \(x=P_C(u)\), \(w=P_C(x-sa)\). Whenever \(x_i=x_{i+1}\), the corresponding inputs to the second projection satisfy \((x-sa)_i\le(x-sa)_{i+1}\). Such an ascending adjacent input pair cannot yield a strictly descending fitted pair under least-squares isotonic projection: replacing the fitted pair by their common average remains feasible and strictly lowers the squared error. Consequently every equality constraint active at \(x\) remains active at \(w\). For the convex cone \(C\), this gives \(N_C(x)\subseteq N_C(w)\), since the normal cone is generated by the normals of active adjacent order constraints. The two projection optimality relations are \(u-x\in N_C(x)\) and \(x-sa-w\in N_C(w)\); adding them yields \(u-sa-w\in N_C(w)\), proving (1).

Apply (1) to \(u=z-ta\). The first output has sorted log-probabilities \(P_C(z-ta)-c\mathbf 1\) for a scalar \(c\). Translation equivariance of \(P_C\) and (1) give the sorted log-probabilities of \(T_s(T_t(y))\) as \(P_C(z-(s+t)a)\) up to an additive constant. If the first output contains ties, all those groups remain tied under a second step by the preceding argument, so arbitrary tie-breaking does not affect the result. Normalizing exponentials proves the semigroup law.

**4. Exact absorption time.** The isotonic projection \(P_C(u)\) is constant if and only if \(\sum_{i=1}^k(u_i-\bar u)\le0\) for each \(1\le k<n\). Indeed these are exactly the inequalities characterizing \(u-\bar u\mathbf1\in N_C(\bar u\mathbf1)\), with the normal-cone sign for descending order. Substitute \(u=z-ta\) to obtain

\[
\sum_{i=1}^k(z_i-\bar z)\le t\sum_{i=1}^k(a_i-\bar a),\qquad 1\le k<n.
\]

For descending nonconstant \(a\), every partial centered sum in the right-hand factor is strictly positive; for descending \(z\), the left-hand sums are nonnegative. Their simultaneous validity is equivalent to \(t\ge\tau_*\). Exponentiation and normalization preserve whether all fitted coordinates are equal, proving both directions of the exact absorption statement.

## Verification

A self-contained Python 3 standard-library program `checker.py` implements PAVA, independently enumerates all contiguous isotonic partitions through order seven, checks first-order optimality multipliers for the entropy minimizer, tests the semigroup on randomized positive probability vectors and arbitrary descending real weight patterns through order eleven, and tests the sharp uniformization boundary. Its latest replay passed **31,080 finite assertions**. These tests supplement rather than replace the analytic proof. For instance, when \(y=(3/5,3/10,1/10)\) and \(a=(2,1,0)\), the exact uniformization time is \(\tau_*=(\log18)/3\), with an earlier first coalescence at \(t=\log2\).

## Relationship to prior work

Blondel, Teboul, Berthet and Djolonga establish both quadratic and entropic projections onto permutahedra and their isotonic reductions; their quadratic isotonic map is algebraically related to the logarithmic coordinate trajectory above. Their central operators are projections onto permutahedra rather than rank-penalized KL proximal maps on the probability simplex, and their reported asymptotic smoothing statements are not the present full input-reapplying semigroup identity. The precise overlap is the established isotonic computational foundation, not a claim to invent PAVA.

The established sticky-particle dynamics literature develops coalescent projections onto monotonic cones and a semigroup on **position–velocity pairs**. The present representation is related after a log-softmax coordinate change; the statement here concerns the *same fixed rank penalty reapplied to new positive probabilities*, and separately determines the minimal time to a uniform probability vector. This analogy creates a substantial possible implication/prior-art risk, especially for the projection lemma (1), and is not disguised as independent invention of sticky particles. General KL/Bregman proximal calculus also precedes this result but does not by itself yield a semigroup for an arbitrary convex penalty. The claim is a narrowly specified exact conjugacy, composition identity, and absorption threshold, not a general semigroup theorem for convex KL prox maps.

## Limitations

The ordered-weight hypothesis is essential to convexity and to the monotone-coalescence proof. No claim is made for arbitrary unsorted weights, zero coordinates of the input, varying rank penalties between steps, infinite-dimensional extensions, or a general KL-proximal semigroup. The finite tests do not prove the infinite statements. Historical priority remains qualified by the possibility of an implicit equivalent formulation in older isotonic/sticky-flow literature; independent mathematical audit has not been performed.
