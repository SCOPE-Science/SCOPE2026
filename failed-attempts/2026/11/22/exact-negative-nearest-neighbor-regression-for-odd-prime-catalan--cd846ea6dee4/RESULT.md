# Exact negative nearest-neighbor regression for odd-prime Catalan valuations

## Finding
Let \(p\) be an odd prime, \(k\ge1\), and \(C_n=\binom{2n}{n}/(n+1)\). Choose \(N\) uniformly from the integers \(0\le N<p^k\), and set
\[
X=\nu_p(C_N),\qquad \Delta=\nu_p(C_{N+1})-\nu_p(C_N).
\]
The following determines the current-valuation/next-step covariance at every odd prime and every prime-power block length.

The valuation ratio gives \(\mathbb E\Delta=0\) on each full block; the exact root-cylinder classification used in the proof accounts for the special overlap at \(p=3\). The main claim here is the **two-point regression covariance**, not the elementary distribution of the ratio’s two linear valuations.

The **exact regression covariance** of the current valuation with the subsequent change is
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Cov}(X,\Delta)=
\begin{cases}
-\frac13+\frac{k}{3^k},&p=3,\\
-\displaystyle\frac{(p+1)(1-p^{-k})-2k(p-1)p^{-k}}{(p-1)^2},&p\ge5.
\end{cases}}
\]
It is strictly negative at every odd prime and every positive depth **except** \((p,k)=(3,1)\), where it is zero. For fixed \(p\), as \(k\to\infty\), it converges to \(-1/3\) when \(p=3\), and to \(-(p+1)/(p-1)^2\) when \(p\ge5\). Equivalently, \(\operatorname{Cov}(X,\nu_p(C_{N+1}))=\operatorname{Var}(X)+\operatorname{Cov}(X,\Delta)\); this distinguishes nearest-neighbor regression from a marginal variance formula.

## Assumptions and scope
All variables are integers except the uniform random choice of \(N\). The prime \(p\) is odd, \(k\ge1\), and \(C_0=1\). The random variable \(\Delta\) uses the actual successor \(N+1\), **not** cyclic wrap-around modulo \(p^k\). All probabilities and covariances use normalization \(p^{-k}\), with each index counted once. The symbol \(\nu_p(m)\) is the exponent of \(p\) in the positive integer \(m\). At the endpoint \(N=p^k-1\), the successor is \(p^k\), so no artificial periodicity is assumed.

## Proof
**1. Reduce the drift to two linear valuations.** The Catalan ratio is
\[
\frac{C_{n+1}}{C_n}=\frac{2(2n+1)}{n+2}.
\]
As \(p\) is odd,
\[
\Delta(n)=\nu_p(2n+1)-\nu_p(n+2).
\tag{1}
\]
For \(1\le j\le k\), define the residue cylinders
\[
A_j=\{0\le n<p^k:p^j\mid2n+1\},\quad
B_j=\{0\le n<p^k:p^j\mid n+2\}.
\]
Each has exactly \(p^{k-j}\) elements. Both linear forms are positive and at most \(2p^k\), so their \(p\)-adic valuations are at most \(k\). Consequently
\[
\Delta=\sum_{j=1}^k(\mathbf1_{A_j}-\mathbf1_{B_j}),\qquad
\mathbb E\Delta=0.
\tag{2}
\]

**2. Inspect the root-cylinder structure.** Write \(p=2h+1\). The unique residue for \(A_j\) is \((p^j-1)/2\); in least-significant-first base-\(p\) digits it is \(h,h,\ldots,h\). The unique residue for \(B_j\) is \(p^j-2\); its digits are \(p-2,p-1,\ldots,p-1\). For \(p\ge5\), these cylinders are disjoint already modulo \(p\). Thus, when \(1\le j<k\), exactly \((p-1)p^{k-j-1}\) indices have drift \(+j\), and equally many have drift \(-j\); the two terminal cylinders each have one index. All indices outside \(A_1\cup B_1\) have drift zero; this describes the elementary drift marginal used as an auxiliary count, not the novel assertion.

For \(p=3\), the first cylinders coincide: \(A_1=B_1=\{n\equiv1\pmod3\}\). Starting at depth two the two branches split: \(A_j\) has digits \(1,1,\ldots,1\), and \(B_j\) has digits \(1,2,\ldots,2\). All integers in their common first cylinder but neither \(A_2\) nor \(B_2\) have drift zero. Hence the zero count is \(7\cdot3^{k-2}\) for \(k\ge2\); exact nonzero drifts are indexed by cylinders of depth \(j+1\), with counts \(2\cdot3^{k-j-2}\) for \(1\le j<k-1\) and one at the endpoint \(j=k-1\). This records the prime-three overlap of the auxiliary root cylinders, including the singleton depth-one case.

**3. Compute conditional carries.** Kummer's theorem says that \(\nu_p\binom{2n}{n}\) equals the number of carries in the base-\(p\) addition \(n+n\). Let \(T_m(c)\) be the expected number of carries generated by \(m\) independent uniform base-\(p\) digits when the carry entering the first digit is \(c\in\{0,1\}\). The conditional probability that the next carry is one is \((h+c)/p\). Thus the expected outgoing carry after \(i\) further digits is \(1/2+(c-1/2)p^{-i}\), and summing gives
\[
T_m(c)=\frac m2+\left(c-\frac12\right)\frac{1-p^{-m}}{p-1},\qquad
T_m(0)-T_m(1)=-\frac{1-p^{-m}}{p-1}.
\tag{3}
\]
The final carry leaving the \(k\)-th digit is counted; a zero digit above it produces no new carry for odd \(p\).

On either \(A_j\) or \(B_j\), \(p\nmid n+1\); hence \(X=\nu_p(C_n)\) is exactly the number of doubling carries. On \(A_j\), the initial \(j\) digits are all \(h\) and create no carries, so
\[
\mathbb E[X\mid A_j]=T_{k-j}(0).
\tag{4}
\]
For \(p\ge5\), on \(B_j\) the first digit \(p-2\) immediately creates a carry and every subsequent specified digit \(p-1\) preserves it. Therefore
\[
\mathbb E[X\mid B_j]=j+T_{k-j}(1),\quad
\mathbb E[X\mid A_j]-\mathbb E[X\mid B_j]
=-j-\frac{1-p^{-(k-j)}}{p-1}.
\tag{5}
\]
For \(p=3\), the sets \(A_1,B_1\) coincide and cancel in (2). For \(j\ge2\), the first digit of \(B_j\) is one and creates no carry, but the next \(j-1\) digits are twos and create a carry at each position. Thus
\[
\mathbb E[X\mid A_j]-\mathbb E[X\mid B_j]
=-(j-1)-\frac{1-3^{-(k-j)}}2\quad(2\le j\le k).
\tag{6}
\]

**4. Sum the exact regression covariance.** Since \(\mathbb E\Delta=0\), (2) and the cylinder probabilities give
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(X,\Delta)=\mathbb E(X\Delta)
=\sum_{j=1}^k p^{-j}\bigl(\mathbb E[X\mid A_j]-\mathbb E[X\mid B_j]\bigr).
\tag{7}
\]
For \(p\ge5\), substitute (5) and sum the two finite geometric-arithmetic series:
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(X,\Delta)
=-\sum_{j=1}^k p^{-j}\left(j+\frac{1-p^{-(k-j)}}{p-1}\right)
=-\frac{(p+1)(1-p^{-k})-2k(p-1)p^{-k}}{(p-1)^2}.
\]
For \(p=3\), the \(j=1\) term vanishes, and (6) yields
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(X,\Delta)
=-\sum_{j=2}^k3^{-j}\left(j-1+\frac{1-3^{-(k-j)}}2\right)
=-\frac13+\frac{k}{3^k}.
\]
Both finite sums contain only positive terms before the minus sign, proving the sharp negativity statement and the sole zero exception. Their limiting sums prove the displayed constants. Every equality is finite and exact; no probabilistic limiting theorem is used.

It compares complete drift histograms and the exact rational covariance against the theorem for 33 prime-depth pairs over primes \(3,5,7,11,13,17,19\), evaluating 108,479 integer indices. It separately checks 505 pairs against literal integer Catalan numbers computed by binomial coefficients. The observed result is:

## Relationship to prior work
Sobolewski (2023) establishes a general framework for regularity of valuation sequences of \(p\)-adic analytic functions and analyzes Lucas-type recurrence valuations. It does not give the present two-point Catalan covariance or prime-three split. Classic work of Barat and Grabner (2001) develops digital-function methods for binomial coefficient distributions; its general methods motivate carry-based calculations but do not state this explicit nearest-neighbor Catalan regression formula. The standard single-index three-adic Catalan valuation sequence appears in OEIS A067397, and a published exact ternary marginal histogram and Gaussian law concerns one value \(\nu_3(C_N)\) at a time. The \(p\ge5\) drift marginal also coincides with the drift law for **consecutive central-binomial** valuations; this marginal coincidence is **not** claimed as a separate originality contribution. The claim of novelty is exclusively the all-odd-prime exact *Catalan current-valuation versus successor-drift covariance*. The prime-three drift branch is a direct consequence of the linear-root data and is a supporting ingredient, not a separate novelty claim. Neither the one-index Catalan histogram nor the consecutive central-binomial two-point histogram alone implies this covariance without additional joint digit information.

## Limitations
This theorem does not determine the entire bivariate law of the two adjacent Catalan valuations. The covariance compares a current valuation with its **increment**, not the two adjacent valuations directly (the latter covariance additionally involves the variance of the current valuation). The result is only for odd primes and complete initial prime-power blocks. Published digital-function frameworks may admit equivalent derivations, and a historical uniqueness guarantee is not asserted. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
