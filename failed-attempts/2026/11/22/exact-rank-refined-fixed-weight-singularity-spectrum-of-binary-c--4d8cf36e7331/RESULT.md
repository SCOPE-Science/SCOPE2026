# Exact rank-refined, fixed-weight singularity spectrum of binary circulants at odd semiprime order

## Finding

Let \(p<q\) be distinct odd primes and put \(n=pq\). Every \(n\times n\) cyclic convolution matrix generated by a sign word \(s\in\{+1,-1\}^n\) has rank in **exactly** the six-element set
\[
\{1,p,q,n-q+1,n-p+1,n\}.
\]
All six ranks actually occur for each such prime pair. A sharper rank-and-weight classification follows. Via the Chinese remainder theorem, represent the sign word by the binary array \(B=(b_{ij})\in\{0,1\}^{p\times q}\), where \(b_{ij}=(s_{ij}+1)/2\), and write \(k=\sum_{ij}b_{ij}\). The indices \(i\) and \(j\) range over \(\mathbb Z_p\) and \(\mathbb Z_q\), respectively.

For any \(0<k<n\), exactly one of the following mutually exclusive cases holds:

| Binary-array structure | Convolution rank |
|---|---:|
| All rows of \(B\) identical | \(q\) |
| All columns of \(B\) identical | \(p\) |
| Row sums all equal, but rows not all identical | \(n-p+1\) |
| Column sums all equal, but columns not all identical | \(n-q+1\) |
| None of the above | \(n\) |

At weights \(k=0\) and \(k=n\), there is one constant word each and both have convolution rank \(1\).

The exact rank counts at any fixed weight are as follows. For \(0<k<n\) divisible by \(p\), set \(r=k/p\); then
\[
N_q(k)=\binom{q}{r},\qquad
N_{n-p+1}(k)=\binom{q}{r}^{p}-\binom{q}{r},\qquad
N_n(k)=\binom{n}{k}-\binom{q}{r}^{p}.
\]
For \(0<k<n\) divisible by \(q\), set \(t=k/q\); then
\[
N_p(k)=\binom{p}{t},\qquad
N_{n-q+1}(k)=\binom{p}{t}^{q}-\binom{p}{t},\qquad
N_n(k)=\binom{n}{k}-\binom{p}{t}^{q}.
\]
For all other interior weights \(k\), every one of the \(\binom{n}{k}\) words has rank \(n\). Since the primes are distinct, the two interior divisibility cases cannot overlap.

Summing over the weights, the complete rank multiplicities are
\[
\begin{aligned}
N_1&=2,\\
N_p&=2^p-2, &N_q&=2^q-2,\\
N_{n-q+1}&=\sum_{t=0}^{p}\binom{p}{t}^{q}-2^p,\\
N_{n-p+1}&=\sum_{r=0}^{q}\binom{q}{r}^{p}-2^q,\\
N_n&=2^{pq}-\sum_{t=0}^{p}\binom{p}{t}^{q}-\sum_{r=0}^{q}\binom{q}{r}^{p}+2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here the powers apply to the whole binomial coefficient; for example \(\binom{q}{r}^{p}\) means \(\bigl(\binom{q}{r}\bigr)^p\). These six counts are positive and sum to \(2^{pq}\).

## Assumptions and scope

All matrix ranks are over \(\mathbb C\). The convolution operator on \(\mathbb C[\mathbb Z_n]\) is \(C_s f=s*f\); changing the left/right convention or complex Fourier-sign convention does not change its rank. The sign vector has full support, even when its associated binary mask has empty or complete support. Exactly two **distinct odd** primes are assumed; no claim is made for prime powers or moduli with three prime factors. The primary result is the full rank-refined, fixed-weight classification, not the previously known aggregate singularity probability.

## Proof

**Fourier orbit decomposition.** Let \(\zeta_p\) and \(\zeta_q\) be primitive roots. For \(a\in\mathbb Z_p\), \(b\in\mathbb Z_q\), let
\[
\widehat B(a,b)=\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}\sum_{j=0}^{q-1}b_{ij}\zeta_p^{ai}\zeta_q^{bj}.
\]
For nontrivial characters \((a,b)\ne(0,0)\), \(\widehat s(a,b)=2\widehat B(a,b)\). At the trivial character \(\widehat s(0,0)=2k-pq\ne0\), since \(pq\) is odd. Thus the complex rank is the number of nonvanishing Fourier multipliers of the binary mask, except for the empty-mask endpoint, where the sign transform still has exactly one nonzero multiplier.

The Galois group of the \(pq\)-th cyclotomic extension is transitive separately on the three character sets
\[
\begin{split}
\mathcal P&=\{(a,0):a\ne0\},\quad |\mathcal P|=p-1,\\
\mathcal Q&=\{(0,b):b\ne0\},\quad |\mathcal Q|=q-1,\\
\mathcal M&=\{(a,b):a\ne0,\ b\ne0\},\quad |\mathcal M|=(p-1)(q-1).
\end{split}
\]
All Fourier coefficients in each orbit vanish together because the matrix entries are integers and Galois conjugation preserves zero.

**Prime-order zeros.** Put \(R_i=\sum_j b_{ij}\) and \(K_j=\sum_i b_{ij}\). Since the minimal polynomial of \(\zeta_p\) is \(1+X+\cdots+X^{p-1}\), vanishing on \(\mathcal P\) is equivalent to \(R_0=\cdots=R_{p-1}\). Similarly, vanishing on \(\mathcal Q\) is equivalent to \(K_0=\cdots=K_{q-1}\). If both sets vanish, then \(p\mid k\) and \(q\mid k\), whence \(pq\mid k\). For a binary array this implies \(k=0\) or \(k=pq\), so no nonconstant sign word has both types of prime-order zeros.

**Mixed-orbit zeros.** If all mixed coefficients vanish, Fourier inversion on the product group (or the orthogonal tensor decomposition of the two prime-cycle character spaces) gives
\[
b_{ij}=A_i+D_j
\]
for suitable complex scalars \(A_i,D_j\). Because the entries are binary, this forces either all rows to be identical or all columns to be identical. Indeed, if any two rows differ at a column, their entrywise difference is a nonzero constant (since the \(A_i\) depend only on \(i\)), necessarily \(\pm1\); those two rows are therefore the constant-zero and constant-one rows. Every remaining row then also has a column-independent value because its constant difference from a constant row belongs to \(\{-1,0,1\}\). Thus the columns are identical. If no two rows differ, the rows are identical. This argument covers every binary array, not only a typical one.

If the rows are identical, the only possibly nonzero Fourier multipliers are the \(q\) characters \((0,b)\). In the nonconstant case, all of these are in fact nonzero: the zero-frequency multiplier is \(2k-n\ne0\), and for a nonconstant binary vector of prime length \(q\), no nontrivial Fourier coefficient vanishes. Therefore rank equals \(q\). The column-identical case analogously has rank \(p\). Otherwise \(\mathcal M\) does not vanish, contributing exactly \((p-1)(q-1)\) to rank. No prime-order zero gives rank \(n\); a single \(\mathcal P\) zero orbit gives rank \(n-p+1\); a single \(\mathcal Q\) zero orbit gives rank \(n-q+1\). This proves the five mutually exclusive interior cases and the six-rank spectrum including endpoints.

**Exact enumeration.** Equal row sums require \(k=pr\) with \(r\in\{1,\ldots,q-1\}\). Each of the \(p\) rows independently chooses one of \(\binom{q}{r}\) binary configurations, yielding \(\binom{q}{r}^{p}\) arrays, of which exactly \(\binom{q}{r}\) have all rows identical. This proves the rank-\(q\) and rank-\((n-p+1)\) counts at weight \(k\), and subtraction from \(\binom{n}{k}\) proves the rank-\(n\) count. The equal-column case is symmetric. The constant endpoints supply the two rank-one words; binomial summation gives the six global formulas.

**Nonemptiness.** Each prime is at least three. At weight \(k=p\), selecting one singleton in each of the \(p\) rows produces a nonidentical row-regular array, while selecting the same singleton for all rows produces a row-identical array. The column versions do the same. Choosing \(k=1\) gives full rank. Thus all six ranks occur.

## Verification

Two standalone source programs accompanying this document are included: `cyclotomic_rank_census.cpp` computes every cyclotomic polynomial factor of the circulant symbol using exact integer polynomial division and exhaustively enumerates all sign words by Gray code; `formula_validation.py` independently constructs the above binomial formulas, invokes the first program, and checks every rank at every Hamming weight. The verified commands are:

```sh
python3 formula_validation.py
```

The exact checker covered all \(2^{15}=32{,}768\) words of \(\mathbb Z_{15}\) and all \(2^{21}=2{,}097{,}152\) words of \(\mathbb Z_{21}\). The global rank multiplicities at length \(15\) were \((1:2,3:6,5:30,11:480,13:2220,15:30030)\) and at length \(21\) were \((1:2,3:6,7:126,15:4368,19:104832,21:1987818)\). All individual fixed-weight counts agreed. These checks support but do not replace the all-prime proof.

## Relationship to prior work

Lam and Leung established the underlying structure of vanishing sums of roots of unity, including the two-prime case. Chen studied nonvanishing of binary circulants at selected weights. Michael Yang (2023) gave the general Fourier-rank identity for group-circulants and a precise weight-only criterion for guaranteed invertibility (Theorem 4.6); these are acknowledged prior. Most closely, Niklas Miller (first preprint in 2024) proved an exact formula for the **aggregate** Bernoulli-circulant singularity probability at products of two primes (Lemma 2(3)), and the associated asymptotics. The aggregate singularity count—and by extracting its weight polynomial, the singular/non-singular fixed-weight count—is therefore **not** claimed as new here. This result gives the **rank-refined** information absent from that formula: a structural criterion for each of six ranks and the multiplicity of each distinct nonzero nullity, separately at every weight. Prior Fourier-orbit arguments are used explicitly, and no claim of independent discovery of the earlier singularity formula is made.

## Limitations

The result concerns ranks, not singular values, numerical conditioning or arbitrary complex convolution coefficients. The asymptotic singularity probability for unbiased Bernoulli circulants is known from Miller and is not an original output here. The rank-refined formulas are short consequences of the cyclotomic decomposition plus binary-row rigidity; unindexed rank tables may already exist. A plausible earlier paper by Ingleton on circulant ranks could not be examined in full, so historical priority of the precise rank refinement is not established absolutely. Independent mathematical verification has not been performed.
