# Exact spectra and a sharp pseudospectral-hole transition for half-flux three-term elements

## Finding
Let \(\mathcal A_{1/2}\) be the universal unital \(C^*\)-algebra generated by unitaries \(U,V\) with \(UV=-VU\). For arbitrary \(a,b,c\in\mathbb C\), put \(T=a\mathbf1+bU+cV\), \(B=|b|\), \(C=|c|\), \(M=\max(B,C)\), and \(m=\min(B,C)\). Then
\[
\sigma(T)=\{\lambda\in\mathbb C:\sqrt{|B^2-C^2|}\le |\lambda-a|\le\sqrt{B^2+C^2}\}.
\]
This includes circles and singletons in degenerate cases. For every \(\lambda\in\mathbb C\), the minimum modulus (the infimum of \(\|(T-\lambda\mathbf1)x\|\) over unit vectors in a faithful Hilbert-space representation) is exactly
\[
\mu(T-\lambda\mathbf1)=\max\!\left\{0,\ |\lambda-a|-\sqrt{B^2+C^2},\ M-\sqrt{|\lambda-a|^2+m^2}\right\}.
\]
Consequently, with the convention that the inverse norm is infinite on the spectrum,
\[
\|(T-\lambda\mathbf1)^{-1}\|=1/\mu(T-\lambda\mathbf1).
\]
For \(\varepsilon>0\), define the open \(\varepsilon\)-pseudospectrum by \(\sigma_\varepsilon(T)=\sigma(T)\cup\{\lambda:\|(T-\lambda\mathbf1)^{-1}\|>1/\varepsilon\}\). Its outer radius around \(a\) is \(R_\varepsilon=\sqrt{B^2+C^2}+\varepsilon\), and its exact inner condition is:

- If \(0<\varepsilon<M-m\), it is the open annulus \(\sqrt{(M-\varepsilon)^2-m^2}<|\lambda-a|<R_\varepsilon\).
- If \(\varepsilon=M-m>0\), it is the punctured disk \(0<|\lambda-a|<R_\varepsilon\).
- If \(\varepsilon>M-m\) (including all \(\varepsilon>0\) when \(B=C\)), it is the open disk \(|\lambda-a|<R_\varepsilon\).

In particular, the pseudospectral hole closes at the **sharp threshold** \(\varepsilon=|B-C|\) (the central point enters only for strictly larger \(\varepsilon\)). This threshold can be strictly smaller than the inner spectral radius \(\sqrt{|B^2-C^2|}\).

The complete singular-value spectrum also has a closed formula. Put \(A=|a|\), \(S=A^2+B^2+C^2\), \(L=\max(A,B,C)\), \(d=\max(0,2L^2-S)\), and \(D=\sqrt{S^2-d^2}\). Then
\[
\sigma(|T|)=\left[\sqrt{S-D},\sqrt{S+D}\right],
\qquad
\|T\|=\begin{cases}\sqrt{2S},&2L^2\le S,\\ L+\sqrt{S-L^2},&2L^2>S.\end{cases}
\]
The element \(T\) is invertible precisely when \(2L^2>S\). In that regime \(\|T^{-1}\|^{-1}=L-\sqrt{S-L^2}\). All assertions hold also for zero coefficients, with the displayed conventions.

## Assumptions and scope
We work in the universal **rational** rotation algebra at parameter \(1/2\), not the single fixed Pauli representation and not an irrational rotation algebra. The spectrum and norms are those of \(\mathcal A_{1/2}\), equivalently of any faithful representation. Complex coefficients are arbitrary, including zeros; \(\varepsilon\) ranges over all strictly positive real numbers. The pseudospectrum inequality is strict; boundary circles are excluded. The inner central point is excluded exactly at the hole-closing threshold when \(B\ne C\).

## Proof
**1. Exhaustive two-dimensional fibers.** The standard rational rotation-algebra representation theorem (Lawton, Theorems 2 and 3) identifies all irreducible fibers with
\[
U_z=z\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
V_w=w\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad |z|=|w|=1.
\]
Every irreducible representation occurs in this family, so its union detects invertibility and the spectrum; its supremum determines the universal \(C^*\)-norm. The same applies to \(|T|\) and shifted elements. Hence the relevant matrix is
\[
T_{z,w}=\begin{pmatrix}a+bz&cw\\cw&a-bz\end{pmatrix},
\quad\operatorname{tr}T_{z,w}=2a,
\quad\det T_{z,w}=a^2-b^2z^2-c^2w^2.
\]
All phase pairs \((z^2,w^2)\) occur independently. A complex number is an eigenvalue of some fiber exactly when \((\lambda-a)^2=b^2z^2+c^2w^2\). The sum of circles of radii \(B^2,C^2\) fills the closed radial interval from \(|B^2-C^2|\) to \(B^2+C^2\). Taking square roots gives the first spectral annulus, including degenerate cases.

**2. Exact minimum modulus.** For any shifted scalar \(h=a-\lambda\), the trace and determinant of the positive two-by-two matrix \(T_{z,w}^*T_{z,w}\), after replacing \(a\) by \(h\), are
\[
\operatorname{tr}(T_{z,w}^*T_{z,w})=2s,
\quad \det(T_{z,w}^*T_{z,w})=|h^2-b^2z^2-c^2w^2|^2,
\quad s=|h|^2+B^2+C^2.
\]
Therefore its squared singular values are \(s\pm\sqrt{s^2-\delta^2}\), where \(\delta=|h^2-b^2z^2-c^2w^2|\). As phases vary, \(\delta\) ranges through a closed interval and has minimum
\[
\delta_{\min}=\max\{0,\,2\max(|h|^2,B^2,C^2)-s\}.
\]
This is the exact three-segment polygon inequality: three planar vectors of prescribed lengths sum to zero iff the largest length does not exceed the other two. The smallest singular value, minimized over all fibers, equals zero in that polygon regime. Outside it, if the largest length is \(q^2\) and the other two sum to \(r^2\), then \(s=q^2+r^2\) and \(\delta_{\min}=q^2-r^2\), so \(\sqrt{s-\sqrt{s^2-\delta_{\min}^2}}=q-r\). Taking the two possible dominating lengths \(|h|\) and \(M\) yields exactly the claimed maximum formula for \(\mu(T-\lambda\mathbf1)\). All other cases give zero, including triangle equality. Universal invertibility is equivalent to a positive uniform minimum modulus in this compact fiber family; the inverse norm is its reciprocal.

**3. The open pseudospectra.** Set \(t=|\lambda-a|\). The identity in step 2 reads \(\mu(T-\lambda\mathbf1)=\max\{0,t-\sqrt{B^2+C^2},M-\sqrt{t^2+m^2}\}\). Thus \(\mu<\varepsilon\) is equivalent to \(t<\sqrt{B^2+C^2}+\varepsilon\) and \(\sqrt{t^2+m^2}>M-\varepsilon\). Solving the latter strict inequality gives the three stated cases. At \(\varepsilon=M-m>0\), its strictness removes exactly the center; at larger \(\varepsilon\), the center is included. This also proves the sharp hole-closing threshold.

**4. The whole singular-value spectrum and norm.** With \(\lambda=0\) in step 2, the determinant modulus \(\delta\) varies continuously over the connected two-torus of phases, attaining its triangle minimum \(d\) and its maximal value \(S\) by suitable phase alignments. It therefore assumes every value of \([d,S]\). The two squared singular values are \(S\pm\sqrt{S^2-\delta^2}\). As \(\delta\) sweeps that interval, their union is exactly \([S-D,S+D]\). Taking nonnegative square roots proves the asserted spectral interval of \(|T|\) and its upper endpoint \(\|T\|\). When \(2L^2\le S\), \(d=0\) and the endpoint is \(\sqrt{2S}\). Otherwise \(d=2L^2-S\) and \(D=2L\sqrt{S-L^2}\), so the endpoints are \((L\mp\sqrt{S-L^2})^2\). The invertibility condition and least singular value follow. The zero triple also satisfies these formulas.

## Verification
The attached standalone file `check.py` numerically checks the analytically exact two-by-two trace and determinant identities for 2,000 root-of-unity fibers with integer coefficients; it also checks 15,150 positive pseudospectral inequalities and supplementary complex-coefficient fibers. Run `python check.py` with Python 3 standard library only; expected final line is `HALF_FLUX_THREE_TERM_CHECK_OK 24110`. These finite diagnostics do not prove the universal theorem; the proof uses the exhaustive irreducible-fiber theorem and continuous polygon geometry.

For example, when \(b=3\), \(c=1\), the inner spectral radius around \(a\) is \(\sqrt8\), yet the pseudospectral hole closes already at \(\varepsilon=2\); at equality only the center remains excluded, and above it the entire center belongs. This difference distinguishes nonnormal pseudospectral geometry from an ordinary Euclidean thickening of the spectrum.

## Relationship to prior work
Lawton's rational rotation-algebra representation theorem supplies the complete matrix-fiber model used as a premise here; it does not classify the coefficients, sharp norm, all singular values, or pseudospectral-hole collapse for this three-term polynomial. Barrett and Gaunt also describe root-of-unity clock-and-shift geometry, in the context of finite spectral triples. Sukochev, Tastankul, Tulenov and Zanin develop twisted convolution and sharp Young/Sobolev bounds for quantum tori; their results do not give the displayed nonselfadjoint polynomial spectral formulas. Fang et al.'s analysis of \(U+V\) concerns irrational rotation parameters, so its conclusions are not transferred to rational half-flux. These differences are about the hypotheses and implied results, not mere differences of titles.

## Limitations
No result is asserted for irrational flux, higher rational denominators, or polynomials containing mixed Fourier words such as \(UV\). The universality and fiber completeness are imported from established rational-rotation theory; the novel calculation is the exact all-coefficient spectral, resolvent and pseudospectral classification. Older treatments of rational nonselfadjoint magnetic operators could contain equivalent formulas, so priority beyond the explicitly compared sources remains subject to further bibliographic inspection. The attached finite checker is supplementary, not a certificate of infinite-dimensional correctness or uniqueness in the literature. Independent audit has not been performed.
