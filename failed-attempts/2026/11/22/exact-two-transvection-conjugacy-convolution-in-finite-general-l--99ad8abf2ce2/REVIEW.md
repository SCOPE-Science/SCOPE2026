# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

Fix A=I+uφ. Each nonzero rank-one square-zero presentation of B is counted q−1 times, giving T. With a=φ(v), b=ψ(u), the product minus I factors as u(φ+aψ)+vψ. It has rank 2 exactly when both vector and covector pairs are independent. On the span of u,v in that case the restriction of AB−I has matrix [[ab,a],[b,0]], with the quotient fixed. For t=ab≠0 the restriction is invertible and non-scalar (a≠0), so it splits from the fixed space and is cyclic with companion polynomial h_t, including the repeated-root case. The constraints on v and ψ give q^(2n−3) B's for every nonzero t. For t=0 the full operator AB−I maps into the same span. If exactly one of a,b is nonzero its square is nonzero and cube zero (its image is the whole span), giving J3; if both vanish it squares to zero, giving J22. Counting independent v,ψ with the required annihilations yields the stated J3 and J22 rows, respecting minimal dimensions. Rank≤1 is the union of shared image line and shared row line, each of size q^(n−1)−1 with overlap q−1; removing the sole inverse factor gives the rank-one row. The identity coefficient, class normalization, standard centralizers and fixed-field limits agree. Fresh full-pair enumeration through n=4 over F2 and through q=5 at n=2 corroborated the rows; in dimension two it also counted the target centralizers by commutation in the full GL group.

Support counts, fixed-field limits and per-target multiplicities add no independent uncovered invariant. This is positive prior-coverage evidence, not a failed search.

## value

FAIL

An evaluated transvection class square is a useful invariant, and the frozen elementary count is correct. But every exact coefficient is known or follows by routine class-size conversion and normalization, and the support counts and limits follow immediately. No additional distinct valuable mathematical claim was established. Recasting a known group-ring product as probabilities does not supply one.
