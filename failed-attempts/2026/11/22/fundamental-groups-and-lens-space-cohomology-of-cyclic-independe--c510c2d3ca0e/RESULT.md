# Fundamental groups and lens-space cohomology of cyclic independence-complex quotients

## Finding
Let \(C_n\) be the simple polygon cycle on vertices \(\mathbb Z/n\), let \(\operatorname{Ind}(C_n)\) be its ordinary independence complex, and write \(X_n=|\operatorname{Ind}(C_n)|\). Let \(D_n\) act by polygon symmetries. For all integers \(n\ge8\) and all subgroups \(H\le D_n\),
\[
\pi_1(X_n/H)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z,&H=C_n\text{ and }n=p^a\text{ for a prime }p\text{ and }a\ge1,\\
1,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
The quotient is the **ordinary geometric orbit space**, not a Borel homotopy quotient. Additionally, for every prime \(p\ge11\), set \(d=\lfloor(p-2)/3\rfloor\) and \(m=(d+1)/2\). Then \(d=2m-1\ge3\), the rotation action on \(X_p\) is free, and \(Y_p=X_p/C_p\) has integral cohomology ring
\[
H^*(Y_p;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z[u,v]/(pu,u^m,uv,v^2),
\qquad |u|=2,\quad |v|=2m-1.
\]
Consequently \(H_{2j-1}(Y_p;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z\) for \(1\le j<m\), \(H_{2m-1}(Y_p;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z\), and all other positive-degree integral homology groups vanish. The number \(m-1\) of torsion degrees is unbounded as primes grow. The cohomology ring is the ring of a standard \((2m-1)\)-dimensional lens space, but the assertion is **not** a homotopy classification as a lens space.

## Assumptions and scope
The graph \(C_n\) has edges \(\{i,i+1\}\) modulo \(n\). A simplex of \(\operatorname{Ind}(C_n)\) is a nonempty set containing no adjacent pair; the empty simplex is included combinatorially but has no point in geometric realization. \(C_n\) also denotes the cyclic **rotation group** when used as \(H=C_n\); its meaning is clear from context. All quotients are taken on geometric realizations. The fundamental-group assertion requires \(n\ge8\); the integral ring statement requires prime \(p\ge11\). All homology and cohomology are singular groups unless otherwise stated.

## Proof
**1. Simple connectivity of the covering complex.** The classical computation of independence complexes of cycles gives
\[
\operatorname{Ind}(C_{3k})\simeq S^{k-1}\vee S^{k-1},\quad
\operatorname{Ind}(C_{3k+1})\simeq S^{k-1},\quad
\operatorname{Ind}(C_{3k+2})\simeq S^k.
\]
For every \(n\ge8\), these spaces are connected and simply connected. In the prime case \(p>3\), \(p\equiv1,5\pmod6\) and the sole sphere dimension \(d\) is odd; its dimension is exactly \(\lfloor(p-2)/3\rfloor\).

**2. Rotation stabilizers.** Let \(r\) be a one-step rotation. If \(r^a\) fixes a point in \(X_n\), uniqueness of barycentric coordinates in a simplex implies that the support of that point is an invariant nonempty independent set. Put \(g=\gcd(n,a)\). The orbit of any graph vertex under \(r^a\) consists of all vertices in one congruence class modulo \(g\). Such an orbit is independent precisely when \(g\ge2\). Indeed, if \(g=1\), invariance would require all graph vertices; if \(g\ge2\), any one orbit is an independent set. Therefore
\[
(X_n)^{r^a}\ne\varnothing\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\gcd(n,a)>1.
\]
Every generator of every proper rotational subgroup moves vertices by at least two places and fixes a simplex barycenter. If \(H=C_n\), its subgroup generated by elements with fixed points is generated by all \(r^a\) with \(\gcd(a,n)>1\). For a prime power \(n=p^a\) it equals \(\langle r^p\rangle\), of index \(p\); if \(n\) has distinct prime divisors, the powers \(r^q\) for distinct prime divisors generate the entire rotation group.

**3. Reflections and ordinary orbit fundamental groups.** Any reflection on an odd cycle fixes a vertex. On an even cycle, a vertex-axis reflection fixes two vertices; an edge-axis reflection has no fixed vertices, but for \(n\ge8\) it interchanges a pair of nonadjacent vertices. Thus every reflection fixes a nonempty simplex barycenter. Any subgroup of \(D_n\) containing a reflection is generated by reflections. Armstrong's orbit-space fundamental-group theorem says that for a simply connected locally compact polyhedron \(X\), \(\pi_1(X/H)\cong H/N\), where \(N\) is generated by elements with fixed points. Steps 1–3 prove the complete formula for all subgroups.

**4. Prime cyclic quotients and integral cohomology.** Let \(p\ge11\) be prime. Every nonidentity rotation is a generator and has no fixed point by Step 2. Thus the free \(C_p\)-quotient has the homotopy type of the Borel construction and fits into a fibration \(X_p\to Y_p\to BC_p\), with fiber homotopy equivalent to \(S^d\). Since \(p\) is odd, the action on its integral top cohomology is trivial (a map of order \(p\) cannot induce sign \(-1\)). The integral cohomological Serre spectral sequence has only rows \(q=0,d\), both with coefficients \(H^*(BC_p;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z[u]/(pu)\), \(|u|=2\). Its sole possible nonzero differential is \(d_{d+1}\) from the top-row unit to \(u^m\), because \(d+1=2m\). It must be surjective onto \(\mathbb Z/p\): otherwise infinitely many powers of \(u\) would survive to cohomology of finite-dimensional \(Y_p\). Multiplicativity then kills all bottom-row terms of degree at least \(2m\) and all positive-degree top-row terms. The top-row integral kernel is \(p\mathbb Z\), which gives \(H^d(Y_p;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z\). The only remaining positive classes below top degree are \(u,u^2,\ldots,u^{m-1}\) of order \(p\). Degree and filtration exclude any additive or multiplicative extensions, proving the stated ring. The universal coefficient theorem gives precisely the integral homology groups asserted.

## Verification
A standalone exact checker `verify.py` enumerates rotation-invariant independent simplex supports and reflection-invariant supports for \(8\le n\le20\). It also constructs signed boundary matrices for the actual free rotation quotients at \(p=11,13,17\), checks \(\partial^2=0\), computes Smith normal forms and rational Betti ranks. The only nonunit Smith invariants are respectively \([11]\), \([13]\) and \([17,17]\), and their rational Betti ranks occur in degrees \(0\) and \(3\), \(0\) and \(3\), and \(0\) and \(5\). These finite computations independently check small instances but **do not establish** the general theorem; Steps 1–4 do.

## Relationship to prior work
Kozlov's classical nonequivariant cycle independence-complex theorem supplies Step 1, and Armstrong's orbit-space theorem supplies the group-theoretic passage in Step 3. Živaljević's May 21, 2004 original preprint treats reflection (\(\mathbb Z/2\))-equivariant cycle independence complexes, not the all-subgroup ordinary orbit fundamental groups or prime-rotation cohomology rings. Engström's original August 8, 2005 preprint develops independence-complex recurrences and anti-Rips complexes. The graph-symmetry quotient comparisons in the cited related records concern different complexes: bidirected multipaths, cycle two-independence, polygon Vietoris–Rips and cyclic nonmatching complexes. None of those statements implies the particular all-subgroup classification and integral ring for \(\operatorname{Ind}(C_n)\). The novelty claim is limited to what these inspected and searched sources establish; the classical general facts used in the proof are not asserted to be new.

## Limitations
There is no homeomorphism or homotopy equivalence between \(Y_p\) and a standard lens space proved here. The ring and homology classifications do not determine the lens-space homotopy invariant or the specific free \(C_p\)-action. Inaccessible and unindexed work may contain an equivalent quotient classification. The earliest precise-day source used for the symmetry question is the original 2004 preprint; an earlier (1999) source already establishes the nonequivariant cycle-complex theorem. The computation covers only the stated finite samples.
