# Gcd-locked checkerboard entanglement in mixed-dyadic three-tori

## Finding

Let \(a\ge2\) and let \(s,t\ge3\) be odd integers. Set \(M=2^a\), \(g=\gcd(s,t)\), and take the standard graph state on the three-dimensional toroidal lattice
\[
G=C_M\square C_{2s}\square C_{2t}.
\]
Every edge represents one controlled-\(Z\) gate. For the bipartition by the parity of the sum of the three vertex coordinates, **all positive Schmidt probabilities are equal**, and their precise count is
\[
\boxed{2^r,\qquad r=2Mst-8g+4.}
\]
Thus the von Neumann entanglement entropy and all positive-order Rényi entropies across this checkerboard cut equal \(r\) bits. The deficit from the maximum possible entropy for this fixed balanced cut is exactly \(8\gcd(s,t)-4\) bits, independent of the dyadic circumference \(M\). In particular, any coprime odd \(s,t\) have a fixed four-bit deficit, whereas a shared factor enlarges it.

The structural statement behind the entanglement formula is that the full binary adjacency operator of \(G\) has **exactly \(16g-8\) zero-eigenvalue Jordan blocks, each of length \(M/2\)**, over an algebraic closure of \(\mathbb F_2\); every remaining primary component is invertible.

As an equally sized comparison, \(C_4\square C_6\square C_{30}\) and \(C_4\square C_{10}\square C_{18}\) both have \(720\) vertices and degree six, but their checkerboard entropies are respectively \(340\) and \(356\) bits. The difference is a purely arithmetic commensurability effect.

## Assumptions and scope

All three cycles are **simple** cycles of lengths at least four, with standard unit nearest-neighbor edges and periodic boundaries. The first length \(M\) is a power of two with \(M\ge4\), and the other lengths are twice **odd integers at least three**. The qubit graph state is the ideal pure state generated by controlled-\(Z\) gates from \(|+\rangle\) at every vertex. The checkerboard cut separates sites where \(x+y+z\) is even from those where it is odd. Each part has \(N/2=2Mst\) qubits, where \(N=4Mst\). The graph has no within-part edges. All ranks and Jordan calculations in the proof are over \(\mathbb F_2\) or its algebraic closure, not over \(\mathbb R\) or \(\mathbb C\).

## Proof

**1. Entanglement from cut rank.** Let \(B\) be the \((N/2)\)-by-\((N/2)\) binary biadjacency matrix from even to odd sites. The computational-basis graph-state amplitude across this cut is proportional to \((-1)^{u^{\mathsf T}Bv}\). Summing over \(v\) by finite-field character orthogonality shows the reduced density operator has precisely \(2^{\operatorname{rank}B}\) positive eigenvalues, all equal to \(2^{-\operatorname{rank}B}\). The full adjacency matrix is \(A=\begin{psmallmatrix}0&B\\B^{\mathsf T}&0\end{psmallmatrix}\), whence
\[
\dim\ker A=N-2\operatorname{rank}B.
\]
It therefore suffices to prove \(\dim\ker A=16g-8\).

**2. Jordan structure on each cycle.** Extend scalars to \(K=\overline{\mathbb F}_2\), which preserves ranks of matrices over \(\mathbb F_2\). Write \(T_L\) for the binary adjacency of the cycle \(C_L\). On the group algebra \(K[Z]/(Z^L-1)\), it acts by multiplication by \(Z+Z^{-1}\).

For \(L=M=2^a\), the relation \(Z^M-1=(Z+1)^M\), together with \(Z+Z^{-1}=Z^{-1}(Z+1)^2\), shows that its \(j\)-th power has kernel dimension \(\min(2j,M)\). Hence
\[
T_M\sim J_h(0)^{\oplus2},\qquad h=M/2.
\]

For \(L=2s\), the odd-order roots \(\zeta^s=1\) are distinct and \(Z^{2s}-1=(Z^s-1)^2\). The Chinese remainder theorem splits into a local algebra of dimension two at each such root. At \(\zeta=1\), the local multiplier \(Z+Z^{-1}=Z^{-1}(Z+1)^2\) vanishes, giving two \(J_1(0)\) blocks. At every \(\zeta\ne1\), write \(\lambda_\zeta=\zeta+\zeta^{-1}\). The identity
\[
Z+Z^{-1}-\lambda_\zeta=Z^{-1}(Z-\zeta)(Z-\zeta^{-1})
\]
shows that the local nilpotent part has a single block of size two, because \(\zeta\ne\zeta^{-1}\) in characteristic two unless \(\zeta=1\). Consequently
\[
T_{2s}\sim J_1(0)^{\oplus2}\oplus\bigoplus_{\zeta^s=1,\,\zeta\ne1}J_2(\lambda_\zeta),
\]
with **one summand per root** even where the eigenvalues repeat. The corresponding statement holds with \(t\) instead of \(s\).

**3. Arithmetic zero resonance in the two odd-core directions.** The adjacency of \(C_{2s}\square C_{2t}\) is the Kronecker sum \(T_{2s}\otimes I+I\otimes T_{2t}\). A pair of root labels \((\zeta,\eta)\) contributes eigenvalue \(\lambda_\zeta+\lambda_\eta\), and over characteristic two
\[
\lambda_\zeta=\lambda_\eta\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad (\zeta-\eta)(\zeta\eta-1)=0.
\]
Thus the only zero-eigenvalue pairs are \(\eta=\zeta\) or \(\eta=\zeta^{-1}\), with \(\zeta\) among the \(g=\gcd(s,t)\) common odd-order roots.

The pair \((1,1)\) combines two two-dimensional zero spaces and contributes four \(J_1(0)\) blocks. For every nontrivial inversion pair \(\{\zeta,\zeta^{-1}\}\), there are four ordered choices of local roots in the two directions, each combining two \(J_2\) blocks. In characteristic two the Kronecker sum \(J_2(0)\otimes I_2+I_2\otimes J_2(0)\) is two blocks \(J_2(0)\), since multiplication by \(u+v\) on \(K[u,v]/(u^2,v^2)\) has two length-two chains. There are \((g-1)/2\) nontrivial inversion pairs. Therefore the transverse zero-primary operator consists of
\[
J_1(0)^{\oplus4}\ \oplus\ J_2(0)^{\oplus 4(g-1)},
\]
and all its other eigenvalues are nonzero.

**4. Dyadic fusion and the exact zero Jordan count.** The three-dimensional adjacency is the Kronecker sum of \(T_M\) and the transverse operator. Adding nilpotent \(T_M\) to an invertible eigen-primary block preserves its nonzero eigenvalue, so it cannot create additional zero eigenvalues. We only fuse the displayed transverse zero blocks with the two \(J_h(0)\) blocks of \(T_M\).

Each of the four transverse \(J_1\) blocks gives two \(J_h\) blocks, for eight in total. For a transverse \(J_2\), the Kronecker sum with \(J_h\) is **two \(J_h\) blocks**. Indeed, on \(K[u,v]/(u^h,v^2)\), the sum is multiplication by \(w=u+v\). Since \(h\) is a power of two and \(h\ge2\), the change of variables is invertible with relations \(w^h=v^2=0\). The two free monomials \(1,v\) give two independent chains of length \(h\). Each transverse \(J_2\) therefore yields four \(J_h\) blocks after both longitudinal blocks are counted, hence another \(16(g-1)\) blocks. Altogether, the zero-primary part has
\[
\boxed{8+16(g-1)=16g-8\quad\text{blocks of length }h=M/2.}
\]
In particular \(\dim\ker A=16g-8\), because every nilpotent Jordan block contributes exactly one kernel vector. Returning to step 1 gives
\[
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_2}B
=\frac{4Mst-(16g-8)}2=2Mst-8g+4=r.
\]
The entire flat Schmidt spectrum, entropy formula, and same-size arithmetic example follow.

## Verification

Two standalone Python 3 programs accompany this result and use only the standard library. The first, `verify_graph.py`, constructs physical graph edges from integer coordinates, splits all vertices by checkerboard parity, and computes the rank of the actual cross-adjacency matrix by exact binary elimination. It passed \(17\) size triples, including equal-volume lattices with differing \(\gcd(s,t)\).

The second, `verify_spectral.py`, separately builds the **full** adjacency operator and successively computes exact ranks of its powers, testing both the claimed multiplicity and the common length of its nilpotent Jordan blocks. It passed \(10\) size triples and all powers through one past the predicted nilpotence depth. Specifically, writing \(q=16g-8\), \(h=M/2\), its exact expected rank at nonnegative power \(j\) is
\[
\operatorname{rank}(A^j)=N-qh+q\max(h-j,0).
\]
The text files `verify_graph_output.txt` and `verify_spectral_output.txt` contain the completed outputs. These finite checks corroborate, but do not replace, the all-size proof.

## Relationship to prior work

The general connection between graph-state Schmidt entropy and binary cut rank comes from Hein, Eisert and Briegel (2003), and is also explicitly given in Hein and coauthors' 2006 survey. This foundational rank correspondence is **not** claimed as new. The characteristic-two convolution methods and Cartesian-product spectral relations appear in Zaidenberg's 2006 lattice studies. Norman's modular Kronecker-sum framework gives general block-decomposition techniques. None of these general tools by itself supplies the concrete mixed-dyadic three-torus kernel and entanglement formula written here.

Previously published two-dimensional periodic checkerboard results compute \(mn/2-\gcd(m,n)\) for even rectangular grids, including single-edge deletion effects. A separate three-dimensional result concerns the **distinct** family \(C_4\square C_4\square C_{2^a}\) and its correlated omitted-bond cancellation. Another published mixed-dyadic **two-dimensional** parity-torus classification treats \(C_{2^a}\square C_{m2^b}\) through its Jordan spectrum. The present theorem treats three factors, **two nontrivial odd cores**, and determines the joint resonance multiplicity \(16\gcd(s,t)-8\); none of those three inspected claims yields the stated three-torus formula as a direct corollary. Vadali and Turner (2025) study multidimensional nearest-neighbor binary automata with null rather than these periodic mixed-dyadic boundaries, so their cycle-length results do not cover this kernel count. Full historical priority is not certified.

## Limitations

The theorem assumes ideal pure qubit graph states, unweighted unit controlled-phase gates, three periodic nearest-neighbor directions, a power-of-two first circumference, and the other two circumferences twice odd integers. It does not apply automatically to generic three-dimensional side lengths, noisy preparations, different bipartitions, composite-dimensional qudits, or Hamiltonian ground-state entanglement. General additive-cellular-automaton and harmonic analysis literature might contain equivalent specializations under different language; the comparison performed does not constitute a proof of absolute novelty.
