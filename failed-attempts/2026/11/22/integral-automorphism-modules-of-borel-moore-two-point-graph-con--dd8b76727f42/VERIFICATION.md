# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

The compact symmetric square S of the geometric graph has the collision diagonal as a closed subpolyhedron. For this compact polyhedral pair the open complement's locally finite homology is the pair homology, naturally for homeomorphisms. Homotopies of graph maps do induce homotopies of compact pairs even when no map of configuration spaces is defined. On a bouquet, the absolute symmetric square has one vertex, single-petal and diagonal degree-one generators a_i and d_i, same-petal triangles with boundary ±(2a_i−d_i), and product tori with zero boundary in degree two. Thus the absolute H₁ is freely generated by a_i; the diagonal map is twice the single-coordinate inclusion there. The latter map on homology is independent of basepoint by path-connectedness. The diagonal map is injective on the free cycle lattice, and both spaces are connected, so the pair sequence gives H₁=V/2V and H₂=H₂(S). Cross products pushed through the symmetric quotient are skew by the degree-one exchange sign. Repeated products vanish because the two-dimensional polyhedron S has torsion-free H₂. Different petals map with degree one onto the oriented mixed-cell generators, proving the integral exterior-square basis and naturality; bouquet homotopy equivalence carries this to all allowed graphs. All other degrees vanish, also for b₁=0 or 1 with the obvious empty sets of generators. The trace formula is the ordinary exterior-square trace identity.

## originality

FAIL

The claim is correct but a direct consequence of older results on the symmetric-product cohomology ring and the known Borel–Moore model, together with the ordinary relative exact sequence. Specifically, for n=2 Gugnin's integral generator and basis theorem gives natural H¹ and H² identifications, and the diagonal is twice the identity in degree one by pullback of its explicit generators. The Kallel–Salvatore graph result eliminates torsion and supplies free absolute homology in these degrees. Duality and the relative sequence then give both claimed integral automorphism modules and exactly the claimed cross-product identification. The failure is this demonstrated implication, not the absence of a new search hit or a claim that Khan writes down the integral actions explicitly.

## value

FAIL

The canonical identifications are a useful way of presenting the known groups, but the claimed advance consists entirely of specializing a known integral symmetric-product ring theorem, taking duals and applying the natural exact sequence with the two-coordinate diagonal. Neither an additional invariant nor a new obstruction or surprising boundary survives that implication. The trace formula and extension to subdivided graph models are routine consequences of the same calculation. The record is an accurate exposition, not an independently needed unknown invariant or substantial new classification under this standard.
