For a complete bipartite graph with up to \(b-1\) edges removed at one vertex, the ordinary three-cut complex has precisely two possible nonzero integral homology degrees with explicit ranks and no torsion. Each successive edge deletion kills \((a-2)(b-t-1)\) lower-degree generators and creates exactly one primitive top-degree generator; this follows from a sphere-wedge model of the Alexander dual, not an unproved homotopy claim about the original cut complex.
