# Integral sphere-wedge and component-module formula for graph tolerance complexes in every tolerance degree

## Finding

For every finite simple graph \(G\) on \(n\ge1\) vertices, regarded as a **strictly one-dimensional** simplicial complex \(K(G)\), and every integer \(t\ge2\), write \(c=c(G)\) for the number of graph-connected components and \(I_j(G)\) for the number of independent vertex subsets of cardinality \(j\). Then

\[
\mathcal T_t(K(G))\simeq \bigvee^{a_t} S^t\vee\bigvee^{b_t}S^{t+1},\qquad
 a_t=\binom{c-1}{t+1},\qquad
 b_t=\binom{n-1}{t+2}-I_{t+2}(G)+a_t.
\]

Out-of-range binomial coefficients and empty wedges are interpreted as zero and a point, respectively. The multiplicities are nonnegative integers. The lower reduced integral homology has the further natural graph-automorphism-module identification

\[
\widetilde H_t(\mathcal T_t(K(G));\mathbb Z)
 \cong\bigwedge^{t+1}\ker\bigl(\mathbb Z^{\pi_0(G)}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{sum}}\mathbb Z\bigr).
\]

Thus there is no integral torsion in any degree, and all other reduced integral homology vanishes. For connected \(G\), the lower wedge is absent and the upper multiplicity simplifies to \(\binom{n-1}{t+2}-I_{t+2}(G)\).

For example, at tolerance \(t=2\), the seven-vertex path yields fourteen three-spheres and the seven-cycle yields fifteen three-spheres. A graph with two disjoint edges and three isolated vertices has \(c=5\), \(I_4(G)=16\), and homotopy type \(\bigvee^4 S^2\vee\bigvee^3 S^3\). Such two-degree behavior distinguishes the formula from an assertion about a single homological rank.

## Assumptions and scope

A graph here is finite, simple, undirected and has at least one vertex. The simplicial complex \(K(G)\) has **only** empty, vertex and graph-edge faces; graph triangles are not filled. The \(t\)-tolerance complex of a complex \(K\) on vertex set \(V\) is defined by

\[
\mathcal T_t(K)=\{\sigma\subseteq V:\text{there is }F\in K\text{ with }F\subseteq\sigma\text{ and }|\sigma\setminus F|\le t\}.
\]

The integer \(t\) is arbitrary but at least two. If \(t+1\ge n\), the tolerance complex is a simplex, and both displayed multiplicities are zero. The symbol \(\pi_0(G)\) denotes the finite set of graph-connected components. The exterior powers and homology groups use coefficients in \(\mathbb Z\).

## Proof

**1. Exact face description.** Every subset of size at most \(t+1\) belongs to \(\mathcal T_t(K(G))\), since it contains a vertex. A set of cardinality exactly \(t+2\) belongs precisely when it contains an edge of \(G\); no larger set can belong because the original simplicial complex has no faces larger than an edge. Hence our tolerance complex is the full \(t\)-skeleton of the simplex on \(n\) vertices together with the \((t+1)\)-simplices indexed by nonindependent \((t+2)\)-sets. There are exactly

\[
q=\binom n{t+2}-I_{t+2}(G)
\]

such top simplices. This rule also handles graphs with no edges.

**2. Integral cycles in the full skeleton.** Let \(F=\mathbb Z^V\) with ordered vertex basis \(e_v\), let \(\epsilon:F\to\mathbb Z\) sum coefficients, and let \(U=\ker\epsilon\). With the augmented simplicial boundary identified as contraction by \(\epsilon\) on exterior powers, the exact augmented chain complex of the full simplex gives the lattice of all degree-\(t\) cycles in the full \(t\)-skeleton:

\[
Z_t=\ker(\epsilon\mathbin{\lrcorner}:\bigwedge^{t+1}F\longrightarrow\bigwedge^{t}F)
 =\bigwedge^{t+1}U.
\]

It is free of rank \(\binom{n-1}{t+1}\). The augmented simplex chain complex is exact by its standard cone contraction. This statement applies verbatim when the relevant exterior powers vanish.

**3. Determine all top boundaries.** Let \(E\le U\) be generated by graph-edge differences \(e_v-e_u\) for every edge \(uv\) of \(G\). Fix an edge \(uv\). For every \(t\)-set \(A\subseteq V\setminus\{u,v\}\), the boundary of the top simplex on \(\{u,v\}\cup A\), when expressed in \(\bigwedge^{t+1}U\), equals, up to overall sign,

\[
(e_v-e_u)\wedge\bigwedge_{a\in A}(e_a-e_u).
\]

Because \(\{e_w-e_u:w\ne u\}\) is a basis of \(U\), with \(e_v-e_u\) one of its elements, these expressions as \(A\) varies span exactly \((e_v-e_u)\wedge\bigwedge^t U\). Every top simplex contains at least one edge, so all of its boundaries occur in the sum of those edge-generated subgroups, while for each edge every required basis boundary comes from an allowed top simplex. Thus the complete integral top-boundary image is precisely

\[
\operatorname{im}\partial_{t+1}=E\wedge\bigwedge^t U.
\]

**4. The component lattice and lower homology.** The quotient of \(F\) by the edge-difference subgroup identifies vertices within each graph component, giving the free group \(\mathbb Z^{\pi_0(G)}\). Restricting the quotient map to \(U\) identifies

\[
U/E\cong\ker(\mathbb Z^{\pi_0(G)}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{sum}}\mathbb Z).
\]

It has rank \(c-1\), so \(E\) is a saturated direct summand of \(U\). Consequently the canonical exterior-power quotient satisfies

\[
\bigwedge^{t+1} U/(E\wedge\bigwedge^tU)
\cong\bigwedge^{t+1}(U/E).
\]

Using Steps 2–3 and the fact that there are no other reduced chain groups beyond degree \(t+1\) or below the full \(t\)-skeleton, one obtains

\[
\widetilde H_t\cong\mathbb Z^{\binom{c-1}{t+1}}.
\]

The quotient and exterior-power isomorphisms are natural under vertex permutations that preserve graph edges, and in particular under all graph automorphisms. This gives the asserted integral representation, not just its rank.

**5. Upper homology and its integral freeness.** The boundary image has rank \(\binom{n-1}{t+1}-\binom{c-1}{t+1}\). Since the group of \((t+1)\)-chains is free of rank \(q\), its kernel is free, of rank

\[
q-\binom{n-1}{t+1}+\binom{c-1}{t+1}
=\binom{n-1}{t+2}-I_{t+2}(G)+\binom{c-1}{t+1}.
\]

These are the claimed upper spheres. The rank is nonnegative because it is the rank of a kernel of a map between free abelian groups. The lower quotient is also free by Step 4, hence no torsion occurs.

**6. Upgrade from homology to integral homotopy.** The full \(t\)-skeleton of a simplex is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of \(t\)-spheres: an elementary collapse or the standard vertex-cone relative cellular reduction yields exactly \(\binom{n-1}{t+1}\) spheres. Add the allowed \((t+1)\)-simplices by their attaching maps. Since \(t\ge2\), the target wedge of \(t\)-spheres is simply connected, and Hurewicz identifies its group of maps of an attaching \(t\)-sphere with its degree vector in \(\mathbb Z^{\binom{n-1}{t+1}}\). Thus the homotopy type of the resulting two-stage CW complex is determined by the integral attaching matrix, up to invertible changes of bases of the bottom and top sphere wedges. Step 4 says its cokernel is torsion-free; therefore every nonzero integer Smith invariant is a unit. Elementary basis transformations give a diagonal matrix with ones and zeros. The unit pairs are contractible and cancel homotopically; the remaining cells are unattached \(t\)- and \((t+1)\)-spheres with the multiplicities computed in Steps 4–5. This proves the displayed **integral homotopy equivalence**, not solely a homology calculation.

## Verification

The standalone script `verify.py` enumerates every labeled simple graph on two through six vertices and every nontrivial tolerance parameter between two and four. For each, it constructs the actual top-dimensional simplicial boundary matrix and performs independent sparse mod-two elimination, comparing both resulting Betti dimensions to the theorem. All 100,416 graph-parameter cases passed. It additionally constructs signed integral boundary matrices and computes Smith normal forms for a seven-path, a seven-cycle, two disjoint triangles, an edgeless seven-vertex graph, and a disconnected seven-vertex graph at tolerance three. Every nonzero Smith invariant is one. The captured output is in `verification_output.txt`; replay uses `python verify.py` with Python and SymPy installed. These finite checks supplement the preceding general proof; no finite sample is asserted to establish the universal statement.

## Relationship to prior work

Tolerance complexes and their general Leray/collapsibility bounds were introduced and studied by Kim and Lew (Combinatorica, 2023). Selvaraja's publicly available November 2026 paper studies tolerance complexes of chordal clique complexes, Leray numbers, collapsibility and squarefree symbolic powers, furnishing the contemporary motivation for examining exact tolerance homotopy types. Their investigated results do not state the above uniform two-degree integral wedge and component-module formula for arbitrary strictly one-dimensional graph complexes. Work on bounded-independence graph complexes uses a distinct face condition and can retain high-dimensional faces not present here. The restriction \(t\ge2\) is essential to the attaching-map argument; no claim is made for tolerance one. The theorem identifies an integral automorphism module. A search of relevant indexed research claims likewise did not locate a dominating theorem, but absence of indexed matches does not establish publication novelty.

## Limitations

The input graph must be represented as its strictly one-dimensional simplicial complex, not its flag or clique complex when triangles are present. No formula is asserted for general higher-dimensional input complexes or for the case \(t=1\), which is addressed separately in existing work. The naturality assertion concerns the lower homology module, not an equivariant homotopy equivalence. A complete global publication-priority review and any independent mathematical validation remain outstanding; unindexed or inaccessible equivalent work remains possible.
