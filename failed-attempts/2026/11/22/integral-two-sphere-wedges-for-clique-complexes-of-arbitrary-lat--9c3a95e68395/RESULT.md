# Integral two-sphere wedges for clique complexes of arbitrary Latin-square graphs

## Finding
Let \(M\) be an arbitrary Latin square of order \(n\ge5\), with \(I(M)\) intercalates (two-by-two subsquares of the form \(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\b&a\end{smallmatrix}\), where \(a\ne b\)). Form its Latin-square graph \(L(M)\), whose \(n^2\) vertices are cells \((r,c)\), with two distinct cells adjacent if they share a row, column, or symbol. For the full flag/clique complex, there is an integral homotopy equivalence
\[
|\operatorname{Cl}(L(M))|\simeq\bigvee^{(n-1)^3-I(M)}S^2.
\]
The equivalence holds for every Latin square, including nonassociative and noncyclic examples and even orders. In particular, its fundamental group is trivial; its reduced integral homology is free of rank \((n-1)^3-I(M)\) in degree two and vanishes in all other degrees. This is a homotopy statement, not just a Betti-number identity.

## Assumptions and scope
A Latin square of order \(n\) uses \(n\) symbols, with every symbol appearing once per row and once per column. A *line* is the set of \(n\) cells in a fixed row, fixed column, or of a fixed symbol; there are \(3n\) such lines. A *mixed triangle* has three edges respectively of row, column and symbol types. An intercalate consists of two rows and two columns whose four entries use two distinct symbols in the alternating pattern. All cliques, including nonmaximal ones, belong to the full clique complex. The word wedge denotes the ordinary based wedge up to unpointed homotopy, with no equivariance assertion.

## Proof
**1. The graph's small cliques.** Distinct cells share at most one of row, column and symbol. Every triangle is either contained in a single line or mixed. For each row edge \(\{(r,c),(r,d)\}\), \(c\ne d\), precisely two mixed triangles contain it: the third point has the column of one endpoint and the symbol of the other, with the roles exchanged. This gives exactly \(2n\binom n2=n^2(n-1)\) mixed triangles. Every four-clique either lies within a line or is the tetrahedron of an intercalate; every clique of size at least five lies within one line. Indeed an external cell can be adjacent to at most two members of any fixed line (once by each of the other two types), so a clique with three collinear cells lies wholly in that line. In a four-clique with no three collinear cells, the three edges incident at any vertex must have all three distinct types. Its edges therefore form a proper three-edge-coloring of the complete graph on four vertices, each type occupying two opposite edges. Taking the row edges and column edges gives a two-by-two rectangle, and the symbol edges across its diagonals give an alternating two-symbol pattern. This is precisely an intercalate; a fifth cell cannot join it without creating a collinear triple, so no nonline five-clique exists. To see uniqueness of a non-line four-clique, start from a mixed triangle, apply row/column/symbol permutations, and write it as \(a=(r,c)\), \(b=(r,d)\), \(z=(s,c)\) with \(M(b)=M(z)\). A fourth cell adjacent to all three and not in their lines must be \((s,d)\), with symbol \(M(a)\). This exists as a completion precisely when these four cells form an intercalate. Thus each mixed triangle belongs to at most one extra four-simplex, and two intercalate tetrahedra have no triangle in common. No line triangle belongs to an intercalate tetrahedron. These classifications may also be checked directly using the Latin property.

**2. A genuine two-dimensional simplicial collapse.** Fix one anchor vertex in each of the \(3n\) line simplices. For each line, order all its subsets \(\sigma\) of size at least three that avoid the anchor by decreasing size, and remove the elementary pair \((\sigma,\sigma\cup\{a\})\). Every such \(\sigma\) is a free face at its turn: any other possible coface within its line has already been removed at a larger size, and no face with at least three cells from a line belongs to any non-line four-clique. Distinct line simplices intersect in at most one vertex, so these operations do not interfere. Each line retains precisely its edges and its \(\binom{n-1}{2}\) anchor-containing triangles. For each intercalate tetrahedron, remove it together with one of its mixed triangular facets; that facet is free because no other tetrahedron contains it. This leaves a two-dimensional simplicial complex \(K\), obtained by elementary collapses from the full clique complex. No edge or vertex is removed. The exact face counts of \(K\) are
\[
 f_0=n^2,\qquad f_1=3n\binom n2,\qquad f_2=3n\binom{n-1}{2}+n^2(n-1)-I(M).
\]
Consequently \(\chi(K)=1+(n-1)^3-I(M)\).

**3. Simple connectivity is proved geometrically.** First, a symbol edge \(p q\) joins cells in different rows and columns. Let \(w\) be the corner in the row of \(p\) and column of \(q\). The three edges \(pq,pw,wq\) form a clique triangle. Thus, in the fundamental group, every symbol edge may be replaced by a row edge followed by a column edge. It remains to contract edge loops in the rook graph \(K_n\square K_n\), using triangles from the full complex. In each row and column, all three-cycles bound triangles. Every elementary rectangle also bounds a simplicial disk:

Let its corners in cyclic order be \(a=(r,c)\), \(b=(r,d)\), \(q=(s,d)\), \(e=(s,c)\), with \(r\ne s\), \(c\ne d\). If either diagonal \(aq\) or \(be\) is a symbol edge, its two incident triangles fill the rectangle. Otherwise put \(t=M(b)\); let \(w\) be the unique cell with row \(s\) and symbol \(t\), and let \(z\) be the unique cell with column \(c\) and symbol \(t\). The six vertices \(a,b,q,e,w,z\) are distinct. The six clique triangles
\[
 (a,b,z),\quad(a,e,z),\quad(b,q,w),\quad(b,w,z),\quad(q,e,w),\quad(e,w,z)
\]
form a combinatorial disk: their only single-incidence edges are \(ab,bq,qe,ea\); all seven other edges occur twice, and the two interior vertex links are cycles. Thus the rectangle is nullhomotopic. The filled row and column triangles together with all rook rectangles form the two-dimensional cellular skeleton of the contractible product \(\Delta^{n-1}\times\Delta^{n-1}\). Its fundamental group is therefore trivial. Every edge loop in the full complex has been homotoped into this product skeleton, so \(\pi_1(\operatorname{Cl}(L(M)))=0\). Elementary collapses preserve this, giving \(\pi_1(K)=0\).

**4. Integral homotopy conclusion.** The finite connected simply connected two-dimensional CW complex \(K\) has no reduced homology in degree one or degrees at least three. As \(C_2(K;\mathbb Z)\) is free abelian and there are no three-cells, its \(H_2\), being a subgroup of \(C_2\), is free abelian. Euler characteristic now gives \(\operatorname{rank}H_2=\chi(K)-1=(n-1)^3-I(M)\). This integer is nonnegative; in fact the known Latin-square intercalate bounds make it positive for \(n\ge5\). Hurewicz supplies based sphere maps representing a \(\mathbb Z\)-basis of \(H_2\). The induced map from the wedge of that many two-spheres to \(K\) is an integral homology isomorphism between simply connected CW complexes. The homological Whitehead theorem makes it a homotopy equivalence. Composing with the collapses proves the displayed claim for all \(M\).

## Verification
The included standard-library program `verify.py` independently reconstructs Latin-square graph edges, maximal cliques and their entire face poset. It executes **every stated elementary collapse pair** and tests that each lower face has exactly one surviving immediate coface. It also constructs every rook rectangle filling and confirms the six-triangle disk incidence criterion. Four test Latin squares were checked: cyclic orders five and six, a nonabelian \(S_3\) multiplication table of order six, and the elementary abelian group of order eight. Their intercalate counts are respectively \(0,9,27,112\), yielding predicted two-sphere multiplicities \(64,116,98,231\). Independent mod-two boundary ranks in the first three cases confirm zero first homology and the correct rank of second homology of the reduced two-complex. The executable's actual output is stored in `expected_output.txt`. Exhaustive finite checks are corroboration only: Step 1–4 prove the unbounded family symbolically.

## Relationship to prior work
Cioabă and Mim, *On the homology groups of clique complexes of strongly regular graphs*, arXiv:2606.27328 (first public arXiv version June 25, 2026), Theorems 3.6, 3.8, 3.12 and Propositions 3.7 and 3.11, already establish the field-homology vanishing and degree-two rank \((n-1)^3-I(M)\) for **every** Latin square of order at least five, and the relevant intercalate clique classification. Those results are explicitly prior and not claimed as new. The integral Smith-form and Laplacian deductions there are also not new here. The previously published *Integral homotopy type of cyclic Latin-square clique complexes of odd order* classifies only cyclic odd-order squares with no intercalates. Neither statement classifies fundamental groups or full homotopy for arbitrary Latin squares. The new result is the two-dimensional elementary-collapse plus explicit integral nullhomotopy of all rook squares, establishing simple connectivity and converting known ranks into complete homotopy types for noncyclic and even-order tables.

## Limitations
The theorem assumes Latin-square graphs of order at least five. It does not classify arbitrary strongly regular graphs, arbitrary orthogonal-array graphs with more parallel classes, homotopy actions of isotopy or automorphism groups, or stable identification of individual sphere generators. The exact count \(I(M)\) is a separate Latin-square invariant, not an algorithm for determining it without inspecting \(M\). A mathematically equivalent theorem in unindexed literature remains a residual publication-priority risk. This result has not undergone independent mathematical validation.
