# Lattice extreme-value law for maximal-entropy frequency shifts

## Finding

For every integer \(k\geq1\), let \(\Sigma_k\) be the one-sided frequency shift with threshold \(p_c=k/(k+1)\) in the sense of Becker--Baraviera and Reyes. Let \(\mu_k\) denote its unique measure of maximal entropy. Under \(\mu_k\), successive **complete** zero--one blocks are \(0^{m}1^{km}\) with independent lengths \(m\in\{1,2,\ldots\}\) and probabilities \(2^{-m}\), observed at the successive \(10\) junctions in the natural two-sided extension.

If \(R_N\) is the maximum zero-run length among blocks entirely contained in the first \(N\) coordinates, with \(R_N=0\) if none occurs, then for any integer sequence \(r_N\) with \(r_N-\log_2N\) bounded,
\[
\mu_k(R_N\le r_N)
 =\exp\!\left(-\frac{N\,2^{-r_N}}{2(k+1)}\right)+o(1).
\]
In particular, \(R_N/\log_2N\to1\) almost surely. Thus the extremal law is logarithmic with a nonconvergent, lattice-periodic centering: if \(\{\log_2N_j\}\to\theta\) and \(\theta+x\notin\mathbb Z\), then
\[
\mu_k\!\left(R_{N_j}\le\lfloor\log_2N_j+x\rfloor\right)
\longrightarrow\exp\!\left(-\frac{2^{\theta-\lfloor\theta+x\rfloor}}{2(k+1)}\right).
\]
Define the centered count \(S_n=\sum_{t=0}^{n-1}((k+1)x_t-k)\). Then for \(\mu_k\)-almost every \(x\),
\[
 \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\max_{0\le n\le N}|S_n|}{\log_2N}=k,
 \qquad
 \sup_{n\ge0}\mathbb E_{\mu_k}|S_n|^q<\infty
 \quad\text{for every fixed }q>0.
\]
Consequently the asymptotic variance per unit time is zero despite the strictly positive entropy \(h_{\mu_k}=\log 2/(k+1)\).

## Assumptions and scope

The alphabet is \(\{0,1\}\) and \(k\) is a fixed positive integer. At any prefix ending with \(1\), the next symbol is forced to \(1\) exactly when the current proportion of ones is at most \(k/(k+1)\); after a zero, either symbol may occur. The frequency shift is the closed union of forward shifts of sequences following this rule. The results concern its unique measure of maximal entropy, **not** every invariant measure and not pointwise worst-case sequences. The block descriptions use the natural two-sided stationary extension; all statistics defined with nonnegative times are also statements about the original one-sided shift.

A *complete block in* \([0,N)\) means a word \(0^m1^{km}\) starting after a visible \(10\) junction (or with that junction at coordinate \(0\)) and ending at a later visible \(10\) junction, both boundaries lying inside the window. Equivalently, it is an entire run pair with both run boundaries observed. This convention excludes at most two boundary fragments, which do not affect the limit.

## Proof

**1. Identification of the stationary equilibrium law.** Reyes (arXiv:2611.10995v1, Theorem C, Proposition 6.6 and Theorem D) proves that every invariant measure on \(\Sigma_k\) is supported on the coded shift generated by \(G_k=\{0^m1^{km}:m\ge1\}\), that its entropy is \(\log2/(k+1)\), and that its maximal-entropy measure is unique. Make two-sided independent integer lengths \((m_j)_{j\in\mathbb Z}\) with \(\Pr(m_j=m)=2^{-m}\), replace each \(m_j\) by \(0^{m_j}1^{km_j}\), and stationarize the resulting concatenation by a size-biased choice of the block covering the time origin, followed by a uniform position inside that block. This is a shift-invariant measure \(\nu_k\) supported on the coded shift. Its mean block length is
\[
\mathbb E L=(k+1)\sum_{m\ge1}m2^{-m}=2(k+1).
\]
Its entropy per block is \(H(m)=\sum m2^{-m}\log2=2\log2\), so the standard induced-entropy (Abramov) formula, or a direct renewal word-count argument, gives
\[
 h_{\nu_k}(\sigma)=\frac{H(m)}{\mathbb EL}=\frac{\log2}{k+1}=h_{\mathrm{top}}(\Sigma_k).
\]
By uniqueness, \(\nu_k=\mu_k\). In particular the block lengths read at consecutive full \(10\) junctions are iid geometric with \(\Pr(m>r)=2^{-r}\) for every integer \(r\ge0\). The origin-block length \(m^\star\) has the size-biased law \(\Pr(m^\star=m)=m2^{-m}/2\).

**2. Exact return-index extreme law.** For the first \(J\) complete blocks after a fixed junction, let \(Q_J=\max_{1\le j\le J}m_j\). Independence yields the **exact** equality
\[
 \Pr(Q_J\le r)=(1-2^{-r})^J\qquad (J\ge1,\ r\ge1).
\]
Using \(J=2^j\), a union bound gives \(\Pr(Q_J>(1+\epsilon)j)\le 2^{-\epsilon j}\), and \((1-u)^J\le e^{-Ju}\) gives \(\Pr(Q_J<(1-\epsilon)j)\le\exp(-2^{\epsilon j+O(1)})\). Borel--Cantelli, followed by monotonic interpolation between dyadic indices, proves \(Q_J/\log_2J\to1\) almost surely.

**3. Renewal time change and the lattice extreme profile.** The complete block lengths in physical coordinates are \(L_j=(k+1)m_j\) with finite mean \(\ell=2(k+1)\). The strong law yields \(J_N/N\to1/\ell\) almost surely, where \(J_N\) counts blocks completely contained in \([0,N)\). For every fixed \(\epsilon>0\), with probability tending to one,
\[
(1-\epsilon)N/\ell-O(1)\le J_N\le(1+\epsilon)N/\ell+O(1).
\]
The initial size-biased fragment consumes only an almost surely finite number of positions and is independent of the subsequent iid blocks. On this high-probability event, maxima of deterministic numbers \(\lfloor(1\pm\epsilon)N/\ell\rfloor+O(1)\) of iid geometric variables sandwich \(R_N\). For \(r_N=\log_2N+O(1)\), expand \(\log(1-2^{-r_N})=-2^{-r_N}+O(2^{-2r_N})\) in the exact return-index formula. The two sandwich probabilities tend uniformly to exponential expressions with coefficients \((1\pm\epsilon)/\ell\). Let \(\epsilon\downarrow0\) to get
\[
 \Pr(R_N\le r_N)-\exp(-N2^{-r_N}/\ell)\longrightarrow0.
\]
The a.s. logarithmic law follows by the strong law and Step 2. Writing \(N=2^{\lfloor\log_2N\rfloor+\{\log_2N\}}\) produces the stated lattice subsequence formula, with the stated non-boundary condition on \(x\).

**4. Symbol-count fluctuations.** Let \(H_t\) be a two-sided block potential, zero at every \(10\) junction, dropping by \(k\) for each zero and rising by \(1\) for each one. Every block \(0^m1^{km}\) brings \(H\) back to zero, and within it \(-km\le H_t\le0\), attaining \(-km\) just after the zero run. Exactly
\[
 S_n=H_n-H_0.
\]
The origin-block potential is bounded by \(k m^\star\), which has moments of all orders; stationarity gives \(\sup_n\mathbb E|S_n|^q\le C_q\mathbb E|H_0|^q<\infty\) for every \(q>0\) (using \(|a-b|^q\le C_q(|a|^q+|b|^q)\)). Every complete block attains depth \(km_j\); at all times the potential is bounded by the maximum depth among the intersected blocks. The initial and terminal partial blocks have negligible effect on the ratio to \(\log_2N\): the initial length is finite almost surely, and the largest length among at most \(O(N)\) intersected blocks is \((1+o(1))\log_2N\) almost surely. Thus \(\max_{n\le N}|H_n-H_0|/\log_2N\to k\) almost surely. Uniform second moments imply \(\operatorname{Var}(S_n)/n\to0\). \(\square\)

## Relationship to prior work

Reyes (arXiv:2611.10995v1, November 7, 2026) establishes the coded structure, entropy and uniqueness used as premises. The manuscript's full Sections 5--7 do not state the stationary block distribution, physical-time lattice extreme-value profile or sharp logarithmic discrepancy maximum. Pavlov's *On entropy and intrinsic ergodicity of coded subshifts* (arXiv:1803.05966, 2018 preprint; Proceedings AMS, 2020), Theorems 1.3--1.5, proves broad entropy/uniqueness and Bernoulli-type properties for uniquely decipherable coded shifts, but does not give this explicit physical-time maximum statistic. The elementary i.i.d. geometric maximum identity is classical; originality is claimed only for the complete **frequency-shift-specific equilibrium extreme and discrepancy theorem**, not for that identity or for general renewal entropy.

## Limitations

No assertion is made for noninteger threshold ratios, for arbitrary invariant measures or for universal statistics of all points in the topological shift. The centered-count law is an almost-sure property of the maximal-entropy measure. The lattice limit has subsequences rather than a single centered limiting distribution. The Markov-renewal identification relies on the established coded-system and intrinsic-ergodicity results. Older unindexed work on these particular frequency shifts or equivalent balanced renewal codings could contain an earlier equivalent of the combined theorem.
