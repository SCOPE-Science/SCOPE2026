Translating by the base Weyl unitary leaves a sum aI+bU+cV with UV=qVU. The two relative frequencies are independent, so the gauge action is onto both phase circles and allows arbitrary coefficient phases to be removed. For two terms the relative unitary has full circle spectrum; for q=1 torus invariance gives the full joint spectrum. For positive weights, a norm-maximizing sequence for the coefficient sum would make both (U-I)h and (V-I)h tend to zero by the exact squared triangle defect. The commutator bound |1-q| <= 2(||(U-I)h||+||(V-I)h||) rules this out, and Cauchy–Schwarz gives the stated |1-q|^2 a min(b,c)/8 gap. Fourier trace orthogonality reduces Young equality for nonzero inputs to proportional coefficient magnitudes and coefficient-sum equality on their common support. At q=-1, Schur's lemma makes the squares scalar in irreducible representations, and the anticommutation eigenspaces give the 2-by-2 Pauli model; a nonempty fiber and the surjective gauge action supply all phases for the norm in the concrete subalgebra. The latter modulus has minimum max(2M-S,0) by the triangle inequality and realizable triangle/collinear configurations. This gives both norm branches, including the junction and all zero-coefficient cases.
