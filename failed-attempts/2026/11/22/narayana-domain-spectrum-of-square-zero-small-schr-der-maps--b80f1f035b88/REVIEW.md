# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

Ordinary partial composition gives f^2 empty exactly when domain and image are disjoint. For a k-point domain d_1<...<d_k with complement e_1<...<e_m, m=n-k, encode x_i=d_i-i and j_i by f(d_i)=e_(j_i). Regressiveness and disjointness give j_i<=x_i, and isotonicity gives weak increase of the j_i. Conversely x_i+i restores the domain in {2,...,n}, the same complement restores the images, and both constraints hold. Reversal and subtraction by one give the stated two-row boxed plane partition, including the empty k=0 case and the all-zero box at k=n-1. The rectangular product gives the two binomial expressions. Vandermonde normalizes the distribution: K is hypergeometric, drawing n-1 objects from two classes of size n each, so its mean and variance agree with the stated formulas. A regressive finite chain map is nilpotent exactly when all defined steps are strict, and shifting its domain down by one identifies these maps with all regressive isotone partial maps on n-1 elements. The large Schröder and Catalan sequences have the same power n^(-3/2) in their asymptotics, so the claimed exponential ratio follows.

## originality

FAIL

The core square-zero domain census is a direct specialization of the published Laradji–Umar partial-map path bijection together with their evaluated horizontal-segment refinement. The overlap is not inferred merely from identical Narayana numbers: the source explicitly records domain by the levels of vertical steps and image by the levels of horizontal steps. Thus square-zero amounts exactly to avoiding a horizontal step immediately followed by a vertical step. Expanding each diagonal step into horizontal then vertical, with inverse obtained by collapsing every peak, reduces this exact domain-conditioned subclass to the paths counted in Theorem 3.8 with n-k horizontal segments. The remaining moment and rarity claims are direct consequences of the resulting known row and the known Schröder count, not a distinct enumerative remainder.

## value

FAIL

The square-zero statistic is natural, but the exact spectrum here is mechanically implied by the existing path model and its evaluated Narayana refinement. The alternative two-row proof gives another standard encoding, not an independently useful unknown invariant or a new structural consequence beyond that implication. The moment formulas are textbook hypergeometric moments and the rarity is a direct comparison of the existing Catalan and shifted Schröder enumerations. No substantive distinct remainder is established by this frozen finding.
