# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

In any degree-at-most-two graph, an embedded cycle occupies a whole component. Thus a union of cycles permits free amalgamation over those components, while completing an old path to cycles of two different lengths gives an incompatible span even if identifications outside the base are allowed. An isolate has the same obstruction with a triangle versus a quadrilateral. The total old deficit is 2(k+i), all of it needing old–new edges because the extension is induced. At most two such incidences can be supplied per new vertex, and a lone old isolate requires two distinct new neighbors. The cyclic bridge construction attains the bound for at least two unfinished components (including the two-isolate four-cycle), with a one-vertex bridge for a lone nontrivial path and a triangle for the lone isolate. At equality outside the exception, every new vertex must use both degree slots on distinct old deficit vertices and there are no new–new edges. Choosing its old neighbor pair is exactly one factor of the displayed polynomial for each fixed new label. This also yields 6, 2, and 6 in the three examples by partitioning the old endpoints into two labelled pairs, attaching both bridges to the sole isolate and one endpoint each, and labelling the three pairs of isolates, respectively. The proposed countable limit has the correct age and is cofinally ultrahomogeneous on whole-cycle unions; these exhaust it and have the ordinary free amalgamation extension property in that cofinal class. In any computable copy search for both neighbors and traverse the cycle to a known starting vertex; one can compute the least vertex of the component and enumerate components by length without duplicates. Matching cycles of each length by enumeration order and extending each chosen cycle orientation gives a computable isomorphism.

## originality

FAIL

The frozen finding presents the sharp all-input minimum-vertex law as part of its exact contribution. Once its base characterization identifies the target with induced 2-regular completion, this quantitative law is a direct specialization of Erdős and Kelly's existing induced regularization theorem, including the exception. Their deficit argument also already identifies the saturated case underlying the labelled census. The coefficient is the elementary encoding of those saturated labelled incidence choices. The degree-two amalgamation-base interpretation and the computable copy argument are not claimed to have been found verbatim in that literature; they do not make the stated minimum-vertex law a distinct result. The failure is verified prior coverage of a material claim, not a correctness failure or an access issue.

## value

FAIL

Induced amalgamation is a natural motivation and the characterization of the bases and their limit is a useful structural description. But as submitted the principal quantitative contribution is a minimum already determined by general induced regularization, accompanied by a coefficient expression that mechanically encodes the saturated incidence choices. It does not classify completion structures beyond this encoding (for example, by resulting cycle lengths), nor establish a new quantitative phenomenon that needs the exact repeated minimum. This does not meet the value bar for the frozen sharp completion-and-census claim. No narrower or strengthened theorem is delivered as a repair.
