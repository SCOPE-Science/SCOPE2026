# Sharp fourth-power Fourier synthesis on finite-field coordinate crosses

## Finding
Let \(q\ge2\) be a prime power, \(G=\mathbb F_q^2\), and \(S_q=\{(x,y)\in G:xy=0\}\), a union of two coordinate lines. For a nontrivial additive character \(\psi\) and an arbitrary complex function supported on \(S_q\), write
\[
\widehat f(\xi)=q^{-1}\sum_{z\in G}f(z)\psi(-\xi\cdot z).
\]
All Fourier-side norms use counting measure. The sharp constant is
\[
\boxed{\mathcal C_4(S_q):=\sup_{0\ne f,\;\operatorname{supp}f\subseteq S_q}\frac{\|\widehat f\|_\infty}{\|\widehat f\|_4}=\left(1+\frac{(q-1)^2}{[1+2(q-1)^{1/3}]^3}\right)^{-1/4}.}
\]
Set \(t=(q-1)^{1/3}\) and \(D=1+2t\). All nonzero extremizers, and no others, are scalar multiples of additive-character modulations of the function
\[
f_q(0,0)=\frac{2+2t-q}{D},\qquad f_q(x,0)=f_q(0,y)=\frac{1+t}{D}\quad(x,y\ne0).
\]
The central weight is positive for \(q\in\{2,3,4,5\}\) and negative for every prime power \(q\ge7\). As \(q\to\infty\) through prime powers, \(\mathcal C_4(S_q)\sim8^{1/4}q^{-1/4}\).

## Assumptions and scope
The ambient additive group is that of \(\mathbb F_q^2\), including nonprime prime powers. The support includes the origin once, so \(|S_q|=2q-1\). The phase \(\psi\) can be any fixed nontrivial additive character. The Fourier transform is unitary with factor \(q^{-1}\); there is no normalization by the support size. A modulation means multiplication of \(f(z)\) by \(\psi(\eta\cdot z)\), for a fixed \(\eta\in G\). The statement is for the fourth-power synthesis ratio with unrestricted complex weights, not for a Fourier extension \(\ell^2\)-to-\(L^4\) ratio.

## Proof
**Reduction to a uniquely symmetric minimizer.** For each nonzero \(f\) and frequency \(\xi_0\) attaining \(\|\widehat f\|_\infty\), modulation shifts that Fourier value to the origin; scalar scaling then makes \(\widehat f(0)=1\). Consequently the reciprocal of \(\mathcal C_4(S_q)\) is the minimum \(\|\widehat f\|_4\) under the affine constraint \(\sum_{z\in S_q}f(z)=q\). Existence follows from finite dimensionality and coercivity. The function \(f\mapsto\|\widehat f\|_4^4\) is strictly convex as a real functional, since \(|w|^4\) is strictly convex and the Fourier transform is invertible. Hence this constrained minimizer is unique.

The finite group generated by independent nonzero dilations of the two coordinates and their interchange preserves \(S_q\), fixes the origin in frequency, permutes all Fourier frequencies and preserves the constraint. Its unique minimizer must be invariant under the group. This group has exactly two orbits on \(S_q\): the origin and all \(2(q-1)\) nonzero axial points. Thus the minimizer has a central value \(a\) and a common outer value \(b\). No potential asymmetric or complex competitor escapes this conclusion. Conjugation also preserves the constraint and the fourth norm, so the unique minimizer is real.

**Exact finite-dimensional minimization.** Write \(x=a-2b\), \(y=qb\). The mass constraint gives \(x+2y=q\). The orthogonality formula \(\sum_{u\ne0}\psi(vu)=q-1\) when \(v=0\), and \(-1\) otherwise, shows the Fourier transform has exactly three values:
\[
\widehat f(0,0)=1,\qquad
\widehat f(\xi,0)=\widehat f(0,\eta)=\frac{q+x}{2q}\quad(\xi,\eta\ne0),
\qquad \widehat f(\xi,\eta)=\frac{x}{q}\quad(\xi\eta\ne0).
\]
Their multiplicities are \(1\), \(2(q-1)\), and \((q-1)^2\), respectively. Therefore
\[
\|\widehat f\|_4^4=1+\frac{2(q-1)}{q^4}\left(\frac{q+x}{2}\right)^4+\frac{(q-1)^2x^4}{q^4}.
\]
Its derivative vanishes precisely when
\[
(q+x)^3+8(q-1)x^3=0.
\]
Strict convexity forces this to be the unique minimum, attained at \(x=-q/D\) with \(D=1+2t\), \(t^3=q-1\). Substitution gives
\[
\min\|\widehat f\|_4^4=1+\frac{t^6}{D^3}=1+\frac{(q-1)^2}{D^3}.
\]
Recovering \(b=(q-x)/(2q)=(1+t)/D\) and \(a=x+2b=(2+2t-q)/D\) proves the constant and the normalized extremizer. Every original equality case becomes this unique minimizer after modulation and scaling; conversely every listed modulation and scaling attains equality.

**Sign boundary and asymptotics.** The sign of \(a\) equals that of \(2+2(q-1)^{1/3}-q\). For real \(q\ge2\), the function \(q-2-2(q-1)^{1/3}\) is strictly increasing, is negative at \(q=5\), and is positive at \(q=6\). There is no prime-power field size strictly between \(5\) and \(7\). The asymptotic follows from \((q-1)^2/D^3\sim q/8\).

## Verification
An independent direct character-table checker in `artifacts/check_certificate.py` constructs normalized Fourier transforms and verifies the three Fourier levels, their multiplicities, the extremal norm formula, and the strict comparison with deterministic nonsymmetric perturbations for \(q=2,3,5,7,11\). It checks numerical identities with explicit tolerances. No finite test is used to infer the all-prime-power theorem: that follows from the strictly convex symmetry reduction and exact cubic equation above.

## Relationship to prior work
Iosevich, Li and Yu (2026) prove exact synthesis constants for supports admitting a transitive affine symmetry group, and discuss the challenge of computing them outside that setting. The split quadratic cone \(S_q\) has two orbits under its natural stabilizing linear symmetry group, so their transitive-indicator formula does not calculate the present constant. The published result *Sharp fourth-moment extension and one-axis rigidity for finite abelian crosses* solves a different optimization: maximizing the fourth Fourier norm subject to a source second-norm constraint. Its extremizers live on one axis, whereas the present synthesis minimizer under fixed Fourier evaluation occupies both axes. The published ternary split-cone fourth-moment extension result likewise maximizes a different ratio. No implication from those upper-moment inequalities yields the constrained fourth-moment minimum or the center-sign transition.

## Limitations
No formula is asserted for other Fourier target exponents or arbitrary unions of lines, nor for the exact synthesis constant at composite non-prime-power moduli. Numerical checks are supplementary to, not substitutes for, the analytical all-size proof. A complete search of older unindexed finite-field Fourier-synthesis literature is not claimed; an independently worded prior theorem remains possible.
