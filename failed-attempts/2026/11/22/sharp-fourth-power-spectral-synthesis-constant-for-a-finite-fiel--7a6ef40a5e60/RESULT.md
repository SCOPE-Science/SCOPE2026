# Sharp fourth-power spectral-synthesis constant for a finite-field coordinate cross

## Finding
Let \(q\ge2\) be any prime power, \(G=\mathbb F_q^2\), and
\[
 S=(\mathbb F_q\times\{0\})\cup(\{0\}\times\mathbb F_q).
\]
Using counting \(\ell^4\) norm on the Fourier side, define the exact synthesis coefficient
\[
 \mathcal C_4(S)=\sup_{0\ne f,\,\operatorname{supp}f\subseteq S}
 \frac{\|\widehat f\|_\infty}{\|\widehat f\|_4}.
\]
Write \(s=(q-1)^{1/3}\). Then
\[
 \boxed{\mathcal C_4(S)=\left(1+\frac{(q-1)^2}{(1+2s)^3}\right)^{-1/4}.}
\]
All nonzero optimizers, and no others, are scalar multiples of additive-character modulations of the two-level function
\[
 f_q(0,0)=\frac{2+2s-q}{q(1+2s)},\qquad
 f_q(x,0)=f_q(0,x)=\frac{1+s}{q(1+2s)}\quad(x\ne0).
\]
The unnormalized zero-frequency Fourier sum is normalized by \(\sum_{z\in S}f_q(z)=1\). For \(q>2\), this optimizer is not a scalar multiple of the support indicator. The center weight is positive at the prime-power orders \(q=2,3,4,5\), and negative for all prime powers \(q\ge7\); at \(q=2\), the two levels coincide. In particular,
\[
 \mathcal C_4(S_{q=9})=(125/189)^{1/4},\quad
 f_9(0,0)=-1/15,\quad f_9(z)=1/15\ (z\in S\setminus\{0\}).
\]
As \(q\to\infty\) through prime powers, \(\mathcal C_4(S)\sim(8/q)^{1/4}\).

## Assumptions and scope
Fix any nontrivial additive character \(\psi:\mathbb F_q\to\mathbb C\) and use the *unitary* Fourier transform
\[
 \widehat f(u,v)=q^{-1}\sum_{(x,y)\in G} f(x,y)\overline{\psi(ux+vy)}.
\]
The Fourier \(\ell^4\) norm is the unnormalized counting sum \((\sum_{u,v}|\widehat f(u,v)|^4)^{1/4}\); the ratio does not depend on the common transform scale. The two coordinate axes share their origin, counted only once. All complex coefficient choices, including zeros, are allowed, and equality is classified up to a nonzero complex scalar and a character \(z\mapsto\psi(\xi\cdot z)\). There is no restriction to nonnegative coefficients.

## Proof
**1. Modulation, normalization and strict convexity.** Let \(f\ne0\) attain the ratio, and choose a frequency where \(\lvert\widehat f\rvert\) is largest. Modulate \(f\) to put this frequency at zero, and rescale so that \(\sum_{z\in S}f(z)=1\). The ratio then equals the reciprocal of the counting \(\ell^4\) norm of the *unnormalized* Fourier array \(H_f=q\widehat f\). In particular, minimizing \(\|H_f\|_4\) subject to the single mass constraint computes the sharp ratio: for any other \(f\), the same modulation and rescaling is available.

Let \(\Gamma\) be generated by independent nonzero coordinate scalings \((x,y)\mapsto(ux,vy)\), with \(u,v\in\mathbb F_q^*\), and the axis swap. It preserves \(S\), fixes \((0,0)\), and acts transitively on the \(2(q-1)\) punctured-axis points. Each \(f\circ\gamma\) has the same total mass and the same Fourier \(\ell^4\) norm as \(f\), since it permutes the Fourier frequencies. Thus averaging over \(\Gamma\) cannot increase that norm, by convexity. Consequently a minimizer may be taken in the two-level form \(f(0,0)=a\), \(f(z)=b\) at every nonzero \(z\in S\). Moreover \(\ell^4\) is strictly convex: an *arbitrary* minimizer must be invariant under every \(\gamma\in\Gamma\), because equality in the averaging inequality of equally normed Fourier arrays forces all those arrays to be identical, and Fourier inversion then forces all source functions to agree. This supplies the complete equality classification after undoing modulation.

**2. Exact three-level count and scalar minimization.** The normalization is
\[
 a+2(q-1)b=1.
\]
Set \(x=qb\in\mathbb C\). Character orthogonality gives exactly three distinct values for the unnormalized Fourier array:
\[
 H_f(u,v)=\begin{cases}
 1 &(u,v)=(0,0),\\
 1-x &\text{exactly one of }u,v\text{ is zero},\\
 1-2x &u,v\ne0.
 \end{cases}
\]
These have multiplicities \(1,\ 2(q-1),\ (q-1)^2\), respectively. Hence the norm's fourth power is
\[
 P_q(x)=1+2(q-1)|1-x|^4+(q-1)^2|1-2x|^4.
\]
For fixed \(\operatorname{Re}x\), either imaginary term grows strictly as \(|\operatorname{Im}x|\) grows, so the unique minimum has real \(x\). The real function is strictly convex because its second derivative is a positive sum of squared affine factors, at least one nonzero everywhere. Its unique critical point solves
\[
 (x-1)^3+(q-1)(2x-1)^3=0.
\]
Writing \(s=(q-1)^{1/3}>0\) and taking real cube roots gives
\[
 x_*=\frac{1+s}{1+2s},\quad
 1-x_*=\frac{s}{1+2s},\quad
 1-2x_*=-\frac1{1+2s}.
\]
As \(s^3=q-1\), direct substitution yields
\[
 P_q(x_*)=1+\frac{2(q-1)s^4+(q-1)^2}{(1+2s)^4}
 =1+\frac{(q-1)^2}{(1+2s)^3}.
\]
This establishes the sharp coefficient; the two-level optimizer satisfies the mass constraint by construction and attains it.

**3. Equality, signs and asymptotics.** Strict convexity in both the averaging and scalar stages ensures every equality function is a character modulation and scalar multiple of the displayed \(f_q\), with no other possibilities. For \(q>2\), equality of the center and punctured-axis weights would require \(s=q-1\), impossible since \(s^3=q-1\) and \(q-1>1\). The center sign is the sign of \(2+2(q-1)^{1/3}-q\). At \(q=2\) both weights are \(1/3\); for integer prime powers \(q=3,4,5\) the expression is positive, and at \(q\ge7\) it is negative because the function \(q-2-2(q-1)^{1/3}\) is increasing for \(q\ge5\) and already positive at \(q=7\). At \(q=9\), \(s=2\) gives the stated exact weights and fourth-power norm \(189/125\). Finally the nonconstant term of \(P_q(x_*)\) is asymptotic to \(q/8\), proving the displayed large-field asymptotic.

It also checks 120 seeded random complex-supported candidates per field and the exact rational \(q=9\) fourth moment. A completed run reports `PASS 734 supplementary checks`. ## Relationship to prior work
Iosevich, Li and Yu [1, Theorem 4.1] calculate \(\mathcal C_r(S)\) for affine-symmetry-**transitive** supports; their general estimates do not state the present constant or equality cases for a support with a distinguished intersection vertex (the coordinate cross is nontransitive for \(q>2\)). Their proof's convex averaging idea motivates the two-orbit reduction, while the additional nontrivial optimization and signed equality geometry are specific to this set. Bhowmik, Deodhar and Iosevich [2] develop synthesis/uncertainty for general supports, but do not supply this exact finite-field-cross sharp \(\ell^\infty/\ell^4\) ratio.

Two particularly close calculations on precisely the same coordinate-cross support concern **different functionals**: sharp \(\ell^2\)-to-\(\ell^4\) Fourier *extension* on finite abelian crosses [3], and its special five-point ternary version [4]. Their sharp extremizers (one-axis characters or phase-balanced center-and-arms, respectively) are not the present spectral-synthesis extremizers. An exact finite-field two-line self-sampling Gram-spectrum theorem [5] optimizes a linear operator's singular values rather than the nonlinear Fourier norm quotient. These results do not directly imply the displayed synthesis constant.

## Limitations
The theorem assumes two transverse coordinate axes in a two-dimensional vector space over a finite field. It does not cover general low-symmetry supports, higher dimensions, Fourier exponents different from four, or stability under support perturbation. At \(q=2\) the support has extra affine symmetries and the equality case is the indicator. This is a self-review only; no independent mathematical validation or priority guarantee has been obtained. Unindexed equivalent formulas for the two-orbit norm problem cannot be excluded.
