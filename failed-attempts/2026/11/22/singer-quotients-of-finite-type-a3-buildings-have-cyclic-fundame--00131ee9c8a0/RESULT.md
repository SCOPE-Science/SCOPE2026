# Singer quotients of finite type-A3 buildings have cyclic fundamental group and explicit integral homology

## Finding

For every prime power \(q\ge2\), put \(E=\mathbb F_{q^4}\). Let \(\mathcal B_q\) be the order complex of the poset of proper nonzero \(\mathbb F_q\)-linear subspaces of \(E\), ordered by inclusion. The vertices have vector dimensions one, two, or three; a simplex is a strict chain of such subspaces. Let \(G_q=E^\times/\mathbb F_q^\times\) act by scalar multiplication, and define the **ordinary geometric orbit space** \(Y_q=|\mathcal B_q|/G_q\), not the Borel construction.

**Theorem.** There is an isomorphism
\[
\pi_1(Y_q)\cong H_1(Y_q;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z/(q^2+1),
\qquad H_2(Y_q;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{q^2(q-1)}.
\]
Moreover \(H_0(Y_q;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z\), and \(H_j(Y_q;\mathbb Z)=0\) for all \(j>2\). The orbit-cell numbers in dimensions zero through two are
\[
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=\big(q+3,\;3(q^2+q+1),\;(q+1)(q^2+q+1)\big).
\]
This gives a family of explicit fundamental groups of unbounded order and torsion-free second homology despite nonfree actions on simply connected complexes.

## Assumptions and scope

The quantifier ranges over **all prime powers** \(q\ge2\), including nonprime finite-field orders and odd characteristic. A Singer group here means the **projective** cyclic group of order
\[
N=|G_q|=\frac{q^4-1}{q-1}=(q+1)(q^2+1).
\]
The action preserves the three subspace dimensions and hence the vertex types of every simplex. Cell counts and homology refer to the topological quotient of the geometric realization. Integral homology uses ordinary, untwisted coefficients. Nothing assumes \(q+1\) and \(q^2+1\) are coprime.

## Proof

**1. Classification of nontrivial stabilizers.** A projective element represented by \(a\in E^\times\) fixes a one-dimensional \(\mathbb F_q\)-subspace \(\mathbb F_qv\) precisely when \(av\in\mathbb F_qv\), hence precisely when \(a\in\mathbb F_q^\times\). Thus the projective Singer group acts freely and transitively on points. The trace pairing \((x,y)\mapsto\operatorname{Tr}_{E/\mathbb F_q}(xy)\) identifies hyperplanes with dual points, and multiplication by \(a\) acts on dual points by \(a^{-1}\); therefore every hyperplane stabilizer is trivial as well.

Suppose a nonidentity projective class represented by \(a\) fixes a two-dimensional \(\mathbb F_q\)-subspace \(U\). Then \(U\) is invariant under the field \(\mathbb F_q(a)\), so \([\mathbb F_q(a):\mathbb F_q]\) divides two. Nonidentity excludes degree one, giving \(\mathbb F_q(a)=\mathbb F_{q^2}\), the unique quadratic subfield of \(E\). Consequently \(U=u\mathbb F_{q^2}\) for some \(u\in E^\times\), and its full stabilizer is
\[
H=\mathbb F_{q^2}^\times/\mathbb F_q^\times\le G_q,
\qquad |H|=q+1.
\]
Conversely, every such \(\mathbb F_{q^2}\)-line is fixed by \(H\). Exactly \(q^2+1\) of these two-dimensional subspaces exist; they form the Desarguesian line spread. Every other two-dimensional subspace has trivial stabilizer. In particular, the subgroup generated by all elements having fixed points on \(|\mathcal B_q|\) is precisely \(H\): an element fixing a point in the relative interior of a simplex preserves its vertex types, so it fixes every vertex of that simplex.

**2. Orbit cells.** The number of projective points, and also hyperplanes, is \(N\), yielding one orbit each. The total number of projective lines is \((q^2+1)(q^2+q+1)\). The \(q^2+1\) spread lines form one orbit, each with stabilizer \(H\); the remaining \(q(q+1)(q^2+1)=qN\) lines form \(q\) free orbits. Hence there are \(q+3\) vertex orbits.

Every one-simplex is a flag of one of the types point–line, point–plane, or line–plane. Each contains a point or hyperplane, so its stabilizer is trivial. There are \(q^2+q+1\) lines through any point, \(q^2+q+1\) planes through any point, and \(q+1\) planes through any line. Counting incidences and dividing by \(N\) gives \(q^2+q+1\) orbit edges of each type, for \(f_1=3(q^2+q+1)\). A two-simplex is a complete point–line–plane flag, and again has trivial stabilizer. The number of these flags is \(N(q^2+q+1)(q+1)\), giving \(f_2=(q^2+q+1)(q+1)\).

Since all simplex stabilizers fix their simplices pointwise, the orbits of open simplices define a finite two-dimensional CW structure on the genuine quotient. Its Euler characteristic is
\[
\chi(Y_q)=f_0-f_1+f_2
=q+3+(q-2)(q^2+q+1)=q^3-q^2+1.
\]

**3. Fundamental group.** The finite spherical building \(|\mathcal B_q|\) is homotopy equivalent, by the Solomon–Tits theorem, to a wedge of \(q^6\) copies of \(S^2\); it is in particular path-connected and simply connected. Armstrong's orbit-space theorem applies to this finite simplicial group action and identifies
\[
\pi_1(Y_q)\cong G_q/\langle\text{elements with fixed points}\rangle
=G_q/H\cong\mathbb Z/(q^2+1).
\]
The first integral homology is its abelianization, hence the same cyclic group.

**4. Second homology and vanishing.** The rational first Betti number is zero because \(H_1\) is finite, and \(Y_q\) is connected. As \(Y_q\) is a two-dimensional finite CW complex, its second cellular homology is a subgroup of the free abelian group \(C_2\), hence is free; all higher homology vanishes. The Euler characteristic identity yields
\[
\operatorname{rank}H_2(Y_q;\mathbb Z)=\chi(Y_q)-1=q^3-q^2=q^2(q-1).
\]
Together these facts prove the theorem.

## Relationship to prior work

Drudge's study of Singer orbits proves general flat-orbit and stabilizer formulas, including the unique spread orbit; Giuzzi and Sonnino use these orbits and incidence structures for finite-geometric coding. These are prior geometric ingredients, not claimed as new here. Armstrong's classical theorem identifies fundamental groups of orbit spaces after the stabilizer subgroup is known. Solomon–Tits supplies the building's simply connected topology. The present result combines a complete flag-orbit count, fixed-simplex classification, and those topological tools to determine the **entire integral homology and fundamental group of the geometric Singer quotient for every prime power**. A separately documented binary special case is already known and is not claimed anew. A general full integral-homotopy classification is not asserted.

## Limitations

This theorem does not determine the \(\pi_1\)-action on \(\pi_2\), the second homotopy group as a module, attaching-map equivalence classes, cohomology operations, or a Moore-space wedge decomposition. It concerns the full projective Singer group; no result for arbitrary proper subgroups is asserted. The corroborating computation was run only at \(q=2,3\). The novelty comparison is relative to inspected sources and cannot exclude unindexed equivalent work.
