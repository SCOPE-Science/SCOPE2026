# Six two-spheres in the transpose quotient of the four-by-four chessboard complex

## Finding
Let \(\Delta_{4,4}\) be the simplicial complex of nonattacking rook placements on a full four-by-four board, and let \(\tau(i,j)=(j,i)\) transpose board coordinates. The **ordinary geometric orbit space** satisfies
\[
|\Delta_{4,4}|/\langle\tau\rangle\simeq\bigvee^{6} S^2.
\]
In particular, the orbit space is simply connected; its only positive-degree integral homology is \(H_2\cong\mathbb Z^6\), without torsion. This is an assertion about the orbit space, not the fixed-point set or homotopy quotient.

## Assumptions and scope
The board has row and column sets both equal to \(\{1,2,3,4\}\). A face is a set of squares containing at most one square in each row and column. The induced involution on the geometric realization sends each rook vertex \((i,j)\) to \((j,i)\). All homology groups use integer coefficients unless otherwise indicated. This is a rank-four result, with no assertion for larger boards.

## Proof
**1. Simple connectivity.** The classical chessboard-complex connectivity theorem [1], restated in [2, Section 1], says that \(\Delta_{m,n}\) is \((\nu-2)\)-connected for \(\nu=\min\{m,n,\lfloor(m+n+1)/3\rfloor\}\). Here \(\nu=3\), so \(|\Delta_{4,4}|\) is simply connected. The involution fixes the vertex \((1,1)\). By Armstrong's orbit-space fundamental-group theorem [4], \(\pi_1(|\Delta_{4,4}|/\langle\tau\rangle)\) is the cyclic group of order two modulo the normal subgroup generated by elements having fixed points. Thus this fundamental group is trivial.

**2. An exact integral quotient chain model.** A simplex of the barycentric subdivision is a strictly increasing chain \(F_0\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq F_k\) of nonempty rook faces. Transposition preserves the cardinality of each \(F_i\); consequently no nontrivial element can reverse the vertex order of a flag. The quotient is a finite \(\Delta\)-complex whose oriented \(k\)-cells are transposition orbits of these flags. Its integral cellular boundary is
\[
\partial[F_0<\cdots<F_k]=\sum_{t=0}^{k}(-1)^t[F_0<\cdots<\widehat{F_t}<\cdots<F_k].
\]
The brackets on the right mean the orbit of the shortened flag. No extra orientation sign or orbit-size multiplier is introduced: every simplex stabilizer acts pointwise on that simplex.

The original complex has \((16,72,96,24)\) nonempty faces by dimension. Exhaustively generating all nonempty flags and identifying each with its transpose produces the quotient cellular counts \((125,586,768,300)\) in dimensions zero through three. The stated algorithm, fully specified and implemented in `verify.py`, constructs all integer boundary matrices, checks \(\partial^2=0\), then cancels a pair of adjacent-degree generators only when their boundary coefficient is exactly \(+1\) or \(-1\). Such a cancellation uses unimodular changes of basis over \(\mathbb Z\) and splits off an acyclic two-term summand. The exact counts of these unit cancellations are \(124\) in degrees one-to-zero, \(462\) in degrees two-to-one, and \(300\) in degrees three-to-two. The remaining chain complex has exactly one degree-zero generator and six degree-two generators, with all differentials zero. Therefore
\[
H_0\cong\mathbb Z,\qquad H_2\cong\mathbb Z^6,\qquad H_i=0\quad(i>0,\ i\ne2).
\]
The integer unit pivots are essential: rational rank or finite-field enumeration alone would not rule out integral torsion.

**3. Homotopy type.** Since the orbit space is simply connected, the Hurewicz map \(\pi_2\to H_2\) is an isomorphism. Choose six based two-sphere maps representing a basis of \(H_2\). Their wedge induces an integral homology equivalence \(\bigvee^6 S^2\to |\Delta_{4,4}|/\langle\tau\rangle\). Both spaces are simply connected CW complexes, so the homological Whitehead theorem makes it a homotopy equivalence.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py` with Python's standard library. It reconstructs all \(208\) nonempty rook faces, all quotient flag-cell orbits, and the signed integral matrices. It verifies all boundary compositions, independently computes the mod-two boundary ranks \((124,462,300)\), and performs exact unimodular chain cancellations leaving residual ranks \((1,0,6,0)\). The terminal marker is `VERIFY_OK`.

As a separate numerical check, the geometric fixed-point space has face counts \((10,21,10,1)\) in its orbit-of-squares triangulation, hence Euler characteristic \(-2\). The original Euler characteristic is \(16\). The involution Burnside formula gives quotient Euler characteristic \((16-2)/2=7\), agreeing with both the quotient-cell alternating sum \(125-586+768-300\) and the claimed six-sphere wedge. The fixed-point face count is only a check, not the basis of the torsion calculation.

## Relationship to prior work
The classical literature establishes the chessboard complex's connectivity and its non-equivariant rational or integral homology [1–3]. Those facts supply the simply-connected covering complex but not the integral orbit-space attaching information. The known transpose **fixed-point-space** classification for graph boards addresses \(|\Delta|^{\tau}\), which is a different functor from the ordinary quotient \(|\Delta|/\langle\tau\rangle\). A previously recorded three-local computation for the five-by-five transpose quotient likewise does not determine the four-by-four integral homotopy type. The present claim is confined to the latter and credits all of these antecedents.

## Limitations
This calculation does not imply a classification for \(\Delta_{n,n}\) at \(n\ge5\), does not determine a homeomorphism type or an equivariant homotopy equivalence, and does not replace independent mathematical review. The unit-pivot checker certifies finite chain algebra; the simple-connectivity step invokes the published connectivity theorem and Armstrong's theorem. An equivalent unindexed orbit-space calculation could affect publication priority.
