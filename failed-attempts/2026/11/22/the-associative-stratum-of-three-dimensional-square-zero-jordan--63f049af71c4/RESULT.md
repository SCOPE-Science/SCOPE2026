# The associative stratum of three-dimensional square-zero Jordan algebras is a Veronese cone

## Finding
Let \(K\) be any field of characteristic two, let \(V\) be a three-dimensional \(K\)-vector space, and fix an ordered basis \(e_1,e_2,e_3\). Every bilinear product on \(V\) that is commutative and satisfies \(x^2=0\) is encoded uniquely by the matrix
\[
M=[\,e_2e_3\mid e_3e_1\mid e_1e_2\,]\in\operatorname{Mat}_{3\times3}(K),
\]
where columns give coordinates in this basis. Such a product is associative **if and only if** \(M\) is symmetric of rank at most one.

Consequently, when \(K\) is algebraically closed, the **reduced** associative-product locus within the nine-dimensional affine space of square-zero Jordan structures is the affine cone over the quadratic Veronese surface in \(\mathbb P^5\). Its reduced coordinate ideal in the nine matrix entries is generated by the three equations \(M_{ij}=M_{ji}\) for \(i<j\), together with all \(2\times2\) minors of \(M\). The locus is geometrically irreducible of dimension three, has projective degree four, and has standard-graded coordinate-ring Hilbert series
\[
\frac{1+3t}{(1-t)^3}.
\]
Its only geometric singular point is the zero multiplication.

Over any finite field \(\mathbb F_q\) of characteristic two, exactly \(q^3\) of the \(q^9\) labelled square-zero products are associative, a proportion \(q^{-6}\). The isomorphism classification of these associative products (zero and a single nonzero isomorphism class) is prior work and is not claimed as new.

## Assumptions and scope
All algebras are bilinear and not assumed unital. The condition \(x^2=0\) for every \(x\) implies commutativity in characteristic two because \((x+y)^2=x^2+xy+yx+y^2=0\). Conversely commutativity and zero basis squares imply \(x^2=0\). The nine free structure constants are the three mixed basis products.

The matrix characterization is valid over arbitrary characteristic-two fields, including nonperfect fields and \(\mathbb F_2\). The geometric description, dimension, degree and singular-locus assertions concern the **reduced** algebraic set after base extension to an algebraic closure. They do **not** assert that the possibly nonreduced scheme defined by the raw associativity equations has no nilpotent structure.

## Proof
**1. Vanishing of triple products in dimension three.** In a commutative associative square-zero algebra, suppose \(xyz\ne0\). The seven elements
\[
x,\ y,\ z,\ xy,\ xz,\ yz,\ xyz
\]
are linearly independent. Indeed, multiply a hypothetical linear relation by \(yz\), \(xz\) and \(xy\) to eliminate successively the coefficients of \(x,y,z\): because multiplication is associative and each square vanishes, each such product isolates the corresponding coefficient times \(xyz\). Next multiply the remaining relation by \(z,y,x\), respectively, to eliminate the coefficients of \(xy,xz,yz\). Finally the remaining coefficient of \(xyz\) must vanish. Thus an associative square-zero algebra with any nonzero threefold product has dimension at least seven. In the present dimension three, \(A^3=0\) and therefore \(A^2\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}(A)\).

**2. Annihilator and alternating form.** If the product is nonzero, its annihilator is nonzero. It cannot have dimension two or three: multiplication descends to an alternating map \(\Lambda^2(A/\operatorname{Ann}(A))\to A\), and this exterior square vanishes for a quotient of dimension at most one. Hence the annihilator is a line \(Kw\), and \(A^2=Kw\). Write \(xy=\beta(x,y)w\). The form \(\beta\) is a nonzero alternating bilinear form with radical exactly \(Kw\). Any such form on a three-dimensional space is, for the chosen volume determinant, of the form
\[
\beta(x,y)=c\det(w,x,y),\qquad c\in K^\times.
\]
Consequently
\[
xy=c\det(w,x,y)w.
\]
Writing \(w=(w_1,w_2,w_3)^T\), one obtains \(M=cww^T\), a symmetric matrix of rank one. The zero algebra corresponds to \(M=0\).

**3. Converse over every field.** Every rank-one symmetric matrix over a field can be written \(M=cww^T\) for nonzero \(w\) and \(c\ne0\); no square-root assumption is required. Such a matrix defines precisely the preceding product, whose image is contained in \(Kw\) and whose multiplication by \(w\) is zero. Thus every product of this form satisfies \(A^3=0\), hence is associative. The matrix equivalence is proved in both directions.

**4. Reduced geometry.** In six symmetric coordinates, the equations for matrices of rank at most one are the \(2\times2\) minors. Their prime ideal is the kernel of the substitution
\[
M_{ij}\longmapsto w_iw_j
\]
into \(K[w_1,w_2,w_3]\). To see generation of the kernel, each product of symmetric-coordinate variables specifies a pairing of an even multiset of indices. Any two pairings of the same multiset are connected by elementary partner swaps, and each such swap is precisely a \(2\times2\) minor relation. Hence the minors generate the entire kernel, which is prime because the image ring is a domain. Over an algebraically closed field, its image is the second Veronese subring \(\bigoplus_{d\ge0}K[w_1,w_2,w_3]_{2d}\); this identifies the reduced variety with the affine cone over \(v_2(\mathbb P^2)\), with the three additional linear symmetry relations inside the original nine-coordinate space. Its degree-\(d\) component has dimension \(\binom{2d+2}{2}\). Summing yields \((1+3t)/(1-t)^3\), dimension three, and projective degree \(1+3=4\). Every nonzero rank-one symmetric matrix has a nonzero diagonal entry. On the chart \(M_{11}\ne0\), the other symmetric coordinates are determined freely by \(M_{11}\), \(M_{12}\), \(M_{13}\) via \(M_{22}=M_{12}^2/M_{11}\), \(M_{23}=M_{12}M_{13}/M_{11}\), \(M_{33}=M_{13}^2/M_{11}\); this chart is \(\mathbb G_m\times\mathbb A^2\), hence smooth. Permuting indices covers the punctured cone. At the vertex all six symmetric coordinate classes survive in the Zariski tangent space, exceeding dimension three. Therefore the vertex is the unique geometric singular point.

**5. Finite-field census.** In \(\mathbb F_q\), each one-dimensional image line \(Kw\) gives \(q-1\) distinct nonzero matrices \(cww^T\), and distinct lines give disjoint sets. There are \((q^3-1)/(q-1)\) lines. Thus the nonzero associative products number \(q^3-1\), and including zero gives exactly \(q^3\). Since there are \(q^9\) unrestricted square-zero products, the density is \(q^{-6}\).

## Verification
The included standalone `verify.py` uses independently implemented finite-field operations and checks every possible nine-coefficient square-zero product over \(\mathbb F_2\) and \(\mathbb F_4\). It evaluates the basis associativity equations and separately tests symmetry and all \(2\times2\) minors. It reports, respectively, 512 and 262,144 tested products, 8 and 64 associative products, and zero discrepancies. Execute `python3 verify.py` with Python 3 (standard library only). These finite checks support, but do not replace, the all-field argument.

## Relationship to prior work
Hernández, Martin, Rodrigues and Gomes Silva (arXiv:2611.00315v1, publicly posted 30 October 2026) establish in Theorem 6.12 that the full three-dimensional characteristic-two square-zero Jordan component is affine nine-space. Their Sections 3 and 6 give the earlier isomorphism and degeneration classification; in particular the nonzero associative square-zero isomorphism class and its zero degeneration are **not** new. Cedilnik and Jerman (2020) classify all three-dimensional zeropotent algebras over arbitrary fields. The present result isolates the associative **embedded** stratum and identifies its reduced defining ideal and precise Veronese geometry, including degree, Hilbert series, singularity and all-finite-even-field labelled counts. Neither cited classification directly states this determinantal embedded stratum.

## Limitations
No assertion is made about the nonreduced scheme structure of the raw associativity-equation ideal, irreducible components beyond the square-zero component, higher dimensions or odd characteristic. The affine cone has only one nonzero isomorphism class, as previously known, but its intrinsic embedded geometry is additional information. Historical priority of the geometric refinement is subject to unindexed literature or an implicit general theorem on spaces of alternating products; failure to locate such a statement is not a uniqueness proof.
