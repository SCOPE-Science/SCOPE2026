# The exposed-vertex core and exact flip geometry of near-perfect matchings in trees

## Finding
Let \(T\) be any finite tree of odd order admitting a matching covering all but one vertex. Write \(D\) for the vertices that can be left uncovered by such a matching, \(A=N_T(D)\), and \(C=V(T)\setminus(D\cup A)\). A *flip* removes one matched edge and inserts an edge incident with the formerly uncovered vertex, leaving exactly one vertex uncovered. Let \(\mathcal F(T)\) be the graph of near-perfect matchings under these flips.

**Canonical core theorem.** The induced tree \(T[D\cup A]\) is the graph obtained by subdividing **every** edge of a uniquely determined tree \(H_T\) once. The original vertices of this subdivision are exactly \(D\); its subdivision vertices are exactly \(A\). Each component of \(T[C]\) has even order, admits a unique perfect matching, and is attached to the core by exactly one edge whose endpoint in the core belongs to \(A\). Conversely, any tree made from the once-subdivision of an arbitrary finite tree \(H\) by attaching disjoint even-order perfectly matchable trees to subdivision vertices, each through one attachment edge, has exactly this core, and its near-perfect matchings are indexed by the vertices of \(H\). For a one-vertex \(H\), the construction gives the one-vertex tree.

**Exact flip geometry and realization.** Mapping a near-perfect matching to its unique uncovered vertex is a canonical graph isomorphism
\[
\mathcal F(T)\cong H_T.
\]
For any two near-perfect matchings \(M_u,M_v\) with uncovered vertices \(u,v\in D\), their minimum number of flips is
\[
\operatorname{dist}_{\mathcal F(T)}(M_u,M_v)=\tfrac12\operatorname{dist}_T(u,v).
\]
In particular, the nonempty flip graphs of near-perfect matchings on trees are **exactly all finite trees** up to graph isomorphism, and their full distance distributions are recoverable from the exposed-vertex distances in the input tree. This is a classification of the flip graphs and their canonical cores, not merely of the number of near-perfect matchings.

## Assumptions and scope
All graphs are finite, undirected, simple. A tree is connected and acyclic; the one-vertex tree is allowed. The input tree has odd order and at least one near-perfect matching. Empty configuration spaces (when no such matching exists) are excluded. The neighborhood \(N_T(D)\) is the *external* neighborhood. A fringe component of \(T[C]\) is required to have a perfect matching as a graph in its own right; the attachment edge is never used in a near-perfect matching. The distance formula is stated for all ordered pairs of configurations, including identical ones.

## Proof
**1. Unique matching per uncovered vertex.** For each \(u\in D\), \(T-u\) has a perfect matching. A forest has at most one perfect matching: the symmetric difference of two distinct perfect matchings would contain an alternating cycle. Therefore its near-perfect matching \(M_u\) is unique.

**2. Exact path difference and flip metric.** Fix distinct \(u,v\in D\). Every component of \(M_u\triangle M_v\) is an alternating path or alternating even cycle. A cycle is impossible in a tree. Its only possible endpoints are the two distinct uncovered vertices \(u,v\), since every other vertex is covered by both matchings. Thus the symmetric difference is the unique \(u\)-to-\(v\) path in \(T\); it has even length, say \(2\ell\), with edges alternating between \(M_v\) and \(M_u\). Successively exchanging the \(\ell\) matched path edges from the \(u\)-end produces \(M_v\) in \(\ell\) flips. Conversely, one flip changes the uncovered vertex along a two-edge path in \(T\), so by the triangle inequality no flip sequence can use fewer than \(\operatorname{dist}_T(u,v)/2\) moves. This proves the distance formula and connectivity of \(\mathcal F(T)\). Moreover every even-indexed vertex on the alternating path can be exposed by a partial flip sequence.

**3. The exposure core is a once-subdivided tree.** Since all exposed vertices have pairwise even distance, \(D\) lies in one bipartition class of \(T\). Thus \(D\) is independent and its external neighborhood \(A\) is in the other class. If \(a\in A\) is adjacent to \(u\in D\), then in \(M_u\) vertex \(a\) is matched to a different neighbor \(w\). Flipping \(aw\) to \(au\) leaves \(w\) exposed; hence \(w\in D\). Thus every \(a\in A\) has at least two neighbors in \(D\).

For any \(u,v\in D\), the alternating path between them has even-indexed vertices in \(D\) and odd-indexed vertices in \(A\). It follows that \(T[D\cup A]\) is connected; being an induced subgraph of a tree, it is a tree. Its edges all run from \(D\) to \(A\). Every near-perfect matching covers \(|D|-1\) vertices of \(D\), and these must be matched to distinct vertices of \(A\). Hence \(|D|-1\le |A|\). On the other hand, the core has \(|D|+|A|-1\) edges and each \(a\in A\) has at least two neighbors in \(D\), so \(2|A|\le |D|+|A|-1\), implying \(|A|\le|D|-1\). Equality holds throughout. Therefore every \(a\in A\) has *exactly* two neighbors in \(D\). Suppressing all vertices of \(A\) creates a simple connected acyclic graph \(H_T\) on \(D\), and the original core is exactly its once-subdivision. The graph \(H_T\) is unique because \(D,A\) are defined intrinsically.

**4. Forced fringe.** The equality \(|D|=|A|+1\) shows that every near-perfect matching pairs *all* vertices of \(A\) with \(|D|-1\) vertices of \(D\). Therefore every vertex of \(C\) is matched within \(C\). Each component of \(T[C]\) consequently has a perfect matching and even order, and that perfect matching is unique since the component is a tree. Connectedness of \(T\) and acyclicity imply that each component of \(T[C]\) has exactly one attachment edge to the core. That edge cannot meet a vertex of \(D\), by the definition of \(A=N_T(D)\), so it meets a vertex of \(A\).

**5. Canonical isomorphism.** Two distinct matching configurations are adjacent in \(\mathcal F(T)\) if and only if their exposed vertices are at distance two in \(T\): the forward direction follows from one flip; in the reverse direction, the unique two-edge exposed-to-exposed path is the symmetric difference and hence one flip. In the core, exposed vertices are the original vertices of a once-subdivided tree; distance two between them is exactly adjacency in \(H_T\). Thus \(\mathcal F(T)\cong H_T\), with exact metric as already proved.

**6. Converse and realization.** Start with any finite tree \(H\); subdivide each edge once, retaining the original vertices as \(D_0\) and the added vertices as \(A_0\). Attach to vertices of \(A_0\), through separate single edges, any disjoint even-order trees that admit perfect matchings. For each \(u\in D_0\), root \(H\) at \(u\), match every subdivision vertex on edge \(xy\) to the endpoint farther from \(u\), and add the unique perfect matchings of all attached fringe components. This is a near-perfect matching exposing \(u\). Conversely, there are \(|D_0|\) original vertices but only \(|D_0|-1\) subdivision vertices, and originals have no neighbors outside the subdivisions. Therefore any near-perfect matching must expose an original vertex and match all subdivision vertices to originals. Fringe edges are then forced internally. By uniqueness for a specified exposed vertex, these are all the near-perfect matchings. Their flip graph is \(H\), and the attachment decomposition is exactly the canonical one.

## Verification
The independent finite checker `verify.py` enumerates all labelled trees of odd orders \(1,3,5,7\), tests which have near-perfect matchings via forced leaf matching and a second direct edge-subset enumeration, computes every flip graph and its graph distances, checks the core/fringe decomposition and validates the realization of all labelled trees of orders at most six. It additionally tests 1,500 deterministically seeded odd-order trees of orders 9–19. Run `python3 verify.py`; the expected final line is `VERIFY_OK`. These finite checks corroborate the general argument and do not imply infinite exhaustive verification.

## Relationship to prior work
Aichholzer, Brenner, Dorfer, Hoang, Perz, Rieck and Verciani (arXiv:2508.18457, first submitted August 25, 2025) define the identical flip operation and prove general connectivity criteria and a linear diameter upper bound. Their discussion of alternating paths supplies an established ingredient; the all-tree canonical core, exact metric and realization classification are the additional claims here. Dorfer (arXiv:2602.09573, first submitted February 10, 2026) proves higher-hardness results for general input graphs, rather than a structural classification for tree inputs. Bousquet, Hatanaka, Ito and Mühlenthaler (arXiv:1812.05419) study broader matching token jumps, and their no-detour observation for matchings with even-hole-free line graphs implies the half-symmetric-difference metric on forest hosts. **This metric identity is prior-implied, not claimed as independently original.**

Cambie, McCoy, Sharma, Wagner and Yap, *Trees Maximizing the Number of Almost-Perfect Matchings*, already proved that an odd-order tree has at most \((|V(T)|+1)/2\) near-perfect matchings, with equality precisely for once-subdivided trees. **That extremal count and equality characterization are prior results, not part of the asserted novelty.** The structural contribution assessed here instead determines the entire flip graph for *every* tree with a near-perfect matching, identifies the canonical forced fringe and proves realization; the exact metric is included as a complete description and also follows from established no-detour methods. Brenner et al. (arXiv:2607.04687) treat Hamiltonicity primarily in complete and complete-bipartite hosts; their results do not classify tree-hosted flip graphs.

## Limitations
The classification is restricted to simple trees with a nonempty near-perfect-matching space; graphs with cycles may have multiple matchings exposing the same vertex and genuine alternating-cycle obstructions. The theorem says nothing about labelled matching flips or weighted/directed flips. Historical priority remains provisional because the tree-specific statement may have appeared under matching-exchange or Gallai–Edmonds terminology. A general matching decomposition and a prior alternating-path lemma give related ingredients, but no inspected primary source states the combined canonical core and exact tree-hosted flip geometry. Independent external mathematical audit has not been carried out.
