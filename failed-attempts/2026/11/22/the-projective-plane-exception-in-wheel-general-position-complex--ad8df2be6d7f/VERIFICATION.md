# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

For n≥4 the wheel has diameter two; a forbidden rim triple is exactly three consecutive vertices, and the rim part of a hub face is a face of the rim polygon. The complex is the mapping cone of the inclusion of that polygon in Ind₂(C_n), where higher independence means induced components of size at most two. For n≥6 each missing triangle on 0,1,2 fills with a cone to 4 after rotation: for {0,1} forbidden completions are n−1 and 2; for {0,2} only 1; for {1,2} only 0 and 3. The 1-skeleton is complete and its loops are normally generated by triangles. Thus the rim complex is simply connected and its attaching circle is null, adding S². Deshpande–Singh Theorem 4.6 with d=4 gives the stated cycle terms. At n=3 the wheel is K₄ and the complex a simplex. At n=4 collapse each hub triangle along its free rim edge, leaving the spoke tree plus two diagonals, a rank-two graph. At n=5 every four-subset of the rim has a forbidden run; the listed triangles give rim-edge multiplicity one, diagonal multiplicity two, and the link of 0 is the path 1–3–2–4, with other links given by rotation. The five-rim complex is a compact connected surface with one boundary and Euler characteristic zero, hence a Möbius band. The hub disk caps that boundary, producing a six-vertex RP² with H₁=Z/2. No finite Morse experiment is used to justify the infinite claims.
