# Triangle-free components control the integral regularization gap in first path homology

## Finding

Let \(G\) be any finite simple undirected graph, and \(D(G)\) its loopless *symmetric orientation*: every undirected edge \(\{u,v\}\) is replaced by both arcs \(u\to v\) and \(v\to u\). Let \(H_1^{\mathrm{nr}}(D(G);\mathbb Z)\) and \(H_1^{\mathrm{reg}}(D(G);\mathbb Z)\) denote the nonregular and regular integral GLMY path-homology groups, respectively. Write \(\mathcal C(G)\) for the connected components which contain at least one edge and contain no triangle. There is a **natural short exact sequence**

\[
0\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{C\in\mathcal C(G)}\mathbb Z\,\beta_C\longrightarrow H_1^{\mathrm{nr}}(D(G);\mathbb Z)\xrightarrow{q_G} H_1^{\mathrm{reg}}(D(G);\mathbb Z)\longrightarrow0.
\]

For any edge \(\{u,v\}\) of \(C\), the generator \(\beta_C\) is represented by the directed backtrack cycle \(B_{uv}:=e_{uv}+e_{vu}\). Its class is independent of the chosen edge. Thus regularization on first integral path homology is an isomorphism precisely when every component containing an edge also contains a triangle. In particular, the **integral free-rank difference** is exactly \( |\mathcal C(G)| \). No universal characteristic-two dimension formula is asserted.

The naturality assertion holds for inclusions of undirected graphs, including nested filtrations on a fixed vertex set: the map on kernels sends the generator of any triangle-free edge-containing component to that of its containing component if the latter is triangle-free, and to zero if that containing component has acquired a triangle. If two such components merge without a triangle, their two generators map to the same generator. Consequently these rules specify the entire kernel persistence module; they do **not** claim that a barcode of the middle group is a union of barcodes of kernel and quotient.

## Assumptions and scope

A finite graph may be disconnected and may have isolated vertices, but has no loops or multiple edges. Chains have coefficients in \(\mathbb Z\) unless another coefficient field is explicitly mentioned. Allowed degree-\(p\) paths in \(D(G)\) have consecutive arcs; the nonregular boundary is the alternating deletion boundary with repeats retained, whereas the regular boundary projects terms with consecutive repeated vertices to zero. As usual, \(\Omega_p\) consists of allowed chains whose boundary is allowed, and \(H_1=\ker(\partial_1)/\operatorname{im}(\partial_2)\). Both chain theories have the same degree-zero and degree-one groups and boundaries, and the nonregular degree-two chains embed in the regular ones. The induced map \(q_G\) is therefore canonical and surjective. The reduced and ordinary conventions agree in degree one.

## Proof

**Step 1: decompose invariant two-paths integrally.** An allowed two-path is \(e_{abc}\), where \(\{a,b\}\) and \(\{b,c\}\) are edges. Its boundary is
\[
\partial e_{abc}=e_{bc}-e_{ac}+e_{ab}.
\]
For distinct \(a,c\) joined by an edge, this two-path is invariant in both theories (an *oriented triangle*). If distinct \(a,c\) are not adjacent, the forbidden \(e_{ac}\) terms must cancel: the corresponding invariant sublattice is generated integrally by differences \(e_{abc}-e_{adc}\) over alternate intermediate vertices \(b,d\) (the *long-square* generators). Finally, when \(a=c\), the two-paths \(e_{aba}\) are allowed. In the regular theory their irregular shortcut \(e_{aa}\) is projected away, so each is invariant. In the nonregular theory the coefficient sum of all \(e_{aba}\) at a fixed base vertex \(a\) must vanish, so the invariant sublattice is generated by differences \(e_{aba}-e_{aca}\). These assertions exhaust the allowed two-path basis, grouped by ordered endpoints; all cancellations are integral, without denominator or field assumptions.

**Step 2: identify the kernel generators.** Under the degree-two boundary, the two first kinds of generators lie in both complexes. The extra regular generators \(e_{aba}\) have boundary \(B_{ab}=e_{ab}+e_{ba}\). Hence the quotient of the regular two-boundary group by the nonregular two-boundary group, which is the kernel of \(q_G\), is generated by the classes of all \(B_{ab}\). Whenever two edges \(\{a,b\}\) and \(\{a,c\}\) share an endpoint,
\[
\partial(e_{aba}-e_{aca})=B_{ab}-B_{ac},
\]
so their classes agree. Connectedness of the line graph of each edge-containing graph component makes all the backtracks within that component equal.

**Step 3: triangles kill the backtrack, and no other relation kills it.** If \(a,b,c\) are distinct mutually adjacent vertices, the two nonregularly admissible triangle paths satisfy
\[
\partial(e_{abc}+e_{bac})=B_{ab}.
\]
The common backtrack class of that component therefore vanishes. Conversely, suppose a component \(C\) has at least one edge and no triangles. Define \(s_C\) on the integral one-chain group by summing coefficients of all arcs inside \(C\), assigning zero to arcs in other components. For any nonregular invariant two-chain in \(C\), Step 1 shows it is a combination of long-square and backtrack-difference generators: all have \(s_C\)-value zero under boundary. Thus \(s_C\) descends to a homomorphism \(H_1^{\mathrm{nr}}\to\mathbb Z\) with \(s_C(B_{uv})=2\). No nonzero multiple of \(B_{uv}\) can be a nonregular boundary. The independent coefficient-sum maps on different triangle-free components rule out intercomponent relations. The kernel is therefore precisely the indicated free abelian group.

**Step 4: ranks and inclusions.** The rank statement follows from the integral short exact sequence. The constructions commute with graph inclusions. An old generator \(B_{uv}\) is represented by the identical directed cycle in the larger graph, so its image is the larger component's generator if there is no triangle, and zero otherwise. These observations prove the natural exact sequence and all claimed transition rules.

## Verification

An independent implementation in `check.py` enumerates every allowed directed two-path, constructs the full boundary and the nonallowed boundary over \(\mathbb F_2\), and computes degree-one ranks using exact bitwise elimination. It does not use the triangle/line-graph proof reduction. It checked all \(1+2+8+64+1024=1099\) labelled simple graphs on at most five vertices and four additional six- or seven-vertex examples: **1103 exact comparisons**, all passing. Reproduce with `python3 check.py`; the recorded output is `check-output.txt`. These finite comparisons are corroboration, not proof for arbitrary graphs or proof over \(\mathbb Z\); the integral claim rests on Steps 1–4.

## Relationship to prior work

Grigor'yan, Lin, Muranov, and Yau introduced path homology and its regular variant. Chaplin (2022), especially Proposition 3.15, Proposition 3.17, Remark 3.18, and Example 3.21, establishes the chain-complex inclusion, the directed triangle/long-square/backtrack generators and differences, and individual cycle examples. These are the essential **prior ingredients** for Steps 1–2; neither these ingredients nor the two-vertex discrepancy are new. The stated contribution is a **global, exact integral kernel classification for all symmetric finite digraphs together with its functorial component-merging and triangle-death behavior**, rather than the inclusion or a single example. The 2026 paper of Muranov and Muranova motivates comparing homology variants within their path-complex-with-concatenation setting, but does not state this kernel classification. Motif computations in the 2021 Applied Network Science paper distinguish regular and nonregular conventions but do not give this theorem. Standard motif results imply much of the proof; priority risk from other equivalent formulations remains.

## Limitations

Only first path homology of finite symmetric loopless digraphs is classified. For general directed graphs, reciprocal edges need not form connected components in the required manner and the statement can fail. The short exact sequence is not claimed to split canonically or to produce a direct sum of persistence barcodes. The equality in dimensions does not imply equality of full integral groups. No claim about higher path homology is made. Independent mathematical audit and exhaustive priority review have not been performed.
