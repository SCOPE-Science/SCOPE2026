On the two-subset Kneser graph, random-order greedy adsorption produces a full star with probability \((n-3)/(n-2)\), but a maximal three-edge triangle with probability \(1/(n-2)\); the rare, linearly costly failure makes final-size variance grow like \(n\). With unit exponential arrival clocks, triangle outcomes jam at order \(1/n\) time with an explicit two-exponential limit, whereas star outcomes jam at order \(\log n\) time with a Gumbel limit.
