An edge consumes exactly the 2r colors, so the supports must be complementary and all choices of signs in the two shores are allowed. There are no 2-simplices. With edges oriented from one shore to the other, integral cycles are precisely matrices with zero row and column sums, freely generated by outer products of differences from two chosen basepoints. This gives the ranks and the negative transpose sign for a shore exchange. On each shore the (C₂)^r action is regular; for its augmentation ideal J the coinvariant relations generate J², and g↦g−1 mod J² identifies the quotient with the abelianization, with inverse ∑n_g g↦∑n_g[g]. The two independent shore actions give the tensor product of these elementary two-groups, hence r² copies per component. Permuting colors is transitive on the resulting basis labels (partition and one coordinate per shore), and the reversal sign disappears mod 2, leaving exactly one copy. On each geometric component an oriented barycentric edge parameter identifies the flip quotient with an interval: edge transitivity has no edge reversals under flips. The full action also identifies complementary parameter values, leaving an interval again, not a loop.
